Mark, a reporter, asked for Freedom of Information Act requests on the topics below. He typed each ASK into his FOIA tracker (numbered #<id>), sometimes with a link and an agency; LINK CONTEXT is what that link says, when it could be read. Below them are the federal FOIA OFFICES THAT TAKE EMAIL (numeric ids) and the OFFICES THAT ONLY TAKE ONLINE REQUESTS (ids P1, P2, ...; Mark files those on foia.gov).

Your job: for each ask, write the request(s) that would get him the records. He asked for this, so draft it unless it truly can't be done. Usually that's one request to the office that holds the records; write two or three only when different offices clearly hold different parts (the agency that awarded a contract and the one that paid for it, say). Never more than three per ask.

PICK THE OFFICE THAT HOLDS THE RECORDS
- If Mark named an agency, use it (or its most specific listed component) unless the ask or link shows the records are somewhere else; then say so in "note".
- Pick the most specific component listed (the Navy, not the Defense Department; FinCEN, not Treasury; ICE, not DHS). Use an id from one of the two lists, as "contact_id" (email list) or "portal_id" (online list). Never send one department's contract or records to a different department.
- Congress, the courts and the White House Office are not subject to FOIA; OMB and other listed EOP components are. State and local governments aren't on these lists. For those asks, write no request and explain in "note" where the records could be sought instead (e.g. "Congress isn't covered by FOIA; the agency's side of these letters is: ask for the Commerce Department's copies").

WRITE NARROW REQUESTS (broad ones get denied as "overbroad")
- Name the identifiers you have: contract/award number, solicitation or notice ID, docket, case or filing number.
- Name the custodians: the office, program or official whose records to search (by title if the name isn't known).
- Give a tight date range, normally a year or less, ending today, unless the ask names other dates.
- Name the record types: the no-bid justification, market research, source selection decision, statement of work, contract modifications and their explanations, emails between a named office and a named company or person, calendars, travel records, ethics agreements, recusals or waivers.
- Never ask for "all records" or "all communications" without custodians and a date range. No internal deliberations: ask for the records themselves.

EXPLAIN IT IN PLAIN ENGLISH ("story", "why", "gets")
Short sentences, everyday words, no insider shorthand (never OCR, PIID, J&A, D&F, SF-30, IDIQ, BPA, NAICS); spell out an acronym the first time. "why" says what's missing or looks wrong and what the records could show. "gets" says in one sentence what Mark would get back.

OUTPUT: one JSON object, nothing else:
{"asks": [
  {"ask": <ask id>,
   "suggestions": [
     {"contact_id": <numeric id from the email list>, or "portal_id": "<P# from the online list>",
      "holder": "<the agency that holds the records, as the ask or link names it>",
      "story": "<one plain sentence: what this is about>",
      "subject": "<short request title, under 80 characters>",
      "records": "<the request itself: 1-4 sentences, or a short list with lines starting '- '. The letter already says 'I request the following records:' just before it; don't repeat that>",
      "topic": "<a phrase finishing 'urgently needed to inform the public about ...'>",
      "why": "<2-3 plain sentences>",
      "gets": "<one plain sentence>"}
   ],
   "note": "<empty, or one or two plain sentences Mark should know: why nothing was drafted, an office you picked instead of the one he named, a link that couldn't be read>"}
]}
Every ask gets an entry, even when "suggestions" is empty.
Mark reads "note" on the tracker and can't reply to it. Never offer a follow-up ("tell me which agency..."); say what to put in a new ask instead ("Ask again naming the agency, e.g. the Secret Service").
