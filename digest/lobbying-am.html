New Nonprofit Registers to Lobby for DOJ Employees' Rights and Independence of the Justice Department
A Delaware-based foundation became its own lobbying registrant this week, the only client-and-registrant-in-one filing among 20 new registrations posted in this window.

The JC155 Foundation filed a new lobbying registration posted September 17, describing itself as a "non-profit advocacy for DOJ employees and rule of law." Unlike the other new registrations posted in the same window, JC155 Foundation is registered as both the client and the registrant — meaning it is lobbying directly on its own behalf rather than through a hired firm.

The filing lists two in-house lobbyists, Sara Zdeb and Stacey Young, working four issue areas: Law Enforcement/Crime/Criminal Justice, Government Issues, Civil Rights/Civil Liberties, and Labor Issues/Antitrust/Workplace. Across all four, the filing gives an identical description of the group's lobbying activity: "Rights of federal employees (specifically in regards to DOJ employees), independence of the Department of Justice, politicization of law enforcement."

The registration lists a Wilmington, Delaware address and contact Cary Cheng, and covers the client relationship effective July 20, 2026. No prior lobbying registration for JC155 Foundation appears in today's data; this is a first-time filing for the entity.

20 New Registrations Span Medical Devices, Defense and Trade; Uber Discloses $144,500 in Lobbyist Contributions

The Senate Office of Public Records posted 20 new lobbying registrations in this window (plus the JC155 Foundation filing covered above), led by a run of medical-device clients for Jeffrey J. Kimbell & Associates, Inc. (four new clients: ClearFlow, Proverum Medical, Flex Vascular and HistoSonics) and three new clients for Continental Strategy, LLC. Amkor Technology, Inc. registered to lobby in-house on trade, tariff and semiconductor-manufacturing policy, including export controls and the advanced manufacturing investment credit.

Beyond new business, 18 additional quarterly reports, amendments and terminations posted in the same window — a routine mix, with one item of note: Jeffrey J. Kimbell & Associates, Inc. also filed a third-quarter termination of its registration for Neuralink Corp., alongside terminations for Sky Marketing Corporation and an amendment adding JCyte, Inc.

On the campaign-finance side, six LD-203 filings posted actual contributions (30 others reported none). The largest by far came from Uber Technologies, Inc., whose mid-year report (covering January–June 2026) disclosed $144,500 across 34 items — a mix of its political action committee's FECA contributions to members of both parties and corporate "honorary expense" payments, including $12,000 to the Congressional Black Caucus Foundation honoring Rep. Jim Clyburn and $10,500 to the Independent Women's Forum honoring White House Chief of Staff Susie Wiles. Lobbyist Kyriakos Pagonis of Murray, Montgomery & O'Donnell reported personal contributions to several California House members, including Rep. Jared Huffman.
