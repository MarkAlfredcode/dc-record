Democracy Forward Registers to Lobby Congress on Its Own Behalf, Citing DOJ “Weaponization” and the AG Nomination
The self-described “national legal organization” filed an in-house LDA registration this week disclosing a sweeping slate of oversight issues, effective retroactively to August 1.

Democracy Forward, which describes itself on its own registration as a “national legal organization that advances democracy and social progress,” filed a new Lobbying Disclosure Act registration posted September 15 in which it names itself as both registrant and client — in-house lobbying rather than hiring outside counsel. The registration's effective date is listed as August 1, 2026, meaning the filing catches Congress up on lobbying that has reportedly already been underway for six weeks.

The registration names two lobbyists: Nikki McKinney, whose disclosed covered-official history includes stints as Associate Deputy Secretary at the U.S. Department of Labor (February 2021–January 2025), labor policy director for the Senate Health, Education, Labor and Pensions Committee (May 2018–February 2021), and earlier Department of Labor and Senate Commerce Committee roles dating to 2007; and Megha Patel, who discloses a 2018 internship in Senator Gary Peters's office.

The filing's list of “specific lobbying issues” is unusually pointed for an LDA registration: election protection and election integrity; “Weaponization of the DOJ”; the nomination of the Attorney General; “protection of the civil service”; “government rebuilding efforts”; oversight of the Department of Defense, the Equal Employment Opportunity Commission, the Federal Housing Finance Agency and the Environmental Protection Agency; an Office of Management and Budget proposed rule on federal financial assistance; and the Continuing Appropriations and Extensions Act, 2027 (H.R. 6500). The registration checks 27 separate general issue-area codes, from budget and appropriations to firearms, intelligence and the Constitution, though the client-facing description field for line 9 (client's business or activities) was left blank.

Nothing in the filing states what triggered the registration or which specific legislative vehicles Democracy Forward is working; the document is a disclosure of lobbying activity, not a policy brief, and this newsletter has not reviewed any lobbying contact reports beyond the registration itself.

K Street
12 new lobbying registrations, a Democracy Forward self-filing, and two matching $1,000 checks to a Michigan congressman

Twelve new client registrations posted to the Senate's LDA system overnight, alongside 22 additional filings — quarterly activity reports, amendments and terminations — for existing engagements. Most of the new business is routine: a fintech consumer-lender, a drone maker watching the FY27 defense authorization bill, a candy-store-turned-land-acquisition client, and several municipal and health-reimbursement mandates.

On the money side, six lobbyists or firms filed LD-203 contribution reports; four disclosed no contributions and are omitted below. The two that did report money show The DLM Group and its lobbyist Marcus P. Lubin each separately disclosing an identical $1,000 contribution to the same recipient on the same day — flagged in the rollup below.
