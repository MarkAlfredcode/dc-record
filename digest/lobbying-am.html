A Decade of Missed Disclosures: MALDEF Reveals $802,000 in Gala Honors for Lawmakers and Officials
Eleven catch-up LD-203 filings posted in a single day account for lobbying-related expenses dating back to 2017.

The Mexican American Legal Defense and Educational Fund posted eleven LD-203 lobbying contribution reports on September 25, 2026 — a single-day catch-up covering reporting periods stretching back to 2017, several filed years after their original due dates. Five of the eleven disclose no contributions; the other six report a combined $802,276 in what the forms classify as "Honorary Expenses" — costs the organization says it paid to outside vendors to recognize sitting or former federal officials at its Annual Awards Galas in Washington, Los Angeles and San Antonio.

The honorees named across the six filings: Rep. Lucille Roybal-Allard ($100,961, gala held September 2022 in Washington); Sen. Catherine Cortez Masto and Robert Santos, then-Director of the U.S. Census Bureau, honored jointly ($101,931, April 2023, Washington); Rep. Greg Casar ($104,382, September 2023, San Antonio); Isabel Casillas Guzman, then-Administrator of the U.S. Small Business Administration ($121,458, April 2024, Washington); former Rep. Grace Napolitano, who had left Congress the previous year ($188,573, November 2024, Los Angeles); and Rep. Joaquin Castro ($184,971, April 2025, Washington). Every filing lists the payee only as "various vendors" and the contributor as "self."

None of the six disclosures is a campaign check — Honorary Expenses is a distinct LD-203 category covering event costs a lobbying organization incurs to recognize an official, reportable because the Lobbying Disclosure Act treats picking up that tab like an in-kind gift to the honoree. Filing them years late still puts the underlying transactions on the public record for the first time; MALDEF's registrant contact digitally signed all eleven forms within a few hours on September 25.
