Top Story
One boutique firm's quarterly filings show K Street swarming a housing bill
Thirteen clients, one lobbying firm, nine filings citing the same bill — all posted within a few hours of each other.

Breakaway, a Washington lobbying firm, posted 13 third-quarter Lobbying Disclosure Act activity reports on September 21, one for each of its clients. Nine of the 13 specifically name H.R. 6644, the "21st Century ROAD to Housing Act," as a bill they're lobbying on — an unusually concentrated single-firm push on one piece of legislation in a single reporting batch.

The client list spans the housing industry from multiple angles: mortgage lenders and servicers (PennyMac Financial Services, Rocket Limited Partnership), trade groups (Mortgage Bankers Association, National Rental Home Council, U.S. Mortgage Insurers), an investment firm focused on single-family rentals (Pretium), a public housing authority (San Diego Housing Commission), a think tank (Bipartisan Policy Center Action) and a nonprofit shared-equity homeownership advocate (Grounded Solutions Network).

Not every Breakaway filing is about H.R. 6644. VantageScore's filing covers alternative credit-scoring models, Forbright Bank's covers multifamily financing, and two clients represented through law firm Arnall Golden Gregory — the Local Housing Administrators Coalition and Southwest Housing Compliance Corporation — are focused on HUD's Project Based Contract Administrator policies and public housing authority issues rather than the bill itself.

K Street
No new registrations, but a full slate of quarterlies and a $15,000 day for one Dentons lobbyist

No new lobbying registrations (LD-1 filings) were posted to the Senate's LDA system in this window. Filing activity instead came from 26 quarterly activity reports and amendments, led by Breakaway's 13-client housing push (see Top Story) and six crypto- and derivatives-focused filings from Sobba Public, LLC covering clients including Virtu Financial, LLC, Cboe Global Markets and Copper.co. None of the 26 filings were terminations.

Separately, seven lobbyist and registrant campaign-contribution reports (LD-203) disclosed actual giving rather than the routine "no contributions" boxes most filers check. The largest: Dentons US LLP lobbyist Joe Crowley reported $15,000 in contributions, including $10,000 to the Democratic Congressional Campaign Committee and four smaller checks to House and Senate Democratic campaigns.

One overlap worth flagging: MZ Advising, LLC shows up in both tables below — it filed a Q2 activity report for defense-tech client Tycho AI Inc. on Defense Appropriations and NDAA issues, and separately disclosed $5,000 in contributions to Moody for Florida, Senator Ashley Moody's campaign committee.
