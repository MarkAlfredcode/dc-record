No New Lobbying Registrations or FARA Filings Today, But $261,648 in Lobbyist Contributions Surface in Overdue Mid-Year Reports
The Senate's LDA portal logged zero new client registrations and DOJ's FARA system logged zero new or terminated foreign-agent registrants overnight, but a batch of 33 LD-203 lobbyist contribution reports — most covering the January-June mid-year period — posted weeks after the routine July 30 deadline, disclosing $261,648.54 across 201 itemized contributions.

It was a quiet night for new lobbying and foreign-agent business: no new client registrations hit the Senate's Lobbying Disclosure Act database, and the Justice Department's FARA e-file system recorded neither a new nor a terminated foreign-agent registrant in the coverage window. The volume today came instead from the LD-203 side — the twice-yearly reports in which lobbyists and lobbying firms disclose their own campaign contributions. Of 243 LD-203 filings posted, 207 reported no contributions; the other 33 (after collapsing a few duplicate firm-and-individual filings covering the same checks) itemized $261,648.54 in giving, mostly for the January 1-June 30, 2026 period — a report normally due by July 30.

Cambia Health Solutions' Washington-state PAC was the single largest discloser, funneling $78,100 through lobbyist Megan Hruby to nearly 30 recipients spanning both parties, including House Democratic Leader Rep. Hakeem Jeffries, Sen. Susan Collins, Rep. Steve Scalise, and Sen. Ron Wyden. Sole practitioner C6 Strategies, LLC (lobbyist Dana Hudson) reported identical personal contributions of $3,000 to $3,500 apiece, on matching dates, to eight House Republicans including Rep. David Valadao, Rep. Ken Calvert, and Rep. Jack Bergman — filed twice, once under the firm's name and once under Hudson's, for the same set of checks.

Maryland firm Perry, White, Ross & Jacobson, LLC (lobbyist Lisa Bianco) gave to a slate of Democratic contenders and incumbents, including Rep. Pete Aguilar, Rep. Joe Neguse, and John Hickenlooper's Senate campaign. The Appraisal Institute's PAC spread $29,500 across a bipartisan mix that included Sen. Mike Rounds, Sen. Jim Banks, and Jeffries. Full detail, including every contribution of $2,000 or more, is in the K Street section below.
