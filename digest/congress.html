Top Story

Discharge petition on immigration status bill sits one signature short of 218

With both chambers in brief pro forma sessions, the closest procedural fight is off the floor.

Discharge Petition No. 25, sponsored by Rep. Sylvia R. Garcia through H. Res. 1414, has 217 of the 218 signatures needed. It would force consideration of H.R. 1589, a bill to authorize the cancellation of removal and adjustment of status of certain aliens, and for other purposes. No open petition gained signatures, and none reached 218, since November 3, 2026.

A second petition is two signatures away. Discharge Petition No. 27, sponsored by Rep. Thomas Massie through H. Res. 1430, has 216 of 218 signatures. It would bring up H.R. 185, a bill "to advance responsible policies."

The floor itself was quiet. The House met for two minutes (11:00 a.m. to 11:02 a.m.) with no recorded votes and no quorum calls. The Senate met in a pro forma session lasting under a minute.

On the Record

Pro forma sessions in both chambers; nine committee reports filed, six of them on nuclear policy

The Senate met at 10:31 a.m. in a pro forma session, adjourned 29 seconds later, and returns at 3 p.m. on Monday, November 9, when it will resume the motion to proceed to H.R. 2347, the Survivor Justice Tax Prevention Act, with a cloture vote at 5:30 p.m. The House met from 11:00 a.m. to 11:02 a.m. with no Yea and Nay votes, no recorded votes and no quorum calls, and meets again at noon on Monday with its program to be announced. Neither chamber held committee hearings.

The House received nine committee reports and seven new bills and two resolutions. Six of the nine reports came from the Committee on Energy and Commerce, and five of those six deal with nuclear licensing, oversight or transparency. Every measure from the day's Record follows.

  • H.R. 5723, sponsored by Rep. Mark Takano (D-CA-39) — the FRAUD in VA Disability Exams Act would require the Secretary of Veterans Affairs to identify and report fraud involving disability benefit questionnaire forms. Reported by the Committee on Veterans' Affairs with an amendment (H. Rept. 119-869).
  • H.R. 9605, sponsored by Rep. Glenn Grothman (R-WI-6) — the Less Bureaucracy, Better Foreign Medical Accreditation Act would have the Department of Health and Human Services manage accreditation for foreign medical schools. Reported by the Committee on Education and Workforce with an amendment (H. Rept. 119-870).
  • H.R. 3978, sponsored by Rep. Robert Latta (R-OH-5) — the Nuclear REFUEL (Recycling Efficient Fuels Utilizing Expedited Licensing) Act would amend the Atomic Energy Act of 1954 so that the definition of "production facility" excludes equipment capable of reprocessing spent nuclear fuel in a way that does not separate plutonium from other transuranic elements. Reported by the Committee on Energy and Commerce with an amendment (H. Rept. 119-871).
  • H.R. 5549, sponsored by Rep. H. Griffith (R-VA-9) — the Efficient Nuclear Licensing Hearings Act would provide for more efficient hearings on nuclear facility construction applications. Reported by the Committee on Energy and Commerce (H. Rept. 119-872).
  • H.R. 3100, sponsored by Rep. Russell Fry (R-SC-7) — the CHILD Act of 2026 would amend the National Child Protection Act of 1993 so businesses and organizations working with vulnerable populations can request background checks on their contractors, and on individuals they license or certify to provide care. Reported by the Committee on the Judiciary with an amendment (H. Rept. 119-873).
  • H.R. 9084, sponsored by Rep. Kathy Castor (D-FL-14) — the Department of Energy Nuclear Transparency Act would increase transparency around the Department of Energy's authorizations of certain nuclear facilities. Reported by the Committee on Energy and Commerce with an amendment (H. Rept. 119-874).
  • H.R. 9612, sponsored by Rep. Russell Fry (R-SC-7) — the American Enrichment Deployment Act would update licensing procedures for uranium enrichment facilities to enable their timely, safe deployment. Reported by the Committee on Energy and Commerce with an amendment (H. Rept. 119-875).
  • H.R. 9614, sponsored by Rep. Robert Menendez (D-NJ-8) — the NRC Staff Pay Alignment Act would amend the Atomic Energy Act of 1954 to provide compensation authority for certain career appointees. Reported by the Committee on Energy and Commerce (H. Rept. 119-876).
  • H.R. 9613, sponsored by Rep. Diana Harshbarger (R-TN-1) — the Nuclear Advisory Committee Reform Act would update the role of the Advisory Committee on Reactor Safeguards in licensing and overseeing nuclear reactor facilities. Reported by the Committee on Energy and Commerce with an amendment (H. Rept. 119-877).
  • H.R. 10954, sponsored by Rep. Sheri Biggs (R-SC-3) — would amend the Federal Food, Drug, and Cosmetic Act to deem an abortion drug misbranded unless it carries a "Do Not Flush" warning label, and to require a catch kit. Introduced; referred to the Committee on Energy and Commerce.
  • H.R. 10955, sponsored by Rep. Judy Chu (D-CA-28) — would amend the Internal Revenue Code of 1986 to modify the age limits on eligibility for the earned income tax credit. Introduced; referred to the Committee on Ways and Means.
  • H.R. 10956, sponsored by Rep. Mike Kennedy (R-UT-3) — would amend the Indian Health Care Improvement Act so Indian Health Service scholarship and loan recipients can fulfill their service obligations through half-time clinical practice. Introduced; referred to the Committee on Natural Resources and, in addition, the Committee on Energy and Commerce.
  • H.R. 10957, sponsored by Rep. Nancy Mace (R-SC-1) — would direct the Administrator of the Environmental Protection Agency to study and report on anti-microplastic strategies and set up a pilot program to evaluate them. Introduced; referred to the Committee on Energy and Commerce, with additional referrals to the Committees on Transportation and Infrastructure and on Science, Space, and Technology.
  • H.R. 10958, sponsored by Rep. Nancy Mace (R-SC-1) — would require companies that sell clothing to disclose the estimated mass of microplastic in it. Introduced; referred to the Committee on Energy and Commerce.
  • H.R. 10959, sponsored by Rep. Andrea Salinas (D-OR-6) — would amend the Patient Protection and Affordable Care Act to require Exchanges to offer special enrollment periods when plan provider networks change. Introduced; referred to the Committee on Energy and Commerce.
  • H.R. 10960, sponsored by Rep. James Walkinshaw (D-VA-11) — would give authority to convert certain employees serving under time-limited appointments to career or career-conditional civil service appointments. Introduced; referred to the Committee on Oversight and Government Reform.
  • H. Res. 1648, sponsored by Rep. Everton Blair (D-GA-13) — the Congress at Work Resolution would require House Members to post monthly activity reports on their official public websites. Introduced; referred to the Committee on House Administration.
  • H. Res. 1649, sponsored by Rep. Joseph Morelle (D-NY-25) — expresses support for designating the first full week of November as "National Animal Shelter Appreciation Week." Introduced; referred to the Committee on Agriculture.
