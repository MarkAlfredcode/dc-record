Top Story

House bill would bar candidates from betting on their own elections

Rep. Donald Davis introduced a measure during a brief pro forma House session on Monday.

Rep. Donald Davis (D-NC) introduced H.R. 10732, which would amend title 52 of the United States Code to prohibit candidates for federal office from trading in contracts on the outcome of their own elections. It was referred to the Committee on House Administration.

The Congressional Record lists no cosponsors for the bill, and it gives only the bill's title, so details such as penalties or which markets it covers are not confirmed here. It was one of 30 House bills introduced Monday, during a session where neither chamber held a vote.

On the Record

Pro forma sessions only; 41 House measures introduced

The Senate met in a pro forma session at 4:00 p.m., lasting under a minute, and adjourned until 1:30 p.m. Tuesday. The House met at 4:30 p.m. and adjourned at 4:33 p.m.; there were no roll call votes and no quorum calls. Both chambers are scheduled for pro forma sessions again Tuesday. (Daily Digest)

The House recorded 30 bills (H.R. 10721-10750) and 11 resolutions (H.Res. 1606-1616). Besides Davis's election-betting bill, notable introductions include H.R. 10740, a Ukraine temporary protected status bill with more than 40 bipartisan cosponsors, and five Medicare and Medicaid bills from Rep. Sharice Davids. Most of the 11 resolutions are commemorative designations. The House also filed a committee report on H.R. 2283, a Veterans Affairs mental health grant pilot program (H.
