Top Story

Raskin bill would put Trump-era “propaganda, corruption, and authoritarianism” on permanent Smithsonian display

The bill was one of 22 measures introduced in a two-minute House pro forma session.

H.R. 10765, from Rep. Jamie Raskin (D-MD-8), would direct the Secretary of the Smithsonian Institution to collect, preserve and display in a permanent exhibition at the National Museum of American History artifacts reflecting the rise of governmental propaganda, corruption and authoritarianism during Donald J. Trump’s two presidencies, covering January 20, 2017 through January 20, 2021 and January 20, 2025 through January 20, 2029.

It was referred to the Committee on House Administration and, for a period to be determined by the Speaker, the Committee on Natural Resources. No cosponsors were listed in the Congressional Record.

The House held no votes: it met at 4 p.m. and adjourned at 4:02 p.m., and the Senate met in pro forma session for 33 seconds. Also introduced was H.Res. 1618 by Rep. Josh Gottheimer (D-NJ-5), marking the third anniversary of the October 7, 2023 attacks on Israel, with more than 40 cosponsors listed.

On the Record

Brief pro forma sessions in both chambers; 22 measures introduced in the House

Neither chamber held votes on Tuesday. The House met at 4 p.m. and adjourned at 4:02 p.m., with no recorded votes or quorum calls; Representative Loudermilk served as Speaker pro tempore. The Senate met in pro forma session for about 33 seconds and adjourned until 1:30 p.m. on Friday, October 9, 2026. Source: Congressional Record, October 6, 2026.

The House Daily Digest records 17 bills (H.R. 10751–10767), one concurrent resolution and four simple resolutions introduced. Among the more newsworthy: the Smithsonian exhibition bill from Raskin, a tariff-relief program from Rep. John James (R-MI-10), and a bipartisan bill from Gottheimer and Rep. Michael Lawler (R-NY-17) to cap U.S. contributions to the United Nations. The House also filed committee reports on H.R. 6698 and H.R. 7260, both on veterans’ matters. The full list follows.

  • H.R. 10751, sponsored by Rep. Jack Bergman (R-MI-1) — amends the Indian Health Care Improvement Act to remove restrictions on dental therapist services under the Community Health Aide Program. Referred to the Committee on Natural Resources and the Committee on Energy and Commerce.
  • H.R. 10752, sponsored by Rep. Troy A. Carter (D-LA-2) — creates a task force on artificial intelligence within the Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency at the Department of Homeland Security. Referred to the Committee on Homeland Security.
  • H.R. 10753, sponsored by Rep. Elijah Crane (R-AZ-2) — directs the Secretary of Housing and Urban Development to set up a program to voluntarily reallocate vouchers under the Tribal HUD-VASH program. Referred to the Committee on Financial Services.
  • H.R. 10754, sponsored by Rep. Elijah Crane (R-AZ-2) — amends the FAA Reauthorization Act of 2018 to update indicators of the local impact of a disaster. Referred to the Committee on Transportation and Infrastructure.
  • H.R. 10755, sponsored by Rep. Jasmine Crockett (D-TX-30) — names the post office at 475 East FM 1382 in Cedar Hill, Texas, the “Eugene Gates Jr. Post Office Building.” Referred to the Committee on Oversight and Government Reform.
  • H.R. 10756, sponsored by Rep. Mike Ezell (R-MS-4) — reauthorizes Regional Ocean Partnerships. Referred to the Committee on Natural Resources.
  • H.R. 10757, sponsored by Rep. Josh Gottheimer (D-NJ-5) — would limit U.S. contributions to the United Nations. Referred to the Committee on Foreign Affairs.
  • H.R. 10758, sponsored by Rep. Jeff Hurd (R-CO-3) — directs the Secretary of Veterans Affairs to award grants to state entities to expand structured outdoor recreation programs for veterans. Referred to the Committee on Veterans’ Affairs.
  • H.R. 10759, sponsored by Rep. John James (R-MI-10) — would establish a tariff-funded program to protect U.S. manufacturers, workers, farmers and supply chains from economic harm caused by tariffs and foreign retaliation, and to encourage reshoring. Referred to the Committee on Financial Services and the Committee on Ways and Means.
  • H.R. 10760, sponsored by Rep. Thomas H. Kean Jr. (R-NJ-7) — would create a multiagency Middle School Mental Health Task Force at the Department of Education and the Department of Health and Human Services. Referred to the Committee on Education and Workforce and the Committee on Energy and Commerce.
  • H.R. 10761, sponsored by Rep. Ro Khanna (D-CA-17) — amends the Jeanne Clery Campus Safety Act to combat campus sexual assault. Referred to the Committee on Education and Workforce and the Committee on the Judiciary.
  • H.R. 10762, sponsored by Rep. Michael Lawler (R-NY-17) — would require sellers of used vehicles to maintain a functional airbag system or clearly disclose when an airbag system is missing or disabled. Referred to the Committee on Energy and Commerce and the Committee on the Judiciary.
  • H.R. 10763, sponsored by Rep. Julia Letlow (R-LA-5) — (the Know Your Risk Act) directs the Secretary of Health and Human Services to report to Congress on breast cancer risk assessment tools. Referred to the Committee on Energy and Commerce.
  • H.R. 10764, sponsored by Rep. Gwen Moore (D-WI-4) — amends title 31 of the United States Code to require certain procedures for compromise settlements or awards involving executive branch officials. Referred to the Committee on the Judiciary.
  • H.R. 10765, sponsored by Rep. Jamie Raskin (D-MD-8) — directs the Secretary of the Smithsonian Institution to collect, preserve and display, in a permanent exhibition at the National Museum of American History, artifacts reflecting “the rise of governmental propaganda, corruption, and authoritarianism” during the two presidencies of Donald J. Trump. Referred to the Committee on House Administration and the Committee on Natural Resources.
  • H.R. 10766, sponsored by Rep. Keith Self (R-TX-3) — directs the Secretary of Defense to add assets critical to the Golden Dome missile defense system to the Department of Defense Critical Asset List. Referred to the Committee on Armed Services.
  • H.R. 10767, sponsored by Rep. Adrian Smith (R-NE-3) — amends the Internal Revenue Code of 1986 to impose an excise tax on colleges and universities that allow specified individuals to participate in intercollegiate athletics. Referred to the Committee on Ways and Means.
  • H. Con. Res. 123, sponsored by Rep. Teresa Leger Fernández (D-NM-3) — recognizes the wage disparity faced by Latinas and its effect on women, families and the economy. Referred to the Committee on Education and Workforce.
  • H.Res. 1617, sponsored by Rep. Earl L. “Buddy” Carter (R-GA-1) — recognizes October as “American Pharmacy Month” in honor of pharmacists and pharmacy teams. Referred to the Committee on Energy and Commerce.
  • H.Res. 1618, sponsored by Rep. Josh Gottheimer (D-NJ-5) — recognizes the third anniversary of the October 7, 2023, terrorist attacks on Israel, honors the victims and hostages, and calls for the complete disarmament of Hamas for a durable end to the war in Gaza. Referred to the Committee on Foreign Affairs.
  • H.Res. 1619, sponsored by Rep. Glenn Thompson (R-PA-15) — reaffirms German-American friendship and supports continued U.S.-Germany cooperation. Referred to the Committee on Foreign Affairs.
  • H.Res. 1620, sponsored by Rep. Nikema Williams (D-GA-5) — supports the goals and ideals of “National Nonviolence Week” to raise awareness of youth violence. Referred to the Committee on Oversight and Government Reform.
