Top Story

Rep. Gwen Moore files bill on procedures for settlements paid to executive branch officials

The House met for two minutes in a pro forma session with no votes, but 22 measures were introduced.

Rep. Gwen Moore (D-WI-4) introduced H.R. 10764, which amends title 31 of the United States Code to require certain procedures for compromise settlements or awards for executive branch officials. It was referred to the Committee on the Judiciary. The Congressional Record gives only the bill's title; no text or cosponsors were listed in the filings reviewed.

Also introduced was H.R. 10765 from Rep. Jamie Raskin (D-MD-8), which would direct the Smithsonian Institution to build a permanent National Museum of American History exhibition on governmental propaganda, corruption and authoritarianism during Donald J. Trump's two presidencies.

On the Record

House holds a two-minute session; 22 bills and resolutions introduced

The House met at 4 p.m. on Tuesday, October 6, and adjourned at 4:02 p.m., with no recorded votes and no quorum calls. The Senate met in a pro forma session for 33 seconds, from 1:31:12 p.m. to 1:31:45 p.m., and adjourned until 1:30 p.m. on Friday, October 9. Reports were filed on two veterans bills, H.R. 6698 (Board of Veterans' Appeals annual report on untimely appeals) and H.R. 7260 (annual report on the National Cemetery Administration).

Introductions were 17 House bills (H.R. 10751–10767), one concurrent resolution and four simple resolutions. The most newsworthy are a bill on settlements for executive branch officials (H.R. 10764), the Smithsonian exhibition bill (H.R. 10765), and a tariff-relief program bill from Rep. John James (H.R. 10759). All are listed below.

  • H.R. 10751, sponsored by Rep. Jack Bergman (R-MI-1) — removes restrictions on dental therapist services under the Community Health Aide Program in the Indian Health Care Improvement Act. Referred to the Committee on Natural Resources and the Committee on Energy and Commerce.
  • H.R. 10752, sponsored by Rep. Troy A. Carter (D-LA-2) — creates a task force on artificial intelligence inside the Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency. Referred to the Committee on Homeland Security.
  • H.R. 10753, sponsored by Rep. Elijah Crane (R-AZ-2) — directs the Secretary of Housing and Urban Development to set up a program to voluntarily reallocate vouchers under the Tribal HUD-VASH program. Referred to the Committee on Financial Services.
  • H.R. 10754, sponsored by Rep. Elijah Crane (R-AZ-2) — updates the indicators of a disaster's local impact in the FAA Reauthorization Act of 2018. Referred to the Committee on Transportation and Infrastructure.
  • H.R. 10755, sponsored by Rep. Jasmine Crockett (D-TX-30) — names the post office at 475 East FM 1382 in Cedar Hill, Texas, the “Eugene Gates Jr. Post Office Building.” Referred to the Committee on Oversight and Government Reform.
  • H.R. 10756, sponsored by Rep. Mike Ezell (R-MS-4) — reauthorizes Regional Ocean Partnerships. Referred to the Committee on Natural Resources.
  • H.R. 10757, sponsored by Rep. Josh Gottheimer (D-NJ-5) — limits United States contributions to the United Nations. Referred to the Committee on Foreign Affairs.
  • H.R. 10758, sponsored by Rep. Jeff Hurd (R-CO-3) — has the Secretary of Veterans Affairs award grants to state entities to expand structured outdoor recreation programs for veterans. Referred to the Committee on Veterans' Affairs.
  • H.R. 10759, sponsored by Rep. John James (R-MI-10) — creates a tariff-funded program to protect United States manufacturers, workers, farmers and supply chains from economic harm caused by tariffs and foreign retaliation, and to encourage reshoring. Referred to the Committee on Financial Services and the Committee on Ways and Means.
  • H.R. 10760, sponsored by Rep. Thomas H. Kean Jr. (R-NJ-7) — establishes a multiagency Middle School Mental Health Task Force at the Department of Education and the Department of Health and Human Services. Referred to the Committee on Education and Workforce and the Committee on Energy and Commerce.
  • H.R. 10761, sponsored by Rep. Ro Khanna (D-CA-17) — amends the Jeanne Clery Campus Safety Act to combat campus sexual assault. Referred to the Committee on Education and Workforce and the Committee on the Judiciary.
  • H.R. 10762, sponsored by Rep. Michael Lawler (R-NY-17) — requires used-car sellers to maintain a functional airbag system or clearly disclose when any airbag system is missing or disabled. Referred to the Committee on Energy and Commerce and the Committee on the Judiciary.
  • H.R. 10763, sponsored by Rep. Julia Letlow (R-LA-5) — the Know Your Risk Act; directs the Secretary of Health and Human Services to report to Congress on breast cancer risk assessment tools. Referred to the Committee on Energy and Commerce.
  • H.R. 10764, sponsored by Rep. Gwen Moore (D-WI-4) — amends title 31 of the United States Code to require certain procedures for compromise settlements or awards for executive branch officials. Referred to the Committee on the Judiciary.
  • H.R. 10765, sponsored by Rep. Jamie Raskin (D-MD-8) — directs the Smithsonian Institution to collect, preserve and permanently display, in the National Museum of American History, artifacts reflecting the rise of governmental propaganda, corruption and authoritarianism during Donald J. Trump's two presidencies. Referred to the Committee on House Administration and the Committee on Natural Resources.
  • H.R. 10766, sponsored by Rep. Keith Self (R-TX-3) — directs the Secretary of Defense to add assets critical to the Golden Dome missile defense system to the Department of Defense Critical Asset List. Referred to the Committee on Armed Services.
  • H.R. 10767, sponsored by Rep. Adrian Smith (R-NE-3) — imposes an excise tax on colleges and universities that allow specified individuals to participate in intercollegiate athletics. Referred to the Committee on Ways and Means.
  • H. Con. Res. 123, sponsored by Rep. Teresa Leger Fernandez (D-NM-3) — recognizes the disparity in wages paid to Latinas and its impact on women, families and the economy; the Congressional Record lists 22 cosponsors. Referred to the Committee on Education and Workforce.
  • H. Res. 1617, sponsored by Rep. Earl L. “Buddy” Carter (R-GA-1) — recognizes October as “American Pharmacy Month.” Referred to the Committee on Energy and Commerce.
  • H. Res. 1618, sponsored by Rep. Josh Gottheimer (D-NJ-5) — marks the third anniversary of the October 7, 2023, attacks on Israel, honors victims and hostages, and calls for the complete disarmament of Hamas; more than 40 cosponsors are listed. Referred to the Committee on Foreign Affairs.
  • H. Res. 1619, sponsored by Rep. Glenn Thompson (R-PA-15) — reaffirms German-American friendship and supports continued cooperation between the United States and Germany. Referred to the Committee on Foreign Affairs.
  • H. Res. 1620, sponsored by Rep. Nikema Williams (D-GA-5) — supports the goals of “National Nonviolence Week” to raise awareness of youth violence. Referred to the Committee on Oversight and Government Reform.
