Top Story

Raskin bill would put Trump-era "authoritarianism" in a permanent Smithsonian exhibit

A pro forma day with no votes still produced 22 new House measures, including one that targets the National Museum of American History.

On Tuesday, October 6, 2026, Rep. Jamie Raskin (D-MD-8) introduced H.R. 10765, which would direct the Secretary of the Smithsonian Institution to collect, preserve and display in a permanent exhibition at the National Museum of American History artifacts reflecting "the rise of governmental propaganda, corruption, and authoritarianism" during Donald J. Trump's presidencies. The bill text defines them as the periods from January 20, 2017 through January 20, 2021, and January 20, 2025 through January 20, 2029.

It was referred to the Committee on House Administration, and additionally to the Committee on Natural Resources. No cosponsors are listed in the Congressional Record. Neither chamber held any votes that day.

On the Record

Both chambers in brief pro forma sessions; House introduces 22 measures

The Senate met in pro forma session at 1:31 p.m. and adjourned 33 seconds later until 1:30 p.m. on Friday, October 9, 2026. The House met at 4 p.m. and adjourned at 4:02 p.m.; the Daily Digest records no roll call votes and no quorum calls.

The House still took in 17 bills and 5 resolutions (H.R. 10751–10767, H.Con.Res. 123 and H.Res. 1617–1620), and its committees filed two veterans-related reports. Among the more newsworthy: H.R. 10765 on the Smithsonian; H.R. 10759, a tariff-funded relief program for manufacturers and farmers; H.R. 10757, which would limit contributions to the United Nations; and H.Res. 1618, marking the October 7 anniversary. Every measure introduced is listed below.

  • H.R. 10751, sponsored by Rep. Jack Bergman (R-MI-1) — would remove restrictions on dental therapist services under the Community Health Aide Program of the Indian Health Care Improvement Act.
  • H.R. 10752, sponsored by Rep. Troy A. Carter (D-LA-2) — would create a task force on artificial intelligence inside the Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency.
  • H.R. 10753, sponsored by Rep. Elijah Crane (R-AZ-2) — the Tribal Veterans Voucher Flexibility Act, which would let the Department of Housing and Urban Development voluntarily reallocate vouchers under the Tribal HUD-VASH program.
  • H.R. 10754, sponsored by Rep. Elijah Crane — the Rural Disaster Fairness Act of 2026, which would update indicators of the local impact of a disaster under the FAA Reauthorization Act of 2018.
  • H.R. 10755, sponsored by Rep. Jasmine Crockett (D-TX-30) — would name the post office at 475 East FM 1382 in Cedar Hill, Texas, the "Eugene Gates Jr. Post Office Building."
  • H.R. 10756, sponsored by Rep. Mike Ezell (R-MS-4) — would reauthorize Regional Ocean Partnerships.
  • H.R. 10757, sponsored by Rep. Josh Gottheimer (D-NJ-5) — would limit United States contributions to the United Nations.
  • H.R. 10758, sponsored by Rep. Jeff Hurd (R-CO-3) — would have the Secretary of Veterans Affairs award grants to state entities to expand structured outdoor recreation programs for veterans.
  • H.R. 10759, sponsored by Rep. John James (R-MI-10) — would establish a tariff-funded program to protect United States manufacturers, workers, farmers and supply chains from economic harm caused by tariffs and foreign retaliation, and to encourage domestic investment and reshoring.
  • H.R. 10760, sponsored by Rep. Thomas H. Kean (R-NJ-7) — the Healthy Minds for a Better Future Act, which would create a multiagency Middle School Mental Health Task Force at the Department of Education and the Department of Health and Human Services.
  • H.R. 10761, sponsored by Rep. Ro Khanna (D-CA-17) — the Campus Accountability and Safety Act of 2026, which would amend the Jeanne Clery Campus Safety Act to combat campus sexual assault.
  • H.R. 10762, sponsored by Rep. Michael Lawler (R-NY-17) — the Anthony Amoros Law Right to Know Act, which would require used-vehicle sellers to keep a vehicle's airbag system functional or clearly disclose when any airbag system is missing or disabled.
  • H.R. 10763, sponsored by Rep. Julia Letlow (R-LA-5) — the Know Your Risk Act, which would direct the Secretary of Health and Human Services to report to Congress on breast cancer risk assessment tools.
  • H.R. 10764, sponsored by Rep. Gwen Moore (D-WI-4) — the Executive Settlement Review Act, which would require certain procedures for compromise settlements or awards for executive branch officials.
  • H.R. 10765, sponsored by Rep. Jamie Raskin (D-MD-8) — would direct the Secretary of the Smithsonian Institution to collect, preserve and display, in a permanent exhibition at the National Museum of American History, artifacts reflecting "the rise of governmental propaganda, corruption, and authoritarianism" during Donald J. Trump's two presidencies.
  • H.R. 10766, sponsored by Rep. Keith Self (R-TX-3) — would direct the Secretary of Defense to add assets critical to the Golden Dome missile defense system to the Department of Defense Critical Asset List.
  • H.R. 10767, sponsored by Rep. Adrian Smith (R-NE-3) — the Fair Play Enforcement Act, which would impose an excise tax on colleges and universities that allow specified individuals to take part in intercollegiate athletics.
  • H.Con.Res. 123, sponsored by Rep. Teresa Leger Fernandez (D-NM-3) — would recognize the wage disparity faced by Latinas and its effect on women, families and the economy; it has 22 listed cosponsors in the Record.
  • H.Res. 1617, sponsored by Rep. Earl L. "Buddy" Carter (R-GA-1) — would recognize October as "American Pharmacy Month."
  • H.Res. 1618, sponsored by Rep. Josh Gottheimer — would mark the third anniversary of the October 7, 2023, attacks on Israel, honor the victims and hostages, and call for the complete disarmament of Hamas.
  • H.Res. 1619, sponsored by Rep. Glenn Thompson (R-PA-15) — would reaffirm German-American friendship and support continued cooperation between the United States and Germany.
  • H.Res. 1620, sponsored by Rep. Nikema Williams (D-GA-5) — would support the goals of "National Nonviolence Week" to raise awareness of youth violence.
