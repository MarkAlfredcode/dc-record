Top Story

Discharge petition on cancellation of removal sits one signature short of 218 as Congress holds only pro forma sessions

Both chambers met for minutes on Monday with no votes, while a House discharge petition on immigration status remains at 217 of 218 signatures.

Discharge Petition No. 25, filed by Rep. Sylvia R. Garcia through H.Res. 1414, would bring up H.R. 1589, a bill to authorize the cancellation of removal and adjustment of status of certain aliens. It stands at 217 of 218 signatures, one short of the number needed to force consideration. No open petition gained signatures, and none reached 218, since October 17, 2026.

Discharge Petition No. 27, sponsored by Rep. Thomas Massie through H.Res. 1430 and tied to H.R. 185, is at 216 of 218 signatures, two short.

The floor itself was quiet. The House met at 10 a.m. Monday and adjourned at 10:02 a.m. with no Yea and Nay votes, no recorded votes and no quorum calls. The Senate met in a pro forma session from 9:00:01 a.m. to 9:00:31 a.m. Both chambers meet again Thursday, October 22, 2026, in pro forma sessions.

No new Congressional Record issue has been published since this one (first covered 2026-10-20); the summary below is carried over.

On the Record

Pro forma sessions in both chambers; the House takes in 15 bills and 5 resolutions

The Senate met in a pro forma session for about 30 seconds and adjourned until 9 a.m. Thursday, October 22, 2026. The House met for two minutes, with Representative Goldman of Texas serving as Speaker pro tempore, and held no votes. No committee meetings were held in either chamber and no committee reports were filed. The 20 measures below were introduced and referred to committee; none was reported by a committee. The batch is mostly routine, including five awareness-day and commemorative resolutions. The bills with the most policy content are a Democratic bill on artificial intelligence in elections, a bill on Medicare payments for mobile stroke units, and a bill that would direct the Department of Homeland Security to designate Yemen for temporary protected status.

  • H.R. 10840, sponsored by Rep. Gus Bilirakis (R-FL-12) — directs the Comptroller General of the United States to study and report on retention practices for non-clinical personnel at Department of Veterans Affairs health care facilities. Referred to the Committee on Veterans' Affairs.
  • H.R. 10841, the Securing Elections From AI Deception Act, sponsored by Rep. Shontel Brown (D-OH-11) — prohibits the use of artificial intelligence to deprive or defraud individuals of the right to vote in elections for public office. Referred to the Committee on Energy and Commerce; the Record lists 29 cosponsors.
  • H.R. 10842, sponsored by Rep. Tim Burchett (R-TN-2) — amends the Internal Revenue Code of 1986 to establish a financial services customer support onshoring credit. Referred to the Committee on Ways and Means.
  • H.R. 10843 (VACRA), sponsored by Rep. Ben Cline (R-VA-6), with Rep. Sydney Kamlager-Dove as cosponsor — amends title 17, United States Code, to reform copyright laws relating to visual artists. Referred to the Committee on the Judiciary.
  • H.R. 10844, sponsored by Rep. Steve Cohen (D-TN-9) — amends title XVIII of the Social Security Act to ensure equitable Medicare payments for items and services furnished by mobile stroke units. Referred to the Committee on Ways and Means and, in addition, the Committee on Energy and Commerce.
  • H.R. 10845, sponsored by Rep. Veronica Escobar (D-TX-16) — requires an assessment of standards and guidelines for managing air, land and sea ports of entry under the control of the Department of Homeland Security. Referred to the Committee on Ways and Means and, in addition, the Committee on Homeland Security.
  • H.R. 10846, sponsored by Rep. Laura Friedman (D-CA-30) — amends the Internal Revenue Code of 1986 to impose an excise tax on certain indebtedness. Referred to the Committee on Ways and Means.
  • H.R. 10847, the Military Justice Leadership and Independence Act, sponsored by Rep. Maggie Goodlander (D-NH-2) — amends title 10, United States Code, to provide for the ranks of the Judge Advocates General. Referred to the Committee on Armed Services.
  • H.R. 10848, sponsored by Rep. Maggie Goodlander (D-NH-2) — requires the Comptroller General of the United States to review the Shipyard Infrastructure Optimization Program. Referred to the Committee on Armed Services.
  • H.R. 10849, sponsored by Rep. Maggie Goodlander (D-NH-2) — amends chapter 388 of title 10, United States Code, to clarify the purpose of, and adjust the limits on, assistance under the procurement technical assistance cooperative agreement program. Referred to the Committee on Armed Services.
  • H.R. 10850, the SCREENS Act, sponsored by Rep. Ashley Hinson (R-IA-2) — amends the Public Health Service Act to require the National Coordinator for Health Information Technology to establish a national computable-guideline framework for cancer screening recommendations. Referred to the Committee on Energy and Commerce and, in addition, the Committee on Ways and Means.
  • H.R. 10851, the Socket Full of Sunshine Act, sponsored by Rep. Robert Menendez (D-NJ-8) — amends the Public Utility Regulatory Policies Act of 1978 to establish a federal standard for states to consider measures promoting portable solar generation devices. Referred to the Committee on Energy and Commerce.
  • H.R. 10852, sponsored by Rep. Andrea Salinas (D-OR-6), with Rep. Glenn Thompson as cosponsor — amends the Internal Revenue Code of 1986 to provide incentives for mass timber products. Referred to the Committee on Ways and Means.
  • H.R. 10853, sponsored by Rep. Rashida Tlaib (D-MI-12) — requires the Secretary of Homeland Security to designate Yemen for temporary protected status. Referred to the Committee on the Judiciary; the Record lists 15 cosponsors.
  • H.R. 10854, sponsored by Rep. George Whitesides (D-CA-27) — directs the Secretary of Energy to establish and carry out a Public Safety Power Shutoff Event Reporting Program. Referred to the Committee on Energy and Commerce.
  • H. Res. 1629, sponsored by Rep. Randy Fine (R-FL-6) — expresses support for designating the third Wednesday of October as "Support Your Local Chamber of Commerce Day." Referred to the Committee on Oversight and Government Reform.
  • H. Res. 1630, sponsored by Rep. Robert Bresnahan (R-PA-8) — expresses the importance of raising awareness of opportunity youth and of investing in reconnection to education and career pathways. Referred to the Committee on Education and Workforce and, in addition, the Committee on Energy and Commerce.
  • H. Res. 1631, sponsored by Rep. Valerie Foushee (D-NC-4) — expresses support for designating the third week of October as "National Hunter Syndrome Awareness Week." Referred to the Committee on Energy and Commerce.
  • H. Res. 1632, sponsored by Rep. Henry Johnson (D-GA-4) — expresses support for designating October 17, 2026, as "National Vitiligo Awareness Day." Referred to the Committee on Energy and Commerce.
  • H. Res. 1633, sponsored by Rep. Glenn Thompson (R-PA-15) — expresses support for designating the week of October 19 through October 23, 2026, as "Careers in Energy Week." Referred to the Committee on Education and Workforce.
