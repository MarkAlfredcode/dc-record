Top Story

Discharge petition on immigration status sits one signature short of 218

Discharge Petition No. 25 stands at 217 of 218 signatures while Congress holds only pro forma sessions.

Discharge Petition No. 25, sponsored by Rep. Sylvia R. Garcia through H.Res. 1414, would bring up a bill (H.R. 1589) to authorize the cancellation of removal and adjustment of status of certain aliens. It has 217 of the 218 signatures needed, with one to go. No open petitions gained signatures, and none were filed or reached 218, since October 31, 2026.

Discharge Petition No. 27, sponsored by Rep. Thomas Massie through H.Res. 1430 (H.R. 185), is at 216 of 218, two short.

The Congressional Record for Monday, November 2, 2026 shows little floor action. The House met for two minutes with no votes, and the Senate met for about 30 seconds in a pro forma session. Both chambers return for pro forma sessions on Thursday, November 5, 2026.

No new Congressional Record issue has been published since this one (first covered 2026-11-03); the summary below is carried over.

On the Record

Pro forma sessions in both chambers; two labor bills reported and five measures introduced

The House met at 2 p.m. and adjourned at 2:02 p.m. There were no roll-call votes and no quorum calls. Rep. Darrell Issa served as Speaker pro tempore. The Senate met in pro forma session from 5:10 p.m. to 5:11 p.m. Eastern and adjourned until 10:30 a.m. on Thursday, November 5, 2026. No committee meetings or hearings were held in either chamber.

Two bills were reported by the House Committee on Education and Workforce, and two bills and three resolutions were introduced. The most notable is a proposed constitutional amendment on campaign contributions for federal elections.

  • H.R. 8347, sponsored by Rep. Burgess Owens (R-UT-4), the RURAL Healthcare Act. Reported by committee, with an amendment (H. Rept. 119-867), by the Committee on Education and Workforce. It would classify qualified locum tenens professionals and advanced care practitioners as independent contractors under the Fair Labor Standards Act of 1938 and the National Labor Relations Act.
  • H.R. 8660, sponsored by Rep. Ryan Mackenzie (R-PA-7), the VEST Act. Reported by committee, with an amendment (H. Rept. 119-868), by the Committee on Education and Workforce. It would amend the Fair Labor Standards Act of 1938 to protect worker access to employer equity.
  • H.R. 10952, sponsored by Rep. Michael Baumgartner (R-WA-5). It would promote forest health and reduce wildfire risk through stewardship in high-risk firesheds, tax incentives for biomass energy production, and support for economic use of forest products. Referred to the Committee on Agriculture, and additionally to the Committees on Ways and Means and Natural Resources.
  • H.R. 10953, sponsored by Rep. Thomas Kean (R-NJ-7). It would direct the Secretary of Health and Human Services to run a program of allotments to States to pay for State Ombudsman Programs that provide information and support to individuals with developmental disabilities and their families. Referred to the Committee on Energy and Commerce, and additionally to the Committee on Education and Workforce.
  • H.J. Res. 221, sponsored by Rep. Hillary Scholten (D-MI-3). It proposes a constitutional amendment giving Congress power to regulate campaign contributions for federal elections. Referred to the Committee on the Judiciary.
  • H. Res. 1646, sponsored by Rep. Troy Carter (D-LA-2). It supports recognizing November as "Bladder Health Awareness Month" and efforts to increase public education, reduce stigma, and improve access to care for bladder health conditions. Referred to the Committee on Energy and Commerce.
  • H. Res. 1647, sponsored by Rep. Madeleine Dean (D-PA-4). It expresses support for recognizing the week of November 1 through November 7, 2026, as "Drowsy Driving Prevention Week." Referred to the Committee on Transportation and Infrastructure.
