Top Story

Raskin bill would put Trump-era "authoritarianism" exhibit in the Smithsonian

The House met for two minutes in a pro forma session with no votes, but 22 bills and resolutions were introduced.

H.R. 10765, introduced October 6 by Rep. Jamie Raskin (D-MD-8), would direct the Secretary of the Smithsonian Institution to collect, preserve and display in a permanent exhibition at the National Museum of American History artifacts reflecting "the rise of governmental propaganda, corruption, and authoritarianism" during Donald Trump's two presidencies. The bill text defines them as January 20, 2017 through January 20, 2021, and January 20, 2025 through January 20, 2029.

It was referred to the Committee on House Administration and, additionally, the Committee on Natural Resources. The House held no votes that day, and the chamber was in a pro forma session, so the bill has no floor action behind it.

Also introduced the same day: H. Res. 1618 by Rep. Josh Gottheimer (D-NJ-5), marking the third anniversary of the October 7, 2023, attacks on Israel and calling for the complete disarmament of Hamas, with more than 40 cosponsors listed from both parties.

On the Record

Pro forma sessions in both chambers; 22 measures introduced in the House

The Senate met in pro forma session at 1:31 p.m. on October 6 and adjourned 33 seconds later until 1:30 p.m. on Friday, October 9. The House met at 4 p.m. and adjourned at 4:02 p.m.; there were no recorded votes and no quorum calls. The House filed two reports from the Committee on Veterans' Affairs: H.R. 6698 (Board of Veterans' Appeals reporting on untimely appeals) and H.R. 7260 (annual report on the National Cemetery Administration).

Seventeen House bills, one concurrent resolution and four resolutions were introduced. The most newsworthy are the Raskin Smithsonian bill, Gottheimer's October 7 anniversary resolution, and a bill by Rep. John James for a tariff-funded program to cushion manufacturers and farmers from tariff harm. The full list follows.

  • H.R. 10751, sponsored by Rep. Jack Bergman (R-MI-1) — would remove restrictions on dental therapist services under the Community Health Aide Program of the Indian Health Care Improvement Act (referred to the Committee on Natural Resources; and the Committee on Energy and Commerce).
  • H.R. 10752, sponsored by Rep. Troy Carter (D-LA-2) — would create a task force on artificial intelligence inside the Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency (referred to the Committee on Homeland Security).
  • H.R. 10753, sponsored by Rep. Elijah Crane (R-AZ-2) — the Tribal Veterans Voucher Flexibility Act, would let unused Tribal HUD-VASH housing vouchers be voluntarily reallocated (referred to the Committee on Financial Services).
  • H.R. 10754, sponsored by Rep. Elijah Crane (R-AZ-2) — the Rural Disaster Fairness Act of 2026, would update the local-impact indicators of a disaster in the FAA Reauthorization Act of 2018 (referred to the Committee on Transportation and Infrastructure).
  • H.R. 10755, sponsored by Rep. Jasmine Crockett (D-TX-30) — would name the post office at 475 East FM 1382 in Cedar Hill, Texas, the "Eugene Gates Jr. Post Office Building" (referred to the Committee on Oversight and Government Reform).
  • H.R. 10756, sponsored by Rep. Mike Ezell (R-MS-4) — would reauthorize Regional Ocean Partnerships (referred to the Committee on Natural Resources).
  • H.R. 10757, sponsored by Rep. Josh Gottheimer (D-NJ-5) — would limit United States contributions to the United Nations (referred to the Committee on Foreign Affairs).
  • H.R. 10758, sponsored by Rep. Jeff Hurd (R-CO-3) — would have the Secretary of Veterans Affairs award grants to state entities to expand structured outdoor recreation programs for veterans (referred to the Committee on Veterans' Affairs).
  • H.R. 10759, sponsored by Rep. John James (R-MI-10) — would set up a tariff-funded program to protect United States manufacturers, workers, farmers and supply chains from harm caused by tariffs and foreign retaliation, and encourage reshoring (referred to the Committee on Financial Services; and the Committee on Ways and Means).
  • H.R. 10760, sponsored by Rep. Thomas Kean (R-NJ-7) — would create a multiagency Middle School Mental Health Task Force at the Department of Education and the Department of Health and Human Services (referred to the Committee on Education and Workforce; and the Committee on Energy and Commerce).
  • H.R. 10761, sponsored by Rep. Ro Khanna (D-CA-17) — the Campus Accountability and Safety Act of 2026, would amend the Jeanne Clery Campus Safety Act to combat campus sexual assault (referred to the Committee on Education and Workforce; and the Committee on Judiciary).
  • H.R. 10762, sponsored by Rep. Michael Lawler (R-NY-17) — the Anthony Amoros Law Right to Know Act, would require used-car sellers to maintain a functional airbag system or clearly disclose when one is missing or disabled (referred to the Committee on Energy and Commerce; and the Committee on Judiciary).
  • H.R. 10763, sponsored by Rep. Julia Letlow (R-LA-5) — the Know Your Risk Act, would require a Department of Health and Human Services report on breast cancer risk assessment tools (referred to the Committee on Energy and Commerce).
  • H.R. 10764, sponsored by Rep. Gwen Moore (D-WI-4) — the Executive Settlement Review Act, would require certain procedures for compromise settlements or awards for executive branch officials (referred to the Committee on Judiciary).
  • H.R. 10765, sponsored by Rep. Jamie Raskin (D-MD-8) — would direct the Smithsonian Institution to display, in a permanent exhibition at the National Museum of American History, artifacts reflecting the rise of governmental propaganda, corruption and authoritarianism during Donald Trump's two presidencies (referred to the Committee on House Administration; and the Committee on Natural Resources).
  • H.R. 10766, sponsored by Rep. Keith Self (R-TX-3) — would direct the Secretary of Defense to add assets critical to the Golden Dome missile defense system to the Department of Defense Critical Asset List (referred to the Committee on Armed Services).
  • H.R. 10767, sponsored by Rep. Adrian Smith (R-NE-3) — the Fair Play Enforcement Act, would impose an excise tax on colleges and universities that allow specified individuals to take part in intercollegiate athletics (referred to the Committee on Ways and Means).
  • H. Con. Res. 123, sponsored by Rep. Teresa Leger Fernandez (D-NM-3) — would recognize the wage disparity affecting Latinas and its impact on women, families and the economy; 22 Democratic cosponsors are listed (referred to the Committee on Education and Workforce).
  • H. Res. 1617, sponsored by Rep. Earl L. "Buddy" Carter (R-GA-1) — would recognize October as "American Pharmacy Month" (referred to the Committee on Energy and Commerce).
  • H. Res. 1618, sponsored by Rep. Josh Gottheimer (D-NJ-5) — would mark the third anniversary of the October 7, 2023, attacks on Israel, honor the victims and hostages, and call for the complete disarmament of Hamas; more than 40 cosponsors from both parties are listed (referred to the Committee on Foreign Affairs).
  • H. Res. 1619, sponsored by Rep. Glenn Thompson (R-PA-15) — would reaffirm German-American friendship and support continued United States–Germany cooperation (referred to the Committee on Foreign Affairs).
  • H. Res. 1620, sponsored by Rep. Nikema Williams (D-GA-5) — would support the goals of "National Nonviolence Week" to raise awareness of youth violence (referred to the Committee on Oversight and Government Reform).
