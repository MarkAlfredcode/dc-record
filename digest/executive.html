OMB Finalizes $610 Million Rescission From VA Medical Accounts, Conditions $3.6 Billion More on New Spending Plans

The rescission, ordered by this year's stopgap funding law, was one of eight apportionment actions OMB approved this week — six of the rest carry new spend-plan or monthly-reporting strings.

The Office of Management and Budget permanently rescinded $610,000,000 from the Department of Veterans Affairs' Veterans Health Administration Medical Support and Compliance account, acting under a rescission requirement written into Public Law 119-37 (Division D, Title II), part of the Continuing Appropriations Act of 2026. The money had become available October 1, 2025, and is now permanently pulled back rather than merely held up for review.

The rescission arrived alongside six other apportionments — together worth more than $3.6 billion — that OMB approved only on condition of added oversight. Two more VA accounts totaling roughly $761.6 million require a detailed spend plan within 10 business days covering a joint Department of War/VA pharmacy pilot and a unified credentialing system; a $69.6 million Department of Education account is frozen pending additional programmatic information; and more than $2.5 billion in State Department and international-assistance funding now requires monthly written reports to OMB on commitments and obligations, compared month-over-month against agency spending plans.

Taken together, the actions show OMB applying tighter, more granular controls on agency obligations just as a new fiscal year begins — well beyond the routine apportionment approvals that make up the rest of this week's filings.

OMB Apportionments

Seven Apportionments Conditioned on Spend Plans and Reports; Veterans Affairs Rescission Approved

OMB approved eight apportioned actions in the window, flagging seven for heightened scrutiny. One permanent rescission of $610 million in Department of Veterans Affairs funding was finalized under Public Law 119-37 (Division D, Title II), while six separate apportionments totaling over $3.6 billion were conditioned on detailed spend plans or monthly reporting—indicating that OMB is applying stepped-up oversight to agency obligations across education, state, veterans, and international assistance programs.

Rescissions

Department of Veterans Affairs: $610,000,000 permanently rescinded from the Veterans Health Administration—Medical Support and Compliance account pursuant to Public Law 119-37 (Division D, Title II), part of the Continuing Appropriations Act of 2026. The funds, which became available Oct. 1, 2025, are permanently pulled back under language requiring the rescission by law. Notice.

Oversight Holds Requiring Spend Plans

Department of Veterans Affairs (FY2026): $374,317,430 in funding for the Annual Program Level account, of which $300 million is conditional on providing OMB with a detailed spend plan within 10 business days. The plan must outline spending and funding needs to support Joint Executive Committee priorities, including a joint Department of War/VA pharmacy pilot and a unified credentialing and privileging system. Notice.

Department of Veterans Affairs (FY2027): $387,318,090 for the same Annual Program Level account, conditioned on the identical spend-plan requirement due within 10 business days covering the pharmacy pilot and unified credentialing system. Notice.

Oversight Holds Requiring Programmatic Information or Reports

Department of Education: $69,588,896 held pending reapportionment, contingent on OMB receiving additional programmatic spending information. The funds, provided under Public Law 119-75 (ED 26-250), are available for obligation only upon reapportionment to an applicable Category B line. Notice.

Department of State (FY2027): $1,002,823,500 conditioned on monthly written updates to OMB, due 10 business days after the end of each month, reporting total commitments and obligations by population, crisis, or subcategory, with month-over-month comparisons to the State Department plan. Notice.

International Assistance Programs (FY2027): $1,241,916,948 for international assistance programs under the same monthly-reporting condition as State—commitments and obligations by category due 10 business days after month-end, with comparisons to agency plans. Notice.

International Assistance Programs (FY2027): Two additional apportionments totaling $310,400,000 are conditioned on "demonstrated programmatic need," allowing Treasury to request reallocation from Category C to Category B lines if spending needs arise during the fiscal year. Notice and Notice.

USAspending Contracts

FAA Awards $148.8 Million to Rebuild Sacramento's Air Traffic Control Tower in a Quiet Week for New Awards

Only three new $2 million-plus, unmodified contract actions cleared the 25-day trailing window: a nine-figure Federal Aviation Administration tower-replacement project, a Veterans Affairs program-support deal, and a State Department IT support award whose own description names only a contracting officer's representative rather than a scope of work. None involve the White House or the Executive Office of the President.

Walsh Federal LLC won a $148,798,000 design-build contract from the Federal Aviation Administration, part of the Department of Transportation, to replace the air traffic control tower at Sacramento International Airport (the award's internal shorthand, "DB SMF ATCT REPLACEMENT," uses the airport's SMF code). The award, dated September 19, 2026, is classified under commercial and institutional building construction and is by far the largest new award in this window — more than 50 times the size of the next-largest. Award record.

Kent Campa & Kate Inc received a $2,869,873.80 award from the Department of Veterans Affairs on September 17, 2026, for program-management and data-quality support tied to the Veteran Identification Card (VIC) program — the VA's replacement for the old paper veteran ID system. The award is classified as general business-support services. Award record.

JSSA Inc won a $2,500,000 IT-management-support-services award from the Department of State, also dated September 17, 2026. Notably, the award's own "description" field in USAspending contains no scope-of-work text at all — just the name of the contracting officer's representative, Erin C. Myers — leaving the actual work unspecified in the public record beyond its IT-labor classification. Award record.

Federal Contracting Actions (SAM.gov)

State Department Cites Middle East Shipping Instability in Rare Sole-Source Justification; Navy Ship-Maintenance and a Nationwide GSA Lease Solicitation Lead a 55-Notice Day

SAM.gov carried just one sole-source justification in the window, but it points to a live operational problem: the Department of State restricted competition on an outbound-shipping contract at the U.S. Embassy in Kuwait because of unreliable shipping through the Strait of Hormuz. Of 55 solicitations posted (all successfully retrieved, no quota shortfall), a handful stand out for subject matter — two Navy ship-maintenance availabilities, a large Army facilities contract in South Korea, a nationwide GSA office-lease solicitation, a Department of Energy particle-accelerator design contract, and an HHS emergency-response services solicitation — with the rest routine parts and services requests, mostly from Navy supply commands.

Sole-Source Justification
  • U.S. Department of State (U.S. Embassy Kuwait) — Limited-competition justification for outbound shipping services, awarded at $26,206. The justification states that "ongoing regional instability" has made standard shipping routes, including through the Strait of Hormuz, "unreliable or unavailable," so the embassy solicited quotes from only three vendors capable of handling alternative routing; three quotes were received and the lowest-priced technically acceptable one was selected. Notice.

The Federal Register

White House Publishes Executive Orders on Procurement Reciprocity and Water Quality Oversight; SEC Proposes Scrapping the Shareholder-Proposal Rule

Two substantive executive orders were published Monday — one on government procurement, one on federal water-quality oversight — alongside two routine observance proclamations and three final rules. The Federal Register's API returned no abstract text for any of the four presidential documents, so descriptions below are limited to their titles pending review of the full text at the source links. Separately, the SEC proposed rescinding the federal shareholder-proposal rule and modernizing proxy-solicitation requirements, and the FAA proposed a new airworthiness directive covering certain Boeing 737 models; four other proposed rules in the window were procedural or narrowly technical and are omitted below.

Executive Orders
  • Executive Office of the President — "Restoring Reciprocity in Government Procurement," published September 21, 2026. No abstract or summary text was available from the Federal Register at fetch time; the full order should be read directly. Full text.
  • Executive Office of the President — "Providing Meaningful Water Quality Improvements Through Collaboration and Oversight of Federal Support," published September 21, 2026. No abstract or summary text was available from the Federal Register at fetch time. Full text.
Also Published
  • Executive Office of the President — Proclamation, "National POW/MIA Recognition Day, 2026." Notice.
  • Executive Office of the President — Proclamation, "Constitution Day, Citizenship Day, and Constitution Week, 2026." Notice.
  • U.S. Coast Guard — Final rule establishing a temporary safety zone on the Illinois River at Morris, Ill., for a September 26 fireworks display; entry is prohibited without authorization from the Captain of the Port Lake Michigan. Notice.
  • Drug Enforcement Administration — Final rule placing the substance diphenidine into Schedule I of the Controlled Substances Act, in part to meet U.S. obligations under the 1971 Convention on Psychotropic Substances. Notice.
  • Department of State — Final rule revising regulations governing public access to information under the Department's control, including provisions for submitting requests by email. Notice.
Proposed Rules Worth Watching
  • Securities and Exchange Commission — Proposal to rescind Rule 14a-8, which currently requires companies to include qualifying shareholder proposals in proxy materials, leaving the matter to state law and corporate governing documents instead. Notice.
  • Securities and Exchange Commission — Companion proposal to modernize proxy-solicitation rules, including eliminating the requirement that companies deliver annual reports to shareholders and shortening the minimum broker-search period. Notice.
  • Federal Aviation Administration — Proposed airworthiness directive for Boeing 737-8, 737-9, and 737-8200 airplanes, after reports that elevator-buss-assembly bearings may have been installed incorrectly, including without required sealant, during production. Notice.
  • U.S. Coast Guard — Proposed 2027 Great Lakes pilotage rates, which the Coast Guard estimates would raise operating costs by about 10 percent compared with 2026, plus a new rate for the Straits of Mackinac following its designation under the FY2026 defense authorization act. Notice.
Four additional proposed rules were filtered out as routine or narrowly technical: a CFTC notice reopening a Privacy Act comment period, an EPA correction notice for a Superfund National Priorities List deletion, an NHTSA denial of a petition for a national road-mapping-database rulemaking, and an FAA correction of typographical errors in an earlier Robinson Helicopter special-conditions notice.
