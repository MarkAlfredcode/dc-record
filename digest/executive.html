Top Story

White House Files Five Same-Day Actions Escalating Canada Tariff Fight Over Autos, Dairy and Alcohol

Two presidential documents modify existing retaliatory duties on Canadian vehicles and alcohol, while three new ones bar entry of Canadian vehicles, dairy and alcohol outright, citing "continued discrimination" against U.S. commerce.

The Executive Office of the President published five trade-related presidential documents in the Federal Register on September 14, 2026, all concerning Canada and all filed the same day. Two of them, titled "Modifying the Scope of Products of Canada Subject to the Additional Duties Imposed To Offset Canadian Discrimination Against the Commerce of the United States," narrow or adjust which Canadian motor vehicles and alcoholic beverages are covered by existing retaliatory tariffs. The other three go further: titled "Excluding Certain Canadian Products From Importation Into the United States in Response to Continued Discrimination Against the Commerce of the United States," they bar entry of specific Canadian motor vehicles, dairy products, and alcoholic beverages altogether.

None of the five documents was published with a Federal Register abstract, so the above is drawn from the titles themselves; the full proclamation text — including which underlying statute or prior executive order this builds on — was not available in today's data pull. The "modifying the scope" language implies an earlier tariff action against Canada already exists, but that predecessor document falls outside today's collection window.

Taken together, the five filings represent a broadening of the dispute across three separate consumer and industrial sectors in a single day, which is why it leads this edition over even a $6.7 billion apportionment story out of the Office of Management and Budget (see below).

OMB Apportionments

Executive Order Redirects $75 Million From VA Electric Vehicle Initiative; Oversight Holds Condition $6.7 Billion in Apportionments

OMB approved four apportionments with conditional requirements across the week, totaling over $6.7 billion. Three flagged items require agencies to submit detailed spending plans or regular reporting updates before funds can be fully obligated. A fourth reflects an executive order action that has already redirected $75 million from a Department of Veterans Affairs electric vehicle program.

  • Department of Agriculture — Food for Peace Title II Grants, $2.04 billion (FY 2026): OMB flagged this apportionment because the agency must provide monthly updates to OMB on total commitments and obligations, organized by population, crisis, or subcategory. Apportionment document.
  • Department of Education — Budgetary Resources: Unappor bal, Federal Fund, $658.2 million (FY 2027): This appropriation is automatically apportioned for guaranty agencies unless it is designated as item 9 under 34 C.F.R. 682.419(c), in which case the agency must submit a detailed spend plan to OMB and wait ten business days before obligations may proceed. Apportionment document.
  • Department of Veterans Affairs — Category B - Non-Recurring Maintenance, $0 (FY 2027): Executive Order "Unleashing American Energy" revoked Executive Order 14057, which had prohibited the Department of Veterans Affairs' electric vehicle initiative; that $75 million has been reallocated to Minor Construction activities. Apportionment document.
  • Department of Veterans Affairs — Total budgetary resources available, $4 billion (FY 2027): OMB made these funds available for obligation only after the agency submits a detailed spend plan; further, if the Department of Veterans Affairs determines it needs to deviate from the spend plan by more than 5 percent, it must notify OMB at least five business days before obligating the increase. Apportionment document.

The Federal Register

Five Presidential Actions Target Canadian Autos, Dairy and Alcohol; FCC, Bank Regulators and EPA Also File Final Rules

Beyond the Canada trade actions covered above, September 14, 2026 also brought six other final rules — from the Federal Communications Commission, the Federal Aviation Administration, three bank regulators acting jointly, and the Coast Guard — plus eight proposed rules, only one of which appears to carry real policy weight beyond routine aviation-safety and state-implementation-plan housekeeping.

Presidential Actions on Canadian Trade
  • Executive Office of the President — Modifies the scope of Canadian motor vehicles subject to existing additional (retaliatory) duties. Document.
  • Modifies the scope of Canadian alcoholic beverages subject to those same additional duties. Document.
  • Excludes certain Canadian motor vehicles from importation into the United States entirely. Document.
  • Excludes certain Canadian dairy products from importation into the United States entirely. Document.
  • Excludes certain Canadian alcoholic beverages from importation into the United States entirely. Document.
Also Published
  • Federal Communications Commission — Announces that the Office of Management and Budget approved, until November 30, 2027, an information-collection requirement tied to the Commission's E-Rate program overhaul for school and library internet subsidies. Document.
  • Federal Communications Commission — Adopts its regulatory fee schedule for Fiscal Year 2026, setting the fees the agency assesses and collects from industry. Document.
  • Federal Aviation Administration — Revises airman certification rules and updates sport-pilot practical test standards to align with its recently adopted rule modernizing light-sport aircraft certification. Document.
  • Office of the Comptroller of the Currency, Federal Reserve System and Federal Deposit Insurance Corporation — Jointly issue an interim final rule implementing a provision of the 21st Century ROAD to Housing Act, raising the asset threshold for small banks (and U.S. branches of foreign banks) to qualify for a longer, 18-month on-site examination cycle from a shorter one, to under $6 billion in total assets. Document.
  • Environmental Protection Agency — Approves a revision to California's clean-air plan for the San Joaquin Valley, updating the region's motor-vehicle emissions budgets used to track compliance with the 2008 ozone air-quality standard. Document.
  • U.S. Coast Guard — Establishes a temporary safety zone on Lake Erie near Oregon, Ohio, closing a 300-yard radius of water to vessels during an overwater drone display. Document.
Proposed Rule Worth Watching
  • Federal Aviation Administration — Responding to a statutory mandate, is weighing whether to let firefighters ride aboard certain restricted-category aircraft when being flown to and from wildfire sites for ground suppression work, and is asking the public for data on current firefighter-transport resources and the cost-benefit tradeoffs involved. Document.
The remaining 7 proposed rules were routine: four Federal Aviation Administration airworthiness directives affecting Airbus, Dassault, Bombardier and CFM International engines/airframes; two Environmental Protection Agency negative declarations confirming the District of Columbia and Maryland have no regulated solid-waste incinerators; and one Environmental Protection Agency clean-data determination for the Sacramento, California ozone nonattainment area.
