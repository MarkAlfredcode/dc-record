EPA Finalizes Repeal of Most 2024 Power Plant Carbon Rules, Proposes to Eliminate Remaining Climate Authority Over the Sector

The agency repealed emission guidelines for existing coal- and gas-fired power plants and carbon-capture-based standards for new and modified units, then proposed going further by rescinding the underlying 2015 findings that EPA's authority to regulate greenhouse gases from power plants rests on.

The Environmental Protection Agency published a final rule today repealing most of the 2024 Carbon Pollution Standards for fossil fuel-fired electric generating units. The repeal covers the emission guidelines for existing coal- and gas-fired steam generating units, the carbon capture and sequestration (CCS)-based standards for coal-fired units undertaking a large modification, and the CCS-based standards for new baseload stationary combustion turbines. EPA frames the repeal as a 'reevaluation of the best system of emission reduction' for these subcategories.

In a companion action published the same day, EPA proposed to go significantly further. The supplemental notice argues that Section 111 of the Clean Air Act does not authorize EPA to regulate power-plant emissions in response to climate change at all. If finalized, that position would require rescinding the agency's own 2015 endangerment findings and repealing both the 2015 and 2024 greenhouse-gas standards for fossil fuel-fired power plants entirely — eliminating, rather than merely scaling back, federal climate regulation of the electric power sector.

Together, the two actions represent the most consequential federal action in today's Federal Register, well beyond the routine airworthiness directives, safety zones and program notices that make up most of the day's filings.
