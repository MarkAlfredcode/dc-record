OMB Approves $15.9 Billion Permanent Rescission of VA Medical Services Funding

The rescission, approved September 17 under Public Law 119-37, cancels roughly 21% of the $75.04 billion Congress had appropriated for veterans' health care under the Full-Year Continuing Appropriations Act, 2025.

The Office of Management and Budget approved a permanent $15.89 billion rescission of Department of Veterans Affairs funding on September 17, cutting deeply into the $75.04 billion Congress had appropriated for Veterans Health Administration Medical Services under the Full-Year Continuing Appropriations Act, 2025. The rescission was authorized under Public Law 119-37 and permanently cancels the funds rather than merely delaying their availability.

The VA cut was the largest single apportionment action OMB approved in a busy four-day stretch, September 17 through 20, that apportioned roughly $30 billion in new, carryover, and rescinded funding across multiple agencies. The Department of Homeland Security and the Department of Housing and Urban Development both received new money in the same window — $1.13 billion for DHS and more than $7.9 billion combined for HUD — but with strings attached: both are subject to OMB oversight holds requiring detailed spend plans or advance notice before the funds can be obligated, and some of the HUD money is conditioned on compliance with recent executive orders governing federal grantmaking oversight and disorder-related enforcement.

Veterans Health Rescission of $15.9 Billion Approved; OMB Issues Oversight Holds on $8 Billion in Appropriations

Between September 17 and 20, OMB approved eight notable apportionments affecting roughly $30 billion in federal funds. The largest action was a $15.9 billion permanent rescission of Department of Veterans Affairs medical services funding, while the Department of Homeland Security and Department of Housing and Urban Development both received new appropriations conditioned on oversight holds and executive-order compliance.

The Department of Homeland Security received $1.13 billion in new apportionments from Public Law 119-21, split between two tranches of $469.6 million and $661.2 million, both approved on September 17. Both tranches are subject to an OMB oversight hold requiring DHS to finalize and submit a detailed spend plan explaining how the funds will be obligated for programmatic activities under Section 100057 of the law. Notice.

The Department of Housing and Urban Development received $50 million in appropriations for the PRO Housing program from Public Law 119-75, approved on September 17. The funds are apportioned with the requirement that competitive grants include award agreements consistent with the directives in Executive Order 14332, "Improving Oversight of Federal Grantmaking." Notice.

HUD also received $6.99 billion in disaster assistance apportionments on the same date, subject to an OMB oversight hold that requires the agency to notify OMB at least 10 business days before making new disaster relief commitments. The notification requirement allows OMB to track grantees, allocation amounts, and underlying disaster declarations before funds are obligated. Notice.

In a separate action, HUD apportioned $865.4 million in carryover and recaptured funds from Public Law 119-75, conditioning the obligation of these amounts on compliance with Executive Order 14321, "Ending Crime and Disorder on America's Streets." Agencies must ensure award agreements incorporate the executive order's directives. Notice.

HUD processed a rescission of $5,036,988 in unobligated carryover balances under Section 234(d) of the Consolidated Appropriations Act, 2026 (P.L. 118-75), approved on September 17. The rescission was applied across two account lines totaling $4,267,239 and $769,750. Notice.

The Department of Veterans Affairs had $15.89 billion in previously appropriated funds permanently rescinded by Public Law 119-37, approved on September 17. The rescission reduces the $75.04 billion appropriated for Veterans Health Administration Medical Services under the Full-Year Continuing Appropriations Act, 2025. Notice.

VA Awards $64.5 Million EHRM Construction Job as ICE Detention and Training Facility Work Tops $47 Million

USAspending.gov logged 164 new contract actions of $2 million or more in the trailing 25-day window, ranging from a $64.5 million Department of Veterans Affairs hospital construction award in Milwaukee down to a $1.46 million federal-prison medical-services contract. The largest awards skewed toward VA and Department of Homeland Security construction and IT-modernization work, and two design-build task orders for U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement detention and training facilities went to the same joint venture in the same week.

The largest new contract of the window went to Sawtooth Construction JV II LLC, which won a $64,489,808 award from the Department of Veterans Affairs to build Tier 1 infrastructure for the Electronic Health Record Modernization (EHRM) program at the Clement J. Zablocki VA Medical Center in Milwaukee, Wisconsin. The award listing cites Executive Order 14398, a citation that recurs across dozens of this week's VA and other agency awards without further explanation in the contract description text. Award.

Award.
