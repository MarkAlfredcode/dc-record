Top Story

Customs and Border Protection awards $332 million design-build contract in Laredo, Texas

The single largest new federal award reported today dwarfs every other item across all five tasks in this edition.

The U.S. Customs and Border Protection component of the Department of Homeland Security awarded a $332,000,000 task order to Clark Construction Group LLC on September 26 — by far the largest new federal award reported in today’s sweep, more than 25 times the size of the next-largest new contract action and nearly as large as the combined $381 million in Department of Defense rescissions flagged separately in today’s OMB Apportionments section.

The task order was issued against an existing indefinite-delivery contract and covers what USAspending’s own award record describes only as "a design build project in Laredo, TX." CBP has not disclosed which facility the money will build; the underlying North American Industry Classification System and product/service codes — commercial and institutional building construction, for "other administrative facilities and service buildings" — point to a headquarters or operations facility rather than a port-of-entry expansion, but that is an inference from the codes, not a confirmed fact.

Laredo is one of the busiest land ports on the U.S.-Mexico border, and CBP has been steadily expanding infrastructure spending there and at other South Texas crossings. Today’s edition otherwise found no comparably large single award, no White House- or Executive Office of the President-tied contracts, and no ethics-filing divestitures or terminations.

OMB Apportionments

Defense rescissions total $381 million across three FY2026 accounts under Public Law 119-75

Three apportionment files approved this week were flagged as rescissions, all tied to the Department of Defense's military programs and the same underlying law. Seventy-seven other apportionment files cleared routinely in the same window, spanning agencies from the Department of Education to the Railroad Retirement Board, and no new or revised OMB Spend Plans were posted in this cycle.

Department of Defense–Military Programs had three separate funding rescissions approved on September 25 under a single law, Public Law 119-75 — a rescission means money Congress had already appropriated is being permanently pulled back rather than spent. A Research, Development, Test, and Evaluation account lost $82,461,000, a second Research, Development, Test, and Evaluation account lost $178,735,000, and a Procurement account lost $119,887,000 — a combined $381,083,000 in unobligated balances clawed back, all signed off by the Acting Program Associate Director for National Security Programs within a five-minute span that afternoon.

USAspending Contracts

Customs and Border Protection's $332 million Laredo award dwarfs today's other eight new contracts combined

Nine new-to-report contract actions of $2 million or more posted in the trailing 25-day USAspending sweep, led by a single $332 million Customs and Border Protection task order. No award in today's batch is tied to the White House or the Executive Office of the President.

General notable awards

Clark Construction Group LLC won a $332,000,000 task order from U.S. Customs and Border Protection, by far the largest new award in today’s window — more than 25 times the size of the next-largest one. The order, issued September 26 against an existing indefinite-delivery contract, funds what USAspending’s own award record describes only as "a design build project in Laredo, TX"; the underlying industry and product/service codes point to construction of an administrative or service facility rather than a port-of-entry expansion, but CBP has not disclosed further detail. Award record.

Quzara LLC received a $6,000,000 order from the Department of the Treasury’s Treasury Common Services Center, placed against an existing National Institute of Standards and Technology blanket contract. The raw award description says the money buys "Compliance.AI software licensing for up to 100 authorization packages" — commercial compliance-automation software used to manage the paperwork behind federal system security authorizations. Award record.

The Kinetic Group Sales LLC won a $3,402,869.88 contract from the Federal Law Enforcement Training Center, part of the Department of Homeland Security, for 9mm 147-grain ammunition — training rounds for federal law enforcement recruits. Award record.

EventStar Structures Corp received $1,777,614 from the Department of State for what its raw award description lists only as "G20 DORAL TENTS" — tent rental and setup, per the underlying product/service code for leased event equipment, tied to a G20-related event in Doral, Florida. Doral is home to the Trump National Doral golf resort, though the award description does not specify which venue the tents were erected at. Award record.

Billings PCE Superfund site remediation, Montana

The Environmental Protection Agency issued two new task orders on September 26 for ongoing cleanup work at the Billings PCE Superfund site (Operable Unit 1) in Montana: $5,412,677.74 to AECOM Technical Services, Inc. to install residential groundwater monitoring, mitigation, and control systems (award record), and $2,030,464.27 to EA Engineering, Science, and Technology, Inc., PBC for remedial design activities at the same site (award record). Both orders address perchloroethylene (PCE) groundwater contamination in the Billings area.

The Federal Register

No executive orders this window; eight FAA airworthiness directives lead 13 final rules as EPA proposes rolling back a 2024 gasoline-terminal emissions rule

No presidential documents or executive orders ran in the Federal Register this cycle. Thirteen final rules published, eight of them FAA airworthiness directives covering Boeing, Airbus, Safran, Guimbal, and Tecnam aircraft and engines; separately, the EPA proposed reconsidering several 2024 emissions-control requirements for gasoline-distribution facilities and the IRS issued a correction affecting Qualified Opportunity Fund reporting rules.

Also published (no executive orders this window)
  • Federal Aviation Administration — cited widespread noncompliance with existing exemptions and is rescinding the exemptions that let certain twin-pilot-certified Cessna aircraft fly with a single pilot, warning pilots who hold single-pilot endorsements from exemption-holding training providers that those endorsements no longer apply. Rule.
  • National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration — implements a rebuilding plan for the overfished Queets River spring/summer Chinook salmon stock off the West Coast, as recommended by the Pacific Fishery Management Council. Rule.
  • National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration — revises recreational vessel limits for gag and black grouper and transit-storage requirements for ropeless black sea bass pots in the South Atlantic snapper-grouper fishery. Rule.
  • Federal Aviation Administration — supersedes a 2023 airworthiness directive for Safran Helicopter Engines' Arrius 2B2 models, updating life-limit and inspection-interval requirements. Rule.
  • Federal Aviation Administration — issues a new directive for Hélicoptères Guimbal Cabri G2 helicopters after weak emergency-locator-transmitter signals were traced to improper antenna installation. Rule.
  • Federal Aviation Administration — issues a new directive for Tecnam P2006T airplanes requiring inspection of aileron hinges after a report of partial aileron detachment caused by a missing hinge pin. Rule.
  • Federal Aviation Administration — issues a new directive for Airbus Helicopters EC 130 T2 models after operators received uncertified main rotor blades that cannot be safely maintained, requiring their replacement. Rule.
  • Federal Aviation Administration — issues a new directive for Airbus Helicopters Deutschland EC135/EC635 models after finding the low-oil-pressure warning light might not activate during a main-gearbox oil loss. Rule.
  • Federal Aviation Administration — issues a new directive for Boeing 787-8/-9/-10 airplanes requiring inspection for missing cargo-liner joint sealing tape, which could let a cargo fire spread into the cabin. Rule.
  • Federal Aviation Administration — issues a new directive for Boeing 777 and 777F models after an overheated landing-gear-wheel-well motor pump caused a fire, requiring inspection and removal of the affected part number. Rule.
  • Environmental Protection Agency — expands two ocean dredged-material disposal sites offshore of Corpus Christi, Texas, to handle material dredged from the Corpus Christi Bay area, subject to ongoing monitoring and management. Rule.
  • Department of Housing and Urban Development — sets the FY2027 fee borrowers will pay to cover credit-subsidy costs on Section 108 Loan Guarantee Program loans. Rule.
  • Pipeline and Hazardous Materials Safety Administration — updates hazardous-materials rules to authorize the 2015 ASME Boiler and Pressure Vessel Code, Section XII, for building and maintaining cargo tank trucks, cryogenic portable tanks, and multi-unit tank car tanks. Rule.
