Top Story
Bureau of Reclamation awards $72.9 million for the next stretch of pipeline in the Navajo-Gallup Water Supply Project
A fully competed construction contract advances a decades-long federal Indian water-rights settlement project in northwestern New Mexico — the largest genuinely new contract award this newsletter could confirm today.

Carstensen Contracting Inc was awarded a $72,863,604 definitive contract by the Bureau of Reclamation, part of the Department of the Interior, on September 4, 2026, newly visible in USAspending's transaction data. The award record describes the work simply as "NGWSP Block 2-3 Pipeline," with place of performance in Farmington, New Mexico (San Juan County). The contract was fully and openly competed, drawing three offers.

NGWSP is the Navajo-Gallup Water Supply Project, a multi-decade federal pipeline system authorized by the Northwestern New Mexico Rural Water Projects Act of 2009 to deliver San Juan River water to the Navajo Nation, the Jicarilla Apache Nation, and the City of Gallup — built out in sequential construction segments (or "blocks") over many years as Congress funds each phase. Today's award is one such segment contract; the underlying award record does not itself describe the project's broader history or timeline, which is drawn here from the program's public authorization.

Nothing in the record suggests this award is unusual for the program — it was openly competed with multiple bidders, and Reclamation has awarded a long series of similar NGWSP construction contracts over the years. It leads today's contracting news simply on dollar value and program significance.
