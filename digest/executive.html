Surgeon General Nominee Nicole Saphier Agrees to Divest More Than 150 Stock Holdings, Cut Ties to Fox News and iHeartMedia

Her ethics agreement, filed with the U.S. Office of Government Ethics ahead of confirmation, lays out sweeping divestitures and recusals — including a special 210-day window for a Blackstone private credit fund.

Dr. Nicole Saphier, the nominee for Surgeon General at the U.S. Department of Health and Human Services, filed an ethics agreement with the U.S. Office of Government Ethics on September 7, 2026, describing the steps she will take to avoid conflicts of interest if confirmed. It is one of eight ethics filings flagged in this window, and among the most extensive: it covers her hospital employer, two resigned media jobs, ownership stakes in two health-care startups, book royalties, and a stock and fund portfolio spanning more than 150 individual holdings.

Upon confirmation, Saphier will take unpaid leave from her position as a physician at Memorial Sloan Kettering Cancer Center in New York, where she holds two mortgages on which the center currently forgives interest; she has agreed to pay that interest herself for the duration of her appointment. She will resign from and divest her interest in Empower MD, LLC (which owns the telehealth prescribing service Drop Rx) and in US Pain Care, LLC. Because she previously worked for Fox News Corp and iHeartMedia, she will also recuse for one year from any matter involving either company.

The bulk of the agreement is a list of more than 150 individual stocks and ETFs — including Apple, Amazon, Pfizer, Microsoft, JPMorgan Chase and Johnson & Johnson — that she must divest within 90 days of confirmation, plus a Blackstone Private Credit Fund holding she must exit within 210 days, a notably longer window carved out separately in the agreement. HHS ethics officials determined she need not divest a smaller group of holdings — including Zimmer Biomet Holdings, Inc. and an unnamed personal-care products maker — held inside two family investment vehicles, Corundrum LLC and ABSA, LLC, concluding that recusal alone will not meaningfully limit her ability to perform the job.

Saphier may keep royalties from four books, including "Make America Healthy Again" and "Panic Attack," but has agreed to do no writing, editing or promotional work on them while in office. Her spouse's ownership stakes in two New Jersey medical practices, Coaxial Neurological Specialists and Summit Intraoperative Neuromonitoring LLC, will also trigger standing recusals for as long as the spouse holds them.
