FAR Council Proposes Four Rules Rewriting Most of the Federal Acquisition Regulation Under Trump's Procurement-Overhaul Executive Order

The Federal Acquisition Regulatory Council published proposed rules touching 17 parts of the FAR, and a same-week Army sole-source notice shows contracting officers are already improvising around the transition.

The Office of Federal Procurement Policy, Department of Defense, General Services Administration, and National Aeronautics and Space Administration — together forming the Federal Acquisition Regulatory Council, or FAR Council — published four proposed rules in the September 18 Federal Register implementing Executive Order 14275, "Restoring Common Sense to Federal Procurement." The Council describes the four rules as part of a set of twelve that will, collectively, "streamline the FAR in its entirety." Today's batch alone touches FAR Parts 8 (Required Sources), 9 (Contractor Qualifications), 12 (Commercial Products and Services), 13 (Simplified Acquisition Procedures), 14 (Sealed Bidding), 15 (Contracting by Negotiation), 16 (Types of Contracts), 17 (Special Contracting Methods), 27 (Patents/Data Rights), 28 (Bonds and Insurance), 35 (Research and Development Contracting), 36 (Construction), 38 (Federal Supply Schedule Contracting), 44 (Subcontracting Policies), 47 (Transportation), and 51 (Use of Government Sources by Contractors), plus conforming changes to Part 52's solicitation clauses.

The proposals arrive against a backdrop where the underlying executive order already took effect February 1, 2026 — meaning agencies have been operating under the new deregulatory framework for months while the formal FAR text catches up. That gap is visible in a Department of the Army sole-source notice posted this week (see today's Federal Contracting Actions section): a contracting officer for the 409th Contracting Support Brigade in Germany explicitly flagged that "SAM.gov has not been updated to reflect current regulation prescriptions" under the overhaul, and that the FAR Part 13.5 authority listed on the form is only a placeholder because no current selection matches the new regulatory structure — the officer instead cited 41 U.S.C. § 1901(e)(2) and FAR 12.102(b) as the operative authority.

Separately in the same day's Federal Register, the Office of Personnel Management proposed a parallel deregulatory rule reshaping the excepted service — conforming its regulations to Schedules E, Policy/Career, and G, and clarifying Pathways-to-Schedule-Policy/Career conversions — another piece of the administration's broader effort to restructure the civil-service and procurement rulebooks in tandem.

OMB Apportionments

OMB Places Oversight Holds on $3.6 Billion in Justice Department Spending and Approves $15 Million Military Rescission

The Office of Management and Budget approved four flagged apportionment actions this week, including conditional releases tied to programmatic review and one rescission. No new or changed spend plan documents were flagged in this window.

Department of Justice received $3,602,632,594 in apportioned funds under the Working Families Tax Cut (Public Law 119-21), but the approval carries an oversight hold. The footnote states that "the Department of Justice will meet with the Homeland Security Council and the Office of Management and Budget (OMB) at OMB's request to provide status updates on activities and personnel funded with amounts provided by the Working Families Tax Cut." This rationale — OMB requests additional information on programmatic spending for some or all of the apportioned funds — signals the agency must brief OMB before these funds are obligated at full scale. Notice.

Department of Defense--Military Programs received a $15,000,000 rescission (negative apportionment) under Public Law 119-75. A rescission means Congress has reclaimed previously appropriated funds; this amount is no longer available for obligation. Notice.

Department of Labor received $217,934,350 for the H-1B Training program, with a 10-business-day oversight hold. OMB will not release these funds until it receives the final Secretary-approved Funding Opportunity Announcement; the agency must demonstrate a spend plan before the funds flow. The footnote cites the hold rationale as: "An agency spend plan or other documentation is necessary to better understand how the agency intends to obligate some or all of the apportioned funds." Notice.

Federal Labor Relations Authority (within Other Independent Agencies) received $186,417 in reimbursement authority for litigation support and detail assignments to other agencies. Under 31 U.S.C. § 1535, the agency seeks approval to collect reimbursement for expenses including external training, salaries for detailed employees providing litigation support and legal services, and costs of other detail assignments. Notice.

OGE Ethics Filings

Presidential Appointees File Ethics Agreements and Divestitures; Terminations Noted for Two Justice Department Officials

Sixteen ethics filings were flagged this week, including ethics agreements for ambassadors and cabinet-level officials, divestiture certificates, and termination notices for two departing officials. Most filings show standard conflict-of-interest recusals and asset management commitments.

Devin Kennington, Ambassador to Somalia (Federal Republic of) at the Department of State, filed an Ethics Agreement. The agreement details standard conflict-of-interest commitments under 18 U.S.C. § 208, including recusal from matters where he has financial interests and compliance with managed-account restrictions. He notes heightened attention to potential conflicts involving companies with a presence in Somalia. Ethics Agreement.

Stephen Husk, Commissioner, U.S. Parole Commission at the Department of Justice, filed an Ethics Agreement. The agreement covers standard recusal obligations and requires a live ethics briefing within 15 days of confirmation. Notably, he will retain his position as trustee of the Family Trust but will not participate in matters directly affecting the trust's financial interests unless he receives a waiver. Ethics Agreement.

William S Muldrow, U.S. Attorney Puerto Rico at the Department of Justice, filed a Termination; only the fact of the termination is confirmed, not the asset details. Request this Document.

Scott A Kupor, Director of the Office of Personnel Management, filed two 278 Transaction reports dated August 7, 2026, and certified by OGE on September 15. The first report discloses sales of Baron Growth Fund Retail Shares ($50,001–$100,000), Cambiar Opportunity Fund Investor Class Shares ($1,001–$15,000), and Gabelli Utilities Fund Class AAA Shares ($15,001–$50,000). The second report discloses sales of Airbnb Inc. Class A Common Stock ($1,000,001–$5,000,000) and Figma Class A Shares ($500,001–$1,000,000), consistent with prescribed holdings limits for federal officials. 278 Transaction.

David S Johanson, Commissioner at the International Trade Commission, filed a Termination; only the fact of the termination is confirmed, not the asset details. Request this Document.

The Federal Register

State Department Eases Export Controls on Underwater Drones and Adds Saudi Arabia, Peru as Major Non-NATO Allies

The September 18 Federal Register carries the four Federal Acquisition Regulation overhaul rules covered in today's top story, alongside a State Department rule loosening arms-export controls on uncrewed underwater vehicles and updating its allies list, a Homeland Security rule on domestic PPE sourcing, and a batch of routine FAA and agriculture corrections. A Presidential Determination on drug-producing countries for fiscal year 2027 was also published.

Presidential Actions

The Executive Office of the President published the annual Presidential Determination on Major Drug Transit or Major Illicit Drug Producing Countries for Fiscal Year 2027, the statutorily required designation that underpins U.S. counternarcotics and foreign-assistance policy; no abstract text accompanied the notice. Notice.

  • Department of State — amends the International Traffic in Arms Regulations to remove certain uncrewed underwater vehicles from the U.S. Munitions List and requests comment on further easing related license requirements. Rule.
  • Department of Homeland Security — final rule amending its acquisition regulation to codify compliance with the Make Personal Protective Equipment in America Act, aimed at sustaining domestic PPE manufacturing. Rule.
