Top Story
NASA hands Blue Origin $660 million to build the communications backbone for Mars Sample Return
A single definitive contract — the largest new federal award found in this edition — makes the space company a lead infrastructure contractor on NASA's most troubled flagship planetary-science program.

Blue Origin, LLC was awarded a $660,000,017 definitive contract by the National Aeronautics and Space Administration on August 31, 2026, newly visible in USAspending's transaction data as of this run. The contract's stated scope, per the award record: "provide design, development, integration, launch, and on orbit, continuous communications in support of a Mars Sample Return (MSR) mission and future Mars surface, orbital, and human exploration missions." Its period of performance runs from September 1, 2026 through December 31, 2030, with options extending to December 31, 2035.

Mars Sample Return — NASA's plan to retrieve rock and soil samples already collected by the Perseverance rover — has spent the past several years under sustained budget and schedule pressure, including an independent review board finding the original architecture unworkable and NASA soliciting alternative mission designs from industry. A $660 million award for dedicated communications infrastructure is a significant new financial commitment to keeping some version of the mission alive, and it extends Blue Origin's federal footprint well beyond its existing lunar-lander and launch-vehicle work.

The underlying procurement record does not indicate whether this award was competed or sole-sourced; USAspending's competition fields were not populated for this transaction as of this run. No sole-source justification for this award appeared in SAM.gov's Justification & Approval postings — though that check could not be completed today; see the Federal Contracting Actions section below.

The Federal Register
Treasury's FinCEN reissues a Southwest border cash-reporting order for money services businesses; no executive orders published

No presidential documents (executive orders or proclamations) published in this window. Ten final rules were published, mostly routine — five Coast Guard/FAA safety-zone, drawbridge, and airspace notices, an Endangered Species Act non-listing finding, and an EPA reporting-deadline extension — but two carry real policy weight: a Financial Crimes Enforcement Network geographic targeting order imposing new cash-transaction recordkeeping on money services businesses along the Southwest border, and a Treasury Department/IRS final rule implementing the new car-loan interest tax deduction. Separately, 9 proposed rules were published; 5 are flagged below for policy significance and 4 were filtered out as routine (two FAA Class E airspace boundary changes, an FAA medical-certification streamlining rule for diabetes, and an HHS deregulatory recodification of an outdated mentally-ill-nationals repatriation rule).
