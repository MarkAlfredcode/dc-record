Ethics Filing Shows Attorney General Pam Bondi's Tenure Ended Months Ago, Made Public Only This Week

Bondi's OGE termination report, signed in June and certified in September, discloses her post-government financial arrangements as she leaves the nation's top law enforcement post.

A termination financial-disclosure report from Pam Bondi, the Attorney General of the Department of Justice, surfaced in this week's U.S. Office of Government Ethics filings and shows her government service in the post ended April 1, 2026 — information not previously reflected in OGE's public filing index. The report itself was electronically signed June 16, 2026, reviewed by the department's ethics office through late June, and only certified by OGE and posted publicly on September 18, 2026, meaning the paper trail of her departure became visible to the public roughly five months after the fact.

The filing discloses that Bondi's consulting firm, PB Advisory LLC, will remain dormant and not advertise during the period covered, with Bondi performing no services for it beyond legal filings, taxes, and any court-ordered obligations. She also disclosed a Florida state pension — a defined-benefit payment of about $6,250 a month once she reaches age 62, tied to 28 years with the State of Florida beginning in 1991 — along with a retained 401(k) balance from her prior position at Ballard Partners.

Her spouse's holdings are also itemized: a salaried position at Varner Commercial Property Consultants, plus ownership stakes in several commercial real-estate LLCs (VW Preserve Lane, VW Willow Lake, VW Northwoods, and VW Manager) generating rental income as high as $1 million a year from properties in Florida and South Carolina. The report lists no other federal positions held by Bondi in the preceding 12 months.

NIH's $237.8 Million Design-Build Award Leads a 117-Contract Day, With an Anti-DEI Executive Order Cited in Unrelated Construction Jobs

USAspending logged 117 new-to-report contract actions of $2 million or more in this window (Aug. 30–Sept. 24 trailing sweep). The largest was a $237.8 million design-build award to the National Institutes of Health; three separate award clusters — a nine-contract USDA food-donation buy, a three-contract CBP land-port repair job, and a three-contract Education Department loan-servicing pilot — are grouped below rather than listed individually. Every other new award is listed by recipient, amount, agency and short description.

The Federal Register

Two H-1B Visa Presidential Actions Headline a Register Window That Also Reforms NRC Reactor Rules and Schedules Five Hallucinogens

Seventeen rules and presidential documents and 12 proposed rules published Sept. 23–24. The most consequential items: two presidential actions narrowing and adding oversight to the H-1B visa program (details limited, since the Federal Register listing carries no abstract for either), an NRC proposal to rewrite its reactor-licensing rules under Executive Order 14300, and a DEA proposal to schedule five tryptamine hallucinogens.

Executive Orders

Two presidential documents on H-1B nonimmigrant visas published Sept. 23: “Enhancing Program Integrity and Interagency Coordination in the Administration of the H-1B Nonimmigrant Visa Program” and “Restriction on Entry of Certain Nonimmigrant Workers.” The Federal Register listing does not include abstracts for either document, so beyond their titles — one aimed at tightening interagency coordination and program-integrity checks on H-1B administration, the other imposing an entry restriction on some category of nonimmigrant workers — the specific mechanics (which visa categories, what triggers a restriction, effective dates) aren't available from this listing. Program Integrity document; Restriction on Entry document.

OMB Apportionments

OMB Flags Education Department Funding Hold and OPM Shutdown Contingency Among 27 New Apportionments

OMB approved 27 new apportionment files in this five-day window (Sept. 19–24). Two carried footnotes that triggered oversight flags — a $69.6 million hold on Department of Education funds pending more information, and a $36.2 million Office of Personnel Management apportionment with a lapse-in-appropriations contingency. No new or changed Spend Plan PDFs were found in this window. The other 25 files were routine bureau-level allocations, listed below by agency.

Department of Education had $69,588,896 placed on an “Unallocated” apportionment line (Line 6100) under Public Law 119-75. OMB's footnote says the funds are “available for obligation upon reapportionment to an applicable Category B line,” and that OMB “requests additional information on programmatic spending for some or all of the apportioned funds” — in plain terms, an oversight hold that keeps the money from being spent until the department answers OMB's questions. Apportionment.

Office of Personnel Management received a $36,166,796 apportionment for “Total budgetary resources” under Public Law 119-75. Its footnote flags a $29,192,000 obligation limitation for administrative expenses that “is not in effect during a lapse in appropriations” — meaning that if the government shuts down, OPM's administrative costs would shift to mandatory funding rather than the capped discretionary amount, then shift back afterward. Apportionment.
