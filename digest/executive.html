Top Story
IRS finalizes new $10,000 car-loan interest deduction, with reporting rules for lenders
Treasury Department and the Internal Revenue Service publish final regulations Tuesday implementing a deduction of up to $10,000 in qualified passenger-vehicle loan interest, alongside new $600-threshold reporting duties for lenders — one of three final rules and two proposed rules to hit the register overnight, in an otherwise quiet weekend for contracting, apportionment and ethics-filing activity.

The Treasury Department and the Internal Revenue Service on Monday issued final regulations (Treasury Decision 10054) allowing certain taxpayers to deduct up to $10,000 a year in interest paid on qualified passenger-vehicle loans, set to take effect November 9, 2026. The rule also imposes new information-reporting obligations on any person who, in the course of business, collects $600 or more in a calendar year in interest on a specified passenger-vehicle loan from an individual — with penalties attached for lenders who fail to file the required returns or furnish payee statements.

The reporting regime effectively turns auto lenders, dealer-finance arms and credit unions into a new category of information-return filer, similar to how mortgage servicers already report interest under Form 1098. Treasury's own abstract frames this as implementation guidance rather than new tax policy — the underlying deduction stems from a statutory change — but the final rule is the document that actually operationalizes it for the 2026 filing season.

Two other final rules published overnight: a U.S. Coast Guard temporary safety zone in the Laguna Madre off South Padre Island, Texas, for a fireworks display, and a Commodity Futures Trading Commission amendment to interest-rate-swap clearing requirements that accounts for the retirement of Canadian and Mexican interbank benchmark rates (CDOR and TIIE) in favor of their risk-free-rate replacements (CORRA and F-TIIE). Full rundown of both, plus two notable proposed rules from the FCC and the Bureau of Land Management, in the Federal Register section below.

Federal Contracting Actions
HHS sole-sources a cybersecurity bridge contract to keep SAMHSA's IT systems protected

SAM.gov logged just one sole-source justification and 81 solicitations/presolicitations posted since Saturday — both well below weekday volume. Of the solicitations, four stand out on subject matter; the rest are overwhelmingly routine military parts and facilities-maintenance postings.

Sole-Source Justifications
  • Health Resources and Services Administration (Department of Health and Human Services) is sole-sourcing a bridge contract, "SAMHSA IT Security Support," to the incumbent vendor Centerpoint LLC to keep cybersecurity services running for the Substance Abuse and Mental Health Services Administration's IT systems while a full competition is underway. The justification, filed under FAR 16.507-6(b)(3) as a logical follow-on order, states that switching vendors now would require a 30-60 day knowledge-transfer period given SAMHSA's numerous unique system configurations, and that a lapse in coverage risks data breaches and compliance violations; the contracting officer's approval authority is capped at $900,000, indicating a modest-dollar bridge rather than a major re-compete. The Federal Register
BLM proposes streamlined permitting for oil and gas production sites in Alaska's National Petroleum Reserve

Three final rules and two proposed rules of note published since Saturday; the Car Loan Interest Deduction rule leads today's edition above. One proposed rule filtered out as not editorially significant for this beat.

  • Final Rule — Treasury Department/Internal Revenue Service, "Car Loan Interest Deduction": finalizes a deduction of up to $10,000 for qualified passenger-vehicle loan interest and new $600-threshold information-reporting requirements for lenders. Effective November 9, 2026. Read the rule
  • Final Rule — U.S. Coast Guard (Department of Homeland Security), "Safety Zone; Laguna Madre, South Padre Island, TX": establishes a temporary safety zone in navigable waters to protect vessels and personnel during an over-water fireworks display. Read the rule
  • Final Rule — Commodity Futures Trading Commission, interest-rate-swap clearing requirement amendment: updates mandatory-clearing rules to reflect the transition from CDOR/TIIE benchmark rates to their replacements, CORRA and F-TIIE, for swaps denominated in Canadian dollars and Mexican pesos. Read the rule
  • Proposed Rule — Bureau of Land Management (Department of the Interior), "National Petroleum Reserve in Alaska Production Site Development": would establish pre-defined criteria for common, repeatable oil-and-gas production-site activities in the NPR-A, streamlining the permitting process for qualifying projects and their rights-of-way. Read the proposal
  • Proposed Rule — Federal Communications Commission, "Unleashing Unlicensed Spectrum for Direct-to-Device": proposes to let certain unlicensed devices communicate directly with satellites on a non-interference basis, adding satellite allocations to specific unlicensed spectrum bands to support direct-to-device connectivity services. Read the proposal
