Top Story
Army Quietly Doubles a No-Bid Fort Sam Houston Inspection Contract to $200.6 Million
A justification posted yesterday adds $109.3 million to a facilities/building-inspection deal held by JLL-RER Solutions, without new competition.
The Mission Installation Contracting Command's field directive office at Fort Sam Houston, Texas — part of the U.S. Army Materiel Command's Army Contracting Command — posted a Justification and Approval yesterday for a noncompetitive modification to contract W9124J-23-D-0016, held by JLL-RER Solutions, LLC. The filing states plainly: the modification "increases the contract ceiling by $109.3M, raising the total value from $91.3M to $200.6M" — more than doubling the deal's potential value without running a new competition.
The underlying work is classified under NAICS code 541350 (Building Inspection Services) and product/service classification code H399 (Inspection — Miscellaneous), consistent with facilities-inspection support at the San Antonio-area Army installation. The notice does not spell out why the increase is being handled as a sole-source ceiling raise rather than a new competitive award; SAM.gov's public notice provides only the justification summary quoted above, not the full underlying J&A document.
It was one of 15 sole-source justifications posted across the government in the past day — see the Federal Contracting Actions section below for the full list, including a $39 million FEMA wildfire-claims task order and a State Department event contract in India.

USAspending Contracts
FEMA Taps Fluor Federal Services for $39 Million in Wildfire-Claims Support
Scanning USAspending's transaction data for new-base ($2 million-plus) contract awards first appearing in the last 25 days turned up just two awards not already tracked in prior editions. The larger is a FEMA task order tied to the federal government's wildfire-compensation program in New Mexico; the smaller is a routine VA medical-equipment purchase. Nothing here suggests a sole-source or unusual-recipient pattern beyond the Army item covered in the Top Story.
Fluor Federal Services Inc. — $39,062,722.19. Awarded by the Federal Emergency Management Agency (contract 70FB8026F00000077, action date September 5), the time-and-materials task order was competed under full-and-open competition. It funds technical support work for the Hermit's Peak/Calf Canyon Claims Office, the federal office Congress created to pay compensation claims from the 2022 Hermit's Peak/Calf Canyon Fire — New Mexico's largest wildfire on record, which was sparked by a U.S. Forest Service prescribed burn that escaped control. The task order covers claims-review support for that office.
usaspending.gov: CONT_AWD_70FB8026F00000077
Datex-Ohmeda, Inc. — $3,199,498.08. Awarded by the Department of Veterans Affairs (contract 36C24526F0386, action date September 6), the firm-fixed-price purchase — made via a multiple-award fair-opportunity competition — funds anesthesia machines, installation and training at VA medical centers in Clarksburg, West Virginia; Baltimore, Maryland; Martinsburg, West Virginia; and Washington, D.C. This reads as a routine equipment refresh; nothing about it stands out.

The Federal Register
Treasury Finalizes the New Car-Loan Interest Deduction
Three final rules and two policy-significant proposed rules published in the window; no executive orders or presidential documents. The Treasury Department's final rule implements the deduction of up to $10,000 in qualified passenger-vehicle loan interest, plus new lender information-reporting requirements. One additional proposed rule was filtered out as routine.
Final rules
  • Treasury Department — "Car Loan Interest Deduction": final regulations on the deduction of up to $10,000 in qualified passenger-vehicle loan interest, with new information-reporting requirements (and penalties for noncompliance) for lenders receiving $600 or more in such interest from an individual in a year. Federal Register
  • Commodity Futures Trading Commission — amends interest-rate-swap clearing-requirement rules to reflect the market shift from Canada's CDOR benchmark to CORRA, and from Mexico's TIIE to F-TIIE, updating which swaps must be cleared through a registered or exempt derivatives clearing organization. Federal Register
  • Homeland Security Department (Coast Guard) — establishes a temporary safety zone on the Laguna Madre near South Padre Island, Texas, for a fireworks display, restricting vessel entry unless authorized by the Coast Guard's Sector Corpus Christi. Federal Register
Proposed rules of note
  • Federal Communications Commission — "Unleashing Unlicensed Spectrum for Direct-to-Device": proposes letting certain unlicensed consumer devices communicate directly with satellites on a non-interference basis, adding satellite allocations in specific unlicensed bands and a framework meant to preserve existing operations while enabling phone-to-satellite connectivity. Federal Register
  • Bureau of Land Management — proposes streamlining permitting for oil and gas production sites and associated rights-of-way in the National Petroleum Reserve in Alaska, setting pre-defined criteria that would fast-track qualifying, repeatable projects with similar environmental effects. Federal Register
