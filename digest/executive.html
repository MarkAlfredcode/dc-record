Top Story

Coast Guard hands Whiting-Turner $173.7 million for a Seattle base project — after just one company bid

The single largest new contract action found in a 25-day USAspending sweep was nominally "full and open competition." Only one offer came in.

The U.S. Coast Guard awarded The Whiting-Turner Contracting Company, a Baltimore-based construction firm, a $173,722,190 firm-fixed-price delivery order on September 9 for what the award record describes only as "acceptance of contractor proposal for Base Seattle CERCLA/PSC Homeport Phase 1B Project." The work is to be performed in Seattle, Washington, with a period of performance running from September 9, 2026, through July 14, 2029 — nearly three years. The order was issued under an existing multiple-award construction vehicle (parent award 70Z04723DPCNI0006) and funded directly by the Department of Homeland Security.

The CERCLA reference ties the project to the federal Superfund cleanup law, suggesting the homeport work is linked to environmental remediation at the Coast Guard's Seattle base. USAspending's contract-data fields list the order as competed under "multiple award fair opportunity" procedures with "full and open competition" — but the same record shows only one offer was received. That combination, a competed vehicle that nonetheless drew a single bidder, is common on large, specialized construction task orders but is worth flagging on a contract of this size.

It's the largest of 61 newly posted contract actions of $2 million or more that DC Record's 25-day USAspending sweep turned up since the last check — a batch worth roughly $590.4 million combined, detailed in the USAspending Contracts section below.

USAspending Contracts

A $590 million batch of 61 new contracts, heavy on VA facility work citing an anti-DEI executive order

DC Record's 25-day trailing sweep of USAspending's transaction-level data turned up 61 new base contract actions of $2 million or more that weren't in our manifest before — worth about $590.4 million combined. Most (55 of 61) carry an action date of September 9, reflecting USAspending's typical multi-day posting lag rather than a single burst of new activity. Beyond the lead story's $173.7 million Coast Guard award, the next-largest are a $59.75 million U.S. Customs and Border Protection design-build order for a new Border Patrol station and a $45.9 million Department of Veterans Affairs electronic-health-record-linked construction project. A notable pattern: at least eight of today's VA and HHS contracts explicitly cite Executive Order 14398, "Addressing DEI Discrimination by Federal Contractors" (signed March 26, 2026), in their description fields as compliance justification — most of them part of an apparent VA-wide elevator-modernization push detailed below.

The Federal Register

Four new executive orders published this week, from livestock markets to Defense Production Act delegations

The Federal Register published 40 Rules and presidential documents since yesterday: four new executive orders (all signed within the past week), a Labor Day proclamation, a notice continuing a terrorism-related national emergency, and 34 final rules — the large majority routine FAA airworthiness directives, Coast Guard local marine-safety zones, OFAC sanctions-license republications, and FDA medical-device classifications. Separately, 17 proposed rules were posted; five carry real policy weight and are detailed below, while the other 12 were narrow technical or single-species fishery/species-listing actions not detailed here.
New executive orders
  • Executive Order 14426, "Accelerating Access to Veterans' Benefits and Employment Opportunities" (signed September 8), directs the Secretary of War and the Secretary of Veterans Affairs to build updated IT systems and policy within 180 days to permanently share military personnel and medical records between the two departments, aiming to cut delays in veterans' healthcare coverage, benefits processing, and job placement after service. Read the order.
  • Executive Order 14427, "Adjusting Certain Delegations Under the Defense Production Act" (signed September 8), amends the government's standing Defense Production Act delegations (Executive Order 13603) to give the Secretary of the Interior shared authority with the Secretary of Energy over energy-related DPA matters, each able to act independently, with disputes between the two on energy issues routed to the National Energy Dominance Council. Read the order.
  • Executive Order 14425, "Supporting America's Ranchers" (signed September 4), directs further action to expand grazing access on federal land and continue rolling back what the order calls "burdensome Biden-era regulations," citing a 75-year low in the national cattle herd against rising beef demand. Read the order.
  • Executive Order 14424, "Promoting Fair Competition in Livestock Markets and Expanding Market Access for American Meat Producers" (signed September 4), directs more vigorous enforcement of the Packers and Stockyards Act of 1921 by the Secretary of Agriculture and directs agencies to use existing authorities to expand interstate market access for ranchers who process and sell their own meat. Read the order.
Also published
  • Presidential proclamation, "Labor Day, 2026," and a notice continuing the national emergency with respect to certain terrorist attacks (originally declared after September 11, 2001), both routine annual/periodic actions. Labor Day proclamation · Emergency continuation.
  • Federal Communications Commission final rule further restricting equipment authorizations tied to national-security risk in the communications supply chain. Read the rule.
  • Treasury Department (Office of Foreign Assets Control) republished six sets of sanctions-related general licenses covering Iran, Venezuela, Nicaragua, global terrorism, and illicit drug trade programs — largely administrative postings of licenses already available on OFAC's website. Example: Iran sanctions notice.
Notable proposed rules
  • Department of Homeland Security (U.S. Citizenship and Immigration Services) proposed rule to set a fee for certain H-1B visa petitions. Read the proposal.
  • DHS proposed rule, "Eliminating the Discretionary 60-Day Grace Period," which would remove agency discretion to grant certain nonimmigrants a 60-day grace period after employment or status changes. Read the proposal.
  • Department of Agriculture (Forest Service) proposed rule on Special Areas/Roadless Area Conservation, revisiting protections for inventoried roadless areas on National Forest System land. Read the proposal.
  • Department of Commerce (Census Bureau) proposed residence criteria and demographic-question regulations for the decennial census. Read the proposal.
  • Securities and Exchange Commission proposed rule on political contributions by certain investment advisers, revisiting "pay-to-play" restrictions. Read the proposal.
