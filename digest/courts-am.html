A Quiet Stretch: No Distinctive New Cabinet or D.C. Litigation Filed

Most of this week's new filings against federal or District of Columbia government parties in D.D.C. turned out to be outside this edition's scope once each was checked against its actual defendant.

Reviewing new civil filings in the U.S. District Court for the District of Columbia over the past several days turned up no lawsuit against a cabinet-level federal agency or the District of Columbia municipal government substantial enough to stand as its own story today.

Several filings that looked promising on a first pass did not hold up under closer review. Two disability-benefits appeals, Jackson v. Bisignano and Grant v. Bisignano, name the Social Security Administration's commissioner, but that agency is independent rather than cabinet-level and falls outside this edition's scope. A Freedom of Information Act suit, Judicial Watch, Inc.'s Judicial Watch, Inc. v. Central Intelligence Agency, names the Central Intelligence Agency, also not a cabinet department. A suit brought by Doe plaintiffs against the Islamic Republic of Iran under the Foreign Sovereign Immunities Act targets a foreign government, not a U.S. one, and a suit styled National Iranian American Council Action v. Trump names only the president, not a cabinet secretary or department, in the docket caption available.

The one case in this week's filings that did fit this edition's criteria was routine enough to be reported only as part of this edition's immigration-litigation count, below.

New Litigation

Single New Immigration-Adjudication Suit Against Homeland Security Component

Only one new filing against a cabinet-level agency survived this edition's screening this week, and it was a routine individual immigration case rather than a distinctive dispute.

1 new suit over delayed federal adjudication was filed: Adriaan Jacobus Mostert and Chiragkumar Vitthalbhai Patel sued the U.S. Citizenship and Immigration Services' Immigrant Investor Program Office on September 24, 2026, seeking judicial review under the Administrative Procedure Act of delayed adjudication of an EB-5 immigrant-investor petition (Mostert v. Immigrant Investor Program Office, U.S. Citizenship and Immigration Services, No. 1:26-cv-03342). Complaint text not available, docket caption only.

FOIA Litigation

No New FOIA Suits Against a Cabinet Agency or the District of Columbia

The only new FOIA filing identified in this week's docket review, Judicial Watch, Inc. v. Central Intelligence Agency, names the Central Intelligence Agency as defendant. Because the Central Intelligence Agency is an independent agency rather than a cabinet department, that suit falls outside this edition's scope and is not counted below.

No new FOIA suits against a cabinet-level agency or the District of Columbia government were filed this week.
