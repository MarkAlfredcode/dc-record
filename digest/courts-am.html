Top Story

Another wrongful-death suit filed over January 2025 Reagan National midair collision, naming the United States

A new Federal Tort Claims Act suit joins the consolidated D.D.C. litigation over the crash that killed 67 people.

Pete G. Diaz, Jr. and Linda D. Diaz filed a wrongful-death suit on September 15 in U.S. District Court for the District of Columbia against American Airlines, Inc., PSA Airlines, Inc., and the United States of America, docketed as Diaz, Jr. v. American Airlines, Inc., No. 1:26-cv-03227.

The docket lists the cause of action as 28 U.S.C. § 1346, the Federal Tort Claims Act, and the nature of suit as "310 Airplane." The same day, the plaintiffs' attorney filed a notice identifying the case as related to No. 1:25-cv-03382 — the docket captioned In re: Mid-Air Collision in Washington, D.C., Jan. 29, 2025, the consolidated proceeding before U.S. District Judge Ana C. Reyes gathering wrongful-death and injury claims arising from the collision between American Eagle Flight 5342, a regional jet operated by PSA Airlines on behalf of American Airlines, and a U.S. Army Black Hawk helicopter near Ronald Reagan Washington National Airport, which killed all 67 people aboard both aircraft.

The complaint itself is sealed and was not available on RECAP as of this writing, so the specific allegations against the United States could not be confirmed by reading the filing. This entry is based on the docket caption, cause code, and the related-case notice only.

The relief sought was not stated in any document available to review. The filing adds one more individual wrongful-death claim to Judge Reyes's consolidated proceeding for suits arising from the collision.

New Litigation

A thin day otherwise: one more federal tort claim, few new cabinet-relevant suits

Beyond the Diaz filing above, only one other new D.D.C. case this run named the federal government as a party in a way that fits this edition's scope, and it could not be meaningfully characterized beyond its caption.

Most of today's other government-adjacent filings — a Congressional Accountability Act suit naming the U.S. Capitol Police, two Freedom of Information Act suits naming the U.S. Postal Service, and a suit by the Associated Press against the Administrative Office of the United States Courts — involve legislative-branch, independent-establishment, or judicial-branch entities rather than a cabinet department or the District of Columbia government, and are outside this edition's scope even though they surfaced in the same search.

A Federal Tort Claims Act personal-injury suit was filed September 16 naming the United States, an individual defendant named Paul Rae, and former Justice Department officials Merrick Garland and Matthew Graves in a docket captioned only "v. Rae" (No. 1:26-cv-03232) — RECAP's index did not carry a plaintiff name, and no complaint document was available to read, so beyond the docketed cause of action (28 U.S.C. § 2671) nothing further about the claim could be confirmed.

FOIA Litigation

No new FOIA suits against a cabinet-level agency or the District of Columbia government today

Two new Freedom of Information Act suits were filed in D.D.C. this run, both against the U.S. Postal Service — American Oversight and, separately, the Project on Government Oversight, Inc. Because the Postal Service is an independent establishment of the executive branch rather than one of the 15 cabinet departments, neither falls within this edition's scope.

No new FOIA suits against a cabinet-level agency or the District of Columbia government were filed today.
