Top Story

Voting-rights and civil-rights groups sue to block armed ICE agents from polling places

A coalition led by the League of United Latin American Citizens invokes a Civil War-era law barring federal troops from election sites.

The League of United Latin American Citizens, Common Cause, the Common Cause Education Fund, UnidosUS, and the City and County of Denver, Colorado, sued the U.S. Department of Homeland Security, Secretary of Homeland Security Markwayne Mullin, U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement, and Acting ICE Director David Venturella in the U.S. District Court for the District of Columbia, seeking to block what the complaint calls the administration's "Polling Place Policy."

According to the complaint, Secretary Mullin confirmed on September 1, 2026, that armed ICE officers may be sent to polling places during the 2026 midterm election to serve warrants and detain people suspected of being in the country unlawfully. The plaintiffs argue this violates an 1865 federal statute, 18 U.S.C. § 592, enacted during the Civil War, which makes it a felony for federal officials to station armed men at polling places except to repel armed enemies of the United States.

The complaint cites ProPublica reporting that ICE has detained more than 170 U.S. citizens and New York Times reporting that at least 23 people have been shot at by federal immigration agents since 2025, arguing that the mere presence of armed ICE officers near polling places would chill turnout, particularly among Latino voters represented by the plaintiff organizations.

The plaintiffs are asking the court to declare the Polling Place Policy unlawful and to permanently enjoin Homeland Security and ICE officers from carrying weapons near polling places, dropboxes, and curbside voting locations during early voting and on Election Day, for any reason other than repelling an armed enemy of the United States. They are represented by the Democracy Forward Foundation and the Public Rights Project.

The case is notable both for its timing — filed with the midterm election approaching — and for its unusual plaintiff coalition of national civil-rights organizations joined by a city government, built around a rarely litigated Reconstruction-era statute rather than a modern voting-rights law.

Read the complaint: League of United Latin American Citizens v. U.S. Department of Homeland Security, No. 1:26-cv-03157 (D.D.C. filed Sept. 10, 2026)
New Litigation

A second suit against Secretary Mullin, a Justice Department challenge to D.C. tuition policy, and agency suits from a union and a bank watchdog

Four other cases filed in the U.S. District Court for the District of Columbia this week stand out from the routine volume: a sealed civil-rights suit against Secretary Mullin and several law-enforcement officials, a Justice Department suit against the District of Columbia's government, and Administrative Procedure Act challenges brought by a public-employee union and a financial-reform group.

  • United States of America sued the District of Columbia, the Board of Trustees of the University of the District of Columbia, and Antoinette Mitchell, in her official capacity as State Superintendent of Education of the District of Columbia, over a D.C. regulation that allows immigrants who are not lawfully present to receive in-state tuition and financial aid at public postsecondary institutions. The complaint argues the rule violates 8 U.S.C. §§ 1621 and 1623 and the Supremacy Clause because the District has never passed a law affirmatively extending that eligibility, and cites similar suits the Justice Department has brought against Illinois and Nebraska. It asks the court to declare the D.C. rule unconstitutional and permanently enjoin the District from enforcing it. Read the complaint: United States v. District of Columbia, No. 1:26-cv-03166.
  • Common Cause and member Michael Khalili filed a civil-rights suit naming Secretary Mullin, Federal Bureau of Investigation Director Kashyap Patel, U.S. Customs and Border Protection Commissioner Rodney S. Scott, Acting ICE Director Venturella, Attorney General Todd Blanche, and a number of unidentified federal officers, along with the FBI, CBP, ICE, Homeland Security, and the U.S. Department of Justice as agency defendants. The docket lists the case as a civil-rights action, not a routine agency-review filing, and it was filed the same week as the polling-place suit above — but the complaint itself has been filed under seal, so its specific allegations are not publicly available. Complaint text not available — docket caption only: Common Cause v. Mullin, No. 1:26-cv-03172.
  • The American Federation of State, County and Municipal Employees, AFL-CIO sued the U.S. Department of Labor and department official Keith Sonderling under the Administrative Procedure Act, represented by the Public Citizen Litigation Group. No complaint has yet been posted to the docket, so the specific rule or action being challenged is not public. Complaint text not available — docket caption only: American Federation of State, County and Municipal Employees, AFL-CIO v. U.S. Department of Labor, No. 1:26-cv-03162.
  • Better Markets, Inc., a financial-reform advocacy nonprofit, sued the Board of Governors of the Federal Reserve System and Federal Reserve Governor Michelle W. Bowman; the docket categorizes it as a banking-related federal-question case. A filing-fee receipt and a corporate-disclosure statement appear on the docket, but the complaint itself is not yet available through RECAP. Complaint text not available — docket caption only: Better Markets, Inc. v. Board of Governors of the Federal Reserve System, No. 1:26-cv-03163.

Also filed: 8 new FOIA suits against federal agencies, including two from Judicial Watch, Inc. (against the U.S. Department of Homeland Security and the U.S. Department of Justice) and one each from the American Center for Law and Justice (U.S. Department of Education), Just Futures Law (U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement), the Union of Concerned Scientists (National Science Foundation), and individual requesters against the U.S. Department of the Interior and the U.S. Department of the Army.

Also filed: 11 new mandamus and Administrative Procedure Act suits over delayed visa, green card, and naturalization adjudications, naming Secretary of State Marco Rubio, U.S. Citizenship and Immigration Services Director Joseph B. Edlow, Secretary of Homeland Security Markwayne Mullin, and other State Department and USCIS officials as defendants.
