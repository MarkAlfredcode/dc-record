Top Story

A New Suit Names Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth, But the Case File Is Still Blank

The complaint hasn't been scanned into the RECAP archive, so what the plaintiff is actually alleging remains unknown.

A plaintiff identified in the U.S. District Court for the District of Columbia's docket only by the surname Childress filed a new civil complaint on September 18, 2026, naming Pete Hegseth, the U.S. Secretary of Defense, as a defendant. The case is docketed as Childress v. Hegseth, No. 1:26-cv-03285.

As of this writing, the docket carries no nature-of-suit code, no cause of action, and no complaint document in the RECAP archive — only the bare caption pulled from the court's own filing index. That means the plaintiff's full name, the specific policy or action being challenged, whether Secretary Hegseth is sued in his official or personal capacity, and the relief sought are all unknown; this edition does not speculate about them.

A second new filing from the same batch, described in New Litigation below, also names a cabinet department but arrived with no further docket detail either. Both dockets were opened only a day before this run — too recent for CourtListener's RECAP mirror to have scraped the underlying case documents. A fuller picture may emerge in a future edition once the docket sheets catch up.
