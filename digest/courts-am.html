Top Story

No Notable New Cabinet-Agency or D.C. Government Litigation Since the Last Edition

A three-day sweep of CourtListener's RECAP mirror of the U.S. District Court for the District of Columbia turned up nothing not already covered in prior editions of this page.

Today's check of CourtListener's RECAP archive covered civil filings in the U.S. District Court for the District of Columbia dated September 17 through September 20, 2026. That window returned 27 new docket entries, but all except one had already been identified and reported in the September 17, 18, or 19 editions of this page — a sign that RECAP's mirror of PACER is still catching up on filings from earlier in the week rather than that little happened.

The single new entry was not a substantive dispute: a miscellaneous, non-adversarial application by the United States, filed September 18, 2026 (docket 1:26-sc-02029), for a grand jury subpoena nondisclosure order under 18 U.S.C. § 2705(b). That is a routine, sealed investigative filing used constantly by federal prosecutors nationwide to keep a subpoena recipient from tipping off its target — it names no opposing party, has no public complaint to read, and is not tied to any specific cabinet-agency policy or dispute. Details are in the New Litigation section below.
