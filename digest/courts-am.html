Top Story

CNN, MS NOW, and Politico Sue Trump Over White House Press Ban

The suit, filed hours after their credentials were deactivated, names President Trump and three senior officials and seeks to restore White House access before his trip to the United Nations this week.

Three of the country's largest news organizations sued President Donald J. Trump and three administration officials on September 21, 2026, over Trump's announcement three days earlier that he was banning their journalists from the White House. The plaintiffs — Cable News Network, Inc. (CNN), MS NOW Cable, LLC (the cable news network that operated as MSNBC until adopting its current name in November 2025, following its spin-off from NBCUniversal as part of Comcast's Versant Media Group spinoff), and Politico LLC — sued alongside three of their White House correspondents, Akayla Gardner, Cheyenne Haslett, and Betsy Klein. The case, Cable News Network, Inc. v. Trump (case no. 1:26-cv-03287), was filed in the U.S. District Court for the District of Columbia and assigned to Judge Timothy James Kelly. Besides the President, the complaint names Susan Wiles in her official capacity as White House Chief of Staff, Steven Cheung in his official capacity as Assistant to the President for Communications, and Sean M. Curran in his official capacity as Director of the U.S. Secret Service.

According to the complaint, Trump announced on September 18, 2026 that he was banning journalists from CNN, MS NOW, and Politico from White House grounds "effective immediately," saying it was the "result of their constant 'reporting' FAKE NEWS!" and warning that "other Fake News Media Outlets" could follow. At an Oval Office press conference the same day, he said no single story prompted the ban, calling it instead the product of "cumulative stories over the last two years" that he considered "one-sided" and "never good." The complaint alleges the three organizations' White House credentials, or "hard passes," were deactivated overnight, and that White House officials denied access to and confiscated the credentials of the affected journalists the following morning.

The complaint raises three claims: that the ban denies the plaintiffs due process by stripping them of their credentials without notice or a hearing; that it retaliates against protected newsgathering and speech in violation of the First Amendment; and that it unconstitutionally discriminates by viewpoint in the White House press area, which the U.S. Court of Appeals for the District of Columbia Circuit held in Ateba v. Leavitt (2025) is a "nonpublic forum" that officials cannot restrict based on a speaker's viewpoint. The plaintiffs are represented by a legal team led by Theodore J. Boutrous, Jr. of Gibson, Dunn & Crutcher LLP.

The plaintiffs are asking the court to declare the ban unconstitutional, to immediately restore the hard passes and White House access the news organizations held before September 18, 2026, and to permanently enjoin the administration from enforcing it. They filed a motion for a temporary restraining order the same day they sued, citing Trump's imminent travel to address the United Nations General Assembly and to meet with Chinese President Xi Jinping at the White House — events the plaintiffs argue they would otherwise be unable to cover.
