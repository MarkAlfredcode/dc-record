Top Story

VAWA Self-Petitioner Deported at the Border Sues Homeland Security Secretary Over Reversal of Victim Protections

A Mexican national with a pending domestic-violence-survivor status petition says she was wrongly denied reentry and deported under a 2025 ICE policy reversal.

Yolanda Barraza Flores, a Mexican national who has lived in the United States since 2000 and has had an immigrant self-petition pending under the Violence Against Women Act since 2021, sued Markwayne Mullin, the U.S. Secretary of Homeland Security, along with three other senior immigration officials, over her July 28, 2026 expedited removal at the port of entry in El Paso, Texas. Filed September 26, 2026 in the U.S. District Court for the District of Columbia, the suit says Ms. Barraza Flores had been granted advance parole by U.S. Citizenship and Immigration Services to leave the country and reenter lawfully while her VAWA self-petition remained pending, but that when she presented that valid parole document at the checkpoint she was instead pulled aside, interrogated, denied entry and issued an expedited removal order.

The complaint argues the removal violated the Administrative Procedure Act, the Immigration and Nationality Act, due process and the Constitution's Suspension Clause, and ties the incident to a policy reversal: a 2021 U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement directive had generally barred civil immigration enforcement against noncitizens with a pending VAWA self-petition absent exceptional circumstances such as a national-security concern, but that policy was rescinded shortly after President Trump's second inauguration by then-Acting ICE Director Caleb Vitello, replaced with guidance the complaint says requires officers only to "coordinate and deconflict" with law enforcement before acting against applicants for survivor-based immigration benefits.

Named as defendants in their official capacities are Secretary Mullin; David Venturella, the senior official performing the duties of the director of U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement; Rodney Scott, the senior official performing the duties of the commissioner of U.S. Customs and Border Protection; and Todd Blanche, the Acting Attorney General of the United States. The suit seeks declaratory and injunctive relief restoring Ms. Barraza Flores to the status and circumstances she would be in had the government followed its own governing statutes and regulations, arguing that an expedited removal order carries a five-year, and in some circumstances lifetime, bar on returning to the United States.

Read the complaint: Barraza Flores v. Mullin, No. 1:26-cv-03358 (D.D.C., filed Sept. 26, 2026)

Coast Guard Discrimination Suit Also Names Secretary Mullin; New District of Columbia Case Filed With No Public Complaint Yet

One additional new cabinet-agency suit and one new case against the District of Columbia municipal government survived screening this run. Neither is part of a broader pattern of similar same-day filings.

  • Jeanette R. Rivera-Breznai, a 51-year-old civilian intelligence employee with more than 23 years of federal service, sued Markwayne Mullin, the U.S. Secretary of Homeland Security, in his official capacity as head of the U.S. Department of Homeland Security, alleging sex discrimination, age discrimination and retaliation under Title VII of the Civil Rights Act and the Age Discrimination in Employment Act. Filed September 25, 2026, the suit says Ms. Rivera-Breznai held a Senior Level intelligence position at United States Coast Guard headquarters and that after she served as a witness in a 2022-2023 hostile-work-environment complaint against two superiors, officials circulated and discussed her confidential witness statement, excluded her from her prior duties upon her return from a detail assignment, and in mid-2025 reassigned her out of her intelligence role into a mismatched position, while a younger, less-qualified man took over her former job. She seeks reinstatement, back pay, compensatory damages and an injunction against further discrimination or retaliation. Read the complaint.
