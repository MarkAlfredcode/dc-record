Top Story

Navy gives its Trident nuclear-missile program three more contracts without competition, all to longtime incumbents

The Navy's Strategic Systems Programs office posted three sole-source justifications on October 30, citing decades of incumbent expertise and estimated transition costs of up to $183 million.

The Department of the Navy's Strategic Systems Programs office, which manages the Trident II (D5) submarine-launched nuclear missile, posted three justifications on October 30 for continuing work with companies it has used for decades. Each cites 10 U.S.C. 3204(a)(1), meaning the Navy says only one company can do the job, and each calls the new contract a follow-on to the fiscal 2022 to 2026 effort.

BAE Systems Technology Solutions & Services, Inc. appears twice. The first justification covers systems engineering and integration across the Trident II weapons system, the Columbia-class submarine missile compartment and the new nuclear-armed sea-launched cruise missile program. The Navy calls it a direct follow-on to two earlier non-competed BAE contracts, from fiscal 2022 and fiscal 2025. It says competing the work would cause "a delay of a minimum of three years and $183M in duplication of cost during transition" (justification). No dollar value could be verified for this one. The second BAE justification covers facilities acquisition and engineering support for the nuclear weapons infrastructure, October 1, 2026 through September 30, 2031. Its own cost table totals about $78.2 million, and the Navy says BAE has done this work since 1968 and that switching contractors would cost "nearly $39M" (justification). The document says the work was last bought from BAE on a sole-source basis in 2021.

Unified Industries Incorporated gets the third: a firm-fixed-price contract for test, measurement and diagnostic equipment lifecycle support for the Ohio/Vanguard and Columbia/Dreadnought classes of Trident submarines. The justification's table totals $29.93 million over a planned base year starting October 1, 2026 and four option years. The Navy says the company has been the program's lead coordinator since 1977, holds 1,105 calibration artifacts at a Springfield, Virginia facility, and that moving the work to another source would take 12 to 18 months (justification).

Raytheon Technologies  ·  $489,860,000  ·  follow-on to the original maker (delay otherwise unacceptable)  ·  Missile Defense Agency
The amended justification extends the ordering period of the contractor logistics support contract for the AN/TPY-2 missile-defense radars two years, to October 31, 2028, and raises the maximum value by $489.86 million to $3.095 billion.
