Top Story

Immigration and Customs Enforcement plans a $137 million no-bid CoreCivic contract to keep two New Mexico detention centers running

The justification ties the sole-source decision to a New Mexico law that bars state and local governments from detaining people for civil immigration violations.

Immigration and Customs Enforcement intends to award CoreCivic, Inc. a two-year contract to run the Cibola County Correctional Center and the Torrance County Detention Facility in New Mexico without competition. According to the justification (J&A-26-0086), the contract would be a single-award indefinite-delivery contract mixing firm-fixed-price and labor-hour work. It covers up to 980 beds at Cibola and up to 975 at Torrance, 1,955 in all, with an estimated total cost of $136,963,356.74. It would pay for housing, guards, meals, medical care and transportation for the agency's El Paso Field Office.

The stated legal basis is that only one responsible source can meet the need. The justification says New Mexico passed the Immigrant Safety Act (House Bill 9), which it describes as prohibiting state and local governments from entering agreements to detain people for civil immigration violations. The notice's summary says that terminates the intergovernmental service agreements under which the facilities were operated, and that CoreCivic, as sole owner and operator, can meet the immediate requirement without disruption. The document cites the solicitation number 70CDCR26R00000014 and a funding source of multi-year operations and support appropriations for 2026 through 2029.

The justification was read in full by the screening pass. The dollar figure is quoted from the document's own estimate paragraph rather than from a separately verified value field.
