Immigration agency signs sole-source follow-on for court-ordered family reunification after prior contract expired

Also today: an Army armed-guard order in Iraq posted weeks after work began, and a protest-frozen Fort Carson extension.

The U.S. Citizenship and Immigration Services posted a justification for a sole-source follow-on with the International Organization for Migration to support families reunited under the Miss L. v. Immigration and Customs Enforcement settlement. The previous contract, awarded sole-source in August 2021, ran through September 12, 2026. The value is redacted.

The agency says a lapse would violate the court order and that a 120-day procurement timeline left too little time to compete. The document says responsibility moved from U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement after a late-fiscal-2025 decision by the Secretary of Homeland Security, with the transfer settled in April 2026.

Separately, the Department of the Army issued a notice to proceed on September 10 to GardaWorld Federal Services for armed guards near Erbil, Iraq, and posted its justification October 8. The Department of the Army also extended base operations at Fort Carson for 60 days with T&H Services LLC while a Government Accountability Office protest holds up the successor contract.

OMB Apportionments

OMB Conditions $2.9 Billion CBP Apportionment on Hiring Plan Revisions

The Office of Management and Budget approved $2.9 billion in apportionments to the Department of Homeland Security for U.S. Customs and Border Protection personnel and retention bonuses, but with a significant condition: OMB is withholding full approval until the agency finalizes a revised CBP hiring plan that relies on existing funding sources to the greatest extent possible. This represents an oversight hold—a mechanism OMB uses to request additional information on programmatic spending before releasing funds.

U.S. Customs and Border Protection received $2.3 billion for personnel (line 6032) and $576 million for retention, hiring, and performance bonuses (line 6033), both under Public Law 119-21 appropriations for fiscal 2027. The footnote on both line items states: "OMB requests additional information on programmatic spending for some or all of the apportioned funds." According to the apportionment documents, DHS will work closely with OMB to finalize the hiring plan using existing sources where possible. Apportionment.

The Corporation for National and Community Service (operating as AmeriCorps) submitted an updated fiscal 2026 spend plan significantly larger than its prior version—652 kilobytes versus 93 kilobytes—indicating material revisions to program allocations. The plan allocates $103.3 million to Volunteers in Service to America, $236.9 million to the National Senior Volunteer Corps, $557.1 million to AmeriCorps State and National operations, and $37.7 million to the National Civilian Community Corps, totaling $975.5 million in operating expenses. While we cannot see a line-by-line diff of what changed from the prior version, the size increase indicates substantial revisions to budget detail or program structure. Spend Plan.
