Top Story

Immigration and Customs Enforcement locks in Amentum for up to $2.8 billion at the Camp East Montana detention site without competition

A sole-source justification posted October 19 definitizes the letter contract, says only Amentum can run the site because it controls the facility infrastructure, and follows ICE's termination of the Army-issued contract.

U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement plans to definitize its letter contract with Amentum Services Inc. for detention and facility management at Camp East Montana, with a total estimated value of $2,796,540,325.26 including options. The justification (J&A-26-0098, Rev. 1) says the contract covers secure housing, medical care, transportation and related services. The priced periods are a 7-month base period at $477,534,464, then option periods of 12 months at $1,114,884,768, 6 months at $566,972,132 and 6 months at $580,019,771, plus a further $57,129,190.19 line.

The agency says Amentum is the only responsible source 'because of its ownership and operational control of the facility infrastructure.' The document says the arrangement was 'originally issued by the Department of the Army on July 18, 2025,' that ICE was not the source selection authority for it, and that the contract and performance work statement 'did not accurately reflect ICE's operational needs or the actual usage of the facility.' ICE 'determined that a termination was in the Government's best interest, understanding that the use of the facility was still a priority.' The October version also cites a preliminary report from ICE's Office of Detention Oversight, and says these circumstances 'created and contributed to the need for immediate contractual action to protect detainees and staff.' The text of that sentence is partly garbled in the redacted copy.

The justification says that 'alternatives such as transitioning to a different provider, segmenting the requirement, or relocating detainees were considered and determined impracticable,' citing the facility's current occupancy and 'the lack of available bedspace nationwide.' It says the definitized action carries a longer period of performance and higher estimated cost than the original letter-contract planning assumptions, and that the extra time lets ICE 'evaluate longer-term alternatives.' The pricing is based on the agency's independent government cost estimate, and final negotiated values will be recorded in the definitized contract action.
