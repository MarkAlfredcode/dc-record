Top Story

Immigration and Customs Enforcement asks industry for AI that monitors detainee calls, builds voice profiles and counts detainees on camera

A request for information describes a single system that would correlate phone, messaging, tablet, video and movement data across detention facilities.

U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement posted a request for information on October 26 for an "Integrated SI-Powered Detention Communications and Video Intelligence Suite." The notice covers software licenses, implementation, integration, testing, training, cybersecurity support and operations and maintenance. It is market research, not a solicitation, and no contract will be awarded from it. The attached salient-characteristics document was posted to SAM.gov on October 9.

According to that document, the agency says detention facilities "generate authorized telephone, messaging, tablet, video-visitation, detainee-management, movement, incident, and camera data at volumes that cannot be reviewed and correlated manually in a timely manner." It says the suite "shall create and improve voice profiles from authorized existing recordings without requiring a separate enrollment session, subject cooperation, or new recording hardware."

The document also asks for video analytics that "detect and alert on Government-approved safety, security, and operational events, including fights or aggression, crowd formation or coordinated group movement, person-down events, falls, prolonged immobility." It further asks for automated detainee headcounts that compare observed counts "with authoritative roster, housing, and movement information." The notice does not name a vendor or give a value.

Sole-Source Justifications

Defense Logistics Agency posted its urgent Western Pacific fuel buy from BP nearly six months after award

Four new justifications came in today. Two were posted under the Award Notice type, and two are covered in Extensions. No repeats from earlier editions were left out. The two below are the ones that stand out.

Must read
Defense Logistics Agency buys urgent Western Pacific jet and ship fuel from BP with no competition, then publishes the justification nearly six months after award
BP Products North America Inc.  ·  value redacted  ·  urgent need, no time to compete  ·  Defense Logistics Agency (DLA Energy)
Three spot-buy purchase orders (SPE60226P0470, P0471 and P0472) for JP-5 and F-76 fuel in the Western Pacific went to BP Products North America Inc., which the justification says was the only offeror that could meet the delivery window, from its Cherry Point refinery. The document says it "has been approved and publicized after award" and that preparing it first "would have unreasonably delayed the acquisition." It states the contracts were awarded May 1, 2026, and it was posted October 26, well past the roughly 30-day window for posting urgent sole-source awards. That makes it a late disclosure. The fuel quantities and dollar values are redacted.

Veterans Affairs keeps a mobile lab trailer running at its Amarillo hospital with an urgent no-bid rental after a construction contract ended in default
Lab-Pro, Inc.  ·  $350,850 estimated, including options  ·  urgent need, no time to compete  ·  Department of Veterans Affairs
The Amarillo VA Healthcare System lab needs a two-module cleanroom trailer rented while its laboratory is under construction. Lab-Pro, Inc. had supplied it as a subcontractor under construction contract 36C25722C0093, which the justification says "was terminated for default in May 2026." Competing the job would mean disconnecting the trailer's electrical, fire-suppression and water connections and stopping patient lab testing, according to the document. The J&A estimates $350,850.20 with options and a performance period starting September 1, 2026. The award notice lists $374,879 awarded October 15. The notice was posted as an Award Notice.
