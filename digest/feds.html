Transportation Security Administration tells vendors to build their cybersecurity demo sandboxes at their own expense, with zero government liability

Documents attached to the agency's Horizon 25 industry day notice shift the cost and risk of demonstrations to bidders.

A compliance document attached to the Transportation Security Administration's industry day notice for its Horizon 25 OrchestrateSI and TalentSI procurements says the offeror "shall establish, configure, host, secure, and maintain the demonstration sandbox environment ENTIRELY AT THE OFFEROR'S OWN EXPENSE," and that the federal government "shall assume ZERO financial liability." The document is headed "CRITICAL MANDATE: VENDOR-FUNDED RISK ALLOCATION." This comes from a file posted October 19 and read today on a notice covered earlier.

The same document says the contracting officer "reserves the unilateral authority" to issue an immediate stop-work order if a contractor fails to fix a critical or high vulnerability on an internet-facing asset within the timeframes in the Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency's directive BOD 22-01.

The two draft statements of objectives dated October 19 describe what the agency wants. OrchestrateSI is an "integrated, data-driven security operations ecosystem" tying together demand forecasting, checkpoint visibility and workforce scheduling. The TalentSI draft addresses hiring and onboarding systems, citing a lack of automated cross-crediting of shared qualifications across job announcements and the need for clearer pathways from entry through training and certification.
