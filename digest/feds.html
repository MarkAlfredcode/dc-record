Top Story

Immigration agency extends GEO Group's Aurora, Colorado detention contract without competition days before it expires

The justification says the facility holds more than half of Colorado's detention beds and that the competitive replacement contract is still being evaluated.

U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement is adding a two-month sole-source option period (Oct. 16 to Dec. 15, 2026) to its contract with The GEO Group, Inc. for detention and transportation services at the Denver (Aurora) Contract Detention Facility. According to the justification (J&A-26-0417), posted Oct. 13, the current contract, 70CDCR22D00000001, expires Oct. 15, 2026. The estimated value is redacted, and the agency cites urgent need as its authority to skip competition.

The justification says the competitive follow-on acquisition “is in the evaluation phase” and that the extension will let the agency “complete the ongoing competition, make an award, and transition” to the new contract. It says the Aurora facility “currently provides capacity for up to 1,530 detainees, representing more than half of the available detention bed space in Colorado.”

One detail stands out: the extension relies on Federal Acquisition Regulation clause 52.217-8 (Option to Extend Services). The justification says the clause is in the current contract but “wasn’t evaluated at the time of award,” and that this “will be the first use of the 52.217-8 for this contract.” The same clause is how the agency says it avoids a gap in service for its Enforcement and Removal Operations office. Related immigration-custody items today include a brand-name Zscaler network order for the same agency (Sole-Source Justifications) and a plan for 250 secure beds for unaccompanied migrant children (Presolicitations).
