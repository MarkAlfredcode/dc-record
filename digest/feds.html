Top Story

Army's $50 billion MAPS professional-services contract adds a small-business-only teaming track

Documents read today on the Army's Marketplace for Acquisition of Professional Services solicitation show a $50 billion ceiling, work in warzones and a new amendment that opens a separate track for small businesses.

The Army Contracting Command at Aberdeen Proving Ground is soliciting the Marketplace for Acquisition of Professional Services (MAPS), a multiple-award indefinite-delivery contract. According to Amendment 010, "The maximum total contract value (ceiling), including the Optional Ordering Period if exercised, is $50B." The statement of work says task-order locations "will be Government or contractor sites within CONUS and/or OCONUS locations, including warzone areas," and that some orders may require 24/7 operations, 365 days a year. These findings come from documents read today on a notice posted earlier. They are not a new notice.

The newest file is Amendment 0011, posted October 13. It states: "Only Small Businesses are permitted to submit SBTA proposals, subject to all terms and conditions outlined in this Solicitation." SBTA stands for small business teaming arrangement.

The question-and-answer spreadsheet posted April 24 shows how contested the terms have been. An industry question says Amendment 001, issued April 7, changed the recency requirement for qualifying projects from four years to three "without explanation." In its answers, the Army says a conditional Level 2 cybersecurity self-assessment is not acceptable and offerors must hold a final Level 2 certification. The Army also says its objective is to negotiate a fee "at a rate equal to or less than half the regulatory limits" in 10 U.S.C. 3322(b). A separate answer says a new Emerging Large Business category will be treated as large business after award.

Air Force seeks whitepapers to modernize a live-fire test site at Wright-Patterson Air Force Base
Solicitation  ·  No value given  ·  Department of the Air Force
The Arnold Engineering Development Complex, under the Air Force Test Center, is asking members of its AEDC Velocity Alliance Consortium and other industry partners for whitepapers. The project would modernize Test Site 2 at the Aerospace Vehicle Survivability Facility at Wright-Patterson Air Force Base, Ohio. The goal is a test facility able to safely and efficiently conduct live fire test and evaluation on current and future aerospace systems. The request uses Other Transaction Authority under 10 U.S.C. § 4022, which sits outside the standard federal contracting rules. The one attached file has not been read yet. A separate notice for the consortium itself is titled "Request for Whitepapers: AEDC Velocity Alliance - A Multi-Billion Dollar OTA Consortium Opportunity."

Conserv, Inc.  ·  No value verified  ·  Sources Sought  ·  Department of Veterans Affairs

Army plans a 9-month sole-source extension to wind down its neurocognitive testing program
Controller Bay, LLC  ·  No value given  ·  Sources Sought  ·  Department of the Army
The Department of the Army request for information for Neurocognitive Assessment Test Support Services quotes a planned modification for Controller Bay, LLC of Anchorage, Alaska. The modification would raise the contract's ceiling and extend its period of performance by 9 months. It cites the "only one responsible source" authority in 10 U.S.C. 3204(a)(1). The document calls it "a limited, time-sensitive phase-out action required to support the orderly phase-out, transition of the program and final termination of the Army NCAT program."
