CBP launches a $420,754,316 same-day border-checkpoint construction blitz as SSA races to replace its expiring mainframe

Four design-build contracts for new checkpoints and a Border Patrol station landed the same day the Social Security Administration moved to keep its core benefits-processing mainframe running past a September 30 contract expiration.

U.S. Customs and Border Protection awarded four separate design-build construction contracts on September 28, 2026, worth a combined $420,754,316, all for new checkpoint or Border Patrol Station facilities along the southern border: Rio Grande City and Laredo, Texas; Sierra Blanca, Texas; and Nogales, Arizona. Environmental Chemical Corporation, Clark Construction Group LLC, Southwest Valley Constructors Co, and Brasfield & Gorrie LLC each won one of the four awards. Stacking four large, geographically spread construction contracts on a single day signals a significant, coordinated push in CBP's physical infrastructure buildout, not a routine one-off award.

The same day, the Social Security Administration awarded $190,924,612 across two contracts — to International Business Machines Corporation for mainframe hardware and storage, and to Carahsoft Technology Corp for Broadcom mainframe software licensing — explicitly to replace an existing support agreement that expires September 30, 2026, the day after this tracking window closes. SSA's mainframe systems still process benefit determinations for tens of millions of Americans, and the tight timeline between contract award and expiration underscores how little slack the agency had left.

Also notable: the Executive Office of the President directly awarded a $3.2 million IT support contract to Strategic Innovation Group LLC — an unusual instance of EOP itself, rather than GSA or another department, appearing as the awarding agency in the daily contract-actions feed. Nothing in SAM.gov's solicitations or sole-source justifications from the past two days rivals the dollar scale or scope of the CBP and SSA awards, so this is where today's edition leads.

White House & Executive Office of the President

The Executive Office of the President directly awarded a $3,242,516 contract to Strategic Innovation Group LLC for business-application and software-development support services, according to USAspending's contract classification, though the award's own description field lists only the word “Services.” The procurement code (business application/application development support labor) indicates IT contractor staff augmentation rather than a specific system purchase, but the office awarding it — a rarity in the daily new-awards feed, since EOP components awarding $2 million-plus contracts directly (rather than through GSA or another agency) don't show up often — is itself the notable fact here. CBP's Border Checkpoint Construction Blitz

U.S. Customs and Border Protection awarded four design-build construction contracts on the same day, September 28, 2026, totaling $420,754,316 for new border facilities across Texas and the Southwest. Environmental Chemical Corporation got the largest, $158,000,000, to design and build a new Border Patrol Station at Rio Grande City, Texas; Clark Construction Group LLC received $123,585,356 for a new checkpoint facility at Laredo, Texas; Southwest Valley Constructors Co received $75,810,908 for a new checkpoint at Sierra Blanca, Texas; and Brasfield & Gorrie LLC received $63,358,052 for a new checkpoint at Nogales, Arizona. All four are firm design-build construction jobs rather than sole-source IT or services work, and together represent one of the largest single-day pushes of border infrastructure spending in this tracking window. Social Security Administration's Mainframe Overhaul

The Social Security Administration awarded two large IT contracts on September 28, 2026, totaling $190,924,612, to replace expiring mainframe infrastructure. International Business Machines Corporation received $130,434,206 to establish a new IBM mainframe hardware and storage contract, explicitly replacing a Blanket Purchase Agreement (BPA SS00-16-60010) that expires September 30, 2026 — the day after this award window closes. Carahsoft Technology Corp received $60,490,406 for a Broadcom Mainframe Consumption License, Portfolio License Agreement, and support services, the software side of the same modernization. Together the two awards show SSA racing to keep its mainframe backbone — which still underpins benefit processing for tens of millions of Americans — running without a lapse. International Business Machines Corporation. Carahsoft Technology CORP.

Department of Health and Human Services' Office of the Assistant Secretary for Preparedness and Response awarded $96,058,494 to Kaleo, Inc. to fund late-stage clinical development and regulatory approval of 2-PAM autoinjectors — an emergency antidote/device combination product for organophosphate poisoning, including nerve agent exposure. The award draws on Project BioShield funds administered by BARDA (the Biomedical Advanced Research and Development Authority), the government's chemical/biological-threat countermeasure stockpile program, and represents a substantial single commitment to a narrow, defense-relevant drug category. USAspending record.

ICE's $40 Million Background-Check Surge

U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement issued three task orders on September 28, 2026, all funding “Call One” under its Background Investigation Services III vehicle, totaling $39,937,238. The work supports background-investigation reports on both ICE contractors and federal employees for the agency's Office of Professional Responsibility. CACI, Inc. - Federal received the largest share, $15,974,895; ADC Ltd NM received $13,312,413; and Celerity Government Solutions LLC received $10,649,930. The Transportation Security Administration awarded $63,606,870 to Smiths Detection Inc. to purchase Medium Speed Explosive Detection Systems, installation services, and ancillary equipment — the checked-baggage scanning machines used at airport security checkpoints nationwide. OMB Apportionments

OMB flags $267 million in permanent USDA and SBA rescissions, plus $135 million in FirstNet funding held pending spend plans

Six line items across five newly approved apportionment files (the Sept. 25–30 window) triggered automatic rescission or oversight-hold flags. Four permanently rescind a combined $267,290,829 from USDA's working capital and watershed funds and SBA's Business Loans Program Account; two more condition $135,274,212 in FirstNet funding on OMB first reviewing a board-approved spend plan.

Another 217 routine, non-flagged apportionment lines across nine agencies were also approved in the window — led by the Department of Energy (67) and USDA (59) — with no rescission, oversight-hold, or other flagged language; they're listed below by agency for the record.

Permanent Rescissions

The U.S. Department of Agriculture permanently rescinded $78,000,000 in unobligated prior-year balances from its Working Capital Fund, citing Public Law 119-37 (139 Stat. 495), Division B, Section 770. The apportionment's footnote quotes the law directly: the funds are rescinded provided none were designated by Congress as an emergency requirement under the Balanced Budget and Emergency Deficit Control Act of 1985. This is a straight pull-back — the money is permanently off the books, not redirected elsewhere. Apportionment.

A second, much smaller USDA rescission pulls back $200,829 from Rural Watershed Operations funds recovered in fiscal year 2026, authorized under General Provision 766, Division B, Title VII of the Consolidated Appropriations Act, 2024 (P.L. 118-42; 138 Stat. 115). The footnote notes the figure is rounded up to the nearest dollar — the actual rescission is $200,773.76. Apportionment.

The Small Business Administration booked two large permanent rescissions from its Business Loans Program Account, totaling $189,090,000 in anticipated recoveries of prior-year unpaid obligations. The larger, $149,090,000, traces to Section 90007(g)(1) of the Infrastructure Investment and Jobs Act (P.L. 117-58); the smaller, $40,000,000, combines that same law's Section 90007(d)(2) with a rescission under P.L. 117-328 targeting unused CARES Act 7(a) business-loan funds. Both footnotes warn that any further recoveries realized will also be rescinded, meaning more of this money could disappear from the books later. First apportionment, second apportionment.

Spend-Plan Holds

The National Telecommunications and Information Administration's First Responder Network Authority (FirstNet) had two large tranches apportioned under spend-plan holds rather than released outright. A $36,073,123 Operational Contingency Reserve and $99,201,089 for FirstNet Operations — the latter split, per FirstNet Board Resolution 122, into $90,182,808 for operations and a $9,018,280 management allowance — both become available for obligation only five calendar days after FirstNet's board submits a spend plan to NTIA and OMB. OMB's own rationale is explicit that this is an oversight check: “An agency spend plan or other documentation is necessary to better understand how the agency intends to obligate some or all of the apportioned funds.” Apportionment.
