Air Force extends U-2 forecasting contractor with $3.88 million sole-source deal after order lapsed

The justification cites a September 29 expiration and a $2.5 million switching cost.

The Department of the Air Force posted a sole-source follow-on to Credence Management Solutions, LLC worth $3,880,000 for development and maintenance of the U-2 Airframe Forecasting Dashboard, a cloud-based analytics tool for logistics and fleet readiness.

The Air Force says the incumbent has 12 months of proprietary knowledge of undocumented Air Force logistics systems, that a transition would cost an estimated $2.5 million or more and take 6 to 9 months, and that the current order was expiring September 29, 2026, which would leave the program without its primary forecasting tool. It used the limited-source authority.

USAspending Contracts

Army awards $82.5 million contract to build a new Agriculture research facility in Kentucky

Nine new awards of $2 million or more were reported, none tied to the White House or Executive Office of the President by agency or description. Under $2 million, 41 new awards were screened and none were flagged.

Most notable awards

Messer Construction Co won $82,471,000 from the Department of the Army on September 17 to construct the U.S. Department of Agriculture's Forage Animal Production Research Unit at the University of Kentucky in Lexington, a design-bid-build laboratory and clinic project. Notably, the Army, not Agriculture, is the contracting agency. Award.

IST Research, LLC received $9,517,432 through the General Services Administration's Federal Acquisition Service on September 30 for a Small Business Innovation Research Phase III delivery order; the description references DARPA and the Department of the Air Force. It is national-defense research and development work bought via a civilian agency's contract vehicle. Award.

Two Federal Emergency Management Agency awards support its chemical, biological, radiological and nuclear (CBRN) program, both dated September 30: Mayvin, Inc. got $8,026,816 for continued advisory and assistance services to the CBRN Operations Support Office (Mayvin), and Noblis, Inc. got $5,387,680.62 for intelligence support services to ensure uninterrupted programmatic support for the CBRN mission (Noblis).

The MIL Corporation was awarded $9,468,630.47 by the Department of State on September 30 for software development life-cycle and operations support for its integrated resource management systems. Award.

OMB Apportionments

Nearly $2 billion in Defense rescissions lead broad federal budget adjustments

Federal agencies received apportionment updates during the window of September 30–October 5, including significant rescissions of prior-year balances and new spending conditions tied to executive orders. Defense accounted for the largest rescissions, while civilian agencies faced either recovery of unobligated balances or spending holds pending documented plans.

Defense Rescissions and Constraints

Department of Defense--Military Programs faced rescissions totaling approximately $1.81 billion across multiple procurement and research accounts, all mandated by Public Law 119-75. Affected programs included Aircraft Procurement, Air Force ($329.4 million); Research, Development, Test and Evaluation, Air Force ($329.4 million); and various operations and maintenance accounts. These rescissions represent unobligated balances being permanently cancelled to meet deficit reduction targets set in the recent appropriations legislation. View apportionment.

Civilian Agency Rescissions and Recovery

Department of Agriculture rescinded two tranches of unobligated balances: $78 million from the Working Capital Fund per Public Law 119-37, Section 770, and $200,829 from Rural Watershed Operations per General Provision 766 of the Consolidated Appropriations Act, 2024. The rescissions represent funds recovered in fiscal year 2026 for conservation programs that were unable to obligate prior appropriations. Notice; Notice.

Department of Transportation rescinded $5.9 million in unobligated balances as of July 31, 2026, with authority to continue rescinding additional recoveries and deobligations in the account until a total of $10.37 million has been rescinded, per Section 109F of Public Law 119-75. Notice.

Department of Housing and Urban Development rescinded $116.9 million in unobligated Homeless Assistance Grants balances as of September 30, 2026, per Public Law 119-103, Section 154. The law simultaneously re-appropriated an equivalent amount of new budget authority to remain available through September 30, 2027, for the same purposes, effectively reallocating dormant funds while freeing space in the prior appropriation. Notice.

Other Independent Agencies (Institute of Museum and Library Services) rescinded $3.1 million in unobligated American Rescue Plan Act balances, per Public Law 119-75, Division B, Title 5, Section 530. The agency confirmed no remaining amounts remain to be rescinded under the program. Notice.

Executive Order-Conditioned Grants

Department of Housing and Urban Development apportioned four tranches of competitive grant funding conditioned on compliance with executive orders on federal grant oversight. Three programs—Rapid Re-Housing and Other Projects for Victims of Domestic Violence ($9.9 million), Continuum of Care ($79.5 million), and Permanent Supportive Housing ($97.7 million)—must obligate funds on award agreements including terms specified in Executive Order 14332, "Improving Oversight of Federal Grantmaking." A fourth tranche for Community Planning and Development ($187.5 million) is conditioned on Executive Order 14321, "Ending Crime and Disorder on America's Streets." Notice.

Department of Housing and Urban Development (FY2027) apportioned an additional $95.8 million across three FY2027 accounts—Contracts, Grants, Cooperating Agreements ($47.9 million), Native American Housing Block Grant-Competitive ($44.9 million), and Indian Community Development Block Grant ($3 million)—all conditioned on Executive Order 14332. Notice; Notice; Notice.

Spending Holds Pending Documentation

Department of Health and Human Services received $116.4 million in Category A first-quarter apportionment for its Administration for Children and Families, but with a holding requirement: amounts not yet obligated must be obligated consistent with an agreed-upon spending plan between HHS and the Office of Management and Budget. HHS must provide detailed breakdowns by spending category (salaries and expenses, training, block grants, etc.), projected disbursement amounts for each grant, and a description of alignment with Administration priorities. In the absence of an agreed-upon plan, HHS may only obligate funds for federal salaries, shared overhead costs, and legally mandated payments. Notice.

Department of Homeland Security (Federal Emergency Management Agency) received $16.7 billion in apportionment but with a notification requirement: FEMA must notify OMB by email at least 15 business days in advance of exercising any additional borrowing authority with the Treasury Department. Notice.
