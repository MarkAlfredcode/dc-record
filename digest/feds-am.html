Customs and Border Protection commits $777,754,316 to seven border construction projects in two days

Border Patrol stations and checkpoint facilities in Texas, New Mexico and Arizona were awarded as the federal fiscal year closed.

U.S. Customs and Border Protection awarded seven design-build task orders on September 28 and 29 worth $777,754,316 combined, according to USAspending.gov. The biggest are $158,000,000 to Environmental Chemical Corporation for a Border Patrol station in Rio Grande City, Texas, and $137,000,000 to Grunley Construction Co., Inc. for a station in Eagle Pass, Texas.

The rest are $123,585,356 to Clark Construction Group LLC for a new Laredo checkpoint facility, $117,000,000 to Clark Construction for a station in Las Cruces, New Mexico, $103,000,000 to Grunley for the Brackettville, Texas station, $75,810,908 to Southwest Valley Constructors Co for a Sierra Blanca, Texas checkpoint and $63,358,052 to Brasfield & Gorrie LLC for a Nogales, Arizona checkpoint. The USAspending descriptions give no further detail on schedules or funding sources.

SAM.gov carried 50 sole-source or limited-competition justifications posted October 1 and 2, and 702 solicitations. The justifications range from Department of State mobile-laboratory buys for U.S. military groups in Africa to a Navy carrier-launch-system pump notice; each is listed below.

OMB Apportionments

Twenty apportionment filings flagged for oversight holds, litigation escrow, and rescissions

Between September 27 and October 2, agencies received approval for $71.2 billion in new apportionments for fiscal 2027, of which $29.7 billion was flagged for OMB oversight conditions, litigation holds, or legislative rescissions. The most significant flag—a $2.9 billion Department of Justice litigation reserve—is tied to large settlement liabilities including cases against Purdue Pharma, McKinsey & Company, and Backpage.com, plus a separate $433.1 million hold pending court resolution.

Department of Agriculture received $1.55 billion for Food for Peace Title II Grants flagged with an oversight condition: the agency must provide OMB with monthly written updates on total commitments and obligations by population, crisis, or subcategory. Notice.

Department of Commerce apportionments totaling $135.3 million are subject to spend-plan holds. The Operational Contingency Reserve ($36.1 million) and FirstNet Operations ($99.2 million) become available only five calendar days after a Board-approved spend plan is submitted to the National Telecommunications and Information Administration and OMB. Contingency Reserve; FirstNet.

Department of Housing and Urban Development received $4.6 million for Disaster Assistance with a condition that OMB receives notice at least ten business days before any new commitment is made, including identification of the grantee and disaster declaration. A separate $4.26 million rescission of unobligated balances was enacted under Section 156 of the Continuing Appropriations and Extensions Act, 2027 (P.L. 119-103). Disaster Assistance; Rescission.

Department of Labor apportionments to the Employment and Training Administration totaling $3.01 billion are subject to multiple conditions. The Dislocated Worker Employment and Training Activities ($197.6 million) requires OMB approval of the Department of Labor Secretary's final version of any Funding Opportunity Announcement before obligation. The Total Budgetary Resources ($2.82 billion) is conditioned on spending in accordance with Executive Order 14332, "Improving Oversight of Federal Grantmaking." Notice.

Department of Transportation apportionments totaling $36.3 billion include multiple oversight and rescission flags. The Federal Aviation Administration's Total Budgetary Resources ($12.01 billion) requires quarterly briefings to OMB on progress implementing work described in the Workforce Training for Career Act Spend Plan. FAA Land Proceeds ($9 million) become available only five days after submission of a spend plan. Essential Air Service funding ($283.6 million) is 75% available immediately, with the remaining 25% available five days after OMB receives a spend plan for all EAS communities and accounts. Two separate rescission flags affect FAA and Federal Highway Administration accounts: $71 million tied to P.L. 119-103 rescission authority, and $23.94 billion in unobligated highway balances (noted as carryover with prior-year sequestration amounts documented). FAA Total Resources; Land Proceeds; Essential Air Service; Rescission; Highway.

Department of the Treasury received $235 million with a lapse-in-appropriations condition: in the event of a funding gap, amounts are automatically apportioned under 31 U.S.C. Section 3129 to pay essential expenses for issuance of government bonds and obligations, capped at 0.2 percent of authorized bond authority. Notice.

International Assistance Programs apportionments totaling $580.5 million carry oversight conditions. The State Department received $306 million with a requirement to provide OMB monthly written updates (10 business days after month-end) on total commitments and obligations by population, crisis, or subcategory, plus month-over-month comparisons to the State Department plan. The Millennium Challenge Account received $274 million apportioned in Category C for FY 2028: $25 million (P.L. 119-4), $52 million (P.L. 119-75), and $197 million (P.L. 119-4), each available for reallocation to Category B if a demonstrated programmatic need arises in FY 2027. The U.S. Trade and Development Agency received $475,960 with a requirement that funds become available 10 business days after the agency identifies the original source(s) to OMB. State Department; MCA (119-4); MCA (119-75); MCA (FY 2028); USTDA.

Small Business Administration received $189.1 million in rescission authority under two separate provisions. The first $149.1 million is permanently rescinded under P.L. 117-58, Division I, Section 90007(g)(1), with additional recoveries subject to rescission. The second $40 million combines two rescissions: one under P.L. 117-58, Division I, Section 90007(d)(2), and a second under P.L. 117-328, Division MM, affecting CARES Act Business Loans Program Account balances, with additional recoveries subject to rescission. First rescission; Second rescission.
