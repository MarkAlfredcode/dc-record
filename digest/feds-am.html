Top Story

ICE adds a 100-bed temporary detention structure in Batavia, New York, to an existing contract without competition

The agency says changes affecting state and local detention space created an increased need for beds in New York.

U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement plans to modify its comprehensive detention services contract with Akima Infrastructure Protection, LLC, of Herndon, Virginia, on a sole-source basis. The modification adds a temporary detention structure, supporting medical and environmental infrastructure, and armed guard services at the Batavia, New York, service processing center, which would house up to 100 detainees of all security levels.

The justification says integrating with existing detention operations avoids duplicated costs and operational and security risks from multiple vendors. It also raises the ceiling of the existing contract; the dollar figures are redacted in the public document.

Elsewhere in today's sole-source notices, the General Services Administration plans a $152,779,419 ceiling increase for CACI on an Army intelligence-support task order, and the Transportation Security Administration is defending a five-year sole-source award to its incumbent after a protest.

Army-backed intelligence-support task order gets a $152.8 million ceiling increase for CACI with no new competition
CACI  ·  $152,779,419 estimated  ·  skipped the competition among existing contract holders  ·  General Services Administration
The General Services Administration, buying for the Army's Combat Capabilities Development Command C5ISR Center, plans to raise the ceiling of a 2024 task order for intelligence, surveillance and threat-analysis support, originally awarded competitively with a $414,857,176 ceiling. The justification says mission demand exceeded estimates and that internal funding realignments are exhausted, so an immediate ceiling increase is the only way to keep operations going.

Navy extends KPMG's financial-management data work, citing an 18-month delay if it switched vendors
KPMG  ·  value redacted  ·  skipped the competition among existing contract holders  ·  Department of the Navy
The Department of the Navy plans to continue financial-management data transformation services, including a Rapid Response Team and the Navy Budget Orchestration System, with KPMG. The document says KPMG has been the Navy's primary vendor for this work since September 2018 and that changing vendors would delay audit remediation by an estimated 18 months.

USAspending Contracts

Department of Veterans Affairs books $2.59 billion in three Optum managed-care awards, plus a $99.6 million Forest Service timber contract

Twenty-eight new awards of $2 million or more appeared in the window; none was tied to the White House or the Executive Office of the President. The largest were three Optum awards on September 30, 2026, a Forest Service timber contract, and two Treasury metal-strip buys for the U.S. Mint.

Department of Veterans Affairs managed-care awards

Optum Public Sector Solutions, Inc. received three awards from the Department of Veterans Affairs dated September 30, 2026, totaling $2,586,504,068.77: $1,085,804,852.71, $770,881,595.07 and $729,817,620.99. All are in the managed healthcare category and are labeled "Express Report" for the fourth quarter of fiscal 2026 (regions R1, R2 and R3, July), which suggests quarterly payment reports under existing contracts rather than newly competed work; the descriptions give no more detail.

Other notable awards of $2 million or more

Peachtree Lumber Company, Inc won a $99,575,262 Forest Service (Department of Agriculture) logging contract on October 6, 2026 for the Buck Helene fuels reduction project. The description says $87,804,491.92 is timber value applied, for a total contract value of $87,836,891.92, with performance running through September 13, 2028. The award amount is higher than the stated contract value, and the description does not explain the difference. Award.

OMB Apportionments

No new flagged apportionments in this window; AmeriCorps spend plan revised

No apportionment files matching keyword triggers for review were approved in the October 3–8 window. OMB did approve two routine bureau-level apportionments and revised the fiscal-year 2026 spend plan for AmeriCorps, which decreased significantly in size.

Corporation for National and Community Service (operating as AmeriCorps) revised its FY 2026 Spend Plan (status marked as changed, though the PDF decreased from 652 KB to 92 KB). The revised spend plan allocates across AmeriCorps programs: VISTA at $103.3 million, AmeriCorps State and National at $557.1 million, National Senior Volunteer Corps at $236.9 million (including Foster Grandparents Program at $125.4 million and Senior Companion Program at $56.4 million), and National Civilian Community Corps at $37.7 million. The plan includes footnotes citing statutory authorities under the Domestic Volunteer Service Act. Spend Plan.
