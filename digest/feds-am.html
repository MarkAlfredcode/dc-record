Navy extends Washington-area custodial contract a third time after two protests

The Naval District Washington bridge is one of several sole-source extensions posted October 9 that lean on stalled competitions.

The Navy posted a justification for a third sole-source bridge of custodial services for Naval District Washington facilities, covering October 2026 through September 2027, with NJS Joint Venture, LLC as the vendor and the value redacted. The Naval Facilities Engineering Systems Command Washington office says a Government Accountability Office pre-award protest filed March 16, 2026 delayed proposal evaluations, and a second protest filed May 22, 2026 followed the dismissal of the first.

The same day, the Army Corps of Engineers posted a bridge for Air Force fuel-facility maintenance, citing active Court of Federal Claims litigation, and the Navy posted a second one-year bridge for a legacy construction-management system run by Bahfed Corp, valued at $3,800,000.

Army Corps of Engineers bridges fuel-facility maintenance for the Air Force while a bid protest is in court
Vendor redacted  ·  value redacted  ·  urgent need, no time to compete  ·  Department of the Army, U.S. Army Corps of Engineers Huntsville Engineering and Support Center
The Army Corps of Engineers is awarding a sole-source bridge of up to 336 days for preventive and corrective maintenance and emergency repair of the Air Force fuels program in the North Atlantic Division, for the Defense Logistics Agency. The justification says Court of Federal Claims litigation is active and the follow-on contractor cannot begin performance, and that without a bridge the government would have no contractor after September 28, 2026.

Navy buys a second one-year bridge for a legacy construction-management system, citing the shutdown
Bahfed Corp  ·  $3,800,000 estimated  ·  limited-source order (only one company can do it)  ·  Department of the Navy, Naval Facilities Engineering Systems and Expeditionary Warfare Center
The justification authorizes a 12-month bridge extension of a task order for maintenance and database-migration support of the legacy eCMS system used for Naval Facilities Engineering Systems Command construction management. The Navy blames a 43-day partial federal government shutdown at the start of fiscal year 2026 and the wait for a Defense Information Systems Agency provisional authorization for stalling the transition, and says Bahfed is the incumbent and exclusive authorized reseller of the legacy platform.

Navy keeps CACI on a budget-defense system because only the incumbent can reach its data
CACI  ·  value redacted  ·  only one company can do it  ·  Department of the Navy, Naval Supply Systems Command Fleet Logistics Center San Diego
The Navy is giving CACI a follow-on contract for technical and analytical support of the Aviation Cost Evaluation System, which supports Naval Air Forces budget defense. The justification says only CACI can reach the legacy data on commercial servers needed for a migration to a government cloud. Separately, this pipeline logged an October 8 justification for CACI at the General Services Administration, a task order ceiling increase valued at $152,779,419.

OMB Apportionments

Education Department's $25.6M held pending OMB information request; Justice apportions $896.6M under continuing resolution rescission

Five new apportionment approvals were published in the past five days. Two files triggered flags for further review, and three routine non-flagged apportionments were also approved across Department of Homeland Security and independent agencies.

Flagged Apportionments

The Department of Education held $25,633,000 of discretionary funds pending reapportionment, citing an OMB request for additional information. The fund line, labeled "Unallocated," is available for obligation only after reapportionment to a "Category B" account. The apportionment footnote states: "Amounts apportioned on this line are available for obligation upon reapportionment to an applicable Category B line. [Rationale: OMB requests additional information on programmatic spending for some or all of the apportioned funds]." A related entry on the same apportionment document, "Total budgetary resources available," holds an additional $26,133,000 under the same rationale. Apportionment.

The Department of Justice apportioned $896,610,000 under a continuing resolution for FY2027 that authorizes operations based on FY2026 enacted levels, adjusted for rescissions and mandated transfers. The footnote cites Public Law 119-103, which provides the continuing resolution authority: "Pursuant to P.L.119-103, the Continuing Resolution rate of operations is based on the FY 2026 enacted appropriations, less rescission and adding or subtracting mandated transfers." Apportionment.
