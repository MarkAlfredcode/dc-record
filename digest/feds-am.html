Top Story

General Services Administration plans another no-bid security upgrade at the White House Complex

The agency says Convergint Technologies LLC is the only responsible source for design-build work on system headends at the White House Complex and Joint Base Anacostia-Bolling.

A justification posted October 7 by the General Services Administration says the agency plans to hire Convergint Technologies LLC for design-build services to upgrade existing system headends at the White House Complex and at Joint Base Anacostia-Bolling in Washington, D.C. Listed sites include the Eisenhower Executive Office Building, the New Executive Office Building, the Winder Building and 726 Jackson Place Northwest. The contract value is redacted.

The agency calls it a logical follow-on to a design-build contract awarded to Convergint in 2020 and completed in 2024, and cites the firm's security clearance and experience in the White House Complex. It invokes the only-one-responsible-source exception to competition, and the document describes the current system as facing planned obsolescence. The agency also posted a sole-source justification for DJB Contracting, Inc to replace the historic balcony on the Winder Building.

OMB Apportionments

Four spend plans updated across DHS, State, and Education as agencies adjust budgets

No new apportionment files were flagged this window, but four existing spend plans were updated. U.S. Department of Homeland Security revised plans for Immigration and Customs Enforcement facilities and Coast Guard operations, while U.S. Department of State submitted an updated congressional notification reflecting recent reorganization and hiring policy changes, and U.S. Department of Education updated its multiyear education spending priorities.

Spend plan updates

DHS Immigration and Customs Enforcement's FY2026 Facilities and Improvements spend plan was revised to reflect a $260.9 million portfolio of detention and enforcement facility projects across multiple states. The updated plan details construction and renovation work including a $32 million processing center dormitory in Miami, facility expansions in El Paso totaling $45 million, and training facility construction at Fort Benning, Georgia. Spend Plan.

DHS U.S. Coast Guard's Operation and Support spend plan was updated with minor revisions to field operations funding allocations for depot maintenance and autonomous maritime systems across surface, air, and shore facilities. Spend Plan.

U.S. Department of State's Diplomatic Engagement spend plan was updated via congressional notification from Paul D. Guaglianone, Senior Bureau Official, documenting $10.5 billion in total resources across diplomatic programs and operations. The updated plan reflects impacts of a January 2025 hiring freeze, a Deferred Resignation Program affecting 258 employees with $50.6 million in salary savings, and closure of the R/Counter Foreign Information Manipulation and Interference Hub. The plan also notes ongoing contract and grant review directed by Executive Order 14222, with further savings expected for FY2026. Spend Plan.

U.S. Department of Education revised its multiyear spend plan for Safe Schools, Citizenship Education, Innovation and Improvement, and Higher Education programs. The plan allocates $168 million for the Fund for the Improvement of Postsecondary Education, $259 million for Education Innovation and Research, $211 million for School Safety National Activities, and $150 million for Full-Service Community Schools. Spend Plan.
