Top Story
Axon Enterprise hires Ben Quayle's firm to lobby on body-worn camera funding
The Taser and police-tech maker's new registration lands with Venture Government Strategies, led by the former Arizona congressman and son of a former vice president

Axon Enterprise, Inc., the Scottsdale, Arizona-based public safety technology company best known for Taser stun guns and police body-worn cameras, registered Venture Government Strategies, LLC to lobby on its behalf in a filing posted September 10. The registration discloses work on "funding and implementation of body-worn camera systems," with Homeland Security listed as the sole issue area.

The lobbyist named on the filing is Benjamin Quayle, a former U.S. Representative for Arizona's 3rd congressional district and the son of former Vice President Dan Quayle. Venture Government Strategies is registered under that name but was formerly known as Hobart Hallaway & Quayle Ventures, LLC, according to the filing.

The filing itself doesn't specify which federal program or funding stream is at issue — only the general issue area of Homeland Security and the brief activity description above. The registration was effective as of the filing's September 10 posting date.

K Street
11 new registrations, from a medical-radioisotope startup to a beagle-rescue's push on the Beagle Liberation Act

Besides Axon Enterprise (see Top Story), the window's new registrations were mostly single-client, single-issue filings. Eden Radioisotopes, LLC registered Holland & Knight LLP to work on medical radioisotope research, development and commercial production — an emerging-industry filing amid a well-documented global shortage of medical isotopes. Big Dog Ranch Rescue registered both Capitol South, LLC and an individual lobbyist, Cameron Ring, the same day; Ring's filing specifically names the Beagle Liberation Act and "PACT Act 2.0" as targets. CAN Community Health, Inc. hired Shumaker Advisors, LLC to work the 340B prescription drug program. Separately, 19 other filings posted — quarterly activity reports, amendments and terminations — none tied to names elsewhere in today's edition; the fullest is a trio of same-day filings (a registration amendment, a 3rd-quarter report and a 4th-quarter amendment) that Rising Tide Associates posted for defense-gear maker High Speed Gear, Inc.

A Canadian "political party" registered to lobby Congress discloses an $1,800 payment to its own founder

Of 17 LD-203 contribution reports posted in the window, 13 disclosed no political contributions. The most notable of the four that did: The National Coalition Party of Canada (NCPC), an Ottawa-based entity that has filed LDA paperwork since 2021 (recently advocating for a "Canada-Organization of American States Trade Bloc" it says would carry a $33.9 trillion valuation), reported a $1,800 contribution to Raphael Louis — who is also listed as the registrant's own contact person. Separately, Economic Policy Strategies and its lobbyist Jeffrey Ziarko each filed mid-year LD-203s disclosing the identical set of 20 recipients spanning both parties, totaling $16,000 across 29 individual contributions; and Geoffrey P. Gray reported $2,000 combined to two House Republicans. None of the recipients below match other names in today's edition.
