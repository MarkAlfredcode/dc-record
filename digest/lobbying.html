A Dozen-Plus Lobbying Disclosures Surface Months Late, Led by an Eight-Month-Overdue Defense Contribution Report
No new lobbying registrations or foreign-agent filings appeared in the window, but the routine compliance filings that did post included a cluster running from two to eight months past their statutory deadlines.

Nothing in Monday's lobbying and foreign-influence filings amounted to a new registration, a new client relationship, or a new foreign-agent signup. What stood out instead was timing: among the 21 Senate lobbying disclosure filings and 16 lobbyist campaign-contribution (LD-203) reports posted between September 27 and September 28, 2026, at least 14 were filed well after the legal deadline for that report — some by two months, one by nearly eight.

The latest single filing belongs to Aerospace Industries Association of America, Inc., the trade group for Boeing, Lockheed Martin, and other defense and aerospace manufacturers. Its in-house lobbyist Cord Sterling filed an LD-203 covering July–December 2025 — due January 30, 2026 — on September 28, 2026, 241 days late. It discloses two $1,000 contributions made in late 2025: to Ken Calvert for Congress and to Senator Cotton/Cotton Majority, the political operation tied to Senator Tom Cotton of Arkansas. Neither contribution was visible in LDA's public disclosure system until this week.

McGuireWoods Consulting, the lobbying subsidiary of law firm McGuireWoods LLP, filed two overdue quarterly activity reports back-to-back on September 28 for client Aspen Consulting Service, LLC on behalf of Amoena USA: a first-quarter 2026 report 161 days late and a second-quarter 2026 report 70 days late. Both describe the same lobbying goal — changing the tariff classification of mastectomy bras. Separately, Center for African Progress, Inc filed a first-quarter 2026 no-activity report 160 days late, and ten additional LD-203 mid-year contribution reports (covering January–June 2026, due July 30) posted roughly two months past deadline, disclosing lobbyist-made contributions from firms including Federal Health Policy Strategies, Council of FHLBanks, and Huntington Ingalls Industries Incorporated. Full detail on every filing, on-time or late, is in the K Street section below.

Three of those 21 were filed well past their statutory deadline; they, and the on-time filings, are listed below.
