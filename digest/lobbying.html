Top Story
A Rental-Housing Giant's PAC Spread Money Across Both Parties' Financial-Services Leaders
K Street's registration file was empty, but a single lobbyist contribution report shows the real story of the day.

The Senate Office of Public Records logged just 14 lobbying filings and zero new LD-1 lobbying registrations in the window since the last edition, and DOJ's FARA e-file system recorded no new or terminated foreign-agent registrants at all. The filing worth dwelling on came through the Lobbying Disclosure Act's other track: a mid-year amendment to a lobbyist campaign-contribution report (LD-203) filed directly by American Homes 4 Rent (AMH), the Las Vegas-based single-family-rental real estate investment trust, disclosing 35 separate federal contributions covering January through June of this year.

The contributions, made through American Homes 4 Rent TRS, LLC Political Action Committee and totaling $67,500 over the six-month period, went to a broad, bipartisan mix of members and party committees — but the largest checks landed with people who sit atop housing-finance policy. Mike Johnson's leadership operation took in $12,500 combined ($7,500 to the Johnson Leadership Fund, $5,000 to American Revival PAC); Hakeem Jeffries got $2,500 through Jeffries for Congress; French Hill, chairman of the House Financial Services Committee, collected $7,000 across three separate checks (two direct to French Hill for Arkansas and one to his In The Arena PAC); and the committee's ranking Democrat, Maxine Waters, received $2,500 via Citizens for Waters. Financial Services Committee member Brad Sherman drew four separate contributions from the same PAC totaling $5,500.

A second LD-203 posted in the same window shows a similar bipartisan-hedge pattern on a smaller scale: Hogan Lovells Cadwalader US LLP (FKA Hogan Lovells US LLP) reported its firm PAC writing identical $15,000 checks to both the National Republican Congressional Committee and the Democratic Congressional Campaign Committee on the same day, February 10, 2026, while lobbyist Cybil G. Roehrenbeck personally gave $1,000 to Nate Morris's U.S. Senate campaign and $1,000 to Rep. Mike Quigley's campaign committee.

Neither filing discloses any lobbying activity tying these contributions to a specific bill — LD-203s report money, not policy asks — so the overlap between the giving and the recipients' committee assignments above is offered as context, not as an allegation of a quid pro quo.
