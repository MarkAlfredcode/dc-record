Top Story
Binance founder Changpeng Zhao is now paying a Washington law firm to lobby Congress and the Trump administration
A newly posted mid-year disclosure shows Simpson Thacher & Bartlett LLP collected $10,000 in the second quarter to advise the cryptocurrency exchange founder on federal policy, with a lobbyist who once served as a senior Justice Department official on the account.

A lobbying disclosure report posted to the Senate's database on September 4 shows Simpson Thacher & Bartlett LLP lobbying on behalf of Changpeng Zhao, the Cayman Islands-based founder of the cryptocurrency exchange Binance, whom the filing lists simply as a "businessperson." The registration itself took effect June 11, 2026; the report posted overnight is the firm's second-quarter activity disclosure covering that registration, showing $10,000 in income for the quarter.

The filing lists a single lobbying activity — "Financial Institutions/Investments/Securities" — described as "advising regarding public policy matters affecting the client, including engagement with the U.S. Congress and Executive Branch." Two lobbyists are named on the account: Raphael A. Prober, whose covered-position disclosure notes he served as Associate Deputy Attorney General at the Department of Justice from July 2015 to December 2016, and Hayley Booker.

The filing does not disclose which specific bills or agency actions are being lobbied, only the general issue area. It is one of just five lobbying-related filings the Senate posted overnight naming a crypto-adjacent client or issue — see the K Street section below for a second, the Crypto Council for Innovation's new registration of Chapman and Cutler LLP.
