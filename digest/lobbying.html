New 501(c)(4) Registers to Fight the Union Pacific–Norfolk Southern Rail Merger
CGCN Group is now lobbying Congress on behalf of a group formed specifically to oppose the deal.

CGCN Group, LLC filed a new lobbying registration on Wednesday, September 23, 2026, disclosing a client called Stop the Rail Merger, Inc., which the filing describes as a 501(c)(4) organization "organized for opposing the merger of Union Pacific and Norfolk Southern railroads." The registration lists a single issue area, "Railroads," and describes the planned lobbying activity as engaging lawmakers to oppose the deal.

Three CGCN Group lobbyists are named on the filing: Alex Renjel, Andrew Gallimore, and Harrison Fields.

The proposed merger between Union Pacific Corporation and Norfolk Southern Corporation, announced in 2025, would combine two of the country's largest freight railroads into a single transcontinental system and remains subject to review by the U.S. Surface Transportation Board. This is the first registration DC Record has seen for a group organized specifically to fight the merger.

K Street
Eleven New Registrations, a Trio for Ballard Partners, and $78,000 in Late LD-203 Contributions

Eleven new lobbying registrations posted overnight, ranging from a Colorado city government to a scientific publisher. Ballard Partners alone registered three new clients — Compass Minerals International, Inc., Sempre, Inc., and Drillmec Inc. — covering trade/tariffs, defense-technology contracting, and oil-and-gas market entry, respectively.

A further 18 filings posted in the same window were quarterly activity reports or amendments for existing clients rather than new business (see list below); none were terminations.

On LD-203 lobbyist contribution disclosures, 25 filings posted, but 19 reported no contributions. The other six disclosed a combined $77,950 across 30 line items. The largest came from Ark Strategy lobbyist Michael Clauser: a mid-year (January–June 2026) report, posted roughly seven weeks after its normal July 30 deadline, disclosing $49,000 spread across 19 contributions to PACs and campaign committees for more than a dozen Republican members of Congress, including Rep. Ken Calvert ($8,500 across three contributions) and a $10,000 gift to Americans 4 Security PAC.
