Top Story
Sonos triples its trade-lobbying team as it deepens its Washington push on tariffs
A registration amendment filed through Mehlman Consulting adds 19 more lobbyists to the speaker company's single-issue trade account — bringing the roster to 24 — just two weeks after the underlying registration took effect.

Sonos, Inc., the Goleta, California-based audio company, sharply expanded its Washington footprint this week. A registration amendment posted by Mehlman Consulting, Inc. on September 8 added 19 additional lobbyists to Sonos's federal lobbying account, bringing the total number of registered lobbyists working the single listed issue — "Trade policy efforts to best promote consumer affordability and domestic manufacturing" — to 24.

The underlying registration is barely two weeks old: it became effective August 24, 2026, initially naming five lobbyists — Bruce Mehlman, Dean Rosen, David R. Thomas, Elise Finley Pickering and Sage Eastman — before this week's amendment nearly quintupled the roster on the same single account.

The new names include a run of former Hill and executive-branch trade hands: Jon Adame, a former chief of staff, legislative director and general counsel to Sen. Marsha Blackburn; Alexander Perkins, formerly counsel to Sen. Ron Wyden and trade counsel to the House Ways and Means Subcommittee on Trade; Rosemary Gutierrez, a former deputy chief of staff and legislative director to Sen. Maria Cantwell; Stephen M. Cote, a former staff director for the House Financial Services Committee; and Erica Jay Chabot, a former staff director for the Senate Agriculture Committee.

The filing adds no new issue codes or additional clients — the entire buildout sits under the same single trade-policy account, indicating Sonos is scaling up firepower on one fight rather than broadening its lobbying agenda, as consumer-electronics makers navigate tariff policy on imported goods and components.

K Street
Sonos's trade buildout headlines an otherwise quiet day for new registrations

Five new lobbying registrations posted in the past day, alongside nine other quarterly reports, amendments and one termination — 14 filings in all, a routine volume. Beyond the Sonos story above, nothing else in the batch is individually notable: issues ranged from Pentagon artificial-intelligence procurement to hemp-beverage advocacy to proton-therapy access. One pattern worth flagging: Capitol Strategies US, LLC filed three separate actions in the same window, all on marine/defense-adjacent clients — a new registration for Outdoor Network, an amendment for Rocket Propulsion Systems, LLC, and a termination for Oxe Marine Inc.

Separately, 18 LD-203 lobbyist contribution reports posted; 13 disclosed no contributions. Of the five that did, amounts ran $250 to $3,000 to federal candidate committees — nothing to a PAC beyond one $500 gift. Two registrants tie back to the registrations above: Harper Downing, which also amended its Humanetics Corporation lobbying report this window, and Nicole Case, who filed the Northwest Public Power Association's quarterly activity report.
