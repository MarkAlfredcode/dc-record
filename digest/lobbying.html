Top Story
GeneDX hires Pentagon and Trump-era communications veterans to fight TRICARE's denial of genetic-testing coverage
Biotech firm's new lobbying registration targets a specific TRICARE coverage decision affecting military families

GeneDX, a Connecticut-based biotechnology company, registered Michael Best Strategies LLC to lobby on its behalf in a filing posted September 10, disclosing a fight over a TRICARE decision not to pay for genome and exome sequencing testing for U.S. Department of Defense personnel and their family members. The registration lists Defense, Health Issues and Insurance as the issue areas, with an effective date of August 24, 2026.

The two lobbyists named on the filing bring an unusually direct blend of health-communications and military-legislative backgrounds to the assignment. Ninio Fetalvo previously served as Director of Strategic Communications and Deputy Director of Communications at the Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services within the U.S. Department of Health and Human Services, and also worked in the Executive Office of the President as a communications advisor, associate advisor and assistant press secretary. Erik Michael Berdy previously served as Special Assistant to the Chairman of the Joint Chiefs of Staff for Legislative Affairs, as a legislative assistant to the Chief of Staff of the Army, and as deputy military legislative assistant to then-Sen. James Inhofe.

Both lobbyists are listed as new to this registration, meaning GeneDX has assembled a team whose government experience spans exactly the two institutions — the Pentagon's legislative apparatus and federal health communications — that intersect in a TRICARE coverage dispute.

K Street
22 new lobbying registrations overnight, led by Vantage Data Centers' 14-issue in-house push and GeneDX's TRICARE fight

Vantage Data Centers Management Company, LLC registered its own in-house lobbying operation, disclosing an unusually broad slate of 14 issue areas — from federal permitting reform and tax treatment to nuclear energy and data-center transparency proposals — reflecting how central the data-center buildout has become to the federal policy agenda. Most of the other 21 new registrations were routine single-client, single-issue filings, though Fahmy Hudome International, Inc. registered on behalf of LeMond Carbon Inc. to seek defense appropriations for domestic carbon-fiber manufacturing capability, and Cornerstone Government Affairs, Inc. picked up three new clients in one window. Separately, 9 quarterly reports, amendments and terminations were posted, including two terminations by Winslow & Co Public Affairs LLC for clients of Thorn Run Partners — the same firm that registered a brand-new client, the Structured Finance Association, in this same window.
