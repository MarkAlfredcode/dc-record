Top Story

OMB apportions $40 million for National Garden of American Heroes statues under three executive orders while a companion $34.8 million sits frozen by litigation

The National Endowment for the Humanities is simultaneously funding monument construction ordered by the White House and watching a parallel pot for the same project stall in court.

The Office of Management and Budget apportioned $40,000,000 in fiscal 2027 funding to the National Endowment for the Humanities explicitly "for the procurement of statues," citing three separate executive orders: Executive Order 13934 on building and rebuilding monuments to American heroes, Executive Order 13978 on the National Garden of American Heroes, and Executive Order 14189 on "celebrating America's birthday." A companion line apportioned another $17,695,000 — funds described as collected from the National Endowment for the Arts — for the same monument-building work.

The endowment's broader discretionary account tells a more constrained story. Of a separate $180,409,437 apportionment, $17,000,000 intended for the National Garden of American Heroes was never awarded, and $34,800,000 earmarked for the agency's Office of Federal/State Partnership cannot be obligated at all because of pending litigation. Another $38,900,000 in already-approved fiscal 2026 awards is being held for obligation until the first quarter of fiscal 2027.

Together, the three apportionments show the National Endowment for the Humanities simultaneously executing the administration's monument-building executive orders while a separate pot of money earmarked for that same National Garden of American Heroes project sits frozen — one tranche never awarded, the other tied up in litigation.

OMB Apportionments

Federal agencies flagged for antideficiency accounting, oversight holds, and executive order monument funding

Five apportionment actions spanning fiscal years 2026 and 2027 triggered OMB oversight flags, ranging from Rural Development's accounting method to EPA's conditional loan-subsidy reporting and three National Endowment for the Humanities accounts tied to executive orders on American monuments. None of the flagged items represent rescissions or deficiency spending; instead they flag complex accounting, conditional funding, or pending legal matters requiring OGE or OMB oversight.

Department of Agriculture Rural Development received $66,875,000 (FY2026) with an antideficiency-related notation. The footnote explains: "The exclusion of actual amounts from the SF-133 on the financing and liquidating accounts does not subject Rural Development to the Antideficiency Act but rather allows estimates to be recorded in the Trial Balance ensuring a clean Financial Statement Audit." This is an accounting method clarification, not a spending restriction. Notice.

Environmental Protection Agency was allocated $3,175,869,874 (FY2026) for WIFIA (Water Infrastructure Finance and Innovation Act) loans, flagged for an oversight-hold rationale. The footnote reads: "The listed subsidy rates, subsidy amounts and loan amounts are notional. The final subsidy rates and subsidy amounts are subject to OMB revision based upon receipt of the final rating opinion letters prior to final acceptance and financing of the WIFIA loans per 33 U.S.C. § 3907(a)(1)(D)(ii). Rationale: An agency spend plan or other documentation is necessary to better understand how the agency intends to obligate some or all of the apportioned funds." Notice.

National Endowment for the Humanities received three apportionments tied to executive orders on monuments and American celebrations. Line 1000 carried $40,000,000 (FY2027) "for the procurement of statues as described in Executive Order 13934 (relating to building and rebuilding monuments to American heroes), Executive Order 13978 (relating to building the National Garden of American Heroes), and Executive Order 14189 (relating to celebrating America's birthday)." Notice. Line 1740 allocated $17,695,000 (FY2027) for "$17 million collected from the National Endowment for the Arts" for the same monument-building work. Notice.

National Endowment for the Humanities discretionary balance line (1000DE) was apportioned $180,409,437 (FY2027) with a litigation flag. The footnote details holds: $17 million from the National Endowment for the Arts for the National Garden of American Heroes that was not awarded; $34.8 million for the Office of Fed/State Partnership that cannot be obligated due to pending litigation; $38.9 million in approved FY2026 awards obligatable in Q1 FY2027; and matching funds for commitments pending awardee match and NEH certification. Notice.

Routine apportionments (50 non-flagged filings across eight agencies): Department of Agriculture, 11 filings (Forest Service wildland fire management, $7.3B; national forest system and maintenance; farm commodity credit; rural housing and development; agricultural marketing); Department of Commerce, 1 filing; Department of Energy, 3 filings (environmental defense and science); Department of the Interior, 9 filings (bureau land management, ocean energy, reclamation, territorial payments); Corps of Engineers, 9 filings (construction $30.4B, operations and maintenance $6.8B, trust funds); National Aeronautics and Space Administration, 6 filings (safety and mission services multi-year accounts); National Science Foundation, 2 filings (research activities); Other Independent Agencies, 9 filings (Smithsonian, NEA, NEH subsidiary accounts).

CFTC finalizes whistleblower-award overhaul as IRS schedules hearing on stripping tax-exempt status from racially discriminatory private schools

The Internal Revenue Service scheduled a public hearing on its proposed rule that would deny federal tax-exempt status to private schools that discriminate by race, and the Environmental Protection Agency proposed handing Indiana's coal-ash permitting authority to state regulators.
