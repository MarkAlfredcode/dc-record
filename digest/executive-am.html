Education Department Finalizes Repeal of the 2024 Title IX Rule, Recodifying Pre-2024 Regulations Nationwide

The rule being repealed was vacated in litigation and has gone unenforced for more than a year and a half.

The U.S. Department of Education published a final rule in today's Federal Register to repeal its 2024 final rule amending the Title IX regulations and to restore the regulatory text that existed before that 2024 rulemaking. According to the rule's own Federal Register text, the department is acting because the 2024 final rule “has been vacated and is not enforceable nationwide” and because the department “has not enforced the 2024 final rule for over a year and a half.”

The rule states that the pre-2024 regulatory text — which has remained the version actually in force during that enforcement gap — “should be recodified in the Code of Federal Regulations” so that the CFR matches what has actually been enforced. The Federal Register notice does not itself detail the substantive content of either the 2024 rule or the pre-2024 text being restored beyond that framing, and this newsletter has not independently verified those provisions beyond what the rule states.

The action closes out, at the regulatory-text level, one of the most litigated federal rulemakings of the prior several years — though because a court had already vacated the 2024 rule nationwide, today's Federal Register notice is better read as formal bookkeeping catching the CFR up to an enforcement status that has been in place since the vacatur, not as a new policy shift in practice.

OMB Apportionments

No apportionment footnotes were flagged this week, but OMB posted seven new or revised agency spend plans, including a $788 million Education Department multiyear plan and $240 million in USDA international food aid awards

OMB's apportionment tracker found no rescissions, executive-order-conditioned footnotes, or other keyword-triggered apportionment actions in the September 24-29 window. Seven new or updated Spend Plan PDFs were posted to apportionment-public.max.gov in that time, ranging from a $28.5 million child-welfare incentive program to a State Department diplomatic-engagement plan whose file size shifted only slightly in a routine revision.

New and Updated Spend Plans

Department of Health and Human Services' Administration for Children and Families posted a new FY2025 Spend Plan for the Child Welfare Services program, which distributes $268.7 million in formula grants to state and tribal public child welfare agencies. The plan states the funding helps agencies develop and expand child and family services programs for homeless, dependent, and neglected children, and those at risk of becoming delinquent. No FTE count or discretionary policy allocations were listed beyond the formula-grant total. Spend Plan.

U.S. Department of Agriculture's Food for Education Program spend plan lists six $40 million awards — $240 million total — to implementing partners including Save the Children, the World Food Program, Counterpart International, and Partners of the Americas for fortified-food commodity shipments to Benin, Honduras, Mozambique, Pakistan (two separate awards), and Senegal. Commodities listed include fortified rice, vegetable oil, split peas, corn-soy blend, and wheat, quantified in metric tons per country. Spend Plan.

The Administration for Children and Families' Adoption Incentives and Legal Guardianship Incentives Program posted an FY2026 spend plan allocating $28,464,500 in formula grants. The funding rewards states that improve their performance in finding permanent homes — through adoption or legal guardianship — for children in foster care who cannot be reunified with their parents. Spend Plan.

USDA's Food and Nutrition Service posted an FY2026 Commodity Assistance Program carryover spend plan showing $80 million in carryover funds plus $6 million in anticipated collections for Commodity Supplemental Food Program commodities, with smaller carryover and recovery lines for the Senior Farmers' Market Nutrition Program, the Nutrition Services Incentive Program, TEFAP administrative funds, and the Farmers' Market Nutrition Program. The plan notes the agency deliberately purchased smaller amounts of food at the end of FY2025 to avoid overfilling contracted warehouses ahead of a pending warehouse-contract award, and says it is keeping safety inventory 'lean' until those contracts are finalized. Spend Plan.

The Department of Education posted a multiyear-funds spend plan, dated as of November 13, 2025, covering four programs totaling $788 million: $211 million for School Safety National Activities, $150 million for Full-Service Community Schools, $259 million for Education Innovation and Research, and $168 million for the Fund for the Improvement of Postsecondary Education. Spend Plan.

The Administration for Children and Families' Preschool Development Grants program posted an FY2026 spend plan totaling $315 million — $310 million in grants and $5 million for technical assistance and evaluation. Spend Plan.

The Department of State's Diplomatic Engagement Spend Plan — a Congressional Notification Transmittal (CN 25-042, dated June 16, 2025, superseding an earlier CN 25-033) covering FY2025 diplomatic-engagement accounts — was flagged as changed, with its extracted text length shifting from roughly 2,817,519 to 2,819,765 characters, a difference of about 2,246 characters out of a 2.8-million-character document. Only that size change is detectable here; no diff of the actual content is available, so it isn't possible to say what specifically was revised. Spend Plan.

USAspending Contracts

Carahsoft collects $35.5 million in ServiceNow licensing across two agencies in a single week, while two unrelated awards each cite “EO 14398” without explanation

USAspending logged 14 new contract awards of $2 million or more this week, ranging from $2,118,390 to $18,188,860. No awards were tied to the White House or the Executive Office of the President this week. The clearest pattern: one reseller landing two large software-licensing deals in 24 hours, and a pair of unrelated agencies both citing the same, unexplained executive order in their procurement descriptions.

Carahsoft Wins Two Large ServiceNow Awards in 24 Hours

Carahsoft Technology Corp won two large ServiceNow-related awards from two different agencies within a single day. The larger, $18,188,860, came from the Internal Revenue Service on September 27 for “ServiceNow Enterprise” licensing covering IT help-desk, workspace, print, and productivity tools. A day earlier, the Department of Homeland Security's Office of Procurement Operations awarded Carahsoft $17,340,253 for ServiceNow (SNOW) licenses supporting DHS's Office of the Chief Information Officer. Together the two awards total more than $35,529,113 in ServiceNow licensing revenue for the same reseller in the same week. IRS award. DHS award.

Two Awards Cite “EO 14398”

Two unrelated awards this week explicitly cite “EO 14398” directly in their government-furnished description text; the underlying USAspending description does not explain what that executive order requires or covers, and this newsletter has not independently verified its content, so no characterization of the order itself is offered here — only that both agencies flagged it. The Federal Bureau of Prisons awarded Nisga'a Ciops LLC $6,071,043 on September 25 for a non-personal-services contract for “various professional services,” with the EO citation appended to the title. Separately, the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention awarded Goldbelt Integrated Logistics Services, LLC $2,890,952 on September 27 for bioinformatics support services, with the same EO citation appended. Bureau of Prisons award. CDC award.

The Bureau of Alcohol, Tobacco, Firearms and Explosives' Acquisition and Property Management Division awarded $10,537,080 to Bering Global Solutions, LLC on September 27 under a “Distribution Supply and Service Center Contract.” The award is classified under an apparel-manufacturing NAICS code and a “special purpose clothing” product/service code, suggesting the contract covers a specialized uniform or tactical-clothing supply chain rather than ATF's typical law-enforcement acquisition categories.
