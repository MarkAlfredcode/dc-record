OMB conditions $1.6 billion in Customs and Border Protection funding on a hiring plan it hasn't finalized

Two apportionments approved the same day tie CBP's personnel account and its retention-bonus account to further coordination with the White House budget office.

The Office of Management and Budget on September 19, 2026 approved two apportionments to the Department of Homeland Security for U.S. Customs and Border Protection worth a combined $1,625,033,000 — $876,198,000 for CBP Personnel and $748,835,000 for CBP Retention, Hiring, and Performance Bonuses — with both notices carrying identical language conditioning the money on the Department of Homeland Security continuing to "work closely with OMB to finalize a CBP hiring plan that relies on existing funding sources to the greatest possible extent."

The dual apportionments mean OMB's hiring-plan condition now reaches both ends of CBP's staffing pipeline at once: the base payroll account that funds existing personnel, and the bonus account CBP uses to recruit and retain new hires. That gives the budget office practical leverage over the pace and shape of the agency's workforce growth beyond what Congress specified when it appropriated the money.

OMB frequently uses apportionment footnotes, rather than public rulemaking, to attach conditions like this one. Because apportionments are rarely challenged and seldom reported on, the CBP hiring-plan condition is likely to shape the agency's staffing decisions well before it draws outside scrutiny.

ICE extends $41 million immigration-monitoring contract as agencies log 87 new awards over $2 million

Federal agencies recorded 87 new contract actions worth at least $2 million apiece over the past three and a half weeks, led by a $51.8 million construction task order for the National Institute of Standards and Technology and a $50.2 million GSA renovation at Boston's Charlestown Navy Yard.

Two awards stand out for their subject matter rather than their size: a $41 million extension of U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement's GPS-monitoring alternative-to-detention program, and a $20 million U.S. Customs and Border Protection contract with a small advertising firm for an unspecified "national media campaign."

Three recurring purchase patterns — U.S. Mint gold bullion, Agriculture Department orange donations for overseas food aid, and VA Da Vinci surgical robots — account for another 14 of the awards; no award in this window was tied to the White House or the Executive Office of the President.

HITT Contracting, Inc. won the largest new contract action in this window: a $51,830,842 task order from the Department of Commerce's National Institute of Standards and Technology, issued under an indefinite-delivery construction contract ("CUP IDIQ") and identified internally only as task order one, "OU19-FY26-015-NEW." Neither the award description nor the product/service code supplies further detail beyond "construction of other industrial buildings" — consistent with lab or research-facility work at one of NIST's campuses — so the specific project scope isn't public beyond the dollar figure. Award record.

Tocci Construct LLC received a $50,247,737 award from the General Services Administration's Public Buildings Service for the renovation of Building 107 at the historic Charlestown Navy Yard in Boston, a former shipbuilding facility now part of Boston National Historical Park and used for federal office space. It is among the largest single construction actions logged in this window. Award record.

B.I. OMB withholds $1.6 billion in CBP apportionments pending hiring plan review

The Office of Management and Budget has placed $1.6 billion in conditional apportionments to the Department of Homeland Security for Customs and Border Protection operations, both contingent on OMB oversight of the agency's hiring plan. Both approvals came September 19, 2026 with identical footnotes requiring CBP to work with OMB to finalize a hiring plan that prioritizes existing funding sources.

Department of Homeland Security received $876,198,000 for Customs and Border Protection Personnel (line 6032, FY2026). The apportionment is conditional on OMB approval of the agency's hiring plan; the footnote states: "Amounts on this line are apportioned with the understanding that the Department of Homeland Security will continue to work closely with OMB to finalize a CBP hiring plan that relies on existing funding sources to the greatest possible extent." The flag indicates OMB is requesting additional programmatic spending information. Notice.

Department of Homeland Security also received $748,835,000 for Customs and Border Protection Retention, Hiring, and Performance Bonuses (line 6033, FY2026), approved the same day. This apportionment carries the identical OMB oversight condition, indicating both the Personnel and Bonuses appropriations are linked to finalization of the CBP hiring plan. Notice.
