White House Publishes Five Same-Day Proclamations Escalating Tariff Fight With Canada

New presidential actions modify or expand duties and import exclusions on Canadian motor vehicles, alcoholic beverages, and dairy products.

The Federal Register's Sept. 14 edition carries five presidential proclamations issued on the same day, all invoking the same justification — "continued discrimination against the commerce of the United States" by Canada — to reshape U.S. tariff treatment of Canadian goods. Two of the proclamations are titled as "modifying the scope" of products already subject to the administration's added duties (one covering motor vehicles, one covering alcoholic beverages); the other three are titled as "excluding" specific Canadian products from importation altogether, covering motor vehicles, dairy, and alcoholic beverages respectively.

The Federal Register did not publish abstracts for any of the five documents, so the precise product lists, effective dates, and duty rates being added or removed are not yet available from this data — only the titles and the administration's stated rationale. The five sequential document numbers (2026-18835 through 2026-18839) indicate they were signed and filed as a single batch, suggesting a coordinated escalation rather than five unrelated actions.

The same day's Federal Register also carried six final rules from the Federal Communications Commission, the Federal Aviation Administration, three bank regulators, the Environmental Protection Agency and the Coast Guard — none related to the Canada actions. Full detail on all eleven documents is in the Federal Register section below.

OMB Apportionments

National Endowment for the Arts Files New Spend Plan Tying ~$278.7 Million to America250 and the Garden of Heroes

No apportionment footnotes matched this window's flag rules, and no other new or changed apportionment files appeared at all — the only activity was a newly required detailed spend plan from the National Endowment for the Arts, which OMB has been withholding funds pending more information on how the agency plans to spend them.

The National Endowment for the Arts filed its first FY2026 detailed spend plan, covering roughly $278.7 million in apportioned budget authority (about $165.5 million under "Category A" activities-by-quarter and $113.2 million under "Category B" activities-by-program, including $62.3 million in salaries and expenses). OMB's footnotes on the underlying apportionment — designated A1 and A3 — state that funds beyond a specific list of protected activities are frozen until the agency submits this plan, because 'an agency spend plan or other documentation is necessary to better understand how the agency intends to obligate some or all of the apportioned funds.' In practice, that means most of the endowment's FY2026 money couldn't be obligated until this document was filed.

The plan itself shows the endowment continuing the funding-priority overhaul it began in 2025, when it terminated 1,636 existing grant awards and withdrew 840 tentative offers that didn't align with the administration's priorities. New spending highlighted in the plan includes a second $17 million payment to the National Endowment for the Humanities as part of a $34 million interagency agreement for the National Garden of American Heroes, plus $1.25 million in grants for arts projects honoring the historical figures named in Executive Order 13978. A further $22.4 million (revised down from an original $27.2 million projection) is earmarked for grants tied to the America250 semiquincentennial and related presidential priorities.

No other apportionment files — flagged or routine — were found in this window at either openomb.org or apportionment-public.max.gov.

The Federal Register

Five Canada-Tariff Proclamations Headline an Otherwise Routine Register Day

Beyond today's top story — five presidential proclamations escalating tariff and import actions against Canada — the Sept. 14 Federal Register carries six final rules from five different agencies and eight proposed rules, only one of which rises above routine aviation-safety and state-implementation-plan housekeeping.

Presidential proclamations: the Canada tariff actions
  • Modifying the Scope of Products of Canada Subject to the Additional Duties Imposed To Offset Canadian Discrimination Against the Commerce of the United States With Respect to Motor Vehicles — adjusts which Canadian motor-vehicle products are covered by the administration's existing added tariff duties. Read.
  • Modifying the Scope of Products of Canada... With Respect to Alcoholic Beverages — same type of scope adjustment, applied to Canadian alcoholic beverages. Read.
  • Excluding Certain Canadian Products From Importation Into the United States... With Respect to Motor Vehicles — bars specified Canadian motor-vehicle products from import entirely, rather than merely taxing them. Read.
  • Excluding Certain Canadian Products... With Respect to Dairy — same import-exclusion approach applied to Canadian dairy products. Read.
  • Excluding Certain Canadian Products... With Respect to Alcoholic Beverages — the import-exclusion version of the alcoholic-beverages action above. Read.
Also published (final rules)
  • Federal Communications Commission announced OMB approval, through November 30, 2027, of an information-collection requirement tied to its E-Rate program overhaul for schools and libraries. Read.
  • The Federal Communications Commission also adopted its regulatory fee schedule for Fiscal Year 2026. Read.
  • The Federal Aviation Administration aligned its sport-pilot practical test standards with its recently adopted MOSAIC light-sport aircraft certification rule. Read.
  • The Office of the Comptroller of the Currency, Federal Reserve System and Federal Deposit Insurance Corporation jointly issued an interim final rule raising, to $6 billion in assets, the threshold for small banks and foreign-bank branches to qualify for an 18-month (rather than more frequent) on-site examination cycle. Read.
  • The Environmental Protection Agency approved updated motor-vehicle emissions budgets for California's San Joaquin Valley ozone plan. Read.
  • The Coast Guard established a temporary safety zone on Lake Erie near Oregon, Ohio, for an overwater drone display. Read.
Proposed rule of note

The Federal Aviation Administration opened an advance notice of proposed rulemaking on whether to permit wildland firefighters to be transported to and from fire sites aboard certain "restricted category" aircraft — a statutorily mandated review the agency says requires more public input on firefighter-transport resources and the cost-benefit tradeoffs involved before it can draft an actual rule. Read.

Seven other proposed rules were also posted but are routine: four FAA airworthiness directives affecting specific Airbus, CFM International engine, Dassault and Bombardier models, and three EPA state-implementation-plan actions (negative declarations for solid-waste incinerators in the District of Columbia and Maryland, and an ozone clean-data determination for the Sacramento, California metro area).
