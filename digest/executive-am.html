Top Story

OMB conditions $76.3 billion in Social Security funding and $808.3 million in Labor Department grants on new oversight documentation

Nearly two-thirds of the $131 billion OMB approved in this apportionment window carries documentation holds, several tied directly to President Trump's grantmaking-oversight executive order.

The Office of Management and Budget apportioned $76.3 billion in FY2026 funding and $27.7 billion in FY2027 funding to the Social Security Administration with a documentation-review flag attached. Per the apportionment footnotes, the agency must give OMB 10 days' advance notice of any approved waiver with gross federal costs exceeding $1 million before that money can move — a condition that does not block the funding outright but inserts a new OMB checkpoint ahead of certain obligations.

At the Department of Labor, $653.3 million across seven workforce programs — including YouthBuild ($98.5 million), Reentry Employment Opportunities ($107.3 million), Native American programs ($62.4 million), Migrant and Seasonal Farmworkers ($2.1 million), Dislocated Worker Employment and Training Activities ($99.7 million) and the Workforce Data Quality Initiative ($6.0 million) — is held up under footnotes specifying that new grant awards can't be obligated until 10 business days after OMB receives the final, Secretary-approved Funding Opportunity Announcement.

A separate $277.2 million FY2027 Apprenticeship allocation follows the same pattern, and the Department of Labor's entire $808.3 million total budgetary resources allocation is explicitly conditioned on compliance with Executive Order 14332, "Improving Oversight of Federal Grantmaking." In practice, that ties routine program funding to a White House directive on grant administration rather than leaving it to each agency's own grants-management process.

Neither the Social Security Administration nor the Department of Labor funding is being rescinded — both remain apportioned and available once the documentation conditions are met. But the scale of the hold, spanning the government's largest entitlement agency and a broad swath of federal job-training programs, makes this the most consequential apportionment action tracked this week.

OMB Apportionments

OMB holds $76.3B Social Security and $808.3M Labor Department allocations pending documentation review

OMB approved allocations totaling over $131 billion across government agencies in this window, with nearly two-thirds subject to documented oversight conditions. Three agencies—the Social Security Administration and Department of Labor—received flagged allocations requiring additional programmatic documentation before certain funds can be obligated.

Social Security Administration received $76.3 billion in FY2026 and $27.7 billion in FY2027 allocations marked for oversight review. According to OMB apportionment footnotes, the agency must provide 10 days' advance notice to OMB of any approved waivers with gross federal costs exceeding $1 million. FY2026 Notice. FY2027 Notice.

Department of Labor allocations totaling $653.3 million were flagged for documentation review across seven workforce programs. For YouthBuild ($98.5M), Reentry Employment Opportunities ($107.3M), Native Americans ($62.4M), Migrant and Seasonal Farmworkers ($2.1M), Dislocated Worker Employment and Training Activities ($99.7M), and Workforce Data Quality Initiative ($6.0M), the apportionment footnotes specify that funds for new grant awards become available for obligation 10 business days after OMB receives the final Secretary-approved Funding Opportunity Announcement. This delay allows OMB to review program documentation before release. Notice.

Department of Labor's Apprenticeship program ($277.2M) for FY2027 follows the same documentation-hold pattern, as does the agency's overall $808.3M total budgetary resources allocation, which is explicitly conditioned on compliance with Executive Order 14332 ("Improving Oversight of Federal Grantmaking"). The Federal Register

Commerce imposes new polysilicon import curbs as FTC drops its post-employment ethics clearance rule

The Federal Register published 20 final rules and 7 proposed rules over September 24-25, with no executive orders or other presidential documents in the window. The most consequential: a temporary final rule from the Bureau of Industry and Security restricting polysilicon imports under a presidential proclamation, a Federal Trade Commission rule eliminating its post-employment "revolving door" clearance requirement, and a Nuclear Regulatory Commission proposal to rewrite its reactor-licensing rules under Executive Order 14300.

  • Fish and Wildlife Service (Interior Department) — correction to a September 1 final rule that opened or expanded hunting on 111 refuge and hatchery stations. Rule.
  • Federal Aviation Administration (Transportation Department) — miscellaneous updates to required instrument-flight-rules altitudes and changeover points for federal airways. Rule.
  • U.S. Postal Service — interim final rule partially rescinding and updating its own National Environmental Policy Act implementing procedures, which had been built on now-rescinded Council on Environmental Quality regulations. Rule.
  • Farm Service Agency (Agriculture Department) — correction to a September 4 final rule implementing its Application Fast Track loan process. Rule.
  • Bureau of Alcohol, Tobacco, Firearms and Explosives (Justice Department) — new requirement for annual, not just initial, notices to local fire authorities about explosives-storage sites. Rule.
  • Bureau of Alcohol, Tobacco, Firearms and Explosives — finalizes two long-pending interim rules implementing the Safe Explosives Act, ending their interim status after responding to public comments filed in 2003. Rule.
  • Department of Transportation — finalizes revisions to its Disadvantaged Business Enterprise and Airport Concession Disadvantaged Business Enterprise program rules, completing a shift away from race- and sex-based presumptions the department determined were unconstitutional. Rule.
  • National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration (Commerce Department) — closes the General category Atlantic bluefin tuna fishery for the rest of September. Rule.
  • Office of Foreign Assets Control (Treasury Department) — adds new Sanctions Penalties Regulations consolidating penalty provisions across its sanctions programs. Rule.
  • Environmental Protection Agency — clarifies when Clean Air Act New Source Review and general-duty accident-prevention requirements can be reviewed through the title V operating permit program. Rule.

  • U.S. Coast Guard — new training requirements for crew on U.S.-flagged passenger ships carrying more than 12 passengers on international voyages, implementing international maritime safety standards. Rule.
