Top Story

EPA Finalizes Repeal of Power Plant Carbon Rules, Proposes to Go Further Still

A final rule unwinds most of the 2024 Carbon Pollution Standards for fossil-fuel power plants, while a same-day companion proposal argues the Clean Air Act doesn't let EPA regulate power-plant greenhouse gases at all.

The U.S. Environmental Protection Agency published a final rule repealing most of the 2024 Carbon Pollution Standards, which set greenhouse-gas emission limits for fossil-fuel-fired electric generating units. The repeal eliminates the emission guidelines for existing coal- and gas-fired steam generating units, the carbon-capture-and-storage-based standards that would have applied to coal plants undergoing large modifications, and the CCS-based standards for new large gas-fired turbines. EPA said the change reflects "a reevaluation of the best system of emission reduction" for the affected plant categories.

In a supplemental proposal published the same day, EPA went further, arguing that Section 111 of the Clean Air Act does not authorize the agency to regulate power-plant greenhouse gas emissions in response to climate change at all. If EPA adopts that legal theory, it would need to rescind the agency's own 2015 endangerment-related findings and repeal both the 2015 and 2024 standards entirely, not just the portions eliminated in today's final rule. EPA said it is not reopening today's final rule to further comment, but is separately soliciting comment on the broader constitutional and statutory theory.

The two EPA actions arrived alongside another major regulatory story in today's Federal Register: a first batch of proposed rules rewriting large sections of the Federal Acquisition Regulation, described below. Both moves reflect a broader push this week to roll back or restructure federal rules governing energy and procurement.

OGE Ethics Filings

Six nominees filed ethics agreements; four divestitures confirmed

Nine executive branch officials filed ethics paperwork this week, including six ethics agreements and compliance certifications and four certificates of divestiture. The agreements cover conflict-of-interest commitments and asset-disposition plans.

Ethics Agreements and Compliance Certifications

Jeremy Wiggins, Department of the Treasury, Assistant Secretary for International Trade & Development, filed an ethics agreement committing to recuse from matters affecting his financial interests, restrict investment accounts to diversified funds and government obligations, and receive an ethics briefing within 15 days of confirmation. Ethics Agreement.

Heidi Overton, Department of Health and Human Services, Commissioner of the Food and Drug Administration, filed an ethics agreement that includes a specific prohibited holdings list reflecting FDA jurisdiction: pharmaceutical, biotechnology, medical devices, food and beverage production, tobacco, healthcare delivery, and communications media. She will restrict managed accounts and receive ethics briefing and training. Ethics Agreement.

David MacNeil, Federal Trade Commission, Commissioner, filed an ethics agreement requiring divestiture of the Advenio Global Equity Fund LP hedge fund within 90 days of confirmation and resignation from numerous entities including MNAPL Holdings LLC (owner of WeatherTech), real estate holdings, and aviation companies. He will retain ownership of watercraft entities for personal use and family trusts. Ethics Agreement.

William Trachman, Department of State, Ambassador to Tanzania, filed a certification of compliance with his ethics agreement confirming completion of required resignations, divestitures, and ethics briefing. Certification of Ethics Agreement Compliance.

Christopher Nassar, Department of Justice, U.S. Attorney for the Northern District of Oklahoma, filed a certification of compliance with his ethics agreement. Certification of Ethics Agreement Compliance.

The Federal Register

OMB, Pentagon, GSA and NASA propose sweeping FAR rewrite as EPA finalizes power-plant carbon rule repeal

The Federal Register carried two major regulatory stories this week: the EPA's final repeal of 2024 power-plant carbon rules (see Top Story) and the opening batch of a planned twelve-rule overhaul of the Federal Acquisition Regulation. A single Presidential Determination and 20 other final rules, plus 13 proposed rules, rounded out the window.

Presidential Determination

The Executive Office of the President published the annual Presidential Determination on Major Drug Transit or Major Illicit Drug Producing Countries for Fiscal Year 2027, identifying countries subject to the annual drug-certification process; no abstract text was published with the notice. Presidential Determination.

OMB, Pentagon, GSA and NASA Propose Wholesale FAR Rewrite

The Office of Management and Budget's Office of Federal Procurement Policy, the Department of Defense, the General Services Administration, and the National Aeronautics and Space Administration — collectively the FAR Council — published the first four of a planned twelve proposed rules implementing Executive Order 14275, "Restoring Common Sense to Federal Procurement," which directs elimination of what the administration calls excessive acquisition regulations. Together the four rules touch FAR Parts 8 (Required Sources), 9 (Contractor Qualifications), 12 (Commercial Products/Services), 13 (Simplified Acquisition), 14 (Sealed Bidding), 15 (Contracting by Negotiation), 16 (Types of Contracts), 17 (Special Contracting Methods), 27 (Patents/Data Rights), 28 (Bonds and Insurance), 35 (Research and Development), 36 (Construction and A-E Contracts), 38 (Federal Supply Schedule), 44 (Subcontracting Policies), 47 (Transportation), 51 (Use of Government Sources), and conforming changes to Part 52 (Solicitation Provisions and Contract Clauses). The FAR Council said the full twelve-rule package will "streamline the FAR in its entirety" once complete, meaning eight more parts of the regulation are still to come.

  • Parts 8, 12, 13, 15, 38, 44 and 51 — Proposed rule.
  • Parts 16, 17 and 35 — Proposed rule.
  • Parts 9, 27 and 47 — Proposed rule.
  • Parts 14, 28 and 36 — Proposed rule.
Civil Service and Export Control Changes

The Office of Personnel Management proposed amendments to its excepted-service regulations, conforming them to current excepted-service schedules including Schedule Policy/Career, modernizing procedures for competitive-service-adjacent Part 302 hires while preserving veterans' preference and injury-restoration rights, and clarifying how Pathways Program employees can convert into Schedule Policy/Career positions. Proposed rule.

The Department of State finalized a rule removing certain uncrewed underwater vehicles from the U.S. Munitions List under the International Traffic in Arms Regulations, easing export controls on that category, while requesting further comment on refining controls and license exemptions for remaining UUV-related activities. Rule.

The Department of State also finalized separate International Traffic in Arms Regulations changes clarifying policy-of-denial provisions, updating country-specific export policies for Ethiopia and Somalia, and adding Saudi Arabia and Peru to the list of major non-NATO allies. Rule.

The Department of Homeland Security finalized a Homeland Security Acquisition Regulation rule codifying how DHS complies with the Make Personal Protective Equipment in America Act, intended to sustain and expand domestic PPE manufacturing for public-health emergencies. Rule.

Also published (routine, final rules — 16 total): Department of Agriculture Agricultural Marketing Service milk-pricing correction; Federal Aviation Administration RNAV route correction near Missoula, MT; two Rolls-Royce Trent engine airworthiness directives; a Department of Energy notice on manufactured-housing conservation standards superseded by the ROAD to Housing Act; two Standard Instrument Approach Procedure amendments; a Class E airspace amendment at Alma, MI; airworthiness directives for Airbus A330, Boeing 767-300F, and Boeing 737 airplanes; an Food and Drug Administration order denying a premarket-notification exemption petition for radiology AI devices; a Consumer Product Safety Commission withdrawal of an infant-bouncer-seat rule after adverse comment; two U.S. Coast Guard safety/special-local-regulation zones (Key West, FL and Toledo, OH); and a Bell Textron helicopter airworthiness directive.
7 other proposed rules were published in this window covering routine airworthiness directives (Pratt & Whitney, Airbus A300, ATR-GIE), Coast Guard drawbridge-operation schedule changes (Connecticut), and a South Atlantic coral-reef/shrimp-fishery habitat amendment — filtered out here as not policy-significant.
