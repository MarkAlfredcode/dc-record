Top Story

White House Escalates Canada Trade Fight With Five New Presidential Actions on Autos, Alcohol and Dairy

Two documents widen the scope of retaliatory tariffs on Canadian motor vehicles and alcoholic beverages, while three more go further and bar certain Canadian motor-vehicle, dairy and alcoholic-beverage products from entering the United States at all.

Five presidential documents are scheduled for publication in the Federal Register on September 14, 2026, all citing "continued discrimination against the commerce of the United States" by Canada. Two of them modify the scope of products already subject to additional duties the administration previously imposed to offset that discrimination: one adjusts which motor-vehicle products fall under the added tariffs, the other does the same for alcoholic beverages. Modifying "scope" in a tariff order typically means adding or removing specific product categories from the list already facing the extra duty — the text of the orders themselves is not yet available, so the precise product changes cannot be characterized further here.

The other three documents are more severe: rather than adjusting a tariff rate, they exclude certain Canadian products from importation into the United States entirely, covering motor vehicles, dairy, and alcoholic beverages respectively. An import exclusion blocks the listed goods from entering the country altogether, regardless of duty paid — a step beyond a tariff increase. Taken together, the five documents suggest an intensification of the trade dispute with Canada across all three sectors rather than a rate adjustment alone, though without the underlying order text, the specific products and dollar exposure affected cannot be confirmed.

No other document in today's window — not the Office of Management and Budget apportionment sweep, new federal contract awards, or ethics filings — approaches this in significance, though SAM.gov coverage is also absent today because of an API quota lockout (see the Federal Contracting Actions section).

Fifty-three new contract actions worth $2 million or more posted in the trailing window through September 12, 2026. Three separate limited-liability companies sharing the Koniag name won three separate General Services Administration Federal Acquisition Service task orders within the same week, all funding technology work for the Small Business Administration: Koniag Operations Services LLC ($23,659,929.92, September 10) for enterprise security and identity services; Koniag Professional Services, LLC ($21,033,489.60, September 9) for CRM integration and data-platform services; and Koniag Advisory and Business Solutions, LLC ($13,865,491.20, September 8) for digital workplace and governance services.
