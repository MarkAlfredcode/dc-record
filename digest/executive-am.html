Top Story

White House issues two executive orders overhauling H-1B visa program administration

Neither order's Federal Register listing discloses operative provisions, but the titles point to tighter federal control over H-1B hiring and new entry restrictions on nonimmigrant workers.

The White House published two executive orders in the Federal Register on September 23: "Enhancing Program Integrity and Interagency Coordination in the Administration of the H-1B Nonimmigrant Visa Program" and "Restriction on Entry of Certain Nonimmigrant Workers." The Federal Register's listing for both carries no abstract text, so the substance of what each order actually requires is not yet public in the data available to this newsletter; the titles alone indicate a move toward stricter federal coordination over H-1B administration and new limits on nonimmigrant worker entry.

The orders follow two conservation-themed executive orders published a day earlier — "Restoring American Saltwater Angling and Recreation" and "Reinvigorating America's Hunting Heritage" — for a total of four new executive orders in the past two days. The H-1B-focused orders are likely to draw the most attention from federal contractors and the technology and staffing firms that make heavy use of the visa program, several of which appear elsewhere in today's contract-award coverage as recipients of federal IT and professional-services work.

USAspending Contracts

84 new contract actions worth roughly $555,314,093 hit the books, led by a $35.8 million NASA facilities deal and a $30 million Secret Service vehicle order

USAspending.gov logged 84 new-to-report contract actions of $2 million or more in the trailing window, together worth roughly $555 million. The largest is a NASA facilities-support task order; the Secret Service, the FBI, the U.S. Marshals Service and the Department of Veterans Affairs also made large individual purchases, and the Department of Agriculture issued a cluster of nine same-day meat and dairy buys for its federal food-donation program.

Amentum Technology, Inc. won the single largest new award in the window: $35,774,648 from the National Aeronautics and Space Administration for "Center Maintenance, Operation and Engineering II (CMOE-II)" support services — the day-to-day facilities upkeep, engineering and operations contract for a NASA center. It is a follow-on task order under an existing indefinite-delivery vehicle rather than a new competition. Award record.

The U.S. Secret Service exercised a $30,066,079 order against an existing indefinite-delivery contract with General Motors LLC to "procure Shield vehicles" — the agency's armored-SUV platform used in protective-detail motorcades. The description does not specify which protective detail the vehicles are destined for. Award record.

Kaptivate LLC, a recipient with a limited public contracting footprint, was awarded $20,055,174.58 by the Department of Education for a "challenge" program meant to build partnerships among secondary and postsecondary schools, adult-education providers, employers, workforce agencies and community groups to improve student outcomes — a sizable award for a workforce-development competition. Award record.

KMC Solutions, LLC won a $19,968,353 construction contract from the Department of Veterans Affairs to expand gravesites at Dayton National Cemetery in Ohio while correcting deficiencies identified in a facility condition assessment. Award record.

The U.S. Marshals Service awarded Cynergy Professional Systems LLC $17,974,884.75 to supply APX Next radios and accessories for deputy marshals, piggybacking on the terms of a separate Department of Homeland Security/Immigration and Customs Enforcement enterprise radio blanket purchase agreement. Award record.

The Federal Bureau of Investigation ordered 25 Bearcat armored vehicles from Lenco Industries, Inc. for $11,054,649.50 — a notable volume purchase of the armored-vehicle model widely used by federal and local SWAT and tactical units. Award record.

OMB Apportionments

Smithsonian funds conditioned on Trump's history-museum executive order; SBA rescinds $33 million in prior-year loan balances

Three apportionment filings were flagged in this window: two Smithsonian Institution FY2027 apportionments conditioned on Executive Order 14253, and one Small Business Administration filing permanently rescinding $33 million in unobligated prior-year loan-program balances. No new or revised spend-plan PDFs were posted in the window.

Smithsonian Institution — two FY2027 apportionments, $120,287,937 and $69,460,720, are each restricted by an identical footnote: "To the extent permitted by law, amounts apportioned shall be available for obligation in a manner consistent with Executive Order 14253 (March 27, 2025), 'Restoring Truth and Sanity to American History.'" OMB's stated rationale is that the footnote specifies the purposes for which the funds may be obligated. Notice (first filing); Notice (second filing).

Small Business Administration — $33,030,391 in prior-year unobligated balances were permanently rescinded from the Business Loans Program Account. The footnote cites two authorities: Public Law 117-58 (the Infrastructure Investment and Jobs Act), which requires rescission of unobligated balances tied to a prior-year appropriations provision, and Public Law 117-328, which requires rescission of unobligated balances used for two paragraphs of Small Business Act loan guarantee authority tied to the CARES Act. The apportionment notes that any additional fund recoveries will also be rescinded. Notice.
