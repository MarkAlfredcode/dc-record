Kuwait Embassy sole-sources shipping over Strait of Hormuz risk; Army posts drone and barracks-OTA solicitations

The U.S. Embassy Kuwait justified a sole-source shipping contract by pointing to regional instability disrupting Strait of Hormuz shipping lanes — the only sole-source justification posted in this window. Of 62 solicitations posted on SAM.gov over the same two days, five stand out for their subject matter; the rest were routine equipment, vehicle, and facilities purchases.

Sole-source justification
  • U.S. Embassy Kuwait (U.S. Department of State) issued a limited-competition justification for outbound shipping services, citing regional instability that has made shipping through the Strait of Hormuz and other regional corridors unreliable and dangerous. The embassy solicited quotes from three vendors under simplified acquisition procedures and selected the lowest-priced technically acceptable offer, valued at $26,908, to keep mission shipping operations running without interruption. Justification.

The Federal Register

White House publishes procurement-reciprocity executive order; SEC proposes scrapping the shareholder-proposal rule

The Executive Office of the President published four presidential documents in today's window, including a new executive order touching on federal procurement. The Drug Enforcement Administration finalized a rule placing the synthetic drug diphenidine in Schedule I, and the U.S. Department of State revised its public-records-access regulations. Among eight pending proposed rules, the Securities and Exchange Commission proposed rescinding the decades-old Rule 14a-8 shareholder-proposal requirement and separately proposed to modernize proxy-solicitation rules, while the U.S. Coast Guard proposed roughly a 10 percent increase in Great Lakes pilotage rates for the 2027 shipping season.

Executive orders
  • Executive Office of the President published an executive order titled "Restoring Reciprocity in Government Procurement." The Federal Register's listing did not include abstract text as of this edition; the order's specific provisions have not been independently confirmed (see Top Story).
  • A second executive order, "Providing Meaningful Water Quality Improvements Through Collaboration and Oversight of Federal Support," was also published without abstract text.
Also published
  • Two ceremonial proclamations: National POW/MIA Recognition Day, 2026 and Constitution Day, Citizenship Day, and Constitution Week, 2026.
  • U.S. Coast Guard established a temporary safety zone on the Illinois River in Morris, Illinois, for a fireworks display on September 26, 2026, restricting vessel and personnel entry during the event. Rule.
  • Drug Enforcement Administration finalized a rule placing diphenidine, a synthetic dissociative substance, in Schedule I of the Controlled Substances Act, imposing the criminal and regulatory controls that come with that classification. Rule.
  • U.S. Department of State revised its regulations governing public access to department records, including provisions allowing requests to be submitted by email, updating rules last revised years earlier. Rule.
Proposed rules worth watching
  • Securities and Exchange Commission proposed rescinding Rule 14a-8, which requires companies to include shareholder proposals in their proxy materials, and would leave decisions about shareholder proposals to state law and corporate governing documents instead. A companion amendment to Rule 14a-4 would expand companies' discretion to vote proxies on matters not included in their proxy statements, while letting individual shareholders opt out of that discretion for their own shares. Proposed rule.
  • In a separate proposal, the Commission also proposed to eliminate the requirement that companies deliver an annual report to shareholders, drop certain delivery deadlines for proxy materials incorporated by reference, and shorten the broker-search period for proxy solicitations, billed as modernizing rules that predate current practice. Proposed rule.
  • U.S. Coast Guard proposed 2027 Great Lakes pilotage rates that it estimates would raise operating costs by about 10 percent compared with 2026, and for the first time proposed a rate for the Straits of Mackinac, newly subject to pilotage requirements under the National Defense Authorization Act for Fiscal Year 2026. Proposed rule.
