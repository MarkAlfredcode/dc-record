Top Story

White House orders agencies to restrict Canadian-origin goods and services from federal procurement

A Presidential Memorandum signed September 16 directs the Office of Management and Budget and the Office of the United States Trade Representative to find every lawful way to keep Canadian products out of federal civilian contracting, citing Canada's "Buy Canadian" policy.

The White House published a Presidential Memorandum titled "Restoring Reciprocity in Government Procurement" in the Federal Register on September 21, 2026; it was signed September 16, 2026. It is a memorandum, not an executive order — it carries no assigned executive order number — but it directs concrete action from multiple agencies on federal contracting policy, squarely inside this newsletter's beat.

The memorandum's stated rationale is that Canada has "unreasonably imposed new barriers" to U.S. companies through its "Buy Canadian" policy and that Canadian provinces restrict U.S. firms' access to their own procurement markets, even as Canadian companies retain preferential access to more than $280 billion in annual U.S. federal procurement under the World Trade Organization's Agreement on Government Procurement.

Section 2 directs the Director of the Office of Management and Budget and the Office of the United States Trade Representative to "identify and take all steps permitted by applicable law" to remove or restrict Canadian-origin products and services from federal civilian procurement, and to notify other executive departments and agencies of domestic alternatives. The memorandum also names the Secretary of War (the renamed Department of Defense), the Federal Procurement Policy Administrator, the General Services Administration, and the National Aeronautics and Space Administration as agencies expected to act, and calls for ongoing progress reports to the President.

The memorandum is explicit that it creates "no right or benefit, substantive or procedural, enforceable at law or in equity" and that agencies may act only "consistent with applicable law" — meaning existing procurement statutes and trade agreements still bound the actual implementation. No specific product list, dollar threshold, or effective date is set in the text itself; that detail would come from follow-on agency action, which this newsletter will watch for in coming editions.

The Federal Register

Same day as the procurement memorandum, the Securities and Exchange Commission proposed rescinding a 50-year-old shareholder-proposal rule and the Federal Aviation Administration proposed a new Boeing 737 inspection mandate

Beyond today's Top Story, the Federal Register carried an executive order rewriting Chesapeake Bay water-quality policy and two routine proclamations, plus three final rules and eight proposed rules — most of them minor, but two Securities and Exchange Commission proposals and an airworthiness directive stand out.

Presidential actions

(Presidential Memorandum) "Restoring Reciprocity in Government Procurement" — directs agencies to restrict Canadian-origin goods from federal procurement; see today's Top Story above. Federal Register.

(Executive Order 14428) "Providing Meaningful Water Quality Improvements Through Collaboration and Oversight of Federal Support" — revokes a 2009 Chesapeake Bay executive order and directs the Department of War, Department of the Interior, Department of Agriculture, Department of Commerce, Department of Homeland Security, and Environmental Protection Agency to redirect fiscal and personnel resources toward "more direct, on-the-ground" Chesapeake Bay pollution-reduction projects rather than indirect activities, with outcomes grounded in "objective metrics." It separately directs the Environmental Protection Agency to coordinate with seven Chesapeake Bay-region jurisdictions to assess local stormwater fees and encourage their repeal, arguing the 2009 order led localities to impose "rain taxes" without measurable benefit. The order reallocates existing agency funding rather than appropriating new money, but it will likely reshape how Chesapeake Bay-region water-quality grants and contracts are prioritized. Federal Register.

  • (Proclamation) "National POW/MIA Recognition Day, 2026." Federal Register.
  • (Proclamation) "Constitution Day, Citizenship Day, and Constitution Week, 2026." Federal Register.
Also published (final rules)
  • Drug Enforcement Administration (part of the Department of Justice) places the substance diphenidine, including its salts and isomers, into Schedule I of the Controlled Substances Act, imposing the full manufacturing, distribution, import/export, research, and possession restrictions that come with that classification; the agency says the move is needed in part to meet U.S. obligations under the 1971 Convention on Psychotropic Substances. Federal Register.
  • Department of State revises its regulations governing public access to information under the department's control, reflecting organizational and procedural changes since the last revision, including new provisions for submitting information requests by email. Federal Register.
  • U.S. Coast Guard establishes a temporary safety zone on the Illinois River in Morris, Illinois for a fireworks display on September 26, 2026; routine and local in scope. Federal Register.
Proposed rules worth flagging

Securities and Exchange Commission proposed two related deregulatory moves the same day: rescinding Rule 14a-8, which for decades has required public companies to include eligible shareholder proposals in their proxy materials, leaving the question to state law and each company's own governing documents; and separately, amending Rule 14a-4 to expand companies' discretionary voting authority over proposals raised at a shareholder meeting but not included in the proxy materials (while letting shareholders opt out of that authority for their own shares). A related notice proposes broader "proxy solicitation modernization," including eliminating the requirement to deliver annual reports to security holders and shortening the minimum broker-search period for proxy solicitations. Rule 14a-8 rescission, proxy modernization.

Federal Aviation Administration proposed a new airworthiness directive for The Boeing Company Model 737-8, 737-9, and 737-8200 airplanes after a report that elevator-buss-assembly bearings may have been installed incorrectly, including without required sealant, during production; the proposed directive would require detailed inspections of those bearings and housings and any needed on-condition repairs. Federal Register.

U.S. Coast Guard proposed 2027 Great Lakes pilotage rates, estimating the change would raise operating costs by roughly 10 percent compared with 2026, and for the first time proposed a rate for the Straits of Mackinac, newly subject to pilotage requirements under the fiscal year 2026 National Defense Authorization Act. Federal Register.

Four other proposed rules in this window were procedural or minor and are not detailed here: a Commodity Futures Trading Commission comment-period reopening on Privacy Act recordkeeping exemptions; an Environmental Protection Agency correction to a Superfund National Priorities List deletion notice; a National Highway Traffic Safety Administration denial of a citizen petition on mapping-database standards; and a Federal Aviation Administration correction to a Skyryse/Robinson R66 helicopter special-conditions notice.
