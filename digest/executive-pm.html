Top Story

Executive Office of the President Issues Five Actions Escalating Tariffs and Import Bans on Canadian Goods

Two proclamations modify existing duties on Canadian motor vehicles and alcoholic beverages, while three others exclude Canadian motor vehicle, dairy and alcoholic-beverage products from U.S. importation entirely.

The Executive Office of the President published five presidential documents in the Federal Register on September 14, 2026, all framed as responses to "continued discrimination against the commerce of the United States" by Canada. Two of the five "modify the scope" of an existing framework of additional duties — that is, tariffs — covering Canadian motor vehicles and Canadian alcoholic beverages, respectively. The other three go further, excluding certain Canadian products from importation into the United States altogether: one covering motor vehicles, one covering dairy, and one covering alcoholic beverages.

None of the five listings carries a Federal Register abstract (the abstract field is blank for all five), so the operative legal text — the specific tariff schedule changes, effective dates, and the underlying order being amended — is only available in the full text at the links below, not summarized here. The titles reference an existing framework of "additional duties imposed to offset Canadian discrimination," indicating these actions modify or extend tariffs that were already in place rather than creating an entirely new regime, though the originating order is not identified in today's filings.

Taken together, the five actions touch three of the most politically sensitive categories in U.S.-Canada trade — autos, dairy, and alcohol — on the same publication day, making this the most consequential document set in today's Federal Register.

OMB Apportionments

Veterans Affairs Reallocates $75 Million from Electric Vehicle Program Under New Executive Order

The federal government approved five apportionment actions totaling $6.7 billion in fiscal 2026 and 2027 funds, with four items subject to OMB oversight holds or executive order restrictions. The most significant change: $75 million previously allocated to the Department of Veterans Affairs' electric vehicle program will be redirected to minor construction work under the new "Unleashing American Energy" executive order.

Department of Veterans Affairs received $0 in apportionment for Category B Non-Recurring Maintenance under the newly approved FY2027 action, as the "Unleashing American Energy" executive order revoked Executive Order 14057 (which had authorized VA's electric vehicle initiative). The $75 million previously allocated to that program will instead be reallocated to Minor Construction. Apportionment.

Department of Veterans Affairs also received $4 billion in FY2027 apportionment for Total Budgetary Resources Available subject to an oversight hold: the agency must submit a detailed spend plan to OMB before obligating funds, and any deviation exceeding 5 percent from that plan requires OMB approval at least 5 business days in advance. Apportionment.

Department of Agriculture received $2.044 billion in FY2026 apportionment for Food for Peace Title II Grants subject to an oversight hold requiring monthly written reports to OMB on total commitments and obligations, broken down by population, crisis, or subcategory as appropriate. Apportionment.

Department of Education received $658.18 million in FY2027 apportionment for Budgetary Resources: Unappropriated Balance, Federal Fund, subject to conditions on automatic apportionment to guaranty agencies. Funds for item number 9 under 34 C.F.R. 682.419(c) are available only after OMB receives a detailed spend plan. Apportionment.

Also apportioned (routine, non-flagged): Department of State, 6; Department of Veterans Affairs, 39; Department of Agriculture, 1; Department of Education, 1; Department of Health and Human Services, 1; International Assistance Programs, 1.

The Federal Register

Executive Office of the President Issues Five Actions on Canadian Trade; FDIC-OCC-Federal Reserve Ease Bank Exam Cycle

Five presidential documents targeting Canadian motor vehicles, dairy and alcoholic beverages headline today's Federal Register (see Top Story). Six other final rules also published, along with eight proposed rules — mostly routine aviation airworthiness directives and regional EPA clean-air actions — of which one, an FAA inquiry into transporting wildland firefighters aboard restricted-category aircraft, stands out for potential policy significance.

Presidential actions on Canadian trade
  • Executive Office of the President — modifying the scope of products of Canada subject to additional duties, covering motor vehicles. Document.
  • Modifying the scope of products of Canada subject to additional duties, covering alcoholic beverages. Document.
  • Excluding certain Canadian motor vehicle products from importation into the United States. Document.
  • Excluding certain Canadian dairy products from importation into the United States. Document.
  • Excluding certain Canadian alcoholic-beverage products from importation into the United States. Document.
Also published
  • Federal Communications Commission — announced OMB approval, through November 30, 2027, of an information-collection requirement tied to its E-Rate program competitive-bidding rules for schools and libraries. Rule.
  • Federal Communications Commission — adopted its regulatory fee schedule for Fiscal Year 2026. Rule.
  • Federal Aviation Administration — revised airman-certification regulations and updated sport pilot practical test standards to align with the MOSAIC final rule on light-sport aircraft. Rule.
  • Office of the Comptroller of the Currency, Federal Reserve System and Federal Deposit Insurance Corporation — jointly issued an interim final rule implementing section 903 of the 21st Century ROAD to Housing Act, raising the asset threshold for banks with less than $6 billion in assets (and comparable foreign bank branches/agencies) to qualify for an extended 18-month, rather than annual, on-site examination cycle. Rule.
  • Environmental Protection Agency — approved a revision to California's San Joaquin Valley state implementation plan, updating motor-vehicle emissions budgets for nitrogen oxides and volatile organic compounds tied to the 2008 ozone air-quality standard. Rule.
  • U.S. Coast Guard — established a temporary 300-yard safety zone on Lake Erie near Oregon, Ohio, for an overwater drone display. Rule.
Proposed rule worth watching

Federal Aviation Administration issued an advance notice of proposed rulemaking on allowing firefighters to be transported to and from wildfire sites aboard certain restricted-category aircraft for ground wildfire-suppression work, responding to a statutory mandate; the agency says it needs more public input on firefighter-transport resources and cost-benefit tradeoffs before drafting a rule. Notice.

Seven other proposed rules were filed today and judged routine: four FAA airworthiness directives affecting Airbus, CFM International, Dassault Aviation and Bombardier aircraft/engines, plus three regional EPA Clean Air Act actions (negative declarations for solid-waste incinerators in the District of Columbia and Maryland, and a proposed clean-data determination for the Sacramento, California ozone nonattainment area).
