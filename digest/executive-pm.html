FAR Council Publishes First Wave of a 12-Rule Rewrite of the Federal Acquisition Regulation

Five proposed rules implementing Executive Order 14275, "Restoring Common Sense to Federal Procurement," touch nearly every major category of federal contracting.

The Federal Acquisition Regulatory Council — made up of the Office of Federal Procurement Policy (part of the Office of Management and Budget), the Department of Defense, the General Services Administration, and the National Aeronautics and Space Administration — published five proposed rules in the Federal Register on September 18, 2026, the first installment of what the council describes as a twelve-rule overhaul of the entire Federal Acquisition Regulation. The rulemaking implements Executive Order 14275, "Restoring Common Sense to Federal Procurement," which directs agencies to eliminate what the administration calls excessive acquisition regulations.

The five proposed rules published this week would rewrite FAR Parts 8, 12, 13, 15, 38, 44, and 51 (required sources of supply, commercial products and services, simplified acquisition, negotiated contracting, and federal supply schedules); Parts 16, 17, and 35 (contract types, special contracting methods, and research and development contracting); Parts 9, 27, and 47 (contractor qualifications, patents and data rights, and transportation); and Parts 14, 28, and 36 (sealed bidding, bonds and insurance, and construction and architect-engineer contracting) — along with conforming changes to Part 52's solicitation provisions and contract clauses throughout.

Because this is a proposed-rule package rather than a final rule, none of the changes are in effect yet; the Federal Register filings do not specify a public-comment deadline in the text extracted so far. The remaining seven rules in the twelve-rule package referenced in the council's own filings have not yet appeared in the Federal Register as of this window.

The Federal Register

FAR Council opens a sweeping procurement rulemaking; State Department loosens export controls on underwater drones.

Beyond the FAR overhaul covered above, the State Department finalized a rule removing certain uncrewed underwater vehicles from the U.S. Munitions List, and the Department of Homeland Security finalized new Buy-America-style restrictions on foreign-made personal protective equipment. Twelve other rules and presidential documents published in the window were routine (aviation directives, airspace corrections, and a dairy-pricing technical fix).

  • Executive Office of the President — Presidential Determination on Major Drug Transit or Major Illicit Drug Producing Countries for Fiscal Year 2027; no abstract text was available beyond the title. Determination.
  • State Department — final rule removes certain uncrewed underwater vehicles from Category XX(a) of the U.S. Munitions List under the International Traffic in Arms Regulations, easing export controls on those systems, and seeks further comment on related license exemptions. Final rule.

  • Federal Aviation Administration — two airworthiness directives for Rolls-Royce Deutschland Trent 768/772/772B and Trent 875/877/884/884B/892/892B/895 engines, requiring de-pairing of affected oil pumps after reports of low-oil-pressure events. First AD / Second AD.

  • Federal Aviation Administration — two miscellaneous-amendment rules updating Standard Instrument Approach Procedures and takeoff/obstacle-departure procedures at various airports nationwide. First rule / Second rule.
  • Federal Aviation Administration — amends Class E airspace at Alma, Michigan, following decommissioning of the Mount Pleasant VOR navigation beacon. Final rule.
