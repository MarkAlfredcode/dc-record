Executive Branch Edition
Top Story

EPA finalizes repeal of power-plant carbon rule, proposes going further and scrapping GHG regulation of fossil-fuel plants entirely

A final rule and a linked supplemental proposal published the same day aim to unwind carbon regulation of coal- and gas-fired power plants under the Clean Air Act.

The Environmental Protection Agency finalized a rule Thursday repealing most of the 2024 Carbon Pollution Standards that limited greenhouse gas emissions from fossil-fuel-fired electric generating units, citing a "reevaluation of the best system of emission reduction" for the affected units. The repeal eliminates the emission guidelines for existing fossil-fuel-fired steam generating units, drops the carbon capture and sequestration (CCS)-based standards for coal-fired units undertaking a large modification, and removes the CCS-based "Phase 2" standards that would have applied to new baseload gas-fired combustion turbines.

The EPA published that final rule alongside a supplemental proposed rule that goes considerably further: it proposes rescinding the agency's underlying 2015 findings and determinations that justify regulating power-plant greenhouse gases at all under Clean Air Act Section 111, arguing the statute does not authorize EPA to regulate power-plant emissions "in response to global climate change concerns." If finalized, that follow-on action would repeal all associated 2015 and 2024 greenhouse-gas standards for the fossil-fuel electric generating unit source category outright, not just the provisions addressed in Thursday's final rule.

The EPA said in the proposal that it is not reopening Thursday's final rule for further comment and is soliciting input only on the broader legal-authority question — meaning the agency has now split its deregulatory push into two tracks: a repeal that already took effect and a foundational-authority rescission still open for public comment.

OGE Ethics Filings

Four officials file ethics agreements and compliance certifications

Four senior executive appointees filed ethics documents this week, including two initial ethics agreements and two compliance certifications submitted after Senate confirmation.

  • Heidi Overton, nominee for Commissioner of the Food and Drug Administration at the Department of Health and Human Services, filed an ethics agreement committing to extensive conflict-of-interest safeguards reflecting her regulatory role. She will recuse from matters affecting her financial interests and will not acquire direct interests in entities on FDA's prohibited holdings list—a broad category encompassing pharmaceutical, biotech, medical device, food and beverage, tobacco, healthcare, insurance, media, technology, and energy companies. Ethics Agreement.
  • David MacNeil, nominee for Commissioner of the Federal Trade Commission, filed an ethics agreement reflecting his extensive business holdings. He will resign from his positions as CEO and manager of MNAPL Holdings LLC (operating as WeatherTech), but will retain ownership and receive only passive investment income from that entity and from more than two dozen other business entities including real estate, aviation, racing, and watercraft interests. He has committed to divest his interest in the Advenio Global Equity Fund LP (hedge fund) within 90 days of confirmation and will recuse himself from FTC matters directly affecting his retained interests. Ethics Agreement.
  • William Trachman, confirmed as Ambassador to the United Republic of Tanzania at the Department of State on August 7, 2026, filed his Certification of Ethics Agreement Compliance, confirming adherence to his previously signed ethics agreement. Certification.
  • Christopher Nassar, confirmed as U.S. Attorney for the Oklahoma Northern District at the Department of Justice on August 7, 2026, filed his Certification of Ethics Agreement Compliance, confirming adherence to his ethics agreement terms. Certification.
Also filed (routine, non-flagged documents): Department of Homeland Security, Department of Health and Human Services, Federal Trade Commission, Department of the Treasury, Department of Veterans Affairs, Department of Labor, National Aeronautics and Space Administration, and Department of Justice (1 filing each).
