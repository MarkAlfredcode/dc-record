Top Story

White House issues executive order on "Restoring Reciprocity in Government Procurement"

The order was published in the Federal Register on September 21, 2026, but the listing does not yet include the order's operative text.

The Executive Office of the President published an executive order titled "Restoring Reciprocity in Government Procurement" in the Federal Register on September 21, 2026 — a title that puts it squarely in this newsletter's lane, since it speaks directly to how the federal government awards contracts to foreign and domestic firms.

The Federal Register's listing for the order does not include abstract or summary text, which is common for presidential documents processed on an expedited basis; the specific procurement provisions the order directs are not yet described in the source record available for this edition. Readers should consult the order's full text at the link below for the operative details.

It was one of six presidential documents published over the September 21-22 window, alongside orders on saltwater angling and hunting-heritage policy and two ceremonial proclamations (see The Federal Register section below).

OGE Ethics Filings

Six ethics filings from Cabinet, diplomatic, and executive officials

Six senior executive officials filed ethics disclosures this week, including four ethics agreements from diplomatic and Cabinet appointees, the Attorney General's departure report, and the President's periodic transaction filing. The filings document conflict-of-interest undertakings, divestiture commitments, and recent investment activity.

  • Bondi, Pam (Attorney General, Department of Justice) — Termination report filed for position ending 04/01/2026. Career included prior roles at PB Advisory LLC (owner), Ballard Partners, the America First Policy Institute, and Panza, Maurer & Maynard, all of which ended by February 2025. Termination Report.
  • Watzlavick, Paul (Ambassador to the Republic of Suriname, Department of State) — Ethics agreement executed for diplomatic appointment. The filing establishes standard conflict-of-interest undertakings and recusal requirements, with specific attention to potential conflicts involving companies with presence in Suriname. Ethics Agreement.
  • Pitt, Asa (U.S. Representative to the Organization for the Prohibition of Chemical Weapons, Department of State) — Ethics agreement with ambassadorial rank. The filing documents standard ethics undertakings including financial conflict-of-interest prohibitions, recusal requirements, and managed investment account restrictions. Ethics Agreement.
  • Jones, Matthew (Assistant Secretary for Housing—Federal Housing Commissioner, Department of Housing and Urban Development) — Ethics agreement requiring divestiture of Finlocker, Inc. interests within 90 days of confirmation. The filing also documents his ownership of eight residential rental properties, which the Department determined do not constitute prohibited real estate-related business interests under agency supplemental standards of conduct regulations. Ethics Agreement.
  • Holmes, Austin (Ambassador to the Republic of Haiti, Department of State) — Comprehensive ethics agreement requiring multiple divestitures and resignations. Planned divestitures include equity interests in Holmes Equity, Inc., CMT Digital Opportunities Fund LLC, CMT Digital Opportunities Fund II LLC, and Ten VC (all within 90 days of confirmation); the agreement also requires dissolution of consulting firm Security Partners LLC and specifies that a family trust will divest Holmes Equity interests within 90 days. Ethics Agreement.
  • Trump, Donald J (President, White House Office) — Periodic transaction report (OGE Form 278-T) disclosing investment activity through July 2026. The filing documents purchases of municipal bonds from multiple states and counties and equity purchases including technology sector holdings. Periodic Transaction Report.
Also filed (routine, non-flagged): Department of State, 3; Department of Housing and Urban Development, 1; Department of Homeland Security, 1; Office of Director of National Intelligence, 1; Office of the U.S. Trade Representative, 1; International Trade Commission, 1.
The Federal Register

White House publishes six presidential documents, including a procurement-reciprocity order, alongside 10 routine final rules

The Federal Register carried six presidential documents over September 21–22, 2026 — three policy-oriented orders and two ceremonial proclamations — plus ten routine final rules spanning aviation, fisheries, banking, and drug-scheduling matters. Of 23 proposed rules also published in the window, a handful touch on federal procurement, bank mergers, and securities governance; the rest are routine and are summarized by count only.

Presidential documents
  • Restoring Reciprocity in Government Procurement — published September 21, 2026; see Top Story above. Federal Register.
  • Restoring American Saltwater Angling and Recreation — published September 22, 2026. No abstract text was available in the Federal Register listing; title suggests recreational-fishing policy. Federal Register.
  • Reinvigorating America's Hunting Heritage — published September 22, 2026. No abstract text was available; title suggests hunting-access policy. Federal Register.
  • Providing Meaningful Water Quality Improvements Through Collaboration and Oversight of Federal Support — published September 21, 2026. No abstract text was available; title suggests directives on federal water-quality program oversight. Federal Register.
  • National POW/MIA Recognition Day, 2026 — ceremonial proclamation, published September 21, 2026. Federal Register.
  • Constitution Day, Citizenship Day, and Constitution Week, 2026 — ceremonial proclamation, published September 21, 2026. Federal Register.
Also published: final rules
  • Federal Aviation Administration — establishes Class E airspace at OSF St Francis Medical Center Heliport in Ottawa, Illinois, to support new instrument flight procedures. Federal Register.
  • National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration — prohibits retention of "other rockfish" in the Aleutian Islands subarea after the 2026 catch limit was reached. Federal Register.
  • Agricultural Marketing Service — clarifies the assessment rate for imported softwood lumber and revises membership of the Softwood Lumber Board. Federal Register.
  • Food and Drug Administration — replaces "animal"/"preclinical" terminology with "nonclinical" throughout FDA regulations; the agency says the change adds no new requirements. Federal Register.
  • Federal Communications Commission — reinstates several vacant FM radio allotments after associated licenses were canceled, without yet opening a filing window. Federal Register.
  • Comptroller of the Currency and Federal Deposit Insurance Corporation — corrects an incorrect OCC docket number in a September 1, 2026 rule on "unsafe or unsound practices." Federal Register.
  • Federal Communications Commission — corrects a section-heading error in a 2024 radio non-duplication rule. Federal Register.
  • U.S. Coast Guard — establishes a temporary safety zone on the Illinois River in Morris, Illinois for a September 26, 2026 fireworks display. Federal Register.
  • Drug Enforcement Administration — places diphenidine, a dissociative substance, into Schedule I of the Controlled Substances Act. Federal Register.
  • Department of State — updates its public-records-access regulations, including allowing information requests to be submitted by email. Federal Register.
Notable proposed rules
  • General Services Administration — proposes moving Federal Supply Schedule ordering procedures out of the Federal Acquisition Regulation and into a new GSAR part 538, implementing Executive Order 14275. Federal Register.
  • Federal Deposit Insurance Corporation — proposes a broad overhaul of how it processes bank merger applications, including a new "deemed approval" fast track for de minimis deals and reforms to its competitive-effects analysis. Federal Register.
  • Federal Deposit Insurance Corporation — separately proposes recognizing regulatory parity between out-of-state state banks and national banks under host-state law. Federal Register.
  • Securities and Exchange Commission — proposes rescinding Rule 14a-8, which currently governs shareholder proposals, and would leave that role to state law and corporate governing documents; a companion proposal would expand companies' discretionary proxy-voting authority. Federal Register / proxy rule proposal.
  • Department of Energy — proposes revising hearing and appeals procedures for its Contractor Employee Protection Program, which covers retaliation claims by DOE contractor employees. Federal Register.
18 additional proposed rules were published in the window (airworthiness directives, fisheries and Coast Guard safety-zone actions, minor corrections, and a CFTC comment-period reopening) that did not meet the bar for individual write-ups.
