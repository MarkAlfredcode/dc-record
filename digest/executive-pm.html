Top Story

VA awards $93.7 million in hospital-infrastructure contracts in a single day

Six contracts issued September 14 cover elevator modernization at VA medical centers from New York to Puerto Rico plus a steam-system overhaul in Alaska.

The Department of Veterans Affairs issued six construction and repair contracts worth a combined $93,661,156 on September 14, all tied to aging hospital infrastructure. Five of the awards, worth $73,356,230 combined, cover elevator modernization projects at VA medical centers in Montrose, New York; Canandaigua, New York; North Little Rock, Arkansas; and San Juan, Puerto Rico. Elevated Technologies Services, LLC won three of those five (a combined $42,787,270), with TK Elevator Corporation taking the fourth-largest, a $13.1 million contract for the San Juan facility.

The sixth contract, $20,304,946 to Pipe Line Utility Contractors, LLC, funds an upgrade to a hospital's steam distribution system under project number 626A4-22-202. Taken together, the day's awards represent a concentrated push to replace decades-old mechanical systems across the VA's aging hospital stock — the kind of infrastructure spending that rarely draws attention item-by-item but adds up to nearly $94 million when it clears in a single batch.

None of the six disclosed a sole-source justification in the data reviewed; all appear to have been competitively awarded under existing VA construction vehicles.

USAspending Contracts

VA's $93.7 million infrastructure batch anchors an 11-award, $177 million window

Eleven new contract actions of $2 million or more cleared USAspending's public feed during the trailing 25-day sweep, worth a combined $177,215,033. Beyond the Department of Veterans Affairs infrastructure batch covered above as today's Top Story, the window includes a Federal Aviation Administration headquarters renovation, an NIH-funded diagnostics research award at Emory University, an SBA disaster-lending IT platform, a Social Security Administration hardware refresh, and a VA advertising task order tied to the PACT Act.

Globe Construction Company won a $20,072,086 contract from the Federal Aviation Administration for mechanical and electrical upgrades to the agency's own headquarters building, under work order 21-002533. The award is one of the largest single actions in this window and reflects continued capital investment in FAA-owned facilities rather than air-traffic infrastructure. No sole-source language appears in the description; the work is described simply as an office-building repair and alteration project.

Emory University received $18,769,273 from the National Institutes of Health for a "RADx Innovation Funnel for Health Technologies" validation center — a facility that tests and vets emerging diagnostic technologies under NIH's RADx rapid-diagnostics program, the initiative NIH stood up during the COVID-19 pandemic and has continued funding since. It is notable both for the size of the award to a university research center rather than a traditional contractor, and because it signals the RADx pipeline is still an active NIH funding priority years after its pandemic-era launch.

Summit Technology Consulting Group, LLC was awarded a $16,504,100 task order by the General Services Administration's Federal Acquisition Service, funding application-development labor for the Small Business Administration's disaster lending platform. The award comes through an existing GSA IT services vehicle and funds continued build-out of the system SBA uses to process disaster loans — infrastructure that draws renewed scrutiny each time a major disaster-declaration cycle hits.

Dell Federal Systems L.P. received $16,422,684.57 from the Social Security Administration to refresh data-center server hardware under the agency's existing Data Center Virtual Infrastructure server-hardware IDIQ contract. The description is a routine hardware refresh rather than a new initiative, but the dollar figure marks it as one of the larger IT-hardware buys in this window.

Powell Strategies, LLC won an $11,755,604.51 task order from the Department of Veterans Affairs for advertising and marketing-management support covering the agency's FY26 fall/winter campaigns and outreach tied to the PACT Act, the 2022 law expanding VA benefits for veterans exposed to toxic substances. Government-funded advertising contracts of this size are worth flagging on their own, and the explicit PACT Act tie-in connects the spending to VA's ongoing effort to drive veterans toward benefits enrollment.

The Federal Register

Treasury sets a hearing on tying private schools' tax-exempt status to nondiscrimination policy as CFTC finalizes its whistleblower-award overhaul

The Federal Register published 20 final rules and Presidential documents and 10 proposed rules on September 15-16. The two Presidential documents continue a post-9/11 terrorism-related national emergency and mark the 25th anniversary of the September 11 attacks; among the final rules, the Commodity Futures Trading Commission overhauled its whistleblower-award process and the Labor Department finalized a rule requiring states to share confidential unemployment data with federal oversight officials. On the proposed-rule side, the Internal Revenue Service scheduled a hearing on a proposal that would strip tax-exempt status from private schools that discriminate on the basis of race, color, or national or ethnic origin.

Presidential Documents

The Executive Office of the President continued the national emergency "With Respect to Persons Who Commit, Threaten To Commit, or Support Terrorism," the underlying authority for a range of terrorism-related sanctions and asset-blocking programs, keeping it in force beyond its anniversary date. Notice.

A separate proclamation designated September 11, 2026 as Patriot Day, marking the 25th anniversary of the September 11 terrorist attacks. Proclamation.
