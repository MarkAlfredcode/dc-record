Executive Branch Edition
Top Story

White House issues companion executive orders tightening H-1B visa program oversight and worker entry

Two presidential actions published September 23 target H-1B administration and nonimmigrant worker entry, though the Federal Register has not yet posted summary text for either.

The Executive Office of the President published two executive orders on September 23, 2026: "Enhancing Program Integrity and Interagency Coordination in the Administration of the H-1B Nonimmigrant Visa Program" and "Restriction on Entry of Certain Nonimmigrant Workers." Both appear in the same day's Federal Register and, by their titles, target enforcement of and entry into the H-1B skilled-worker visa system.

Neither order was published with a summary abstract in the Federal Register's public feed — common for presidential documents, whose full text sometimes trails the initial listing — so this edition cannot yet confirm specific provisions such as fee changes, which agencies gain new coordination roles, or which visa categories or classes of workers are restricted. Agencies that jointly administer H-1B visas — U.S. Citizenship and Immigration Services, the U.S. Department of Labor, and the U.S. Department of State — are the most likely to be assigned new roles once the full text is available.

The orders capped a busier-than-usual week of presidential action: two additional executive orders on outdoor recreation policy were published September 22 (see the Federal Register section below), and the U.S. Department of Health and Human Services issued an interim final rule the same day pausing new insurance-broker registrations on the ACA exchanges.

The Federal Register

Two H-1B executive orders headline a heavy Federal Register week; HHS pauses ACA broker registrations

The Federal Register published four executive orders and 22 total final/presidential documents this week, headlined by two same-day orders overhauling H-1B nonimmigrant visa administration and entry rules (see this edition's Top Story). The Department of Health and Human Services also issued an interim final rule immediately pausing new agent and broker registrations on the ACA exchanges, and 20 proposed rules were filed, including a General Services Administration plan to move Federal Supply Schedule ordering procedures out of the Federal Acquisition Regulation under a named executive order.

Also published this week

The U.S. Department of Health and Human Services and the Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services issued an interim final rule immediately pausing new agent and broker registrations on the federally-facilitated ACA exchanges for brokers who did not already hold a Plan Year 2026 agreement with CMS. The agencies invoked the Administrative Procedure Act's "good cause" exception to skip advance notice-and-comment, citing fraud and consumer-data-misuse concerns tied to unauthorized enrollment activity; the pause runs through February 1, 2027, and does not affect state-based exchanges. Rule.

Notable proposed rules
  • Drug Enforcement Administration proposes classifying five synthetic tryptamine hallucinogens — 4-OH-DiPT, 5-MeO-AMT, 5-MeO-MiPT, 5-MeO-DET, and DiPT — as Schedule I controlled substances, making their manufacture, distribution, or possession a federal crime. Proposed rule.
  • General Services Administration proposes moving Federal Supply Schedule ordering procedures out of the Federal Acquisition Regulation and into its own acquisition regulation, implementing Executive Order 14275 — a change that would alter how federal buyers place orders against GSA's multiple-award schedule contracts. Proposed rule.
  • Federal Deposit Insurance Corporation proposes a significant overhaul of its bank-merger review process, including a faster "deemed approval" path for small deals, a competitive-effects analysis that would account for credit unions, and filing requirements tailored to a merger's size and risk. Proposed rule.
  • FDIC also proposes giving out-of-state, state-chartered banks the same treatment as national banks when a host state's laws would not otherwise apply to a national bank — extending a form of national-bank preemption to state banks operating across state lines. Proposed rule.
  • U.S. Department of Energy proposes updated procedures for resolving whistleblower-retaliation complaints filed by its contractor employees, including clarified deadlines and updated references to DOE offices. Proposed rule.
