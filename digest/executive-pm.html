Top Story

White House escalates Canada trade dispute with five new tariff and import-ban proclamations

Documents set for Wednesday's Federal Register modify existing duties on Canadian motor vehicles and alcohol, and add new import exclusions covering motor vehicles, dairy, and alcohol.

Five documents from the Executive Office of the President are queued for publication in the September 14, 2026 Federal Register, all citing "continued discrimination against the commerce of the United States" by Canada. Two of the five modify the scope of products already covered by an existing punitive tariff regime — one for motor vehicles, one for alcoholic beverages — meaning the administration is changing which specific Canadian products fall under duties that were already in place, not creating a new tariff track from scratch.

The other three go further, excluding specific categories of Canadian products from importation into the United States entirely: one covering motor vehicles, one covering dairy, and one covering alcoholic beverages. Taken together, the five actions point to an intensifying dispute across multiple sectors of U.S.-Canada trade rather than a one-off adjustment.

The Federal Register's own metadata for all five documents lists no abstract, so the specific legal authority invoked and the precise mechanics of the modified duties and new import exclusions are not detailed here — only the fact and subject matter of the five filings, which is independently confirmed by the Federal Register listing itself.
