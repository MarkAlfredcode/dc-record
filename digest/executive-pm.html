Top Story

FAR Council Proposes Sweeping Overhaul of Federal Acquisition Regulation Under Trump Procurement Order

Four coordinated proposed rules published this week would rewrite 17 parts of the FAR, from commercial-item buying to construction contracting, as part of a planned twelve-rule package.

The Federal Acquisition Regulatory Council — made up of the Office of Federal Procurement Policy (housed in the Office of Management and Budget), the Department of Defense, the General Services Administration, and the National Aeronautics and Space Administration — published four proposed rules this week implementing Executive Order 14275, "Restoring Common Sense to Federal Procurement." The order directs agencies to eliminate what the administration calls excessive acquisition regulations, and the four proposals are the first wave of a planned twelve-rule package that the council says will collectively rewrite the Federal Acquisition Regulation in its entirety.

Together, the four rules touch 17 FAR parts. One would amend parts 8, 12, 13, 15, 38, 44, and 51. A second explicitly targets Part 16 (Types of Contracts), Part 17 (Special Contracting Methods), and Part 35 (Research and Development Contracting). A third covers parts 9, 27, and 47, and a fourth covers parts 14, 28, 36, and 52. None of the four proposals has been finalized — each is open for public comment, meaning contractors, industry groups, and agencies now have a window to weigh in before any of the changes take effect.

The procurement overhaul lands the same week the Environmental Protection Agency finalized a repeal of most 2024 power-plant carbon standards and proposed rescinding the underlying legal findings altogether — both actions covered in the Federal Register section below — underscoring a broader push this window to unwind Biden-era regulation across agencies.

The Federal Register

FAR Council Opens Comment on Procurement Overhaul as EPA Finalizes Repeal of Power-Plant Carbon Rules

The Federal Register carried 21 final rules and presidential documents and 13 proposed rules over the past two days. Beyond the FAR overhaul detailed in today's Top Story, the window included a final EPA repeal of most 2024 power-plant carbon standards, two State Department rules easing export controls on undersea drones and expanding major non-NATO ally status, and a Homeland Security rule on domestic PPE manufacturing.

Executive Office of the President — The White House published its annual Presidential Determination on Major Drug Transit or Major Illicit Drug Producing Countries for Fiscal Year 2027. No abstract text accompanied the notice; the determination is a statutorily required annual designation with implications for foreign aid and other country-specific restrictions. Notice.

Environmental Protection Agency — EPA finalized a rule repealing most provisions of the 2024 Carbon Pollution Standards for fossil fuel-fired power plants, eliminating emission guidelines for existing coal- and gas-fired steam generating units along with the carbon-capture-based standards for large modifications and new baseload turbines. Rule.

State Department — The department finalized a rule removing certain uncrewed underwater vehicles from the U.S. Munitions List under the International Traffic in Arms Regulations and made conforming changes to related export controls, while seeking further comment on refining UUV controls and possible license-exemption enhancements. Rule.

State Department — In a separate ITAR rule, the department clarified policy-of-denial provisions, updated country-specific export policies for Ethiopia and Somalia, and added Saudi Arabia and Peru to the list of major non-NATO allies, a status that can ease U.S. arms sales and defense-cooperation restrictions for those countries. Rule.

Department of Homeland Security — DHS finalized a Homeland Security Acquisition Regulation change (HSAR Case 2024-003) codifying how the department complies with the Make Personal Protective Equipment in America Act, intended to sustain and expand domestic PPE manufacturing capacity for public health emergencies. Rule.

OGE Ethics Filings

Nine Officials File Ethics Agreements and Divestitures, Including FDA and FTC Commissioners

Nine ethics filings were processed this window involving presidential appointees and nominees, including senior executive positions at the Food and Drug Administration and Federal Trade Commission. The filings include ethics agreements outlining conflict avoidance commitments and certificates of divestiture for financial holdings. Four additional filings indicate divestiture certificates without detailed documentation available.

  • Jeremy Wiggins, Assistant Secretary for International Trade & Development, Department of the Treasury — Filed an Ethics Agreement containing standard conflict of interest recusal obligations. The agreement requires prior approval for any managed account investments outside of cash, cash equivalents, diversified mutual funds, and U.S. obligations or municipal bonds, and commits to an ethics briefing within 15 days of confirmation.
  • Heidi Overton, Commissioner, Food and Drug Administration, Department of Health and Human Services — Filed an Ethics Agreement that lists prohibited holdings in 13 industries and sectors including pharmaceuticals, biotechnology, medical devices, food and beverage, tobacco, healthcare management, insurance, communications, computer technology, research organizations, energy, and utilities. The agreement requires managed account oversight and ethics briefing within 15 days of confirmation.
  • David MacNeil, Commissioner, Federal Trade Commission — Filed an Ethics Agreement detailing his role as sole owner and CEO of MNAPL Holdings LLC (parent of WeatherTech Direct LLC). He will resign from executive positions but retain passive investment income, will receive a pro-rated bonus before assuming federal duties, and commits to divest his interest in Advenio Global Equity Fund LP within 90 days of confirmation. The agreement also covers his interest in multiple real estate, aviation, and other holding entities retained for passive income only.
  • William Trachman, Ambassador to the United Republic of Tanzania, Department of State — Filed a Certification of Ethics Agreement Compliance confirming all required resignations were completed before assuming duties and all divestitures were completed or marked N/A. He completed his initial ethics briefing.
  • Christopher Nassar, U.S. Attorney, Oklahoma Northern District, Department of Justice — Filed a Certification of Ethics Agreement Compliance confirming completion of all required resignations before assuming duties and all divestitures as specified. He completed his initial ethics briefing.
  • Linda Thai, Department of Health and Human Services — Certificate of Divestiture OGE-2026-150 (Request this Document). Only the fact of the divestiture is confirmed; the specific assets are not disclosed.
  • Karen Sessions, Commissioner, Consumer Product Safety Commission — Certificates of Divestiture OGE-2026-145 & 146 (Request this Document). Only the fact of the divestitures is confirmed; the specific assets are not disclosed.
  • David Foley, Commissioner, International Trade Commission — Certificate of Divestiture OGE-2026-152 (Request this Document). Only the fact of the divestiture is confirmed; the specific assets are not disclosed.
