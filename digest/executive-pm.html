Top Story

Justice Department awards $158 million contract for "international asset recovery" legal services

The standalone, non-competed-looking award to a company called V2X Systems LLC is the single largest and most unusual new contracting action in today's data.

The Department of Justice, through its Offices, Boards and Divisions component, awarded a new $158,000,000 contract to V2X Systems LLC on September 23. USAspending.gov categorizes the work under "all other legal services," with the department's own product/service code describing it as professional legal support; the department's brief description for the award reads only "international asset recovery."

Two things make this award stand out. First, the size: $158 million is an unusually large sum for a professional legal-services procurement, a category more typically associated with smaller task orders. Second, the contract identifier shows no parent indefinite-delivery vehicle behind it — unlike some of today's other new awards, this one was not issued as an order under an existing multi-year contract, meaning it was made as its own standalone action.

The public USAspending record does not include a longer scope-of-work narrative, a competition-type code, or the name of a program office within "Offices, Boards and Divisions," so it is not possible from this data alone to say what specific asset-recovery matter the contract supports or whether it was competed. The underlying award should be checked directly via the link below for procurement-history and competition details before further reporting.

USAspending Contracts

Justice Department's $158 million 'asset recovery' legal-services award headlines a light day of new contracts

USAspending.gov surfaced only three new-to-report awards of $2 million or more in this sweep, and a second page of results failed to load because of an API timeout — so today's list should be read as a partial, not complete, picture of the period's contract actions.

None of the three ties to the White House or the Executive Office of the President. All three are written up individually below.

V2X Systems LLC won a new $158,000,000 contract from the Department of Justice's Offices, Boards and Divisions component on September 23, categorized as professional legal services and described only as "international asset recovery." The award carries no reference to an existing contract vehicle, meaning it was issued as its own standalone action rather than an order against a prior multi-year contract — unusual for a procurement this size with so little public description. See the Top Story above for further detail.

TriWest Healthcare Alliance Corp received a $1,200,153,022.86 obligation from the Department of Veterans Affairs on September 23, coded under government health insurance programs. The award's own description reads simply "Express Report: August 2026," and its contract number references an existing indefinite-delivery vehicle (36C10G19D0038) dating to 2019 — indicating this is a recurring funding action under a long-running VA health-benefits contract rather than a newly competed award, despite the headline-grabbing dollar figure.

Workwear Outfitters, LLC was awarded $55,999,961.50 by the Transportation Security Administration on September 23 for Transportation Security Officer uniforms, TSO Honor Guard uniforms, and Federal Security Director jackets. Like the TriWest award, this one references an existing IDIQ contract (70B01C20D00000004) awarded in 2020, indicating it is an order under a standing uniform-supply contract rather than a new competition.

The Federal Register

Treasury unwinds a Syria sanctions carve-out as NRC presses ahead with Trump-ordered regulatory overhaul

No executive orders or other presidential documents published in this window; the day's substantive final rules were led by the Department of the Treasury's Office of Foreign Assets Control formally removing a Syria-specific general license from the Terrorism List Governments Sanctions Regulations, now that Syria's designation as a State Sponsor of Terrorism has been rescinded, and by a new Commerce Department rule implementing Presidential Proclamation 11052 to restrict stockpiling of polysilicon and its derivatives ahead of December tariff-style import adjustments.

Among proposed rules — a much larger, unfiltered list this period — the most policy-significant is the Nuclear Regulatory Commission's wholesale review of reactor licensing and decommissioning rules, explicitly tied to Executive Order 14300 ordering NRC reform.

Final rules published September 24-25
  • U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service — correction to the 2026-2027 station-specific hunting and sport fishing regulations across 111 refuge and hatchery units. Rule.
  • Federal Aviation Administration — miscellaneous amendments to IFR (instrument flight rules) altitudes and route changeover points. Rule.
  • U.S. Postal Service — interim final rule partially rescinding and updating its National Environmental Policy Act procedures, following the rescission of the underlying Council on Environmental Quality regulations. Rule.
  • Farm Service Agency — correction to a September 4 final rule implementing its Application Fast Track loan-processing changes; no substantive changes. Rule.
  • Bureau of Alcohol, Tobacco, Firearms, and Explosives — new requirement for annual (not just initial) notices to local fire authorities on explosive-materials storage. Rule.
  • Alcohol, Tobacco, Firearms, and Explosives Bureau — finalizes two 2003 interim final rules implementing the Safe Explosives Act, ending their interim status and responding to decades-old public comments. Rule.
  • U.S. Department of Transportation — finalizes removal of race- and sex-based presumptions from its Disadvantaged Business Enterprise and Airport Concession DBE programs, completing a transition begun in an October 2025 interim rule. Rule.
  • National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration — closes the General category Atlantic bluefin tuna fishery for the rest of September 2026, reopening automatically October 1. Rule.
  • Office of Foreign Assets Control — adds new Sanctions Penalties Regulations consolidating penalty information across multiple existing OFAC sanctions programs. Rule.
  • Environmental Protection Agency — clarifies the scope of "applicable requirements" reviewed under Clean Air Act Title V operating permits, including a carve-out for accidental-release prevention duties. Rule.
  • Environmental Protection Agency — approves Kentucky's second 10-year maintenance plan for the Campbell-Clermont area under the 2010 sulfur dioxide air-quality standard. Rule.
  • Internal Revenue Service — raises the user fee for an estate tax closing letter from $56 to $76. Rule.
  • Office of Foreign Assets Control — removes a Syria-specific general license from the Terrorism List Governments Sanctions Regulations following the rescission of Syria's State Sponsor of Terrorism designation. Rule.
  • Food and Drug Administration — permits vitamin D2 inactive bakers yeast as a food additive source of vitamin D2, in response to a petition from Lallemand Inc. Rule.
  • U.S. Coast Guard — temporary special local regulation restricting vessel traffic on Milwaukee's rivers during an October 3 river-challenge event. Rule.
  • U.S. Coast Guard — new emergency-familiarization and crowd-management training requirements for crew on U.S.-flagged passenger ships carrying more than 12 passengers internationally. Rule.
  • Federal Trade Commission — eliminates the agency's post-employment ("revolving door") clearance rule for former staff. Rule.
  • Federal Trade Commission — separately updates its rules of practice on Second Request procedures, administrative law judge assignments, FOIA submission methods, quorum calculations, and commissioner-disqualification motions. Rule.
  • Environmental Protection Agency — approves an Alabama transportation-conformity state implementation plan update. Rule.
  • Bureau of Industry and Security — temporary final rule implementing Presidential Proclamation 11052, setting the criteria and process for monitoring and restricting companies stockpiling polysilicon ahead of December 4 import adjustments. Rule.
Proposed rules worth flagging
  • Nuclear Regulatory Commission — wholesale proposed revision of reactor licensing, decommissioning, and operational-oversight rules, explicitly tied to Executive Order 14300, "Ordering the Reform of the Nuclear Regulatory Commission." Proposed Rule.
  • Small Business Administration — extends the comment period on its proposed overhaul of small-business size-standards methodology by 60 days, to November 20. Proposed Rule.
  • Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services — request for information on "reasonable and relevant" pharmacy contracting standards for Medicare Part D, required by the Consolidated Appropriations Act, 2026 ahead of a 2029 effective date. Proposed Rule.
  • Department of Justice — formally declines to initiate rulemaking in response to 10 outside petitions requesting it do so. Proposed Rule.
  • Consumer Product Safety Commission — proposes designating a range of custom window-covering cord and loop configurations as substantial product hazards over strangulation risk. Proposed Rule.
  • Animal and Plant Health Inspection Service — proposes cutting the required highly-pathogenic-avian-influenza-free waiting period for live bird imports from 90 days to 28 days. Proposed Rule.
Also proposed (routine corrections, extensions, and airworthiness/technical actions, not detailed here): 9 additional proposed rules, including FAA airspace and pilot-oxygen-mask changes, two ATF explosives-storage streamlining proposals, an FCC broadcast correction, an EPA Kentucky air-plan companion proposal, an FDA color-additive petition filing, and a Bombardier airworthiness directive revision.
