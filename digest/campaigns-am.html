Top Story
The Existentialist Republic PAC tells the FEC its former treasurer repaid $7,438.74 he moved into an unauthorized account
The committee says it recovered every dollar, refunded all contributors and has asked the FEC to approve its termination.

In a Form 99 received October 31, The Existentialist Republic PAC responds to FEC requests for additional information dated October 25, 2026, about its July Quarterly Report and its Termination Report. It says the three receipts on Schedule A, Line 17 of the July Quarterly Report are the repayment by former treasurer Clifford Michael Pickens of $7,438.74 in committee funds that he deposited into an unauthorized account, plus $61.26 reported as reimbursement of filing fees.

According to the filing, the committee reported the misappropriation to the FEC on a Form 99 on June 2, 2026 (FEC-1981008). Pickens repaid $3,500.00 on June 9 and $3,938.74 plus the $61.26 on June 15. The eight unauthorized disbursements are itemized to him on Schedule B, Line 29: $4,593.82 on the amended April Quarterly Report (FEC-2015104) and $2,844.92 on the July Quarterly Report (FEC-2015296). The amended April report carries the $4,593.82 as a debt owed to the committee on Schedule D, and the July report shows it repaid.

The committee says it has refunded all $8,648.00 in contributions through ActBlue, as reported on its Termination Report (FEC-2021762), and holds no cash and no debts. It says it has found no error on the July Quarterly Report or earlier reports that needs amending, and it asks the FEC to approve its termination. Christopher Armitage signed as treasurer. These are the committee's own statements, and the filing does not say whether any other agency has been told. The filing was received October 31, so there is no indexing lag.

Requests for Additional Information
Ten candidate Form 2 letters dominate a mostly routine batch of 15 RFAIs

All 15 letters are dated October 30, 2026, and carry a December 4 response deadline. Ten go to House and presidential candidates and concern basic errors on their FEC Form 2 statements of candidacy: a wrong or missing year of election, or a missing or non-compliant principal campaign committee name. The other five go to independent-expenditure PACs, a corporation filing electioneering notices and a multicandidate PAC. They cover late 24-hour and Form 9 reports, missing contributor itemization, missing support/oppose information and possible excessive contributions.

Candidates: year of election on Form 2 (candidate committees are not involved in these letters; the problems are on the candidates' own statements of candidacy)

  • FEC asks House candidate Tynesha Marie Rice (D-NY) to correct the year of election on her Form 2. The letter says the year provided appears to be incorrect and requests an amended Form 2. (RFAI)
  • FEC asks presidential candidate Derrick Michael Reid (Libertarian) to supply the year of election missing from his Form 2. The letter asks for an amended Form 2 that includes it. (RFAI)
  • FEC asks presidential candidate Shawniquea Kmoneytmt R Mayweather Jr. (independent) to correct the year of election on her Form 2. The letter's text is the same year-of-election item sent to Tynesha Marie Rice. (RFAI)
  • FEC asks presidential candidate Lois Willow Allen (Republican) to correct the year of election on her Form 2. The letter's text is the same year-of-election item sent to Tynesha Marie Rice. (RFAI)

Candidates: principal campaign committee name or address on Form 2

  • FEC asks presidential candidate Jared Charles Martin to provide the name and address of his principal campaign committee. The letter says neither was given on his Form 2 and asks for an amendment. (RFAI)
  • FEC asks presidential candidate Nicole Renee Rising (Republican) to rename her principal campaign committee to include her own name. Commission rules (11 CFR § 102.14(a)) require the committee name to include the name of the authorizing candidate. (RFAI)
  • FEC asks presidential candidate Scott Conroy Palmer (independent) to provide the name and address of his principal campaign committee. The letter's text is the same missing-committee item sent to Jared Charles Martin. (RFAI)

Candidates: both problems in one letter

  • FEC asks presidential candidate Cortay Johnson (Republican) to fix both the year of election and his committee's name on his Form 2. The letter says the year appears incorrect and that the principal campaign committee name does not include his name. (RFAI)
  • FEC asks presidential candidate Jeff Allen Tank Geddes Sr. to fix both the year of election and his committee's name on his Form 2. The two items match those sent to Cortay Johnson: an apparently incorrect election year and a committee name that omits the candidate's name. (RFAI)
  • FEC asks presidential candidate Daniel White (independent) to supply both the year of election and his principal campaign committee's name and address. The letter says neither was provided on his Form 2. (RFAI)

PACs and party committees

  • FEC asks the 32BJ United American Dream Fund to explain why its 24-hour independent expenditure reports appear to have been filed late. The July Quarterly letter also says the Form 5 shows independent expenditures on Line 7 and contributions on Line 6 but lacks the Schedule 5-A memo text identifying donors of more than $200 who gave to further independent expenditures. (RFAI)
  • FEC asks the Community Water Center Action Fund why its July Quarterly Form 5 reports independent expenditures but no contributions. Line 7 shows independent expenditure disbursements and Line 6 is blank, so the letter asks for Schedule 5-A itemization of donors above $200 or a clarification. (RFAI)
  • FEC asks Stand Up for Ohio (Ohio Organizing Campaign) to say whether its independent expenditures supported or opposed each federal candidate. The letter says its 48-hour report, received October 11, 2026, is missing that detail on Schedule 5-E. The letter's reference line gives the report period as 10/09/2026 to 11/03/2026. (RFAI)
  • FEC says USPB JV LLC filed a Form 9 electioneering notice late and left out the federal candidates it named. The agency asks the company to explain the untimely disclosure and to add the name, state, office and district of each candidate clearly identified. The letter says the Form 9 was received October 16, though its reference line says October 15. (RFAI)
  • FEC asks the Women's Political Committee Federal about contributions on its September Monthly report that appear to exceed the $5,000-per-election limit. The letter asks treasurer Stephen J. Kaufman to give the date and method of any refund or redesignation. (RFAI)
