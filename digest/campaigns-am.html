Top Story
PAC blames dead “primary decision maker” and fired treasurer for a wave of 2024 reporting errors
Get Out the Vote PAC told the FEC it is still untangling how its 2024 filings went wrong more than a year after the fact.

Get Out the Vote PAC, a Washington, D.C.-based political action committee, filed a lengthy Form 99 letter with the Federal Election Commission explaining a batch of amendments to its 2024 October Quarterly and Post-General reports — and the explanation points to a chain of compliance failures rather than a single fix. The committee says it discovered in July 2025 that its then-treasurer had made “significant errors” on reports filed throughout the 2024 cycle “despite procedures that were in place to ensure accurate reporting,” and fired that treasurer as a result.

Untangling the damage was complicated further, the committee wrote, because “the Committee’s primary decision maker during the 2024 election cycle passed away suddenly in May of 2025.” With neither the former treasurer nor that decision maker available, the committee says it could not determine exactly where receipts had gone wrong and instead reconstructed its receipts from bank records. It separately disclosed that independent-expenditure spending had been incorrectly reported as ordinary operating expenditures, and that a handful of 2024 in-kind expenditures were also misclassified as operating expenses.

The letter, filed September 11 by treasurer Cassie Tinsmon, does not disclose a dollar total for the corrections. It follows amendments the committee has already filed to the 2024 reports in question.

Six new Form 99 filings, none of them terminations

The FEC's filings API surfaced 39 Form 99 submissions with a receipt date on or after September 9; 33 were already reported in prior editions (including a batch of routine committee terminations).   • Get Out the Vote PAC (Washington, D.C., committee ID C00839407) filed a detailed letter explaining amendments to its 2024 October Quarterly and Post-General reports, citing a fired treasurer's errors and the sudden death of the committee's "primary decision maker" in May 2025 — see this edition's Top Story. Filing.
  • United Food and Commercial Workers International Union Local 400 PAC responded to an RFAI questioning whether a $5,000 contribution to Walkinshaw For Congress on June 24, 2026 exceeded federal contribution limits for the 2026 general election. Treasurer Christopher Hoffmann wrote that the committee had misdesignated three earlier contributions to the same campaign — $5,000 on 9/3/2025, $1,000 on 11/19/2025, and $4,000 on 3/11/2026 — to the wrong elections (primary vs. general vs. a 2025 special general), and has amended its 2025 Year-End and 2026 April Quarterly reports to correct the designations, which the PAC says brings the June 24 contribution back within federal limits. Filing.
  • David Trone for Maryland, Inc., the campaign committee of former Rep. David Trone (D), responded to an RFAI about contributions received for the 2026 general election, saying they were refunded within 60 days of the primary and will show up on the committee's October Quarterly report. Trone is filing as a House challenger this cycle, not an incumbent. Filing.
  • Crisanta Duran Public Service Exploratory Committee responded to an RFAI about residual cash on hand from its April and July Quarterly reports, saying it intends to keep the funds on hand for exploratory activity or a potential future candidacy by Crisanta Duran, a Colorado Democrat and former state house speaker, who is filed as a House challenger. Filing.
  • Mike Bloomberg 2020, Inc., the defunct 2020 presidential campaign committee of Michael Bloomberg, responded to an RFAI dated September 2 regarding its April and July Quarterly reports, saying it remains an active committee that will terminate once it no longer anticipates further disbursements and that cash on hand will not be put to personal or other prohibited use. Treasurer Hayden Horowitz did not address the RFAI's specific line items. Filing.
  • Faith In Policy Super PAC filed a one-page notice stating it has not exceeded the $1,000 registration threshold and has had no receipts, disbursements, debts, or other financial activity since voluntarily registering. Filing.
On the Record
House marks 9/11 anniversary with no votes, then adjourns; 35 bills and resolutions introduced

The House of Representatives convened September 10 under Speaker pro tempore Rep. Tim Walberg and, without any yea-and-nay or recorded votes, agreed by unanimous consent to H. Res. 1520, expressing the House's sense on the 25th anniversary of the September 11, 2001 terrorist attacks. The chamber then adjourned at 3:33 p.m. “as a further mark of respect to the memory of the victims.” The Senate met only in pro forma session, gaveling in and out in 30 seconds before standing adjourned until Monday, September 14.

Members introduced 25 public bills (H.R. 10326–10350) and 10 resolutions (H. Res. 1520–1529) on the floor that day. Beyond the 9/11 commemorations, notable introductions include Rep. Christopher H. Smith's bill to block goods made with child or forced cobalt labor from the Democratic Republic of the Congo from entering the U.S. market, and a bipartisan bill from Reps. Kean, Dean, Keating, and Lawler to award a Congressional Gold Medal to the Naval SEAL unit that killed Osama bin Laden. The full list follows below with no omissions.
