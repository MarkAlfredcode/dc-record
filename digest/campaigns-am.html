FEC Questions $510,000 in Apparent Excessive Contributions and PAC-to-PAC Transfers by SD PAC
A Request for Additional Information flags a $100,000 individual contribution and $410,000 in transfers to three other committees as apparently exceeding federal limits — unless the recipient PACs turn out to be legally affiliated.

SD PAC, a Washington, D.C.-based Hybrid PAC (a committee that maintains both a regular contribution account and a separate, unlimited "Non-Contribution Account" under the Carey v. FEC settlement) affiliated with Crush MAGA PAC, received a Request for Additional Information dated September 24 covering its 2026 August Monthly report. The letter, signed by FEC Senior Campaign Finance & Reviewing Analyst Sarah Vivian, flags three separate apparent problems totaling roughly $510,000.

First, the report discloses a $100,000 contribution from an individual identified as Marcia Grand on July 14, 2026 — twenty times the $5,000 calendar-year limit an individual may give a political committee. Second, the report discloses a receipt from an unregistered organization, Floridians for Better Government PAC, which the FEC says must be shown to have used only permissible funds. Third, and largest, Schedule B shows transfers of $150,000 to Alameda Come Together PAC, $85,000 to Blue Florida Majority, and $175,000 to Safer Alameda County PAC, all dated July 27, 2026 — each far above the $5,000-per-year cap on contributions between political committees.

Critically, the FEC's letter itself offers SD PAC an out: Commission records "indicate that your committee may actually be affiliated" with the three recipient PACs, in which case the transfers would be permissible unlimited inter-affiliate transfers rather than violations — SD PAC would simply need to amend its Statement of Organization to disclose the affiliation. If the committees are not affiliated, SD PAC must instead seek refunds of the excess amounts. The same letter also flags a line-placement error unrelated to the contribution questions. SD PAC's treasurer is Jim Henry; the committee has until October 29, 2026 to respond.

Sitting Lawmakers
Two Incumbents' Committees Get RFAIs, One Over an Unresolved Repeat Problem

Two sitting members of Congress had campaign committees receive Requests for Additional Information in this window. One is routine; the other flags a compliance problem the FEC says the committee has already been told about once and still hasn't fixed.

  • Rep. Ralph Norman (R-SC-5)'s committee, Ralph Norman for Congress, was asked to clarify receipts on its July Quarterly report listed with the purpose "Uncashed Check." The FEC's concern: if the committee received goods or services from a vendor without ever actually paying for them, that could amount to a prohibited corporate contribution requiring a refund or disgorgement within 30 days. Filing.
  • Rep. Mike Bost (R-IL-12)'s committee, Mike Bost for Congress Committee, received an RFAI on its July Quarterly report noting that the committee had already been told about two problems in a prior response and, per the FEC, still hasn't fixed them: $10,000 in contributions from the National Pork Producers Council Pork PAC (split $5,000 on April 28 and $5,000 on June 30, both coded to the general election) exceeds the $5,000-per-election limit for a multicandidate PAC, and the committee has also kept contributions designated for the 2026 primary that came in after the primary already happened, beyond what its outstanding primary debt would allow. The FEC's letter twice notes it is still waiting on a real fix. Filing.
Requests for Additional Information
30 More RFAIs, From Late Independent-Expenditure Reports to Compliance-Firm Cleanups

Beyond SD PAC and the two incumbent committees above, the FEC sent 30 more Requests for Additional Information in this window (plus the separate Form 99 correspondence below). Most concern routine reporting mechanics — math errors, missing depository disclosures, incomplete contributor identification — but several involve real dollar amounts: two campaigns in Maine and New York with six-figure independent expenditures reported late, and two Colorado congressional campaigns with general-election contributions from lost primaries that still haven't been refunded months later.

  • DE-INC. U.S. Gov, a House-affiliated committee treasured by Thahbia Asad, was told its Statement of Organization lists a principal campaign committee name that doesn't include its candidate's name, as FEC rules require. Filing.
  • Kiley Victory Fund was asked to fix Column B (election-cycle-to-date) totals on its July Quarterly report that don't match the sum of the prior report's totals and this period's new activity. Filing.
  • Colorado Turnout Project was flagged for accepting apparently excessive contributions from a single donor, Donald Henley, totaling $11,500 across six separate contributions between April and June 2026 — more than double the $5,000-per-year limit — and for disclosing a contribution to Fighting For Colorado PAC on the wrong line if that PAC turns out to be a non-federal committee. Filing.
  • American Bus Association Political Action Committee (BUSPAC) was told to amend its Statement of Organization to disclose Atlantic Union Bank as a depository, after its July Monthly report showed interest income from that bank without it being listed. Filing.
  • Los Angeles County Democratic Central Committee was asked to clarify whether insurance, payroll-tax, and salary payments allocated on Schedule H4 are for staff who spend more than 25% of their time on federal election activity — which, if so, must be paid entirely from federal funds rather than split with the non-federal account. Filing.
  • Maine People was flagged for apparently failing to file required 24-hour and 48-hour reports on roughly $66,000 in independent expenditures made in support of Senate candidate Graham Platner between late April and early June 2026, including a $30,000 payment on April 29 and a $23,895 payment on May 29, both to Maine People's Alliance. Filing.
  • Visionary PAC was asked to explain a $5,000 contribution to Don Davis for NC disbursed on March 16, 2026 — after North Carolina's March 3 primary had already occurred — since post-election contributions for an election that's already happened are only permitted toward retiring debt from that election. Filing.
  • Mike Collins for Senate — the Senate campaign committee of sitting U.S. Representative Mike Collins (R-GA-10), who FEC records list as a challenger for Georgia's open Senate seat rather than an incumbent for that particular race — was told to itemize the original vendors behind its credit-card payments on its July Quarterly report. Filing.
  • Wanda James for Congress (CO-1) was told that general-election contributions it collected as far back as September 2025 — small amounts from donors including Siddhartha Rathod and Jamie Pearson — still haven't been refunded or redesignated even though the candidate isn't participating in the general election. Filing.
  • Friends of Michael McCord (GA-1) was told its Year-End 2025 report's beginning cash balance doesn't match the prior report's ending balance. Filing.
  • Pettry for President Committee, treasured by Patricia Corica, was told its committee name includes candidate Dena Loraine Pettry's name despite not being registered as her authorized committee — even though Pettry's own Statement of Candidacy designates it as her principal campaign committee, creating a contradiction the FEC wants resolved. Filing.
  • Freedom Force PAC was asked to correct a memo entry showing a contributor's year-to-date total as negative $5,000, which appears to be a prior-year refund incorrectly carried into this year's total. Filing.
  • New York Fights – Luchando por Nueva York was flagged for a late 24-hour report on a $174,000 independent-expenditure payment to Amare Public Affairs, disseminated June 13, 2026 in support of Antonio Reynoso, that wasn't reported to the FEC until June 16. Filing.
  • National Creditors Bar Association PAC was flagged for apparently excessive $5,000 contributions to Andy Barr for Congress in both its 2025 April Quarterly and 2026 July Quarterly reports, both attributed by default to the 2026 primary since neither carried an election designation. Filing.
  • CLECO Federal PAC (CLECO Corporation) was told its July Quarterly report wasn't signed by an authorized treasurer or agent. Filing.
  • Hawaii Republican Party was asked to fix Column B (calendar-year-to-date) totals across more than a dozen lines of its August Monthly report. Filing.
  • Alex Kelloff for Colorado (CO-3) received a second RFAI over general-election contributions — dozens of donors across five reporting periods stretching back to mid-2025 — that, despite an earlier committee explanation the FEC references directly, "do not appear to have been remedied." Filing.
  • Gun Rights America was flagged for missing 24-hour independent-expenditure reports totaling roughly $18,000 spent in Kentucky's 2026 special election, split between supporting Thomas Massie and opposing Ed Gallrein, paid to vendors Colorspray Media, Mudshare, and PXI Inc. Filing.
  • Hudson for Alabama, the Senate campaign of challenger Jared Hudson, was told it omitted roughly $14,000 in previously itemized debts — mostly small amounts owed to HSP Direct LLC and Onpoint Data Strategy LLC — from its latest report. Filing.
  • United Party, treasured by Peter Johnson, was told its Statement of Organization says it isn't an authorized committee even though candidate Peter Andrew Johnson's Statement of Candidacy designates it as his principal campaign committee. Filing.
  • Gallego Victory Fund was asked to clarify a vaguely described disbursement listed only as "Reimbursement - Event Production." Filing.
  • Hallmark Cards, Inc. PAC (HALLPAC) was told several itemized contributors on its July Monthly report are missing required identification — full name, address, occupation, or employer — and must either supply it or document "best efforts" to obtain it. Filing.
  • Friends of Michael McCord also received a second, separate RFAI on its April Quarterly report over the same underlying issue — a beginning cash balance that doesn't match the prior report's ending balance, this time relative to its 2025 Year-End report. Filing.
  • Republican Party of Arkansas was asked to correct running year-to-date allocation totals on Schedule H4 and to reclassify a payment to Vibrant Occasions Catering that was coded as an administrative expense rather than fundraising. Filing.
  • National Committee to Preserve Social Security & Medicare PAC was asked to clarify $29,754.18 in "salary reimbursement" payments to its connected organization, since corporations may only be paid back for a separate segregated fund's solicitation and administrative costs, not for activity intended to influence elections. Filing.
  • American Independent Party, a Tennessee committee treasured by Thomas Datwyler, was told it must petition the FEC for an advisory opinion confirming state-party status before using state-party contribution limits. Filing.
  • Arizona Democratic Party was asked to correct Schedule H4 running-total math and to reclassify payments to Grace Carroll Rocky Mountain Labor School and Samsung Electronics America, both coded administrative but described as event sponsorship and a fundraising venue. Filing.
  • Washington State Republican Party was told three amended 2026 monthly reports disclose no salary or wage payments at all, which the FEC wants explained, and that affiliated-committee transfers were disclosed on the wrong line. Filing.
  • International Association of Sheet Metal, Air, Rail and Transportation Workers Political Action League was flagged for two separate $5,000 contributions to Lilac PAC (January and July 2026) that together exceed the $5,000-per-year limit on committee-to-committee giving, plus miscategorized disbursement and refund lines. Filing.
  • Burgess Foster for Congress (MI), treasured by the candidate himself, was told its Statement of Organization's committee-type selection conflicts with the fact that no principal campaign committee has yet been designated for candidate Burgess Foster. Filing.

  • Melissa Hernandez for Congress also filed a second Form 99 the same day, clarifying that a contribution originally misattributed to Rep. Pete Aguilar personally actually came from Pete Aguilar for Congress, and that a separate $1,000 contribution from Stephen Harris was voided and never received. Filing.
  • New Democrat Majority, a PAC, told the FEC that a 24-hour report for a $156,603 expenditure to Five Boroughs Media disseminated June 1, 2026 was filed nine days late, on June 9, due to what it called an unintentional oversight, and said it will review its internal tracking procedures. Filing.
  • Progressive Promise, a PAC, filed an amended 24-hour report adding a missing state and congressional district (California's 14th) to disclosures of independent expenditures in a special election. Filing.
  • Defending the American Dream PAC (AZ) said a formatting error in its August Monthly report had thrown off its year-to-date totals and that it filed a corrective amendment on September 25. Filing.
  • Kara Hahn for Congress (NY-1, open seat) told the FEC it intends to remain active rather than terminate immediately, and will use its remaining cash on hand for lawful purposes such as party-committee transfers, charitable donations, or other candidates' campaigns before eventually winding down. Filing.
  • Ruben Kihuen for Congress responded to an RFAI about its residual cash on hand by saying it hasn't decided whether to terminate or convert to a multi-candidate committee, since Ruben Kihuen "may consider a future campaign for office." Kihuen represented Nevada's 4th District from 2017 to 2019 and is not a current member of Congress — the seat is now held by Rep. Steven Horsford — so despite FEC's own candidate database still carrying an "incumbent" designation left over from his last active filing in 2017, this is a former officeholder's long-dormant committee, not a sitting lawmaker's. Filing.
  • NY Republican Federal Campaign Committee told the FEC it amended its August Monthly report on September 20 to correct Column B of the Detailed Summary Page. Filing.
  • Nexstar Media Group, Inc. Political Action Committee (NEXSTAR PAC) filed amended reports covering April through August 2026 to properly reflect bank interest received as Other Receipts. Filing.
  • Tre Dobbins for President 2032, an Alabama committee, filed a Form 99 asking to terminate its own registration after voluntarily registering but never crossing the $5,000 threshold that would actually require it. Filing.
  • Tre Dobbins for President 2032 filed a second Form 99 four minutes later explaining that the FEC's website had rejected the first termination submission and asking Commission staff for help completing it. Filing.
  • ClearPath Action Fund, Inc. (NC) responded to an RFAI about its August Monthly report by clarifying that a disbursement to vendor Topham Guerin used the wrong obligation date, and that it has since amended its 24-hour and 48-hour reports to show the correct July 9, 2026 disbursement date. Filing.
