House Pauses for 25th Anniversary of Sept. 11 Attacks; FEC and Financial-Disclosure Trackers Stay Quiet
Lawmakers gaveled in for barely three minutes to mark the anniversary before introducing dozens of new bills, while this edition's compliance trackers turned up nothing that hasn't already been reported.

The House of Representatives met at 3:30 p.m. on Thursday, September 10, 2026, with Rep. Mike Johnson (R-LA-4), the Speaker, having appointed Rep. Tim Walberg to preside as Speaker pro tempore for the day. The House immediately agreed to H. Res. 1520, offered by Speaker Johnson, expressing the House's sense regarding the terrorist attacks of September 11, 2001, on the eve of that date's 25th anniversary — then adjourned at 3:33 p.m. "as a further mark of respect to the memory of the victims." There were no recorded votes, no yea-and-nay votes, and no quorum calls. The Senate met the same afternoon only in pro forma session, gaveling in and out in under a minute before adjourning until 3 p.m. Monday, September 14.

Despite the short session, the House introduced a full slate of legislation: 25 public bills (H.R. 10326–10350) and 10 resolutions (H. Res. 1520–1529). The fullest list, with sponsors and plain-English descriptions, is in "On the Record" below.

Elsewhere, it was a quiet compliance day. This edition's three-day lookback window turned up 23 FEC Requests for Additional Information, 21 Form 99 correspondence filings, four House personal financial disclosures, and three Senate personal financial disclosures — but every one of them had already been reported in the September 11 or September 12 editions of this newsletter, so none appear again here. No House or Senate committee meetings are scheduled to convene in the next 24 hours.

House Marks 9/11's 25th Anniversary With 3-Minute Session; 25 Bills, 10 Resolutions Introduced

The House's floor action on September 10 was almost entirely ceremonial — a single resolution, agreed to without objection, followed by adjournment — but the day's introduction docket was full. Two items stand out: a bill to award a Congressional Gold Medal to the Naval Special Warfare Development Group ("SEAL Team Six") for the raid that killed Osama bin Laden, and three separate resolutions tied to the September 11 anniversary itself. Every bill and resolution introduced that day is listed below.

  • H.R. 10326 ("PROOF Act"), Rep. Jefferson Van Drew (R-NJ-2) — would let federal law enforcement and state agencies share information to detect and prosecute fraud in federal programs while protecting individual privacy.
  • H.R. 10327 ("Cost Estimates Improvement Act"), Rep. Michael Cloud (R-TX-27) — would require Congressional Budget Office and Joint Committee on Taxation cost estimates to include the cost of servicing the public debt.
  • H.R. 10328 ("Badge-to-Business Act"), Rep. Robert P. Bresnahan (R-PA-8) — would create no-collateral Small Business Administration express loans for career law enforcement officers.
  • H.R. 10329 ("Combating Foreign Threats to Main Street Act of 2026"), Rep. Roger Williams (R-TX-25) — would direct the Small Business Administration to disseminate information on foreign-adversary conduct that harms small businesses.
  • H.R. 10330 ("Higher Education Oversight for Nonprofits Ensuring Standards and Transparency Act"), Rep. Alma S. Adams (D-NC-12) — would place restrictions on university tax inquiries and examinations under the tax code.
  • H.R. 10331 ("Patient Navigation Assistance Act"), Rep. Mark DeSaulnier (D-CA-10) — would create Medicaid payment for patient navigator services.
  • H.R. 10332 ("Sunset Section 230 Act"), Rep. Mark DeSaulnier (D-CA-10) — would repeal Section 230 of the Communications Act, the liability shield for online platforms.
  • H.R. 10333, Rep. Debbie Dingell (D-MI-6) — would fund national training, technical-assistance, and resource centers to expand access to augmentative and alternative communication for people with significant speech disabilities.
  • H.R. 10334, Rep. Ron Estes (R-KS-4) — would require validation of the electronic filing identification numbers used to e-file tax returns and other documents.
  • H.R. 10335, Rep. Glenn Grothman (R-WI-6) — would clarify how attorney's-fee awards apply in certain civil actions under federal law.
  • H.R. 10336, Rep. Diana Harshbarger (R-TN-1) — would revise the FDA's regulatory framework for dietary-supplement "drug preclusion" to expand consumer access.
  • H.R. 10337, Rep. Erin Houchin (R-IN-9) — would prohibit certain platforms from letting "covered users" create or maintain an account or profile.
  • H.R. 10338, Rep. Thomas H. Kean (R-NJ-7) — would award a Congressional Gold Medal, collectively, to the Naval Special Warfare Development Group ("SEAL Team Six") for its role in the raid that killed Osama bin Laden.
  • H.R. 10339, Rep. Thomas H. Kean (R-NJ-7) — would clarify the timing of Terrorism Risk Insurance Act assessments for entities deemed an agency or instrumentality of a terrorist party.
  • H.R. 10340, Rep. Thomas H. Kean (R-NJ-7) — would establish a Library of Congress program to collect oral histories related to the September 11, 2001 attacks.
  • H.R. 10341, Del. Kimberlyn King-Hinds (R-MP-At Large) — would amend immigration law's visa waiver program for Guam and the Northern Mariana Islands.
  • H.R. 10342, Rep. Seth Magaziner (D-RI-2) — would expand eligibility for Department of Agriculture loans and grants to fishing and mariculture businesses.
  • H.R. 10343 ("CLEAR Feedback Act"), Rep. Analilia Mejia (D-NJ-11) — would require federal agencies to give small businesses enhanced debriefing materials when denying SBIR or STTR award applications.
  • H.R. 10344 ("Fast Track Disaster Relief Act"), Rep. Analilia Mejia (D-NJ-11) — would require disclosure of tax return information to the Small Business Administration to carry out its Disaster Loan Program.
  • H.R. 10345 ("EBOLA Act"), Rep. Kweisi Mfume (D-MD-7) — would require continued U.S. participation in the World Health Organization.
  • H.R. 10346, Rep. Christopher H. Smith (R-NJ-4) — would bar goods made with cobalt extracted or processed using child or forced labor in the Democratic Republic of the Congo from entering the U.S. market.
  • H.R. 10347 ("Data Science and Literacy Act of 2026"), Rep. Haley M. Stevens (D-MI-11) — would fund grants to expand access to data-literacy education.
  • H.R. 10348, Rep. Thomas R. Suozzi (D-NY-3) — would strengthen program-integrity oversight for Medicare's Health Care Fraud and Abuse Control Program.
  • H.R. 10349, Rep. Derek Tran (D-CA-45) — would direct the Department of Health and Human Services to establish a Public Safety Mental Health Fellowship Program.
  • H.R. 10350, Rep. George Whitesides (D-CA-27) — would impose new requirements on financial institutions aimed at protecting elderly customers from wire fraud.
  • H. Res. 1520, Rep. Mike Johnson (R-LA-4) — expresses the House's sense regarding the September 11, 2001 terrorist attacks on the 25th anniversary of that date; considered and agreed to the same day.
  • H. Res. 1521, Rep. Rick W. Allen (R-GA-12) — honors the 100th anniversary of the American Cleaning Institute.
  • H. Res. 1522, Rep. Wesley Bell (D-MO-1) — recognizes the 30th anniversary of the Missouri-Panama State Partnership Program.
  • H. Res. 1523, Rep. Thomas H. Kean (R-NJ-7) — supports designating November 20, 2026, as "Carbon Monoxide Poisoning Awareness Day."
  • H. Res. 1524, Rep. Brian J. Mast (R-FL-21) — supports designating September 19–26, 2026, as "National Estuaries Week."
  • H. Res. 1525, Rep. Doris O. Matsui (D-CA-7) — recognizes September 11, 2026, as a "National Day of Service and Remembrance" and the 25th anniversary of the September 11, 2001 attacks.
  • H. Res. 1526, Rep. John R. Moolenaar (R-MI-2) — supports designating the week of September 11–17, 2026, as "Patriot Week."
  • H. Res. 1527, Rep. Brittany Pettersen (D-CO-7) — commemorates one year since a fatal act of violence on September 10, 2025, in Evergreen, Colorado, and expresses condolences to victims, survivors, and their communities.
  • H. Res. 1528, Rep. Shri Thanedar (D-MI-13) — recognizes suicide as a public health problem and supports designating September 2026 as "National Suicide Prevention Month" and September 10, 2026, as "World Suicide Prevention Day."
  • H. Res. 1529, Rep. Dina Titus (D-NV-1) — congratulates the Paseo Verde Little League baseball team on winning the 2026 Little League World Series United States Championship.
