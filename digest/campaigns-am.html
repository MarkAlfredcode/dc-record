Top Story
FEC presses Cory Booker's Senate campaign over a $9,178.33 'unauthorized transaction'
An RFAI to the New Jersey senator's committee says the disbursement appears to be an unauthorized use of committee funds.

The Federal Election Commission sent an RFAI on October 21 to Cory Booker for Senate, the campaign committee of Sen. Cory A. Booker (D-NJ). It asks about a Schedule B disbursement on Line 21 described as 'Unauthorized transaction,' totaling $9,178.33. The disbursement appears on the committee's amended Year-End 2025 report, which the FEC received on July 1, 2026.

The letter says the transaction 'appears to be related to an apparent unauthorized disbursement of Committee funds.' It also says the Commission 'notes your explanation of the unauthorized activity,' and asks for any additional information as it becomes available. That explanation is not in the filing text, so what happened is not stated. The letter warns the Commission may still take further legal action over the apparent improper use of committee funds. The treasurer is Scott Kobler, and the response is due November 25.

Also filed this week: Harris County Democratic Party told the FEC that 42 receipts totaling $148,176 meant for state or nonfederal activity were mistakenly deposited into its federal account. That statement is covered in the Form 99 section.

Form 99 Correspondence
25 Form 99s: loan paperwork from both Senate party committees, late-disclosure admissions and a $50 million license valuation

Most of these are one-line threshold statements from tiny campaigns, but several matter. NRSC and DSCC filed bank-loan paperwork. Harris County Democratic Party reported $148,176 of misdirected receipts. Three committees admitted late disclosures: BlueWaveAmerica, You Can Push Back and The Wisco Project PAC.

The red-flag scan flagged the NRSC filing on 'grand jury,' 'money launder' and 'theft.' Those hits come from standard boilerplate in the 127-page loan agreement (a jury-trial waiver, anti-terrorism and anti-money-laundering definitions, and an uninsured-loss clause). They are not findings about the committee.
