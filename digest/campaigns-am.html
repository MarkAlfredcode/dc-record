Center Aisle PAC Discloses Court-Ordered Restitution From Former Treasurer
A federal court has ordered Katherine Buchanan, who once served as the group's treasurer, to repay $78,031.41 to the committee she managed.

Center Aisle PAC, a centrist political action committee, told the Federal Election Commission on September 24 that a federal court has ordered its former treasurer, Katherine Buchanan, to pay restitution totaling $78,031.41 back to the committee. The disclosure came in a Form 99 responding to a Request for Additional Information the FEC sent on September 7 about the committee's second-quarter 2026 report.

The committee, now run by treasurer Lauren Decot Lee, says it has listed the $78,031.41 as a debt owed to the committee on Schedule D of its report. The filing cites two decades-old FEC advisory opinions on debt disclosure — Advisory Opinion 1991-38 (DeConcini) and Advisory Opinion 2005-04 (Boehner) — and points to "previous Form 99s submitted by the Committee on June 6, 2025 and subsequently," indicating this compliance matter has been unfolding for well over a year.

The filing does not spell out what Buchanan did to trigger a court-ordered restitution judgment, and the committee says only that it "continues to work with the Commission to fully resolve this matter." A pattern of serial Form 99 filings on the same underlying issue, combined with a court-ordered repayment from a former treasurer, is exactly the kind of red flag this newsletter watches for — we will keep following the docket for the underlying case.

Sitting Lawmakers
Five Incumbents' Committees Drew FEC Letters This Week

Five sitting members of Congress had campaign-committee filings surface in this window — four responding to Requests for Additional Information and one clearing up an excessive-contribution problem on its own. None involve allegations of personal wrongdoing by the member; all are routine compliance back-and-forth.

  • Rep. Jahana Hayes (D-CT-5), via Friends of Jahana Hayes: Responded to an FEC letter about excessive contributions from three donors — Joan Rechnitz, Steve English and Molly Munger — saying the excess amounts were refunded in July and will show up on the October 15 quarterly report. Filing.
  • Rep. Ralph W. Norman Jr. (R-SC-5), via Ralph Norman for Congress: Got an RFAI over Schedule A receipts logged as "Uncashed Check" — the FEC's way of flagging that if the committee got goods or services from a vendor without ever paying, that could count as a prohibited corporate contribution requiring a refund or disgorgement. Filing.
  • Rep. Michael Bost (R-IL-12), via Mike Bost for Congress Committee: The FEC flagged two $5,000 contributions from the National Pork Producers Council Pork PAC (April 28 and June 30) as exceeding the $5,000-per-election limit, and says the committee has not yet refunded, redesignated or reattributed either one. The letter also asks the committee to document net debts outstanding before keeping several post-primary contributions designated for the primary. Filing.
  • Rep. Mike Collins (R-GA-10), via his Senate campaign committee Mike Collins for Senate: A comparatively minor RFAI asking the committee to itemize the original vendors behind its credit-card payments as memo entries. Notably, the committee's treasurer, Thomas Datwyler, is also the treasurer of record for the American Independent Party, which drew its own RFAI this window (see below) — the same compliance professional serving both a sitting Republican congressman's Senate bid and a minor third party. Filing.
  • Rep. Diana DeGette (D-CO-1), via Diana DeGette for Congress: A lengthy RFAI listing dozens of contributions across 2025 and 2026 — from individual donors and PACs including Johnson & Johnson PAC, AmeriPAC and Quest Diagnostics Incorporated PAC — that were designated for the general election but, per FEC records, don't appear to have been refunded or redesignated. The letter also flags three contributions of $1,000 or more, totaling $9,000, that arrived in the 12-day pre-primary window without the required 48-hour notice. Filing.

Form 99 Correspondence
A Pennsylvania Campaign Files to Settle Its Debts for Pennies on the Dollar

Seven Form 99 letters outside the incumbent set and outside today's Top Story, mostly routine responses to Commission questions. One stands out: a Pennsylvania candidate committee has filed a formal debt settlement plan, a step usually reserved for committees winding down.

  • Daniels for Congress filed a Debt Settlement Plan (FEC Form 8) proposing to settle its outstanding debts with creditors for less than the full amount owed — a step available only to a committee that has certified it is terminating and won't raise or spend further funds beyond wind-down costs. The scanned submission is a handwritten, partly illegible paper filing; what is legible shows at least three creditors, including a consulting vendor who agreed in writing to accept a reduced settlement after ongoing efforts to collect went nowhere as the committee's fundraising did not improve. Candidate Shamaine Daniels represented Pennsylvania's 10th district campaign. Filing.
  • SF Solidarity PAC disclosed four personal-funds loans from its candidate totaling $2,486,000 on its July Quarterly report: $1,100,000 (May 4), $200,000 (June 2), $186,000 (June 4) and $1,000,000 (June 10). Filing.
  • Whatley for Senate, the campaign committee of North Carolina Senate candidate Michael Whatley, responded to an RFAI on 28 apparently excessive contributions: 27 were returned, reallocated through a joint fundraising committee, redesignated or refunded, and the 28th turned out to be two different donors who don't actually exceed the limit. Filing.
  • New Jersey Republican State Committee responded to an RFAI covering its May, June and July monthly reports, explaining that its office rent is an in-kind contribution from the Monmouth County Republican Committee and that roughly $60,000 in payroll costs were allocated between federal and non-federal funds based on each employee's share of time spent on federal election activity. Filing.
  • Missouri Democratic State Committee (Federal) responded to a Schedule H4 payroll-allocation question, saying its staff spend 25% or less of their time on Federal Election Activity and that the committee owns its building outright, so it pays no office rent; the committee says it confirmed directly with an FEC analyst that no further action is needed. Filing.
  • Stronger Together PA said a 24-hour independent-expenditure report covering activity disseminated May 5 was filed late because of an administrative error, and that it has updated its internal procedures. Filing.
  • Democratic Grassroots Victory Fund confirmed it correctly reported a contribution from Progressive Turnout Project, which has since amended its own report to match. Filing.
