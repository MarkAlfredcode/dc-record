Top Story
Klobuchar campaign tells FEC its $42,695 'asset sale' to a successor committee was at fair market value
Filed Wednesday, October 1, in response to a federal reviewer's questions about the senator's April Quarterly Report.

Klobuchar for Minnesota, the campaign committee of Sen. Amy Klobuchar (D-MN), answered an August 27 Reports Analysis Division letter about a $42,695.14 receipt from Minnesotans for Klobuchar on March 31, 2026. The committee says it was payment for assets — mailing lists, photographs, video footage and a website domain — that were built for the committee's own use and had an ascertainable market value.

Treasurer Samuel Clark writes that the assets were bought at fair market value in an arm's-length transaction, citing FEC Advisory Opinions 2002-14 and 2003-19, and that the committee will amend its April Quarterly Report to clarify the nature of the assets and the pricing. The filing does not say what Minnesotans for Klobuchar is or how the price was determined.

Sitting Lawmakers
Two sitting members answer FEC questions

Two incumbents' committees filed Form 99 replies to FEC review letters in this window, per the FEC's incumbent-status data: Sen. Amy Klobuchar and Rep. Stephen Lynch.

  • Sen. Amy Klobuchar (D-MN, incumbent) — Klobuchar for Minnesota answered a Reports Analysis Division letter dated August 27, 2026 about a $42,695.14 receipt from Minnesotans for Klobuchar on March 31, 2026. The committee says the money was payment for transferred assets (mailing lists, photographs, video footage and a website domain), sold at fair market value in an arm's-length transaction rather than as a fundraising sale, and that it will amend its April Quarterly Report to say so. Filing.
  • Rep. Stephen Lynch (D-MA-9, incumbent) — Stephen Lynch for Congress filed a six-page Form 99 saying it contacted three unregistered organizations that had given money, that one did not respond in a reasonable time so the committee refunded it, and that excess contributions from individuals were also refunded. The supporting attachments are image-only and could not be read as text, so the amounts and recipients aren't known. Filing.
Requests for Additional Information
13 new RFAIs, led by excessive and post-election contributions

The FEC posted 13 new RFAI letters, all dated October 1, 2026, all from non-incumbent filers (PACs and party committees). The most common problems were contributions designated for primaries already held and mismatches between 24-hour independent-expenditure reports and later filings. Responses are generally due 11/05/2026.
