Top Story
Energy Transfer's PAC tells the FEC its filings have been wrong since 2023 and says it has hired counsel for an audit
A short Form 99 from the corporate PAC acknowledges unspecified discrepancies across four years of reports.

Energy Transfer Employee Management Company PAC (Energy Transfer PAC) told the FEC in a Form 99 received October 19 that it 'acknowledge[s] several discrepancies stemming from previous filings beginning 2023, 2024, 2025 and 2026.' The note says the committee is 'engaged with counsel to assist with audit, reconciliation and providing accurate reporting/resolution' and hopes to finish within 30 days if the FEC finds that acceptable.

The filing does not say which reports are wrong, what kinds of errors are involved, or how large they are. It is signed by SuSu Levine of Legislative Affairs at Energy Transfer LP, who apologizes and thanks the commission for its consideration. The input flagged no red-flag keywords, so this is a self-reported, multi-year reporting problem and not evidence of personal use or misappropriation.

Two other filings from the same batch involve lateness. Alicia Limtiaco for Congress (Guam) says Typhoon Champi kept it from filing its October Quarterly Report on time and that the report went in on October 20. Rogers for Florida says it reported three WinRed-conduit contributions on a 48-hour report filed October 19, which it concedes may be late.

Sitting Lawmakers
One sitting House member drew an RFAI over joint-fundraising transfers

Only one filing from a sitting lawmaker surfaced in this batch: an RFAI to a New York Republican. It is a routine paperwork question about how a joint fundraising partner is disclosed.

  • FEC asks Rep. Andrew R. Garbarino (R-NY) why transfers from 'Friends of Fongressman' are not backed by a joint fundraising representative listed on his Statement of Organization. The letter to Garbarino for Congress concerns Schedule A, Line 12 of the July Quarterly Report (06/04/2026 to 06/30/2026) and asks the committee to amend its Statement of Organization or the report. Treasurer is Lisa Lisker. Response due 11/23. (RFAI)
Requests for Additional Information
A five-item letter to a Nebraska House hopeful stands out among five RFAIs

Five other RFAIs arrived, all with responses due 11/23. The most substantial went to an open-seat Nebraska candidate and covers excessive contributions and money from an apparent independent-expenditure-only committee. The rest are standard reporting-format questions. The two Equality California Votes letters share a treasurer.

  • FEC presses Brinker Harding for Congress on five problems, including contributions that appear to come from an independent expenditure-only political committee. The open-seat Nebraska Republican's committee (a candidate committee) is also asked about apparently excessive contributions not yet refunded, redesignated or reattributed, gifts from unregistered organizations, a refund to Lori A. Miller with no original contribution reported, and transfers from 'Brinker Victory Fund' that are not listed as a joint fundraising representative. The October Quarterly letter notes the committee's partial corrective action. Treasurer is Taylor Moose. (RFAI)
  • FEC tells Christina Bohannan for Congress it left out required conduit details for earmarked contributions. The Iowa Democratic challenger's committee (a candidate committee) must itemize the conduit as a memo entry on Schedule A of its July Quarterly Report. Treasurer is Jennifer Fihr. (RFAI)
  • FEC asks Equality California Votes why a $15,000 debt to 'David Binder Research' for apparent independent expenditures has no memo Schedule E. The PAC's May Monthly Report lists the Schedule D debt but no matching Schedule E memo entry. Treasurer is Cary Davidson, who also signs the committee's second RFAI below. (RFAI)
  • FEC says Equality California Votes' June Monthly Report has wrong year-to-date totals and Schedule E memo entries that inflate cash on hand. The totals on Lines 21(b), 21(c) and 24, Column B look incorrect, and memo-entry dollar values should not be counted in schedule totals. Same treasurer, Cary Davidson. (RFAI)
  • FEC tells FAMB Federal PAC its name must spell out the 'Florida Association of Mortgage Professionals'. The PAC (a PAC) filed an amended Statement of Organization on 10/15/2026, and the FEC says filings must carry the connected organization's full official name. Treasurer is Ryan Riesterer. (RFAI)
Form 99 Correspondence
Late-report excuses, a self-reported audit and a stack of 'below $5,000' statements

Twenty Form 99s arrived, nearly all received October 19, the day after the quarterly pre-election crunch. Most are small campaigns saying they have not reached the $5,000 reporting threshold. A few are more notable: Energy Transfer PAC's multi-year disclosure (see Top Story), a Guam committee citing a typhoon for a late quarterly report, and a Florida campaign explaining a possibly late 48-hour report. One Form 99 is a paper-filed Form 3 whose scan is largely unreadable.

  • Alicia Limtiaco for Congress tells the FEC Typhoon Champi prevented it from filing its October Quarterly Report by the deadline. The Guam Democratic challenger's committee (a candidate committee) says power, water and internet outages lasted from October 14 until power returned on October 20, and it asks for consideration under the best-efforts provisions of 11 CFR 111.35. The statement is signed by Deputy Treasurer William Iglesias Jr. and is dated October 20, though the FEC stamped it October 19. The October Quarterly was due October 15, so this is a late-filing explanation. (Form 99)
  • Allegheny County Democratic Committee - Federal discloses that $13,548.58 on its July quarterly report was unitemized. The party committee says the contributions did not meet the itemization threshold. (Form 99)
  • Bryan Martin for Congress Committee says it plans to dissolve and return all money to contributors. The Tennessee Democratic challenger's committee (a candidate committee) says it has not raised or spent more than $5,000. (Form 99)
  • Committee for Edward John Roland TN 3 files a paper-submitted FEC Form 3 whose scan is largely illegible. The Tennessee independent challenger's committee (a candidate committee) filed it as a miscellaneous submission. The OCR text is mostly garbled, so the covering period and totals could not be read, though the summary figures appear to be small. (Form 99)
  • Ed Pope for Congress asks the FEC to terminate its registration. The open-seat Florida Republican's committee (a candidate committee) made the request in a Form 99 signed by Treasurer Phillip Greaves. (Form 99)
  • Energy Transfer PAC acknowledges discrepancies in its filings from 2023 through 2026 and says it has hired counsel for an audit. The PAC hopes to resolve the problems within 30 days. The note is signed by SuSu Levine of Energy Transfer LP. Reporting problems spanning four years make this a serial compliance issue. (Form 99)
  • George A Benavides tells the FEC he is cancelling his candidacy. The Texas challenger's committee (a candidate committee) filed a one-line notice. (Form 99)
  • Heidi Cochran for US Senate says the candidate is forgiving $5,250 in personal loans to wind down the campaign. The New York Democrat also forgives other unreimbursed expenses and says the only remaining debts are owed to herself. She signs as candidate and treasurer. (Form 99)
  • Justice League PAC says it amended its April and May Monthly Reports to add full candidate detail for itemized disbursements. Treasurer Patrick Swymer says the recipient 'Ammar for Congress' changed its registration on August 13, 2026 to 'Electing Ethical Democrats' under the same FEC ID. He says the contribution carried no direction or control, so the original name was correct and needs no amendment. The letter is addressed to FEC analyst Andrea M Chamorro. (Form 99)
  • Kenneth Korcak for Congress tells the FEC it received no donations last quarter and has $1,115.46 in the bank. The Georgia open-seat Democrat's committee is a candidate committee. (Form 99)
  • Lowell Andre Reynolds tells the FEC he has not yet received or spent $5,000. The Tennessee independent challenger's committee is a candidate committee. (Form 99)
  • Marcus Pearson Man of the People says his campaign raised no money this quarter. The North Carolina Democratic challenger's committee is a candidate committee. (Form 99)
  • Massachusetts Republican Party tells the FEC it corrected two Schedule H4 problems on its August Monthly Report. Responding to a September 14 letter, the party says it fixed the administrative year-to-date totals and checked the proper activity-category box, both in an amended report filed at the same time. The response is dated October 16. (Form 99)
  • Pettus for Congress tells the FEC it has not met the $5,000 reporting threshold for its 2026 12-Day Pre-General Report. The Michigan House committee (a candidate committee) is the first of three similar statements filed October 19. (Form 99)
  • Pettus for Congress files a second statement that it has not met the $5,000 threshold, this time for its October Quarterly. (Form 99)
  • Pettus for Congress files a third statement for the public record that it has not met the $5,000 threshold. It names no specific report. (Form 99)
  • Rogers for Florida tells the FEC its 48-hour report on three WinRed contributions may be late. The open-seat Florida Republican's committee (a candidate committee) says Jeremy Sunseri, Thomas Feeney and Reynolds Henderson gave through WinRed on October 16, 2026, but that WinRed had not yet transferred the money. The report was filed October 19, and the committee says that is within 24 hours of the due date and that it has no prior violations. (Form 99)
  • Steven Fuller for Congress tells the FEC it has not raised or spent more than $5,000 for its 12-Day Pre-General Report. The Colorado independent challenger's committee is a candidate committee. (Form 99)
  • Steven Jacob for Kansas 1 tells the FEC it raised zero dollars. The Kansas Libertarian challenger's committee is a candidate committee. (Form 99)
  • The Wisco Project PAC cuts a reported independent expenditure for Rebecca Cooke from $5,150 to $2,575. The PAC amended its October 16 24-hour notice for ads with LED Truck Media Group LLC supporting Rebecca Cooke in Wisconsin's 3rd District, after deciding to use half of the buy for a nonfederal Wisconsin candidate. The remaining $2,575 will appear on its Post-General Report. (Form 99)
