Top Story
House Rules Tees Up Russia Sanctions Bill, a New Fraud Division, and EPA Waiver Rollbacks
The House Committee on Rules meets this afternoon to write the floor rule for the Senate's amended Sanctioning Russia and Iran Act, a fraud-enforcement overhaul, and two resolutions undoing California emissions waivers.

The House Committee on Rules is scheduled to meet Monday, September 14, 2026, at 4 p.m. Eastern in H-313 of the Capitol to set the terms of floor debate on five separate measures. The most consequential is the Senate's amended version of H.R. 5334, the Lindsey O. Graham Sanctioning Russia and Iran Act of 2026 — legislation that already passed the House in an earlier form and now returns so the House can decide whether to accept the changes the Senate made before sending it to the President.

Also on the agenda: H.R. 9576, the National Fraud Enforcement Division Act of 2026, which Rules is advancing with a substitute text (Rules Committee Print 119-41) that modifies the introduced bill; and H.R. 10326, the Preventing Rip-offs and Obtaining Oversight of Funds Act (PROOF Act) from Rep. Jefferson Van Drew — a fraud-information-sharing bill that was introduced only four days earlier, on September 10, an unusually fast turnaround to a Rules hearing.

Rounding out the docket are two Congressional Review Act resolutions, H.J. Res. 210 and H.J. Res. 213, both disapproving Environmental Protection Agency decisions granting California authority to enforce its own emissions standards — one for oceangoing vessels at berth, the other for commercial harbor craft.

On the Record
House Marks 9/11's 25th Anniversary With a Brief Session, Then a Wave of New Bills

The most recent Congressional Record available is from Thursday, September 10, 2026 — the House and Senate were dark Friday through Sunday, and the House's own next session is today, September 14. On the 10th, the House convened at 3:30 p.m., agreed without objection to H. Res. 1520 expressing the sense of the House on the 25th anniversary of the September 11, 2001 attacks, and adjourned at 3:33 p.m. as a further mark of respect for the victims — with no roll-call or recorded votes taken. The Senate held only a pro forma session, gaveling in and out in 30 seconds before adjourning until 3 p.m. today.

Before gaveling out, the House introduced 25 new bills (H.R. 10326–10350) and 10 resolutions (H. Res. 1520–1529). The most notable of the bunch: the PROOF Act (H.R. 10326) from Rep. Jefferson Van Drew, which heads to the House Rules Committee for a hearing just four days after introduction (see Top Story); a bill from Rep. Thomas H. Kean to award a Congressional Gold Medal to the Navy's SEAL Team Six; and a bill from Rep. Mark DeSaulnier to repeal Section 230 of the Communications Act. The full list, with no omissions, follows below.

  • H.R. 10326, Preventing Rip-offs and Obtaining Oversight of Funds Act (PROOF Act) — Rep. Jefferson Van Drew (R-NJ-2). Would strengthen information-sharing between federal law enforcement and state agencies to detect and prosecute fraud in federal programs while protecting individual privacy.
  • H.R. 10327, Cost Estimates Improvement Act — Rep. Michael Cloud (R-TX-27). Would require CBO and Joint Committee on Taxation cost estimates to include the cost of servicing the additional public debt a bill would create.
  • H.R. 10328, Badge-to-Business Act — Rep. Robert P. Bresnahan (R-PA-8). Would create no-collateral, expedited Small Business Administration loans for qualified career law enforcement officers.
  • H.R. 10329 — Rep. Roger Williams (R-TX-25). Would require the Small Business Administration to disseminate information about foreign-adversary conduct that poses material harm to small businesses.
  • H.R. 10330, Higher Education Oversight for Nonprofits Ensuring Standards and Transparency Act — Rep. Alma S. Adams (D-NC-12). Would impose restrictions on IRS tax inquiries and examinations of universities.
  • H.R. 10331, Patient Navigation Assistance Act — Rep. Mark DeSaulnier (D-CA-10). Would create Medicaid payment for patient navigator services.
  • H.R. 10332, Sunset Section 230 Act — Rep. Mark DeSaulnier (D-CA-10). Would repeal Section 230 of the Communications Act of 1934, the law shielding online platforms from liability for user-posted content.
  • H.R. 10333 — Rep. Debbie Dingell (D-MI-6). Would fund national training, technical-assistance, and resource centers so people with significant expressive-communication disabilities can access augmentative and alternative communication technology.
  • H.R. 10334 — Rep. Ron Estes (R-KS-4). Would require validation of the electronic filing identification numbers used to e-file tax returns and other documents.
  • H.R. 10335 — Rep. Glenn Grothman (R-WI-6). Would amend federal law to clarify when attorney's fees may be awarded in certain civil actions.
  • H.R. 10336 — Rep. Diana Harshbarger (R-TN-1). Would change FDA rules on drug preclusion to protect consumer access to dietary supplements.
  • H.R. 10337 — Rep. Erin Houchin (R-IN-9). Would prohibit certain platforms from letting covered users create or maintain an account or profile.
  • H.R. 10338 — Rep. Thomas H. Kean (R-NJ-7). Would collectively award a Congressional Gold Medal to the Naval Special Warfare Development Group, known as SEAL Team Six, for its role in the raid that killed Osama bin Laden.
  • H.R. 10339 — Rep. Thomas H. Kean (R-NJ-7). Would clarify the timing of Terrorism Risk Insurance Act assessments on entities linked to a state sponsor of terrorism.
  • H.R. 10340 — Rep. Thomas H. Kean (R-NJ-7). Would direct the Library of Congress to establish a program collecting oral histories related to the September 11, 2001 attacks.
  • H.R. 10341 — Del. Kimberlyn King-Hinds (R-MP-At Large). Would amend the Immigration and Nationality Act's visa waiver program for Guam and the Northern Mariana Islands.
  • H.R. 10342 — Rep. Seth Magaziner (D-RI-2). Would expand eligibility for Department of Agriculture loans and grants to fishing and mariculture businesses.
  • H.R. 10343, CLEAR Feedback Act — Rep. Analilia Mejia (D-NJ-11). Would require federal agencies to give small businesses enhanced debriefing materials when SBIR or STTR award applications are denied.
  • H.R. 10344, Fast Track Disaster Relief Act — Rep. Analilia Mejia (D-NJ-11). Would let the IRS disclose taxpayer return information to the Small Business Administration to speed disaster-loan processing.
  • H.R. 10345, EBOLA Act — Rep. Kweisi Mfume (D-MD-7). Would require continued U.S. participation in the World Health Organization.
  • H.R. 10346, Stop China's Exploitation of Congolese Children and Adult Forced Labor through Cobalt Mining Act — Rep. Christopher H. Smith (R-NJ-4). Would block goods made with cobalt mined using child or forced labor in the Democratic Republic of the Congo from entering the U.S. market.
  • H.R. 10347, Data Science and Literacy Act of 2026 — Rep. Haley M. Stevens (D-MI-11). Would direct the Secretary of Education to fund grants expanding access to data-literacy education.
  • H.R. 10348 — Rep. Thomas R. Suozzi (D-NY-3). Would strengthen Medicare program-integrity oversight under the Health Care Fraud and Abuse Control Program.
  • H.R. 10349 — Rep. Derek Tran (D-CA-45). Would direct the Department of Health and Human Services to establish a Public Safety Mental Health Fellowship Program.
  • H.R. 10350 — Rep. George Whitesides (D-CA-27). Would impose new requirements on covered financial institutions to protect elderly customers from wire fraud.
  • H. Res. 1520 — Rep. Mike Johnson (R-LA-4). Expresses the sense of the House on the 25th anniversary of the September 11, 2001 attacks; agreed to the same day.
  • H. Res. 1521 — Rep. Rick W. Allen (R-GA-12). Honors the 100th anniversary of the American Cleaning Institute.
  • H. Res. 1522 — Rep. Wesley Bell (D-MO-1). Recognizes the 30th anniversary of the Missouri-Panama State Partnership Program.
  • H. Res. 1523 — Rep. Thomas H. Kean (R-NJ-7). Supports designating November 20, 2026 as "Carbon Monoxide Poisoning Awareness Day."
  • H. Res. 1524 — Rep. Brian J. Mast (R-FL-21). Supports designating the week of September 19-26, 2026 as "National Estuaries Week."
  • H. Res. 1525 — Rep. Doris O. Matsui (D-CA-7). Designates September 11, 2026 as a "National Day of Service and Remembrance" marking the 25th anniversary of the 9/11 attacks.
  • H. Res. 1526 — Rep. John R. Moolenaar (R-MI-2). Supports designating September 11-17, 2026 as "Patriot Week."
  • H. Res. 1527 — Rep. Brittany Pettersen (D-CO-7). Commemorates one year since a fatal act of violence on September 10, 2025 in Evergreen, Colorado.
  • H. Res. 1528 — Rep. Shri Thanedar (D-MI-13). Recognizes suicide as a serious public health problem and supports designating September 2026 as "National Suicide Prevention Month."
  • H. Res. 1529 — Rep. Dina Titus (D-NV-1). Congratulates the Paseo Verde Little League team on winning the 2026 Little League World Series United States Championship.
