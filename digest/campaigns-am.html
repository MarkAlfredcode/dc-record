Top Story
Rep. Anthony Brown's campaign committee reports more than $800,000 in charity donations and says it will wind down
The Form 99 was filed Wednesday, October 7, answering an FEC letter dated September 2.

Anthony Brown for Congress, the campaign committee of Rep. Anthony Brown (D-MD), told the Federal Election Commission that its October quarterly report disclosed over $800,000 in donations to charities and that the committee "intends to finalize operations in the coming months." The letter, signed by compliance director Allison P. Murray, responds to a September 2 FEC request about the committee's status.

The letter does not say which charities received the money or give the exact total, and it does not explain why a committee tied to a sitting member of Congress is winding down. Federal rules bar converting campaign funds to personal use but allow charitable donations.

The same compliance director signed an October 7 letter for Eric Lynn for Congress saying that committee will stay open while Lynn considers a future run.

Sitting Lawmakers
Nine committees tied to sitting House members filed today: three Form 99 replies, five RFAIs and one termination approval

Three members answered September 2 FEC letters with Form 99 submissions. The five RFAIs cover contribution-limit, refund, vendor-disclosure and purpose-of-disbursement problems. The FEC candidate database also flags Friends of Elizabeth Esty and Brenda Lawrence for Congress as incumbent committees, but both belong to former members and are listed under Form 99 Correspondence.

  • Rep. Anthony Brown(D-MD) — Form 99 answering a Sept. 2 FEC letter about the committee's status: the committee says its October quarterly report disclosed over $800,000 in donations to charities and that it 'intends to finalize operations in the coming months.' Filing.
  • Rep. Brian Jack(R-GA-3) — Form 99 replying to a Sept. 2 request for information: the previous treasurer amended the July quarterly report to remove expense checks that were never issued but had been reported, which changed the reported expense total. The committee says it is changing treasurer and compliance consultant. Filing.
  • Rep. Ted Lieu(D-CA) — Form 99 saying the committee amended its 12-day pre-primary report (04/01–05/13/2026) to add a credit card transaction and the matching card balance as debt, because the statement arrived after the filing deadline. Filing.
  • Rep. Darrell Issa(R-CA), April quarterly RFAI — FEC says the report shows primary-designated contributions received after the candidate's 3/6/2026 announcement that he would not seek office; those must be refunded unless the committee had net campaign debts when each arrived. Filing.
  • Rep. Steve Scalise(R-LA-1), July quarterly RFAI — Asked about refunds of contributions on Schedule A that don't match any originally reported contribution, and about earmarked contributions passed through a conduit without the original donors itemized. Filing.
  • Rep. Troy Carter(D-LA-2), July quarterly RFAI — Asked to itemize the original vendors behind credit card payments on Schedule B (memo entries are required once a vendor passes $200 in the cycle). Filing.
  • Rep. Adriano Espaillat(D-NY-13), pre-primary RFAI — Asked to explain vague disbursement purposes: 'Administrative Fees,' 'March April Consulting Services,' 'May Consulting Services,' 'Payment' and 'Professional Services.' Filing.
