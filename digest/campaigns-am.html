Top Story
Defense contractor's PAC discloses $9,500 in unclaimed checks to sitting senators and House members after a years-long recordkeeping gap
Day & Zimmermann's federal PAC says the treasurer and filing administrator who handled the checks left the company three years ago, and that "archived internal PAC records are under review."

Day & Zimmermann Inc Federal PAC (AKA "DAYPAC – Federal," FEC ID C00341271), the corporate political action committee tied to the Philadelphia-based defense and government-services contractor, filed a Miscellaneous Submission to the FEC on September 10 amending its 2025 Year-End Report for the third time. The filing revises the committee's Line 8 cash-on-hand figure to account for $9,500 in disbursements from 2022 and 2023 that had never been properly reported — because the checks themselves were voided years after being written and never cashed.

The committee listed seven voided checks by recipient: two $1,000 checks to Sen. Jim Risch written February 24, 2022 and voided June 27, 2023; a $1,000 check to Rep. Glenn Thompson written the same day and voided June 16, 2023; a $2,000 check to Sen. Jerry Moran written October 30, 2022 and voided June 16, 2023; a $1,000 check to Sen. John Cornyn's Alamo PAC, also written October 30, 2022 and voided June 16, 2023; a $1,000 check to Rep. Brian Fitzpatrick written February 24, 2022 and voided April 3, 2023; and a $2,500 check to the Chamber of Commerce for Greater Philadelphia written December 28, 2022 and voided October 17, 2024.

The committee's explanation is blunt about why it took years to surface: "The treasurer and filing admin from this time left the company three years ago. Archived internal PAC records are under review." None of the named recipients — all incumbents — appear to have done anything wrong; the checks were simply never cashed and the PAC is now cleaning up its own books. But the episode is a reminder that corporate PAC recordkeeping can lag reality for years before an amendment surfaces it publicly.

Form 99 Correspondence
A defeated Washington House candidate forgives his own $14,000 campaign loan; Ohio's GOP fields four near-identical RFAI responses in one day

Ten non-incumbent Form 99 filings (beyond Day & Zimmermann's, covered above) came in yesterday, all read in full. Four came from the same committee and treasurer — Diane Cunningham Redden, treasurer of the Ohio Republican Party State Central & Executive Committee — responding to what appear to be nearly identical RFAI letters across four separate monthly reports (M4 through M7), all on the same two issues: employee time-logging for federal election activity and Schedule B disclosure of related disbursements.

  • Ohio Republican Party State Central & Executive Committee — response to RFAI on the 2026 Amended M4 Report: no employees exceeded the 25% federal-election-activity compensated-time threshold; any that do aren't allocated between federal/non-federal accounts.
  • Ohio Republican Party (M6 Report) — same committee, second response of the day: same 25%-threshold explanation, plus a commitment to disclose future related expenditures on Schedule B, Line 30(b).
  • Ohio Republican Party (M7 Report) — third response, dated from a September 7 FEC letter, same boilerplate explanation.
  • Ohio Republican Party (M5 Report) — fourth response of the day, same explanation again.
  • Democratic Executive Committee of Florida — treasurer West Hodge confirms the committee filed an updated Statement of Organization disclosing its representative relationship with the "Friends of State Democratic Parties," in response to an RFAI on its June Monthly Report.
  • Accomack County Republican Unit — treasurer Jeff Procter explains that of $2,525 reported as cash-on-hand, only one contribution — a $1,000 donation from the Northampton County Republican Unit received October 4, 2025 — exceeded the $200 itemization threshold; the rest ran $15–$100 each.
  • Let's Get to Work PAC — treasurer Salvatore Purpura confirms three contributions questioned by the FEC — $3,500 from Mosaic Company PAC, $5,000 from Pancake PAC and $5,000 from QSR Leaders PAC Inc. — were properly received and deposited in July, and attributes the donor committees' own non-disclosure to their quarterly (vs. the PAC's monthly) filing schedule.
  • John Rice for President — treasurer Jonathon Mark Rice submits a detailed timeline defending the committee against an RFAI questioning the timeliness of its Statement of Organization, arguing the roughly two-week delay was caused by the FEC's own electronic-filing password enrollment process, not the committee.
  • Kevin Fagan for Congress — Loan Forgiveness filing: candidate Kevin Fagan, who lost his primary in Washington's 5th District, forgave the remaining $13,973.59 balance of his personal campaign loan.

On the Record
House pauses for 3 minutes to mark 9/11's 25th anniversary before adjourning; 25 bills and 10 resolutions introduced

Yesterday's session was brief and symbolic. The House convened at 3:30 p.m., with Rep. Tim Walberg presiding as Speaker pro tempore, and immediately took up H. Res. 1520 — sponsored by Speaker Mike Johnson (R-LA-4) — expressing the House's sentiments on the 25th anniversary of the September 11, 2001 terrorist attacks. The resolution was agreed to without a recorded vote, and the House adjourned at 3:33 p.m. "as a further mark of respect to the memory of the victims." There were no yea-and-nay votes, recorded votes, or quorum calls. The Senate met only in pro forma session and adjourned until 3 p.m. Monday, September 14. Two Senate-passed bills, S. 1051 and S. 1377, were referred to the House Committee on Natural Resources.

Members introduced 25 new bills (H.R. 10326–10350) and 10 resolutions (H. Res. 1520–1529). Beyond the 9/11 resolution itself, notable introductions include H.R. 10338, awarding a Congressional Gold Medal to the Naval Special Warfare Development Group ("SEAL Team Six") for its role in the operation against Osama bin Laden, and H. Res. 1525, designating September 11, 2026 as a "National Day of Service and Remembrance." The full list follows.
