Top Story
Former Rep. Eric Massa's committee says it is holding cash for legal fees over alleged forged House pay records and theft
Filed Friday, October 2, in response to a September 2 Federal Election Commission letter about the committee's leftover funds.

Massa for Congress told the Federal Election Commission it has kept its cash on hand because of "pending legal matters that require a contingency fund for legal fees," and that it has done nothing else since ceasing operations. It says it will dispose of the excess funds and terminate once those matters are resolved. The filing doesn't say how much cash is involved. Form 99 submission.

The letter says the committee has informed the Justice Department, the House employment counsel's office and the House Ethics Committee of legal issues that may require representation before those bodies. It describes "documented proof of forgery of House pay records that resulted in the theft of tens of thousands of dollars by a former employee," which it says was uncovered during a multi-year investigation of what it calls "debunked allegations" against Eric Massa. It says the documents have been presented to those agencies and the matter remains unresolved. The letter names no employee and gives no dollar figure, and the claims are the committee's own.

Three House incumbents file Form 99 letters, two about refunding general-election money

All three letters were filed Friday, October 2. Two members say they are refunding contributions for a general election in which they are not on the ballot, and one answers a request about excessive contributions and foreign addresses.
