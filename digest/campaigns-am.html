Top Story
A quiet day: four sitting members of Congress cleared minor FEC correspondence, nothing more serious surfaced
No voided/negative transactions, no personal-use or embezzlement red flags -- the closest thing to news is that four incumbents' committees all responded to routine FEC paperwork within a day of each other.

Nothing in today's compliance window rises above routine. The Voided & Negative Transactions sweep found zero qualifying transactions, and a red-flag keyword hit on an Asian Legal Aid Request for Additional Information turned out to be boilerplate legal-penalty language in a standard Statement of Organization verification letter -- not a substantive allegation -- and that filing had already been reported in a prior edition (2026-09-28), so it is not repeated below.

The one thread worth noting: four sitting members of Congress -- Rep. Lucy McBath, Rep. Scott Franklin, Sen. Ben Ray Luján, and Rep. Greg Steube -- all had their campaign committees file FEC correspondence within the same 48-hour window. Every one of the four is a minor clerical or reporting-technicality matter -- a misdated 48-hour contribution notice, a conduit-disclosure format fix, an earmarked-contribution correction, and a submission with no substantive text attached -- with nothing suggesting anything beyond ordinary compliance housekeeping.

Elsewhere, twenty new RFAI and Form 99 filings from non-incumbent candidates, PACs, and party committees were largely dollar-figure reconciliations and standard loan-forgiveness terminations -- detailed in full below.

Sitting Lawmakers
Four incumbents' committees filed FEC correspondence this window, all routine

Every incumbent filing found in this window was a Form 99 response or submission, not a fresh Request for Additional Information -- meaning each of the four is a committee following up on FEC correspondence it had already received, not a new compliance flag being raised against them.

  • Rep. Lucy McBath (D-GA-6), via Friends of Lucy McBath, filed a Form 99 explaining that four contributions totaling $5,500 (from American Express Company Political Action Committee, Cummins Inc. Political Action Committee, Lockheed Martin Corporation Employees' Political Action Committee, and National Rural Letter Carriers' Association Political Action Committee) were received April 29, 2026 but inadvertently reported as received April 30 on the committee's 48-Hour Notice due to a clerical error; the committee says the contributions were correctly dated on the subsequent Pre-Primary report. Filing.
  • Rep. Scott Franklin (R-FL-15), via Scott Franklin for Congress, filed a Form 99 responding to an RFAI on its July Quarterly report, saying it has now disclosed a conduit-earmarked contribution in the proper format and corrected the election-year designation on a refund that had exceeded the original contribution amount, both via an amended report filed concurrently. Filing.
  • Sen. Ben Ray Luján (D-NM), via People for Ben, filed a Form 99 stating that contributions questioned in a September 2 RFAI on its July Quarterly report were already updated in an amended 48-Hour Notice filed July 1, 2026. Filing.
  • Rep. Greg Steube (R-FL-17), via Greg Steube for Congress, filed a Miscellaneous Submission to the FEC on September 28 -- the extracted document contains only the committee's name and address header, with no accompanying explanatory text visible in the filing as posted. Filing (filed 09/28, not surfaced in this edition's window until 09/30).
Requests for Additional Information
Five RFAIs went out, mostly reporting-math and disclosure-completeness questions

All five are non-incumbent candidate committees, PACs, or party committees; none involve personal-use, embezzlement, or restitution language.

  • Democratic Party of Virginia was asked to (1) amend its Statement of Organization to disclose "First Indiana Bank Of Indiana" as a depository not previously listed, and (2) correct its August Monthly report because two Schedule H3 transfer-in breakdowns don't add up to the totals claimed -- $105,172.89 for a "Non Federal Administrative" transfer-in and $120,166.83 for a "Non Federal Direct Fundraising" transfer-in. RFAI.
  • Common Power was asked to correct its September Monthly report because in-kind contributions to its non-contribution account were reported incorrectly -- they should appear as Schedule A receipts and also be added to operating expenditures on Line 29, since leaving them off Line 29 inflates the committee's reported cash-on-hand. RFAI.
  • Texas Corn PAC of the Texas Corn Producers Association was asked to clarify an amended Statement of Organization that checked the box identifying it as a separate segregated fund: if that's correct, it must name its connected organization (the Texas Corn Producers Association) in the PAC's own title and on the form; if not, it needs to amend the form to reflect the correct committee type. RFAI.
  • Justin Wagner for Congress (Rep. candidate challenging in PA-16) received two separate RFAIs the same day, both signed by the same FEC analyst: one on its 12-Day Pre-Primary report, flagging that the beginning cash balance doesn't match the prior April Quarterly report's ending balance and that several cycle-to-date (Column B) totals appear wrong; and a second on its July Quarterly report, flagging more Column B discrepancies, missing disbursement purposes on itemized Schedule B expenditures, and PAC contributions that should have been reported on a separate Schedule A supporting Line 11(c) rather than wherever they were filed. Pre-Primary RFAI, July Quarterly RFAI.
Form 99 Correspondence
Eleven Form 99 filings from non-incumbent committees and PACs, mostly RFAI responses and two loan-forgiveness terminations

Five of the eleven were filed 09/28 and only surfaced in this edition's window as of today's 09/30 run.

  • Democratic Party of South Carolina told the FEC it issued refunds on September 29 to all the organizations named in an RFAI on its 2026 June Monthly report. Filing.
  • Bernard Taylor for Congress (Democratic challenger, FL-21) responded to an August 24 RFAI covering three separate reports, saying it has identified errors involving opening cash-on-hand and ActBlue deposits that were mistakenly recorded as expenditures, but that reconciliation of cash balances, cycle-to-date totals, conduit information, and contributor aggregates is still ongoing and the required amendments are not yet complete. Filing.
  • OJC PAC filed a Disavowal Response in which Treasurer Joshua R. Sanders reinstates himself as the PAC's treasurer, saying he intends to step down but will keep filing required reports until a successor treasurer is appointed and on file with the FEC. Filing.
  • Navajo County Democratic Committee responded to a September 1 RFAI on its July Monthly report, stating that all employees reported on its Schedule H4 administrative split spent 25% or less of their compensated time on Federal Election Activity, and that individuals paid for "Compliance Consulting" were consultants paid to a business, not committee employees. Filing.
  • Unity & Justice Fund filed a Miscellaneous Submission to the FEC on September 29 -- the extracted document contains only the committee's name and address header, with no accompanying explanatory text visible in the filing as posted. Filing.
  • Association of State Democratic Committees filed a Miscellaneous Submission to the FEC on September 29 -- the extracted document contains only the committee's name and address header, with no accompanying explanatory text visible in the filing as posted. Filing.
  • South Dakota Democratic Party - Federal said it completed amendments on September 20 addressing an August 26 RFAI, updating payroll, payroll-tax, and payroll-processing-fee disbursements for employees who spend more than 25% of their time on Federal Election Activity under 11 C.F.R. § 300.33(c)(2), while noting other spending was properly treated as exempt fundraising activity or fell under the committee's Schedule H4 uniform-split practices. Filing.
  • In This Together PAC responded to an August 24 letter about a contribution made to a federal candidate after that candidate's 2026 primary, saying the contribution was properly designated when made but was reported to the wrong election due to a clerical error, and that an amendment has been filed to correct it. Filing (filed 09/28, not surfaced in this edition's window until 09/30).
  • Lemmo for Congress (Republican challenger Alfred Lemmo, MI-8) filed a paper termination report certifying the committee closed out with no outstanding debts and that all loans the candidate personally made to the committee are forgiven. Filing (filed 09/28, not surfaced in this edition's window until 09/30).
  • Jenna Preston for Colorado (Democratic challenger Jenna Michalik Preston, CO-4) filed a paper debt-forgiveness letter in which the candidate forgives $1,255.20 in invoices she personally paid to her own committee, effective August 15, 2026. Filing (filed 09/28, not surfaced in this edition's window until 09/30).
  • Maine Republican Party responded to an August 24 letter about an individual contributor whose donation appeared to exceed federal contribution limits, saying the excess was returned to a joint fundraising committee the party participates in, for reallocation to be reflected on a future report. Filing (filed 09/28, not surfaced in this edition's window until 09/30).
