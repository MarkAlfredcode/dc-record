Top Story
Campaigns tell the FEC an electronic filing outage made them miss 48-hour notice deadlines
Two House challengers' Form 99 letters, filed October 30 and dated October 28, say the FEC's e-filing system was down when their Form 6 notices came due, days before Election Day.

Vega for Congress, the campaign of New York 15th District Democratic challenger Jose David Vega, sent a paper 48-hour notice dated October 28 by overnight delivery and fax. It reports a $1,000 contribution received October 27 from Mario Vella of Wesley Chapel, Florida. Treasurer Adrian Spector Pearl wrote that the committee is a mandatory electronic filer and that the FEC's system, including the Form 6 webform, 'remains offline.' The letter encloses an FEC notice dated October 27 listing Form 1, Form 2, Form 5, Form 6, Form 9, Form 24 and Form 99 among the services that were down. That notice says the Commission may decline to pursue administrative fines against filers kept from filing by unforeseen circumstances, if they file within 24 hours after the circumstances end (11 CFR 111.35).

Eisenhauer for Congress, the campaign of Montana independent challenger Michael D. Eisenhauer, told the FEC in a one-line Form 99 that two Form 6 submissions dated October 26 and October 29 were late because the filing system was down, and that both were submitted October 30. The Form 99 itself carries an electronic image stamp of October 30 at 2:35 pm.

Neither filing shows when the outage ended or whether the FEC accepted the paper notice. Election Day is November 3, so late-contribution notices are at their densest.

Form 99 Correspondence
Thirteen Form 99s: replies to FEC letters, new committee registrations and a candidate repaying himself

Four are replies to FEC information requests or letters (Boren, Democratic Socialists of America, Los Angeles County Democratic Central Committee, Wanda James). Three are Form 1 organization statements filed under Form 99 (Amalgamated Bank PAC, Gun Rights America, Kiros for Congress). The rest include two filings tied to the FEC e-filing outage (Vega, Eisenhauer), a loan forgiveness letter and a termination companion letter in which the candidate repaid himself. The Mandel letter is the one worth reading closely.

  • David Mandel for Congress 2024 tells the FEC that candidate David Mandel moved the campaign's remaining $11,000-plus to himself as partial repayment of loans he says total nearly $50,000. Mandel, a California Democratic challenger, writes that his former treasurer stopped responding, that earlier quarterly reports wrongly showed negative cash on hand, and that re-created data files showed only $20,000 in personal loans. He then closed the account and filed a termination on October 9. Filed 10/30. (Form 99)
  • Boren for Congress is told by former Rep. Dan Boren that printing and compliance consulting costs were campaign related and the committee will not be redesignated. Boren's October 22 letter to the FEC's Reports Analysis Division replies to a question about the April and July quarterly reports. He says he may run again and will not convert to a multicandidate committee. Boren, an Oklahoma Democrat, was in Congress from 2005 to 2013. Filed 10/30. (Form 99)
  • Wanda James for Congress tells the FEC it refunded all nine general-election contributions totaling $6,750 on July 29. The reply to the September 24 RFAI argues that the refunds fell outside the July Quarterly Report period because June 30, the Colorado primary date, was also the close of books. They appear on the October Quarterly Report. The letter says no amendment is needed and suggests the FEC review whether activity falls in a report period before sending such requests. Filed 10/29.
