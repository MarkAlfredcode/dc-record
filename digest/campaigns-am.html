Top Story
Virginia House challenger's campaign reports fraud on its bank account, and says an FEC analyst told it to report the charges as expenses
Joy for Virginia filed a Form 99 on October 15 describing fraudulent activity on its campaign account.

Joy for Virginia, the committee of Democratic House challenger Joy Powers (Virginia), told the Federal Election Commission in a Form 99 received October 15 that it found fraudulent activity on its campaign bank account. The committee says it spoke with an FEC staffer named Michael at about 18:30 that day. At his instruction, it says, it filed the fraudulent expenses under Line 21 of its Form 3 while it pursues the matter with the financial institution. It promised more information to the FEC when available. The filing gives no dollar amount, no dates of the transactions and no account details, and it does not say who is suspected. The keyword hit is a lead and not a finding: the filing describes a loss to the committee, not misconduct by it.

Two other filings from the batch concern large corrections. Huerta Democracy PAC told FEC analyst Aaron Carrell on October 16 that outside counsel is reviewing how it calculated independent expenditures on its July Quarterly Report (05/14/2026–06/30/2026). The PAC says its preliminary review indicates some amounts may have been reported on an overinclusive basis, including general overhead and personnel costs. It says the review covers hundreds of accounting transactions and asks for the additional time it discussed with the Reports Analysis Division. Rolling Sea Action Fund says it filed amendments on October 15 reaching back to the first quarter of 2024 after a self-audit of cash on hand and Line 8 discrepancies that an FEC request for additional information (RFAI) dated February 24, 2026 had raised.

Sitting Lawmakers
Two House Democrats explain reporting errors in Form 99 letters

Two sitting members filed Form 99 correspondence this window. Both are routine fixes to dates and debt reporting. Neither involves a termination.

  • Rep. Yvette D. Clarke (D-NY), committee Clarke for Congress, Form 99 received 10/15, the response due date. This answers an FEC request about inconsistent debt reporting on the April 2026 Quarterly Report. The debt to Berger Hirschberg Strategies carried an incorrect date because of an administrative oversight; the committee says the date is fixed in an amended April Quarterly. The debt to Katz Compliance was not correctly linked to a repayment because of an administrative oversight and a software error. An amended Year-End 2025 Report adds a $2,800 debt incurred 12/15/25 and shows an 11/04/25 payment resolving a $2,844.52 debt incurred 8/6/25. Signed by treasurer Una Clarke. Filing.
  • Rep. Diana DeGette (D-CO), committee Diana DeGette for Congress, Form 99 received 10/16. While preparing the 48-hour report for 6/14/26, the committee found a discrepancy in the receipt dates of two contributions, from Kurken Alyanakian and Deborah Shpall. Both were made through ActBlue on 6/12/26 and appeared in the July Quarterly as received 6/12, but the committee received them 6/14/26. The Form 99 explains the difference and corrects the receipt date. Filing.
