House Budget Committee Takes Fiscal-Commission Debate to Dallas, With Panetta, Portman and Manchin Testifying
The field hearing is the clearest news item across an otherwise quiet three-day window for FEC correspondence, Hill financial disclosures and the Congressional Record.

The House Committee on the Budget holds a field hearing this afternoon, September 21, 2026, at 2 p.m. Eastern (11 a.m. Pacific) at Old Parkland in Dallas, Texas, titled "America's Fiscal Future: Examining the Need for a Fiscal Commission." The hearing explores whether Congress should revive a bipartisan commission structure to address long-term federal debt and deficits.

Scheduled witnesses are three former high-ranking Washington officials: Leon Panetta, a former member of Congress from California who also served as White House Chief of Staff, CIA Director and Secretary of Defense, now chairman of the Panetta Institute for Public Policy; Rob Portman, a former U.S. Senator and Representative from Ohio who once directed the Office of Management and Budget, now founder of the Portman Center for Policy Solutions; and Joe Manchin, a former U.S. Senator and Governor of West Virginia.

It is one of the few substantive items to surface anywhere in this window: no RFAIs were filed with the FEC in the past three days, the House Clerk's financial-disclosure index and the Senate's eFD system have shown no new filings since September 17, and both chambers of Congress remain in pro forma recess with no bills introduced. Two Non-Routine F99s: A Resolved RFAI and a Denied Filing-Frequency Switch

Beyond the three routine terminations tallied separately below, two non-routine Form 99 filings came in over the past three days — a Colorado Senate campaign closing out an old RFAI, and a PAC's rebuffed attempt to change how often it files reports.

  • Julie for Colorado, the U.S. Senate candidate committee for Julie Gonzales (D-Colo., a challenger who does not currently hold federal office), filed a Miscellaneous Submission responding to an RFAI the FEC issued August 2, 2026 over the committee's July Quarterly report. Treasurer Howie Stanger tells the commission the "excessive contributions in question" were refunded in the third quarter of 2026 and that the refunds will appear on the committee's upcoming October Quarterly report. Filed September 19, 2026. Filing.
  • No Going Back PAC Inc. had a request to switch to monthly FEC filing rejected. In a letter to Treasurer Charles Gantt, the commission's Compliance Branch (signed by Chief Kristin D. Roser) states the PAC's September 1, 2026 request "will not be considered a filing frequency change" because FEC regulations (11 C.F.R. § 104.5(c)) permit a committee to change its filing frequency no more than once per calendar year; the commission attached the PAC's existing reporting schedule for the remainder of 2026, including an October Monthly report and pre- and post-general reports tied to the November election. Filed September 18, 2026. Filing.
