Senate Kills Two Disapproval Votes, Heads Into Wednesday Cloture Fight Over Who Pays for Data Centers' Power

Both Tuesday votes fell short along mostly party lines, while a fight over the electricity costs of AI data centers reaches a cloture test this morning.

The House of Representatives was not in session on Tuesday, September 29, 2026, and will next convene only for a pro forma session on Thursday, October 1, 2026. The Senate, by contrast, ran a full day, convening at 10 a.m. and adjourning at 8:16 p.m., and took two roll-call votes that both failed.

By a vote of 48 yeas to 51 nays (Record Vote No. 251), the Senate declined to take up S.J. Res. 197, sponsored by Sen. Tammy Baldwin (D-WI), a resolution of congressional disapproval under the Congressional Review Act that would have voided a Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services rule on Affordable Care Act benefit and payment parameters for 2027 and the Basic Health Program. Shortly after, by 47 yeas to 51 nays (Record Vote No. 252), senators also rejected a motion to discharge the Committee on Foreign Relations from S. Res. 852, sponsored by Sen. Chris Van Hollen (D-MD), which sought information on Israel's human rights practices under Section 502B(c) of the Foreign Assistance Act of 1961.

The bigger fight is still unresolved: the Senate spent much of the day on a motion to proceed to H.R. 9340, the Ratepayer Protection Act, sponsored in the House by Rep. Gabe Evans (R-CO-8), which would amend the Public Utility Regulatory Policies Act of 1978 to set a federal standard for recovering the full, incremental cost of grid upgrades needed to serve "large-load customers" — in practice, the surging electricity demand from AI data centers. Senators reached a unanimous-consent agreement that cloture motions filed Monday, September 28 would ripen at 11:30 a.m. on Wednesday, September 30 — this morning — setting up the decisive test of whether the bill can advance.

Separately, the Senate passed roughly a dozen additional bills and resolutions by voice vote or unanimous consent Tuesday, including measures on tribal land transfers in Alaska and New Mexico, veterans' and Traumatic Brain Injury programs, wildfire air-quality monitoring, and historic-site designations; see the day's Daily Digest for the complete rundown. Separately, senators introduced 31 new bills and 13 new resolutions, detailed in full below.

House Stays Dark, Senate Introduces 44 New Measures in a Single Day

With the House out of session, all of Tuesday's action was on the Senate side, where members introduced thirty-one bills, one joint resolution, and twelve additional resolutions — forty-four measures in a single legislative day, all read the first and second times by unanimous consent and referred as indicated below. None were House measures, so none required the congress.gov cross-check that this edition otherwise applies to introduced H.R. or H.Res. numbers.

Two bills stand out for their overlap: Sen. Jeff Merkley (D-OR) introduced a pair of companion measures — one barring federal funds from any new construction at Memorial Circle at Columbia Island/Lady Bird Johnson Park in Washington, D.C., the other prohibiting the demolition of any presidential memorial — both with bipartisan cosponsors.
