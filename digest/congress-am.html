Top Story

Raskin bill would put Trump-era 'authoritarianism' on permanent display at the Smithsonian

On a two-minute House session with no votes, Rep. Jamie Raskin introduced the day's most politically charged bill.

The House met for two minutes on October 6, 2026, with no recorded votes and no quorum calls, and the Senate held a pro forma session of about half a minute. Most of the 22 measures introduced were routine, so the substantive news is in the text of the bills themselves.

Rep. Jamie Raskin (D-MD-8) introduced H.R. 10765, which would direct the Secretary of the Smithsonian Institution to collect, preserve and display, in a permanent exhibition at the National Museum of American History, artifacts reflecting "the rise of governmental propaganda, corruption, and authoritarianism" during the two presidencies of Donald J. Trump. The bill was referred to the Committee on House Administration and, in addition, the Committee on Natural Resources.

Also introduced was H.R. 10759 from Rep. John James (R-MI-10), a tariff-funded program to shield manufacturers, workers and farmers from harm caused by tariffs and foreign retaliation.

On the Record

House meets for two minutes; 22 measures introduced, no votes

The House met at 4 p.m. and adjourned at 4:02 p.m. on October 6, 2026, with no Yea and Nay votes, no recorded votes and no quorum calls. The Senate met in a pro forma session for under a minute (1:31:12 to 1:31:45 p.m.) and adjourned until 1:30 p.m. on Friday, October 9, 2026 (Congressional Record, October 6, 2026).

Seventeen House bills (H.R. 10751–10767), one concurrent resolution and four simple resolutions were introduced. Beyond the Raskin and James bills, the list includes a bipartisan resolution, H. Res. 1618, marking the third anniversary of the October 7, 2023, attacks on Israel. The House also filed two Veterans' Affairs committee reports, on H.R. 6698 and H.R. 7260. The full list follows; sponsor party and state are from the Congress.gov API.

  • H.R. 10751, sponsored by Rep. Jack Bergman (R-MI) — Removes restrictions on dental therapist services under the Community Health Aide Program in the Indian Health Care Improvement Act. Referred to Natural Resources and Energy and Commerce.
  • H.R. 10752, sponsored by Rep. Troy A. Carter (D-LA) — Creates a task force on artificial intelligence inside the Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency. Referred to Homeland Security.
  • H.R. 10753, sponsored by Rep. Elijah Crane (R-AZ) — The Tribal Veterans Voucher Flexibility Act: lets the Department of Housing and Urban Development voluntarily reallocate vouchers under the Tribal HUD-VASH program. Referred to Financial Services.
  • H.R. 10754, sponsored by Rep. Elijah Crane (R-AZ) — The Rural Disaster Fairness Act of 2026: updates indicators of a disaster's local impact in the FAA Reauthorization Act of 2018. Referred to Transportation and Infrastructure.
  • H.R. 10755, sponsored by Rep. Jasmine Crockett (D-TX) — Names the post office at 475 East FM 1382 in Cedar Hill, Texas, the Eugene Gates Jr. Post Office Building. Referred to Oversight and Government Reform.
  • H.R. 10756, sponsored by Rep. Mike Ezell (R-MS) — Reauthorizes Regional Ocean Partnerships. Referred to Natural Resources.
  • H.R. 10757, sponsored by Rep. Josh Gottheimer (D-NJ) — Sets a limit on United States contributions to the United Nations. Referred to Foreign Affairs.
  • H.R. 10758, sponsored by Rep. Jeff Hurd (R-CO) — Directs the Secretary of Veterans Affairs to award grants to state entities for structured outdoor recreation programs for veterans. Referred to Veterans' Affairs.
  • H.R. 10759, sponsored by Rep. John James (R-MI) — Creates a tariff-funded program to protect United States manufacturers, workers, farmers and supply chains from economic harm caused by tariffs and foreign retaliation, and to encourage reshoring. Referred to Financial Services and Ways and Means.
  • H.R. 10760, sponsored by Rep. Thomas H. Kean (R-NJ) — The Healthy Minds for a Better Future Act: sets up a multiagency Middle School Mental Health Task Force at the Department of Education and the Department of Health and Human Services. Referred to Education and Workforce and Energy and Commerce.
  • H.R. 10761, sponsored by Rep. Ro Khanna (D-CA) — The Campus Accountability and Safety Act of 2026: amends the Jeanne Clery Campus Safety Act to combat campus sexual assault. Referred to Education and Workforce and the Judiciary.
  • H.R. 10762, sponsored by Rep. Michael Lawler (R-NY) — The Anthony Amoros Law Right to Know Act: requires sellers of used vehicles to keep a working airbag system or clearly disclose when an airbag is missing or disabled. Referred to Energy and Commerce and the Judiciary.
  • H.R. 10763, sponsored by Rep. Julia Letlow (R-LA) — The Know Your Risk Act: requires the Secretary of Health and Human Services to report to Congress on breast cancer risk assessment tools. Referred to Energy and Commerce.
  • H.R. 10764, sponsored by Rep. Gwen Moore (D-WI) — The Executive Settlement Review Act: requires certain procedures for compromise settlements or awards involving executive branch officials. Referred to the Judiciary.
  • H.R. 10765, sponsored by Rep. Jamie Raskin (D-MD) — Directs the Smithsonian Institution to display, in a permanent exhibition at the National Museum of American History, artifacts reflecting the rise of governmental propaganda, corruption and authoritarianism during the two presidencies of Donald J. Trump. Referred to House Administration and Natural Resources.
  • H.R. 10766, sponsored by Rep. Keith Self (R-TX) — Directs the Secretary of Defense to add assets critical to the Golden Dome missile defense system to the Department of Defense Critical Asset List. Referred to Armed Services.
  • H.R. 10767, sponsored by Rep. Adrian Smith (R-NE) — The Fair Play Enforcement Act: imposes an excise tax on colleges and universities that allow specified individuals to take part in intercollegiate athletics. Referred to Ways and Means.
  • H. Con. Res. 123, sponsored by Rep. Teresa Leger Fernandez (D-NM) — Recognizes the disparity in wages paid to Latinas and its impact on women, families and the economy. Referred to Education and Workforce.
  • H. Res. 1617, sponsored by Rep. Earl L. "Buddy" Carter (R-GA) — Recognizes October as American Pharmacy Month. Referred to Energy and Commerce.
  • H. Res. 1618, sponsored by Rep. Josh Gottheimer (D-NJ) — Marks the third anniversary of the October 7, 2023, attacks on Israel, honors the victims and hostages, and calls for the complete disarmament of Hamas. Referred to Foreign Affairs.
  • H. Res. 1619, sponsored by Rep. Glenn Thompson (R-PA) — Reaffirms German-American friendship and supports continued cooperation between the United States and Germany. Referred to Foreign Affairs.
  • H. Res. 1620, sponsored by Rep. Nikema Williams (D-GA) — Supports the goals of National Nonviolence Week to raise awareness of youth violence. Referred to Oversight and Government Reform.
