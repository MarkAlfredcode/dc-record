Senate passes Protect College Sports Act after beating back a stack of contested amendments

A 77-22 vote sends Sen. Ted Cruz's NCAA-backed overhaul to the House after senators fought over ticket prices, coaches' pay and athlete insurance funding.

The Senate voted 77 to 22 (Record Vote No. 250) to pass S. 4668, the Protect College Sports Act of 2026, sponsored by Sen. Ted Cruz (R-TX). The bill protects the name, image and likeness rights of college athletes and aims to promote fair competition among intercollegiate athletics programs; it now heads to the House.

Passage came after a marathon amendment fight on the underlying substitute. Senators adopted, 96 to 1 (Vote No. 245), an amendment from Sen. Tammy Baldwin (D-WI) modifying how the bill relates to existing law, and also adopted amendments from Sen. Tim Scott (R-SC) requiring disclosure of foreign financing of intercollegiate athletics and from Sen. Ashley Moody (R-FL) narrowing the bill's restrictions on conference mergers and acquisitions. A one-word-title amendment from Sen. Rand Paul (R-KY) "relating to collegiate sports" failed 1 to 94.

A trio of amendments aimed at expanding athlete protections and consumer safeguards all fell short of the 60-vote threshold senators had agreed would apply and were ruled not agreed to: a cap on college-sports ticket price increases from Sen. John Kennedy (R-LA) failed 42-54; a proposal from Sen. Cory Booker (D-NJ) and Sen. Richard Blumenthal (D-CT) to raise the funding limit for post-eligibility insurance and catastrophic-injury coverage for student athletes failed 49-50; and a Booker amendment with Sen. Chris Murphy (D-CT) setting new standards for varsity-sport coaches failed 47-52.

On the Record

Senate clears eight bills in a single day while the House sits for two minutes

The House held only a pro forma session, gaveling in at noon and adjourning two minutes later with no yea-and-nay votes, no recorded votes and no quorum calls; Rep. Adrian Smith of Nebraska presided as Speaker pro tempore. The chamber's only substantive business was introducing 25 public bills and five resolutions.

The Senate, by contrast, cleared a full slate of legislation beyond the college-sports fight. It discharged and passed the Common Cents Act (H.R. 10167), which directs the Treasury Secretary to stop minting the penny and round cash transactions to the nearest five cents, and passed six more bills by unanimous consent or voice vote: a Young Women's Breast Health Education and Awareness reauthorization (S. 2339), an ALS-therapy-access reauthorization (H.R. 8205), a bill imposing sanctions over forced organ harvesting in China (S. 4009), a food-safety bill (S. 4974), a Terrorism Risk Insurance Program reauthorization (S. 4395), a National Taxpayer Advocate authority bill (H.R. 997), and a bankruptcy-eligibility threshold adjustment (H.R. 7730). Senators also agreed to a resolution honoring the late Sen. Nancy Kassebaum, former senator of Kansas, and several commemorative resolutions.

The Senate began but did not finish cloture proceedings on two bills — a restriction on stock trading by members of Congress and their families (H.R. 7008) and a utility cost-recovery standard (H.R. 9340) — with cloture votes now teed up for Wednesday, September 30. It also reached a unanimous-consent agreement to vote at 2:15 p.m. Tuesday on S.J. Res. 197, a resolution of congressional disapproval targeting an HHS rule on Affordable Care Act benefit and payment parameters for 2027, and began cloture on the nomination of Keith Sonderling of Florida to be Secretary of Labor. Five bills and six resolutions were introduced in the Senate.

Two pairs of same-day introductions were near-identical in subject: H.R. 10631 and S. 5568 are House and Senate companions on class-size reduction grants, and H.R. 10628 and S. 5571 both target the timing of Medicare price negotiations for biosimilar drugs.
