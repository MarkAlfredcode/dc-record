Top Story

Senate confirms Keith Sonderling as Secretary of Labor, 47–41, then leaves for a five-week recess

The Senate cleared a late-night run of nominations and bills on September 30 before adjourning until November 9; the House of Representatives was not in session.

The Senate confirmed Keith Sonderling as Secretary of Labor by a vote of 47 yeas to 41 nays (Vote No. EX. 256), according to the Congressional Record Daily Digest. Earlier in the day it agreed, 53 to 47 (Vote No. 255), to close debate on the nomination. It also confirmed ten ambassadors, including nominees to Sierra Leone, Kenya, Malaysia, Nicaragua, Kyrgyzstan, Togo, Gabon, Benin, Malawi and Cabo Verde, and one Air Force general officer nomination.

The chamber convened at 10:15 a.m. and adjourned at 11:25 p.m. It will hold pro forma sessions, with no business conducted, on a set schedule from Thursday, October 1 through Thursday, November 5, and return in full at 3 p.m. on Monday, November 9. Before leaving, it began work on the motion to proceed to H.R. 2347, the Survivor Justice Tax Prevention Act, which would exclude from gross income damages, other than punitive damages, received on account of sexual acts or sexual contact. A cloture vote is set for 5:30 p.m. on November 9.

The House of Representatives was not in session on September 30; its next meeting is a pro forma session at 11:30 a.m. on October 1.

On the Record

Senate introduces 101 measures and clears two dozen bills before recess

The Congressional Record for September 30, 2026 (the most recent issue published as of this writing) shows 78 Senate bills (S. 5604–5681), 2 Senate joint resolutions (S.J.Res. 219–220) and 21 Senate resolutions (S.Res. 929–949) introduced, a typical pre-recess flood. The House of Representatives was not in session, so no House measures were introduced.

Several stand out. Senator Ashley Moody (R-FL) introduced a run of bills and a balanced budget constitutional amendment (S.J.Res. 219). S. 5618 would create an Inspector General for the judicial branch. S. 5680, S. 5679 and S. 5678 from Senator Cory A. Booker (D-NJ) would restrict how Navy ships are named, including keeping the name “USS Doris Miller” on carrier CVN-81. Many of the rest are routine commemorative resolutions. The full list follows, none omitted.

The Senate also passed or agreed to the following measures on September 30. Items numbered H.R. cleared the Senate without changes unless noted. All descriptions come from the Daily Digest, linked here; the Digest also lists S.Res. 866, supporting October 2026 as Substance Use & Misuse Prevention Month, which was agreed to.
