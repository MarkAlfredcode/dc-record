New Nonprofit Registers to Lobby Congress on Justice Department Independence, 'Politicization of Law Enforcement'
The JC155 Foundation, a Delaware-based nonprofit, filed its first lobbying registration disclosing two in-house lobbyists working on federal employee rights and Justice Department independence.

The JC155 Foundation posted a new Senate Office of Public Records registration this window, listing itself as both the registrant and its own client — a self-select filing structure typically used by newly formed advocacy nonprofits lobbying on their own behalf. The Wilmington, Delaware-based group describes itself as "non-profit advocacy for DOJ employees and rule of law," and lists Sara Zdeb and Stacey Young as its two lobbyists, both marked new to the registration.

The filing's issue description is explicit: "Rights of federal employees (specifically in regards to DOJ employees), independence of the Department of Justice, politicization of law enforcement." The registration covers four general issue areas — Civil Rights/Civil Liberties, Government Issues, Labor Issues/Antitrust/Workplace, and Law Enforcement/Crime/Criminal Justice — and was posted by contact Cary Cheng.

A new organization registering specifically to lobby Congress on Justice Department independence and the treatment of the Department of Justice's own workforce is a notable addition to the third-quarter lobbying rolls, arriving amid continued public scrutiny of leadership turnover and internal disputes at the department.

33 New Lobbying Registrations Led by Medical-Device and Trade Clients; Uber and Hospital Group Disclose Six-Figure Contributions

The Senate Office of Public Records posted 33 new lobbying registrations in this window, beyond the JC155 Foundation filing covered above. Jeffrey J. Kimbell & Associates, Inc. picked up four new medical-device clients (ClearFlow, Proverum Medical, Flex Vascular and HistoSonics), and Continental Strategy, LLC added three new clients spanning maritime, dermatology and Latin America investment issues. Amkor Technology, Inc., a semiconductor packaging and testing company, registered to lobby in-house on trade and tariff policy, export controls, and the advanced manufacturing investment credit. Bellementis PLLC registered two related clients on the same day — aerospace manufacturer Destinus Group BV and an individual, Mikhail Kokorich — both on defense and financial-services issues.

Beyond new business, 32 additional quarterly reports, amendments and terminations posted in the same window, mostly routine renewals and amendments. One item of note: Jeffrey J. Kimbell & Associates, Inc. filed a third-quarter termination of its registration for Neuralink Corp., alongside a termination for Sky Marketing Corporation and a registration amendment adding JCyte, Inc.

On the campaign-finance side, seven of 52 LD-203 filings posted in this window disclosed actual contributions; the other 45 reported none. The largest by far came from Uber Technologies, Inc., whose mid-year report (covering January–June 2026) disclosed $144,500 across 34 items — a mix of its political action committee's FECA contributions to members of both parties and corporate "honorary expense" payments, including $12,000 to the Congressional Black Caucus Foundation honoring Rep. Jim Clyburn and $10,500 to the Independent Women's Forum honoring White House Chief of Staff Susie Wiles. America's Essential Hospitals Political Action Committee disclosed $60,500 across 34 contributions spread across both parties, from $5,000 to Lori Trahan and David Valadao to $1,000 apiece for members including Ron Wyden and Josh Hawley. Lobbyist Kyriakos Pagonis of Murray, Montgomery & O'Donnell reported personal contributions to several California House members, including Rep. Jared Huffman.
