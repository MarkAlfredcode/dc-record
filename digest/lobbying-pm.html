Top Story
A year-long DOJ pardon push for Richard Scrushy comes to a close
Fahmy Hudome International, Inc. told the Senate this week it is no longer lobbying the Justice Department for a pardon on behalf of the former HealthSouth Corp. chief executive.

Fahmy Hudome International, Inc., a Washington consulting firm run by lobbyist Randa Fahmy, filed a termination notice with the Senate on September 22 ending its lobbying engagement for client Richard Scrushy, a Texas-based "private citizen" under the filing's own description. The termination is effective September 15, 2026. Every activity report the firm has filed on Scrushy's behalf — going back to the original registration — lists a single lobbying issue: "Granting of Pardon," with the U.S. Department of Justice as the only government entity contacted.

The engagement ran for more than a year. Fahmy Hudome registered to lobby for Scrushy in the second quarter of 2025 and filed activity reports every quarter since, through the second quarter of 2026, each one repeating the same single-issue description of the work. No dollar figures for income or expenses were disclosed on the termination filing.

Scrushy is the former chief executive of HealthSouth Corp., the rehabilitation-hospital chain, who was convicted in a federal bribery case in Alabama in the 2000s. The Senate's lobbying database does not disclose why the engagement ended, and nothing in the public filings indicates whether a pardon was granted, denied, or never acted on.

36 new lobbying registrations, dozens of LD-203 contribution filings

New client business skewed heavily toward health care and defense: Cozen O'Connor Public Strategies alone filed nine new registrations, mostly Medicare/Medicaid-focused nonprofit and hospital clients, while Ballard Partners registered three new clients spanning tariffs, defense technology and foreign market entry. C2 Strategies both registered two new clients (BC3 Technologies, Hop Aero) and terminated a third (Motiv Space Systems) on the same day.

Separately, a batch of newly posted LD-203 contribution reports — mostly "Mid-Year" and "Year-End" catch-up filings covering contributions made earlier in 2026 — showed lobbyist Michael Clauser of Ark Strategy and lobbyist Barbara Comstock, the former Virginia congresswoman now at Baker Donelson Bearman Caldwell & Berkowitz, among the most prolific individual givers, each spreading contributions across more than a dozen candidate committees.
