Top Story
New nonprofit registers to fight Union Pacific–Norfolk Southern rail merger
Stop the Rail Merger, Inc. hired CGCN Group to lobby Congress against the proposed tie-up, backed by chemical, farm-bureau and rival-railroad interests.

A new 501(c)4 called Stop the Rail Merger, Inc. registered to lobby against the proposed merger of Union Pacific and Norfolk Southern, retaining CGCN Group, LLC effective April 20, 2026. The registration, posted September 23, lists three lobbyists — Alex Renjel, Andrew Gallimore and Harrison Fields — tasked with "engaging with lawmakers" to oppose the deal.

The filing's disclosed "covered positions" show two of the three lobbyists came from government: Fields previously served as an assistant press secretary in the Trump White House, and Renjel spent six years as a staffer on the Senate Environment and Public Works Committee.

The group's disclosed affiliated organizations sketch the coalition arrayed against the merger: the American Chemistry Council, Soo Line Railroad Company (doing business as CPKC), The American Farm Bureau Federation, BNSF Railway Company and the Teamsters Rail Conference — a mix of chemical shippers, a competing railroad, farm interests and organized labor. Soo Line Railroad Company also separately disclosed a lobbyist campaign contribution today; see K Street below.

K Street
11 new registrations, and an unusually heavy day for lobbyist campaign-contribution disclosures

Eleven firms filed new Senate lobbying registrations in the past day, alongside roughly 75 routine 2nd- and 3rd-quarter activity reports, amendments and terminations (rollup below). The day's most notable new filing — CGCN Group's registration for an anti-merger nonprofit — is covered in the Top Story above.

Separately, LD-203 lobbyist campaign-contribution reports posted in unusually high volume: of 258 disclosure filings posted, 39 reported actual contributions, totaling roughly $248,900 in itemized gifts to federal candidates, party and leadership committees, and PACs — far more than DC Record typically sees in a single day's window, likely reflecting a batch of mid-year (semiannual) reports posted together. Soo Line Railroad Company, disclosed above as a member of the new coalition opposing its own merger, reported a $2,500 contribution to Onder for Congress.
