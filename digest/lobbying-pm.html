Top Story
MALDEF discloses $802,000 in previously unreported “honorary expense” spending on lawmakers, some dating to 2022
A batch of newly filed and amended LD-203 reports shows the civil-rights group covered gala costs tied to events honoring six sitting members of Congress and federal officials.

Mexican American Legal Defense and Educational Fund (MALDEF) posted six LD-203 lobbying contribution reports on lda.gov this week, each disclosing spending under the “Honorary Expenses” category — a distinct LD-203 line for organizations that cover the cost of an event honoring a covered legislative or executive branch official, separate from direct campaign contributions. Several are original reports filed years after their due date; others are amendments correcting previously filed reports. In every case the contributor is listed as “Self” (MALDEF), the payee as “Various vendors,” and the comments cite MALDEF's Annual Awards Gala.

The six filings, read directly from the Senate Office of Public Records' LD-203 database, total $802,276: $121,458 honoring Isabel Casillas Guzman, then-Administrator of the U.S. Small Business Administration, dated April 2024 and filed as a Mid-Year Report; $100,961 honoring Rep. Lucille Roybal-Allard, dated September 2022 and filed as a Year-End Amendment; $101,931 honoring Sen. Catherine Cortez Masto and Robert Santos, then-Director of the U.S. Census Bureau, dated April 2023 and filed as a Mid-Year Amendment; $188,573 honoring Rep. Grace Napolitano at a Los Angeles gala, dated November 2024 and filed as a Year-End Report; $104,382 honoring Rep. Greg Casar at a San Antonio gala, dated September 2023 and filed as a Year-End Report; and $184,971 honoring Rep. Joaquin Castro, dated April 2025 and filed as a Mid-Year Amendment.

The same batch includes two more MALDEF filings with no dollar figures: a Year-End Report for 2023 whose “No Contributions” box is checked despite a comment noting a San Antonio gala service award, and a 2017 Year-End Amendment reporting no contributions. A current, clean Mid-Year 2026 report was also posted. None of the newly filed documents allege wrongdoing by the honorees — LD-203's honorary-expense category exists precisely so that groups footing the bill for their own award events involving covered officials disclose it.

K Street
19 new registrations track defense-budget season and AI/uncrewed-systems spending; disclosed campaign giving tops $527,000

Nineteen new lobbying registrations posted since yesterday cluster around FY27/FY28 defense appropriations, aerospace and uncrewed-systems programs, and health-policy fights over Medicare physician payment and the No Surprises Act.

Separately, lobbyists and registrants filed 73 LD-203 contribution reports with actual disclosed giving. After collapsing duplicate organization/lobbyist filings covering the same underlying checks, that's at least $527,732 in FECA campaign contributions from K Street to federal candidates and party committees — on top of MALDEF's $802,276 in honorary-expense disclosures detailed in today's Top Story.

Elsewhere, 75 additional quarterly activity reports, amendments and terminations were posted; most are routine renewals from repeat filers, with ten terminations and a handful of notable client amendments called out below.
