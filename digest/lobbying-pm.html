Ten Aerospace and Defense Clients Jump Firms in a Single Afternoon, Filings Show
The same two lobbyists filed terminations at one registered firm and new registrations for nearly the same client roster at another within a two-hour window Wednesday.

In a tightly sequenced set of Lobbying Disclosure Act filings posted Wednesday afternoon, Velocity Government Relations, LLC — a Washington firm describing itself as a "DC-area consultancy, focused on supporting aerospace & defense companies & organizations," with lobbyist Erin Neal as its listed contact — filed third-quarter terminations for 11 clients between 1:42 p.m. and 2:09 p.m. Eastern. Starting two minutes after the last of those terminations posted, Strategic Marketing Innovations, a separate registrant based at 200 Massachusetts Avenue NW whose contact is Kenneth Wetzel Jr., filed brand-new registrations for 10 of those same clients between 2:07 p.m. and 3:26 p.m. — with Neal and a second lobbyist, Jo-Anne Sears, listed as lobbyists of record on the new filings.

The roster that moved reads like a who's-who of the small-satellite and munitions-production world: Decision Sciences Incorporated (DSI), Earth Observant Inc., Innovative Rocket Technologies, Inc. (iRocket), L3Harris Technologies, Inc., Exquadrum, NextGen Aeronautics, Science Applications International Corporation (SAIC), Savit Corporation, TRL11, and Umbra Lab, Inc. Every one of them was re-registered at Strategic Marketing Innovations under the "Defense" issue code, with activity descriptions covering propulsion and munitions production, national-security space, ISR and defense-industrial-base work — closely mirroring the descriptions on the Velocity terminations. One Velocity client, Ad Astra Rocket Company, was also terminated Wednesday but had not resurfaced in a new registration anywhere in Wednesday's filings as of this report.

Nothing in the public record explains the move — LDA filings don't require registrants to state a reason for terminating or registering — but the near-total, same-day migration of an entire aerospace/defense client book from one boutique shop to another, carried by the same two named lobbyists, leaves an unusually clean paper trail of a K Street team changing firms.

52 New Lobbying Registrations Posted, Plus a $144,500 Uber LD-203 Disclosure

The Senate's Lobbying Disclosure Act system logged 52 new registrations and 64 additional quarterly reports, amendments and terminations in the past day. Ten of the new registrations — the aerospace/defense roster that moved from Velocity Government Relations, LLC to Strategic Marketing Innovations — are covered in today's Top Story and aren't repeated in the table below.

Seven registrants also posted LD-203 lobbyist campaign-contribution reports for the mid-year period. The biggest by far came from Uber Technologies, Inc., which disclosed $144,500 in political and officeholder-related spending: $44,000 across 27 separate contributions to 21 House and Senate campaign committees via its PAC, plus $100,500 in honorary- and meeting-expense payments to congressional-linked organizations, including $30,000 to the Congressional Institute and $25,000 to the Congressional Hispanic Leadership Institute.
