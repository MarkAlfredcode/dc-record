Top Story
Two Ex-Members of Congress Turned Lobbyists Disclose Broad 2026 Campaign Giving
New LD-203 filings show Barbara Comstock and Joe Crowley spreading money across a bipartisan mix of 2026 candidates and party committees.

Two lobbyists who once sat in the House disclosed sizable rounds of campaign giving in LD-203 semiannual reports posted to the Senate's lobbying database overnight. Barbara Comstock, the former Republican congresswoman from Virginia who is now a registered lobbyist at Baker Donelson Bearman Caldwell & Berkowitz, reported $9,550 in contributions made across the first half of 2026 to a bipartisan and often anti-Trump slate of 12 candidates and committees — including Pennsylvania Governor Josh Shapiro's re-election campaign, Republican-turned-independent commentator George Conway's congressional bid, Senate campaigns for Roy Cooper in North Carolina and Seth Moulton in Massachusetts, and gubernatorial campaigns for Kelly Ayotte in New Hampshire and Ghazala Hashmi's lieutenant governor bid in Virginia.

Separately, Joe Crowley, the former Democratic congressman from New York and one-time House Democratic Caucus chair who now lobbies at Dentons US LLP, reported $15,000 in contributions, including a $10,000 check written directly to the Democratic Congressional Campaign Committee and four-figure gifts to Representative Joyce Beatty's campaign, Representative Linda Sanchez's Senate committee Stand With Sanchez, Representative Robin Kelly's Illinois Senate campaign, and former Virginia first lady Dorothy McAuliffe's congressional bid.

Between the two filings, the former lawmakers-turned-lobbyists account for $24,550 of the roughly $43,850 in itemized lobbyist and registrant contributions reported in today's LD-203 batch — more detail in the K Street section below.

K Street
30 new lobbying registrations, led by a health-policy hiring spree at Cozen O'Connor

Cozen O'Connor Public Strategies filed 10 of today's 30 new registrations, all but two of them for hospital systems, safety-net health plans and disability-services groups working Medicare and Medicaid issues — a notable single-day build-out of a health lobbying practice. The rest of the day's registrations were scattered across defense, pharma, financial services and municipal-government clients.

Separately, lobbyists and registrant firms reported $43,850.05 in itemized 2026 campaign contributions on LD-203s posted overnight; the two largest filers, Barbara Comstock and Joe Crowley, are detailed in today's Top Story above.
