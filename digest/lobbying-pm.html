Top Story
Eight aerospace and defense contractors swap K Street reps in a single afternoon
Velocity Government Relations terminated its registrations for 11 space, rocket-propulsion and defense-technology clients on September 16 — the same day eight of them re-registered with a different firm, Strategic Marketing Innovations.

A same-day filing pattern in the Senate's lobbying database shows a cluster of small aerospace, satellite and munitions-technology companies moving their federal lobbying representation from one boutique firm to another within a few hours on September 16. Velocity Government Relations, LLC filed third-quarter terminations, all between 1:42 p.m. and 2:10 p.m. Eastern, for 11 clients: Ad Astra Rocket Company, Umbra Lab, Inc., NextGen Aeronautics, Inc., L3Harris Technologies, Inc., Earth Observant Inc., Science Applications International Corporation, Exquadrum, TRL11, SAVIT, Innovative Rocket Technologies Inc. (IROCKET), and Decision Sciences Incorporated (DSI).

Within roughly the same window — new registrations posted between 2:07 p.m. and 3:26 p.m. Eastern that day — eight of those exact same companies filed brand-new LDA registrations naming a different firm, Strategic Marketing Innovations, as their new registrant: Decision Sciences Incorporated (DSI) (defense supply-chain issues), Earth Observant Inc. (very-low-earth-orbit imaging satellites), Innovative Rocket Technologies, Inc. (IROCKET) (solid rocket motor propulsion), Exquadrum (propellant mixing and energetics), NextGen Aeronautics (defense aerospace R&D), SAVIT Corporation (munitions and counter-UAS), TRL11 (space domain awareness), and Umbra Lab, Inc. (commercial remote sensing) — each new registration lists Defense as its lobbying issue.

The overlap is exact: every one of Strategic Marketing Innovations' eight new clients that day is a company Velocity Government Relations terminated hours earlier. Three of Velocity's former clients — Ad Astra Rocket Company, L3Harris Technologies and Science Applications International Corporation (SAIC) — do not appear in Strategic Marketing Innovations' new filings from this window, meaning they either signed with a different firm outside this reporting window, went in-house, or dropped federal lobbying representation entirely. The pattern reads as a single-day bloc transfer of a small-satellite and rocket-propulsion lobbying book from one shop to another, not a coincidence of unrelated client decisions.

K Street
41 new lobbying registrations, led by a 12-client single-day surge at one firm

The Senate lobbying database logged 41 new registrations and 107 total filings (including quarterly reports, amendments and terminations) posted between September 15 and 16. BGR Government Affairs filed 12 of the day's new registrations by itself, covering clients from Apple Inc.'s fintech-adjacent peer Block, Inc. to nuclear-fusion developer Shine Technologies, LLC — a volume that looks like batch onboarding rather than a single news event. Elsewhere, Bellwether Government Affairs LLC registered to lobby for Apple Inc. on tax, trade and technology policy, and Ballard Partners registered Aldyl Energia C.A., a Venezuelan-linked energy services company, on oil and gas issues tied to its Venezuelan operations. Also notable: Democracy Forward, a legal advocacy group, self-registered across 28 separate LDA issue codes — nearly the full code list — with activity described as covering "election protection and integrity," oversight of the Department of Justice and the Attorney General nomination, and oversight of several federal agencies.

As detailed in the Top Story above, the day's terminations and new registrations from Velocity Government Relations, LLC and Strategic Marketing Innovations show eight small aerospace/defense clients switching representation in the same afternoon.

On LD-203 lobbyist contributions, only 3 of 13 filings posted this window reported actual contributions; the rest checked "no contributions." Neither recipient below appears elsewhere in today's report.
