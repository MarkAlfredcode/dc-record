Top Story
A Fringe Florida Campaign Buries Its FEC File in Plaques, Posters and Ballot-Conspiracy Claims
The committee behind a minor congressional candidacy filed six unrelated "miscellaneous" documents with the FEC on Sept. 17, including a claim that a sitting congresswoman and several named officials engineered his illegal removal from the ballot.

The Campaign Committee To Elect Clifford G. Fleetwood For U.S. Congress (C00951848), the committee for Florida's 20th Congressional District candidate Clifford G. Fleetwood, filed six separate FEC Form 99 "Miscellaneous Submission" documents on Sept. 17 alone. None of the six appears to respond to any pending FEC compliance request — Form 99 is ordinarily used to answer a Request for Additional Information or amend a report, but nothing in FEC's public file shows an open RFAI to this committee.

Four of the six are promotional ephemera: a "2026 U.S. Congressional Campaign Poster," a campaign sign filing, and two "commemorative plaques" — one citing "NRSC Member in Good Standing" status with the National Republican Senatorial Committee, the other citing recognitions from the National Security Agency, National Police Association and Florida Sheriff's Association, plus "U.S. Army and U.S. Coast Guard Honorable Service Discharges" recognition.

The most striking of the six, titled "Valid 2026 Florida Republican Party Affiliation Documentation," asserts that Fleetwood "was illegally removed from all Florida elections ballots" and states the document will be used as evidence in a forthcoming federal injunction against Rep. Debbie Wasserman Schultz, Florida Secretary of State Cord Byrd, Secretary of State records division director Ikay Reeve, Broward Chief Judge Carol Lisa-Phillips and Broward County Circuit Judge Marlon Jay Weiss — a case the filing says has already been "commenced" before U.S. Chief District Judge James E. Boasberg in Washington. FEC's own candidate database lists Fleetwood's committee as unaffiliated with any party, not Republican, a discrepancy the filing doesn't address. The filing is signed by "The Law Offices of Clifford G. Fleetwood P.A."

All six filings were received by FEC on Sept. 17 but did not clear FEC's receipt_date-filterable index in time for this edition until today — a two-day lag consistent with filings surfacing late elsewhere in today's batch (see Requests for Additional Information, below).

Sitting Lawmakers
Five incumbents' committees hear from the FEC — two are approved to wind down entirely

Five sitting House members' committees appear in Thursday's FEC correspondence: two draw fresh Requests for Additional Information over recurring compliance issues, one defends its continued operation, and two see the FEC formally approve termination of their committees.

  • Rep. William P. Huizenga (R-MI-2): Huizenga For Congress received an RFAI on its July Quarterly report flagging apparent excessive individual contributions. The Commission acknowledges the committee's "additional explanation regarding the committee's corrective action taken for some of these contributions" but notes at least one excessive contribution still hasn't been refunded, redesignated or reattributed more than 60 days after receipt. Filing.
  • Rep. Gus M. Bilirakis (R-FL-12): Bilirakis For Congress received an RFAI asking the committee to clarify a Schedule B disbursement described only as "Fundraising Services" — FEC rules require a specific statement of purpose for itemized disbursements. Filing.
  • Rep. Robert Gibbs (R-OH-18): Gibbs For Congress filed a Form 99 response to an RFAI on its April and July Quarterly reports, asserting all committee expenses were proper under Section 527 of the tax code and confirming it does not intend to terminate, convert to a multicandidate committee, or redesignate as a principal campaign committee. Filing.
  • Rep. Robin Kelly (D-IL-2): the FEC approved termination of Robin Kelly For Congress (C00539866). Filing.
  • Rep. Julia Letlow (R-LA-5): the FEC approved termination of Julia Letlow For Congress (C00766428). Filing.
Requests for Additional Information
A Senate challenger draws a fourth straight RFAI; two oddly named PACs face a legitimacy check

Twelve new RFAIs surfaced today, all received by FEC on Sept. 17 but only indexed into today's window (a two-day lag). Filings not covered under Sitting Lawmakers, above, break down as follows.

  • Angela Nixon's Florida Senate campaign, Angie For Florida — a Democratic challenger — received four separate RFAIs on Sept. 17 covering its April Quarterly, July Quarterly, Year-End 2025 and 12-Day Pre-Primary reports, all treasurer David Geller. Each cites the same recurring problem: dozens of apparent excessive individual contributions (from donors including Zouhaier Hamami, Barbara Stiefel, Autumn Stokes and others, many spanning multiple reporting periods) that have not been refunded, redesignated or reattributed. The Year-End 2025 letter adds a $5,000 apparent-excessive transfer from Rep. Rashida Tlaib's campaign committee, a $300 contribution from an unregistered organization (NW St Johns County United For Progress), and an unresolved conduit-disclosure issue. April Quarterly, July Quarterly, Year-End 2025, Pre-Primary.
  • James Gallagher's California campaign, split across two committees sharing treasurer Vona L. Copp — Gallagher For Congress and The Gallagher Committee — drew three RFAIs: undisclosed inter-committee transfers not reflected as an affiliation on either committee's Statement of Organization (raised twice, once for each committee), and roughly $90,000 in contributions designated for a 2026 runoff election Gallagher is not participating in that still need refunding or redesignating, along with an incorrect special-election date on the post-election summary page. July Quarterly, Post-Special (runoff contributions), Pre-Primary, July Quarterly (Gallagher Committee).
  • Two oddly named PACs sharing treasurer Jaleel Rashid Shabazz (also filed as Jaleel R. Shabazz-Blue) and a Truckee, Calif. address — John Madison And Martha Washington Philosophy Club Thoughts For Future Election Case Being Illustrating Hillary Clinton And Bernie Sanders and God Please Save Us I Repeat Phil Murphy Vs Bernie Sanders — each received a "Verification of Form 1" letter asking the treasurer to confirm under penalty of law that the committee's Statement of Organization is true, correct and complete, or file to withdraw it. Both letters include FEC's standard warning that knowingly making a false statement to the Commission is a federal crime under 18 U.S.C. § 1001 — boilerplate language present in every Verification of Form 1 letter, not a finding of wrongdoing, though it is what triggered today's automated fraud-keyword flag. Failure to respond within 35 days will get both committees' filings pulled from FEC's public searchable database. Philosophy Club filing, Phil Murphy Vs Bernie Sanders filing.
Form 99 Correspondence
PACs and campaigns explain late reports, software glitches and a forgiven loan

Beyond the incumbent and RFAI items above, six non-incumbent committees filed substantive Form 99 responses or miscellaneous documents this window.

  • Mike Beltran For Congress (Rep. candidate Mike Beltran, R-FL-14 challenger) responded to an RFAI on its July Quarterly report, describing its best-efforts process for collecting missing donor employer/occupation information. Filing.
  • Common Defense Action Fund explained a $200,000 independent-expenditure memo that was mistakenly left on its Q2 report after a planned ad was cancelled (attributed to FECFile software behavior) and acknowledged late 24-Hour Reports on other independent expenditures, attributed to an internal miscommunication; both have been amended/corrected. Filing.
  • NetChoice PAC explained a $300,000 increase in receipts and $150,025 increase in disbursements on its amended July Quarterly report as an inadvertent omission from the original filing, since corrected, and reclassified a non-federal contribution to the correct summary-page line. Filing.
  • Working Families Party PAC explained a late 24-hour report for digital billboard ads (via Advanced Mobile LEC) supporting Robert Peters, saying the ad-design cost wasn't included in the original invoice and was reported once the vendor, Changing Dynamics, issued a follow-up invoice. Filing.
  • Save America Fund explained an amended report adding $10,875 in additional production costs tied to a previously disclosed $60,000 media buy, filed within 24 hours of the committee learning of the additional cost. Filing.
  • Jotham Stein For Congress LLC (candidate Jotham Stein, CA-16 challenger) filed a loan-forgiveness letter: Stein forgave the remaining $25,799.73 balance on a $32,000 personal loan to his own committee, which had already repaid $6,200.27. Filing.

On the Record
Senate confirms a Texas judge and opens a fight over college-athlete pay; House gavels in and out in two minutes

The Senate had a full working day: it discharged the Finance Committee from further consideration of, and passed, H.R. 5345, the Improving Social Security's Service to Victims of Identity Theft Act; agreed to S. Res. 862 honoring the life and legacy of Dolly Parton; discharged and agreed to S. Res. 816 marking 35 years of independence for the Central Asian republics; and confirmed Kasdin Miller Mitchell as U.S. District Judge for the Northern District of Texas, 49–45. The Senate also began consideration of the Protect College Sports Act (S. 4668), teeing up dueling cloture votes for Tuesday, Sept. 22 — one on a substitute amendment to the bill, one on the nomination of Angela Veronica Colmenero to be U.S. District Judge for the Southern District of Texas.
