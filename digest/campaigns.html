Top Story
Booker for Senate flagged for unreported refunds, mishandled contribution redesignations
The FEC's review of the incumbent's July quarterly report turned up refunds with no matching original contribution on file, plus redesignations that skip the required three-entry paper trail.

On September 20, 2026, the Federal Election Commission sent a Request for Additional Information (RFAI) to Cory Booker for Senate, the reelection committee for Sen. Cory Booker (D-N.J.), over its July Quarterly Report covering May 14 through June 30, 2026.

The letter, addressed to campaign treasurer Scott Kobler, raises two issues. First, Schedule B shows refund disbursements — $397.50 to a donor identified as Sandy Bandy on June 21 and $500 to Mary Genz on June 28 — that don't correspond to any contribution the campaign had previously reported receiving from either person. The FEC says that can mean the original contribution was never disclosed, was designated to a different election than the refund reflects, or the campaign refunded more than it actually received. Second, the commission flagged multiple contributions that appear to have been redesignated or reattributed to a different election without the required three-entry memo format — an entry showing the contribution as originally reported, a negative entry backing out that amount, and a new entry reflecting the redesignation with its date and election.

The campaign has until October 26, 2026 to respond in writing. An RFAI is the FEC's standard mechanism for requesting clarifying information during its preliminary review of a report; it is not itself a finding of wrongdoing, but an inadequate response could factor into an audit decision.

Requests for Additional Information
Ten more RFAIs went out the same day, to House and presidential campaigns, two nominee funds, and a corporate PAC

The FEC dated ten more Requests for Additional Information September 20, 2026, to non-incumbent House and presidential campaigns, two joint-fundraising "nominee fund" vehicles, and one corporate PAC. The most common issues were negative ending cash balances, missing 48-hour notices for last-minute contributions, and incomplete donor identification — recurring compliance gaps typical of first-cycle campaign treasurers. Two of the filings share a common thread: committees called "EMB Nominee Fund" for candidates Casey and Scott Singer list the same treasurer and the same Athens, Georgia mailing address, and were flagged for the identical defect — their names include a federal candidate's name without being that candidate's authorized committee.

  • Casey for Congress - EMB Nominee Fund FL-22, RFAI on an amended Statement of Organization — the committee's name includes a federal candidate's name without the committee being that candidate's authorized committee; treasurer Paul Kilgore, Athens, Ga. Response due 10/26/2026. Filing.
  • Joshua McKee for Congress (AL-02, open seat), RFAI on its 12-Day Pre-Primary Report — a $3,871.73 negative ending cash balance that may reflect an overdraft or an undisclosed debt. Response due 10/26/2026. Filing.
  • Dan Green for Congress (FL-09, challenger), RFAI on its July Quarterly Report — a payroll-style disbursement to Gulf State Strategies LLC for "Canvassing Team Management" that needs an individual-recipient memo entry, plus a committee contribution disclosed on the wrong line of the summary page. Response due 10/26/2026. Filing.
  • Alpha POTUS (presidential), RFAI on an amended Statement of Organization — its committee name doesn't include the candidate's name, as FEC rules require for a principal campaign committee. Response due 10/26/2026. Filing.
  • Dan Green for Congress drew a second RFAI the same day, on its 12-Day Pre-Primary Report — repeating the Gulf State Strategies payroll-memo question and asking for a fuller disbursement description than "Consulting." Response due 10/26/2026. Filing.
  • McGinnis for the People (NC-02, challenger), RFAI on its July Quarterly Report — cycle-to-date totals that don't reconcile with the prior report's ending figures, a beginning cash balance that doesn't match the prior report's ending balance, and a $13,363.36 negative ending cash balance. Response due 10/26/2026. Filing.
  • Scott Singer for Congress - EMB Nominee Fund FL-25, RFAI on an amended Statement of Organization — same defect, same treasurer Paul Kilgore, and same Athens, Georgia address as the Casey for Congress nominee fund above. Response due 10/26/2026. Filing.
  • Valero Energy Corporation Political Action Committee, RFAI on its July Monthly Report — Schedule B shows two $5,000 contributions that together appear to exceed the $5,000-per-election limit a multicandidate PAC may give one candidate: $5,000 to Ashley Hinson for Congress on 4/30/2025 and $5,000 to Ashley for Iowa on 6/1/2026, both marked for the 2026 primary. Response due 10/26/2026. Filing.
  • Ed Fitzgerald for Congress (OH-07, challenger), RFAI on its July Quarterly Report — four items: an apparent failure to file 48-hour notices for several last-minute contributions of $1,000-$3,500 just before the primary; two 48-hour-noticed contributions, from Robert Jacobs ($1,800) and Summer Mansour ($3,000), that don't appear on Schedule A of the report itself; a $76,245.87 negative ending cash balance; and conduit-transmitted contributions that weren't itemized with the original donors' identities. Response due 10/26/2026. Filing.
  • McGinnis for the People drew a second RFAI, on its earlier April Quarterly Report — contributions listed with donor occupation and employer both recorded simply as "blank/blank," which requires the campaign to document its "best efforts" to obtain the missing information. Response due 10/26/2026. Filing.

A New Lunar Crater: McGetchin. A comet or asteroid roughly the size of a humpback whale struck the Moon sometime between April and May 2024, and NASA's Lunar Reconnaissance Orbiter captured before-and-after images of the resulting crater — named McGetchin — which is about two soccer fields across. Craters of this size are expected only about once every 132 years.
