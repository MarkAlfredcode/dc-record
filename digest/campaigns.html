Top Story
No Labels PAC Routed $7.5 Million to Candidates in One Period Through Its 'Democracy Engine' Solicitation Network
A September 18 filing shows the group used its allowance for internal member communication to direct money to more than 100 candidates in both parties, with cycle-to-date totals running into the hundreds of thousands of dollars for some.

In a Miscellaneous Submission to the FEC filed September 18, No Labels PAC (C00629709) disclosed that during the reporting period it used its allowance for "internal communication" — a mechanism that lets a membership organization solicit its own members on behalf of specific candidates — to direct its members to contribute directly to candidates the group has aligned itself with, using a conduit platform called Democracy Engine. The filing puts this period's total at $7,557,916.11, spread across more than 30 recipients, including $20,400 to Johnny Garcia, $22,250 to Darren Soto, $72,000 to Hillary Scholten and $90.70 to Rick Scott.

The same filing also updates cycle-to-date totals moved through Democracy Engine for the 2026 election cycle, and the scale is larger: $714,028.81 to Susan Collins, $710,845.38 to Bill Cassidy, $403,602.90 to Josh Gottheimer, $353,551.72 to Mike Lawler, $314,960.71 to Marie Gluesenkamp Perez, $297,000 to an entity listed as "LA Victory," $236,260.72 to Markwayne Mullin, $230,205 to Max Miller, $224,965.79 to Dusty Johnson, $223,571.83 to Jared Golden and $197,836.82 to Thomas Suozzi, among well over a hundred other names spanning both parties and both chambers.

No Labels frames the money as facilitated contributions from its own individual members rather than PAC-to-candidate giving, which is why the amounts aren't capped the way a direct PAC contribution would be. The filing itself makes no claim beyond describing the mechanism and listing the totals; nothing in it alleges wrongdoing. But the sheer breadth of candidates covered — and the size of some of the cycle-to-date figures — makes this one of the more consequential disclosures on the calendar this week.

Four sitting House members show up in this window's FEC correspondence: two facing live Requests for Additional Information over their committees' reports, and two whose principal campaign committees received FEC approval to terminate — notable simply because it's unusual for a sitting member's committee to wind down, though the termination filings themselves don't explain why and weren't read in detail (see the Terminations Wire methodology below).

  • Rep. Gus M. Bilirakis (R-FL-12) — The FEC sent Bilirakis for Congress an RFAI on its July Quarterly Report (04/01/2026–06/30/2026), asking the committee to amend a Schedule B disbursement currently described only as "Fundraising Services" with an actual explanation of what the money paid for, as required under 11 CFR 104.3(b)(4)(i)(A). RFAI.

First, Huizenga for Congress filed a Form 99 explaining that several contributions flagged as excessive were only "temporarily excessive" while the committee worked through the 60-day window to seek spousal reattributions, and that all will be resolved by refund or reattribution on the upcoming October Quarterly Report. Qazi, four times the $3,500 individual limit — still hadn't been refunded, redesignated or reattributed, and warned that continued inaction could factor into an audit or enforcement decision. RFAI.

Requests for Additional Information
A Senate Challenger Draws Four RFAIs in One Window, and a Special-Election Winner's Two Committees Get Cross-Examined

Every FRQ in this window belongs to one of three storylines: a Florida Senate challenger whose committee has now drawn an RFAI on nearly every report it has filed this cycle; a California House candidate juggling two committees that don't disclose each other as affiliated; and two oddly-named committees that both got the FEC's most serious form letter — a demand to verify their Statement of Organization — from the same filer at the same Truckee, California address.

  • Angela Nixon, a Democratic candidate for U.S. Senate from Florida, drew four separate RFAIs on her committee, Angie for Florida (C00915041), covering four different reports. On the 12-Day Pre-Primary Report, the FEC flagged incorrect cycle-to-date (Column B) totals. RFAI. On the amended April Quarterly Report, it flagged apparent excessive contributions from four individual donors (including Ava, Chris and Noah Touchton, each at $7,000) plus a separate $78,941.28 unexplained drop in reported receipts. RFAI. On the amended July Quarterly Report, it flagged more apparent excessive contributions (including Autumn Stokes, who made 25 separate small-dollar contributions the FEC says added up past the limit) alongside the same Column B math problem. RFAI. And on the 2025 Year-End Report, the FEC raised three issues at once: more excessive individual contributions, an apparent excessive contribution from another campaign's committee (Rashida Tlaib Campaign, $5,000), and a contribution from an organization — NW St Johns County United For Progress — that doesn't appear to be a registered political committee. RFAI.
  • James Gallagher, a Republican running in California's 1st District, files through two committees, and both got RFAIs pointing at each other. The Gallagher Committee (C00942458) was told its Schedule A discloses transfers from Gallagher for Congress that look like they came from an affiliated committee, but Gallagher for Congress isn't listed as an affiliated committee on its Statement of Organization — the same defect flagged on both its amended 12-Day Pre-Primary Report and its July Quarterly Report. RFAI (Pre-Primary), RFAI (July Quarterly). Gallagher for Congress itself — evidently his special-election committee, since its Post-Special report covers a Special General Election the filing says was actually held June 2, 2026 — was hit with a three-item RFAI: it listed the wrong special-election date (6/22/2026 instead of 6/2/2026); it disclosed apparent excessive contributions from the National Association of Home Builders PAC (Build PAC), three separate $2,500 contributions that together exceed the $5,000 multicandidate-committee limit; and it still holds a long list of contributions originally designated for a 2026 runoff election that, because Gallagher won the special outright, were never remedied by refund or redesignation. RFAI (Post-Special). A separate RFAI on the committee's July Quarterly Report flagged the same Column B math problem seen elsewhere this window. RFAI (July Quarterly).
  • Two committees — John Madison and Martha Washington Philosophy Club Thoughts for Future Election Case Being Illustrating Hillary Clinton and Bernie Sanders and God Please Save Us I Repeat Phil Murphy vs Bernie Sanders — each received an RQ-1 letter, the FEC's demand that a committee verify its Statement of Organization (Form 1) is true, correct and complete or amend/withdraw it within 35 days. Both letters were sent to filers sharing the same Truckee, California address (12315 Deerfield Dr, Suite 1-9), and the treasurer names — Jaleel Rashid Shabazz-Blue and Jaleel R. Shabazz — appear to be the same person. Both letters were flagged by this edition's automated red-flag keyword sweep for containing the word "fraud"; on a full read, that's the FEC's standard boilerplate warning that false statements to the agency are punishable under 18 U.S.C. § 1001, not an allegation specific to either committee. RQ-1 (Philosophy Club), RQ-1 (God Please Save Us).
