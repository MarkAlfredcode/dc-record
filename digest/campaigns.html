Top Story
A rare quiet day on the compliance beat: no new RFAIs, no fresh stock trades, one campaign confirms it's finished
The overnight docket produced a single FEC filing — a Texas Democrat's committee notifying regulators it has no more money to raise or spend after losing its primary — while the House worked through a light pre-recess-return Friday and the Senate stayed in pro forma session until Tuesday.

Every data source this edition draws on — the FEC's filings API, the House Clerk's bulk financial-disclosure index, the Senate's eFD search system, and Congress.gov's committee-meeting calendar — came back essentially empty for the period since Friday, September 5. No Request for Additional Information (Form FRQ) letters went out to any candidate or committee. No House member or senator filed a new Periodic Transaction Report or financial disclosure. No House or Senate committee has a meeting scheduled in the next 24 hours.

The only FEC filing in the window came from Texans for Regin Vanburg, the House campaign committee of Democrat Regina Vanburg, who ran in an open-seat primary in Texas's 21st Congressional District. Filed as a Miscellaneous Submission to the FEC (Form 99), the one-page letter — signed by treasurer Regina Vanburg — states plainly that "the campaign concluded following the primary election, and the committee is no longer conducting campaign activity," that there have been no receipts or disbursements since its last report, and asks the FEC to terminate the committee and its reporting obligations. There is no dollar figure, no debt, and no red flag in the letter; it reads as a routine post-primary wind-down.

On Capitol Hill, the House introduced six public bills (H.R. 10298-10303) and two resolutions (H. Res. 1512-1513) on September 4 before adjourning after just two minutes, with no recorded or yea-and-nay votes. The Senate gaveled in and out of a pro forma session in 34 seconds and does not reconvene for business until 1:15 p.m. Tuesday, September 8. Full rundown of both below.
