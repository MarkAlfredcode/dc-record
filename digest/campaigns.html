Top Story
Guam delegate candidate’s campaign draws three FEC information requests in a single day
Cash contributions over the $100 limit, an undisclosed loan interest rate, and disbursements labeled “Wine Mixer” and “Antigu Brewery” are among the questions the FEC wants answered.

Torres for Congress Committee, the campaign committee for Mary Camacho Torres, a Republican candidate for Guam’s non-voting House delegate seat, received three separate Requests for Additional Information (RFAI) from the Federal Election Commission on September 8 — each covering a different report filed this cycle. Three RFAIs landing on one committee in a single day is unusual, and a pattern of repeated compliance problems on the same committee is exactly the kind of signal worth flagging.

The RFAI covering the committee’s amended April Quarterly report flags cash contributions on Schedule A that appear to exceed the $100 federal limit on cash gifts, and asks the committee to properly memo-itemize reimbursements made to a staffer, JoAnn Camacho, and to the candidate herself for out-of-pocket spending. A second RFAI, tied to the campaign’s 12-Day Pre-Primary report, says the committee failed to disclose the interest rate on a $17,219.30 loan Torres made to her own campaign on July 12. A third RFAI, covering the July Quarterly report, asks the committee to explain vague disbursement descriptions on Schedule B — including line items labeled “Antigu Brewery,” “Wine Mixer - HO Chin Fe,” “Get Out The Vote FR Exp,” and several “RMB” entries for Google, Meta and Wix.com — none of which satisfy the FEC’s purpose-of-disbursement disclosure rules.

All three notices, signed by FEC senior campaign finance analyst Mary Seiler, were sent to treasurer Jesse John Gumataotao Garcia with a response deadline of October 13, 2026. FEC data available today does not classify Torres as an incumbent or challenger — Guam’s delegate seat is non-voting and the field appears to still be forming.

Sitting Lawmakers
Four incumbents filed routine FEC correspondence yesterday — nothing rises above the ordinary

The FEC’s incumbent-candidate lookup flagged four sitting House members with Form 99 filings dated September 8, all responding to routine RFAI-driven corrections. Rep. Steve Stivers confirmed his campaign plans to terminate by year’s end; the other three are standard over-limit-contribution or duplicate-entry corrections. None involve personal use, misappropriation, or anything beyond ordinary bookkeeping fixes.

  • Glenn Grothman for Congress (Rep. Glenn Grothman, R-Wis., incumbent) — Form 99: responded to an RFAI about possible over-the-limit donors in its July Quarterly report. The committee said two of the three flagged donors were duplicate entries caused by an administrative error and have been corrected in an amendment; the third donor, Lawrence Wiley, was refunded his excess contribution on August 5, to be reflected in the October Quarterly report.
  • Stivers for Congress (Rep. Steve Stivers, R-Ohio, incumbent) — Form 99: the committee notified the FEC it plans to file a termination report by the end of the year, and will continue filing on its regular schedule until then.
  • Celeste for Congress (Rep. Celeste Maloy, R-Utah, incumbent) — Form 99: in response to an RFAI on its Pre-Primary report, the committee said an omitted $7,769.39 debt to District City Consulting was a duplicate entry, removed via amendment to reflect the debt was owed only once.
  • Fedorchak for ND (Rep. Julie Fedorchak, R-N.D., incumbent) — Form 99: a detailed RFAI response covering multiple over-the-limit contributions — a $600 refund to Cunningham for NM, a $25.25 refund to the American Israel Public Affairs Committee Political Action Committee, a $5,000 refund to donor George Haux, and a timely redesignation from Hunton Andrews Kurth LLP. Signed by treasurer Cabell Hobbs.

Form 99 Correspondence
A Nevada House candidate refunded a prohibited corporate contribution and flagged an over-limit gift in the same letter

Committees filed 21 non-termination Form 99s on September 8 (2 more were routine terminations, covered below). Matthew William Fonken's Nevada House campaign disclosed the most serious issue of the batch: a $3,500 donation it accepted from a family friend turned out to be drawn on a corporate account, which federal law flatly prohibits regardless of amount, and the same letter flags a second donor whose combined gifts exceeded the legal limit. We read all 10 candidate-committee Form 99s in full; 11 additional PAC/party Form 99s are listed below without a full read due to volume.

  • Matthew Fonken for Nevada (H, Nev., Matthew William Fonken, open seat) — Form 99: responding to an FEC notice dated August 16 flagging two issues. First, the campaign accepted a $3,500 donation actually funded from a corporation's business account — a prohibited corporate contribution regardless of size — and says it mailed a $3,500 cashier's check refund to Heritage Excavation Inc. of Beebe, Ark., on September 4, to be reported on the October 15 Quarterly report. Second, donor Vivian Leal gave $3,000 in December 2025 and another $2,500 in February 2026 via ActBlue, together exceeding the legal contribution limit; the campaign says it caught the issue itself while preparing its July 15 Quarterly report and disclosed it on the Loans, Debts & Obligations schedule.
  • Jerrod Sessler for Congress (H, Wash., Jerrod Sessler, open seat) — Form 99: explaining why an amended July Quarterly report showed a large jump in receipts and disbursements — the campaign processed more than 1,900 WinRed credit-card transactions between April and June. Treasurer Tom Perry, who wrote he serves as treasurer for more than 30 campaigns this cycle, said he filed an incomplete report by the July 15 deadline rather than miss it entirely, then spent the following week entering the backlog before filing the amended report on July 23.
  • Esther Kim Varet for Congress (H, Calif., Esther Kim Varet, challenger) — Form 99: responding to a September 3 FEC notice about over-limit contributions and general-election funds received before the primary; says the flagged contributions were refunded to donors on July 14 and will appear on the committee's next report. Signed by assistant treasurer Shawnda Deane.
  • Ben Diamond For Congress (H, Fla., Ben Diamond, open seat) — Form 99: a brief response to an RFAI stating the committee is maintaining funds for a potential future run for Congress. Signed by treasurer Michael Magidson.
  • Earl Cooper (H, Del., Earl Cooper, challenger) — Form 99: a newly registered committee's 12-Day Pre-Primary statement noting it has not met the $5,000 reporting threshold, filed for the public record.
  • Stanley Leavell for US Senate (S, Ill., Stanley Leavell, open seat) — Form 99: informational filing stating the committee did not receive or spend more than $5,000 and so did not trigger the July 15 federal disclosure requirement.

On the Record
Both chambers gaveled in and out in under three minutes; 22 House bills and 6 resolutions introduced

The House and Senate each held brief pro forma sessions on September 8. The Senate met for 27 seconds and immediately adjourned until 3 p.m. Thursday, September 10. The House met at 2 p.m. and adjourned at 2:03 p.m., with Rep. Daniel Meuser presiding as Speaker pro tempore; there were no yea-and-nay votes, no recorded votes and no quorum calls. The House also disclosed that, under House Resolutions 1504 and 1505, the Speaker certified to the U.S. Attorney for the District of Columbia the refusal of Michael Joseph and Hector Roos to produce documents to, and appear before, an investigative subcommittee of the Committee on Ethics. Despite the light floor schedule, the House introduced 22 public bills (H.R. 10304–10325) and 6 resolutions (H. Res. 1514–1519); the most notable include a Gottheimer-Stefanik bill directing a federal study of 9/11 education in schools, a Houlahan bill that would have the President declare a national housing emergency and invoke the Defense Production Act, and a Takano bill to cut the standard workweek from 40 to 32 hours.
