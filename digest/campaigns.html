Top Story
FEC Presses Rep. Nick LaLota's Campaign Over Excessive Contributions From Billionaire Donor Leonard Blavatnik
A four-item Request for Additional Information, filed September 15, flags apparent excessive and prohibited contributions plus disbursement-disclosure gaps in an amended report covering LaLota's first quarter of 2026.

The Federal Election Commission has sent LaLota for Congress, the campaign committee of Republican Rep. Nick LaLota of New York's 1st District, a Request for Additional Information over an amended April Quarterly Report covering January through March 2026. The letter, dated September 15, flags four separate problems the committee must address by October 20.

The largest concerns apparent excessive contributions the FEC says have not been fully refunded, redesignated, or reattributed. Industrialist Leonard Blavatnik is named repeatedly across both the primary and general election accounts: a $5,000 primary contribution on October 14, 2025, was partially reversed by two same-day entries (-$3,500 and -$1,500), but Blavatnik is shown giving another $3,500 in March 2025 and $3,500 more in March 2026, with only a $3,500 general-election refund recorded in June 2026. Also flagged: two $3,500 primary contributions from Stacey Siebel (also recorded as "Stacy Siebel") in August 2025 and March 2026, plus a general-election mirror of the same amounts; a string of sub-limit contributions from Lewis Stahl that the FEC says aggregate above the $3,500 general-election cap; and four separate 2025-26 contributions from Andrea Wynn that appear to do the same.

The second item flags contributions from organizations that are not registered political committees — including a $5,000 primary contribution from an entity called "ATU COPE Voluntary Account," $1,000 from "Friends of Anthony Manetta," and $100 from the "Suffolk County Detectives Association" — that the committee must either document as drawn from permissible funds or refund. Two smaller items round out the letter: a Schedule B disbursement descriptor reading simply "Event," which the FEC says doesn't meet the disclosure standard, and undisclosed original-vendor detail behind the committee's credit card payments.

LaLota's committee has until October 20 to respond or amend the report; the FEC's letter warns that failure to adequately address the items could lead to an audit or enforcement referral.

Sitting Lawmakers
Two Incumbents Draw RFAIs: LaLota Over Contribution Limits, Daines Over Unreported Refunds

Two sitting members of Congress received Requests for Additional Information in this window, both filed September 15. The FEC's letter to Rep. Nick LaLota is this edition's Top Story, detailed above; Sen. Steve Daines' campaign faces a narrower question about refunds that don't trace back to any contribution the committee ever reported.

  • LaLota for Congress (Rep. Nick LaLota, R-NY-1) — RFAI on the amended April Quarterly Report flags apparent excessive contributions from donors including Leonard Blavatnik, contributions from unregistered organizations, an inadequate disbursement description, and missing credit-card vendor itemization. Response due 10/20/2026. See Top Story above. Filing.
  • Steve Daines for Montana (Sen. Steve Daines, R-MT) — RFAI on the July Quarterly Report: Schedule B discloses five refunds, including to Live Nation Entertainment Inc. PAC (-$1,500) and the National Association of Broadcasters PAC (-$2,500), that don't correspond to any contribution the committee previously reported from those same sources — a gap that can arise from an unreported original contribution, a refund designated to the wrong election, or a refund that exceeds what was actually given. The FEC wants the committee to either disclose the original contributions or explain the discrepancy. Response due 10/20/2026. Filing.

On the Record
House Tables Trump Impeachment Resolution, Passes War Powers Measure on Iran; Senate Advances Student-Athlete NIL Bill

The House's September 15 session (Congressional Record Volume 172, Issue 145) was dominated by a resolution to impeach President Donald John Trump: members voted 232-147, with 47 voting "present," to table Rep. Massie's privileged resolution (H. Res. 1486) rather than take it up directly. The House separately passed H. Con. Res. 93, directing the President to remove U.S. forces from hostilities with Iran under the War Powers Resolution, 220-204. A bipartisan quartet of redistricting-reform bills was introduced the same day — H.R. 10378 through H.R. 10381, led respectively by Reps. Ed Case, Michael Lawler, Brian Fitzpatrick, and Ryan Mackenzie, with overlapping cosponsors including Rep. Nick LaLota and Rep. August Pfluger — addressing mid-decade redistricting limits, district-drawing standards, independent redistricting commissions, and litigation procedures. The House also adopted a resolution recommending the House find Leon D. Black in contempt of Congress for refusing to comply with Oversight and Government Reform Committee subpoenas (H. Rept. 119-831). In the Senate, a cloture vote to advance the Digital Asset Market Clarity Act (H.R. 3633) failed 49-50, while a separate cloture vote to move forward on the Protect College Sports Act (S. 4668), covering student-athlete name, image, and likeness rights, succeeded 74-24; the Senate also confirmed Matthew R. Byrne as U.S. District Judge for the Southern District of Ohio, 52-45.
