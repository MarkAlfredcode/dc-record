Top Story
FEC presses Tafoya for Senate on corporate, excessive, LLC and unregistered-group contributions
A nine-page request for additional information to the Minnesota Republican open-seat Senate campaign raises four separate contribution problems.

The FEC sent a request for additional information, dated October 26, to Tafoya for Senate, the campaign committee of Michele Tafoya, the Republican candidate in Minnesota's open Senate race. It covers the committee's October Quarterly Report (07/23/2026 - 09/30/2026). The letter raises four contribution problems: apparent corporate or labor union contributions, apparent excessive contributions, contributions from limited liability companies whose tax treatment is unclear, and contributions from organizations that are not registered political committees.

On the excessive contributions, the FEC notes the committee's additional explanation of its corrective action for some of them. It also says that to date one or more of the apparent excessive contributions have not been refunded, redesignated or reattributed, and that the 60-day window for doing so runs from receipt. The LLC item gets the same acknowledgment of partial corrective action. The donors and amounts are in attachments not reproduced in the text available here.

The letter adds two smaller points. A refund of a contribution appears to be disclosed on the wrong line of the Detailed Summary Page. The committee ID listed for contributions from Citizens United (C30002877) appears to be incorrect. The treasurer is Chris Marston and the response is due 11/30/2026.

Sitting Lawmakers
Five filings from sitting lawmakers' committees, mostly corrections

One RFAI, three Form 99 explanations and one termination approval were tagged to sitting members of Congress. The Form 99s are corrections of earlier disclosures. Rep. Scott Perry's committee reports contributions that were missing from its 48-hour notices.

  • FEC asks Rep. August Pfluger (R-TX) to add missing addresses to Schedule B disbursements and to explain a payment described only as 'ENTERTAINMENT'. The letter targets the amended October Quarterly Report received 10/16 and is addressed to treasurer Steve Martin of August Pfluger for Congress, a candidate committee. The response is due 11/30/2026. (RFAI)
  • Sen. Cory A. Booker (D-NJ) tells the FEC that refunds to Sandy Bandy and Mary Genz were primary-election refunds wrongly disclosed as general-election refunds. Cory Booker for Senate blames a clerical error, says it amended its July Quarterly Report (5/14/2026 - 6/30/2026) and also changed how it discloses reattributions. The reply answers an FEC letter dated 9/20/2026 and is signed by treasurer Scott Kobler. Page 2 repeats page 1. (Form 99)
  • Rep. Scott Perry (R-PA) reports that four WinRed contributions totaling $5,000 were left off one of his committee's two 48-hour notices. Patriots for Perry says Jeff Troesh gave $1,000, Todd Lord $2,000, Carson Adcock $1,000 and James Downs $1,000 between 10/19 and 10/20. It says it checks WinRed daily but did not see them until a payout on 10/26, when it amended the notice it had filed 10/21. Late disclosure: the gifts were reported about a week after they were given. The filing is signed by Joel Jukus, who also signed the Republican Federal Committee of Pennsylvania's Form 99 below. (Form 99)
  • Rep. Keith Self (R-TX) notes to the FEC that a 48-hour report covers a contribution received 10/21 and that notification arrived 10/26. The one-line Form 99 from Self for Congress cites filing FEC-2042239 and gives no further detail. (Form 99)
  • Becerra for Congress, tagged to Xavier Becerra (D-CA), filed a termination approval. FEC received it 10/26. The filing was not read. (Filing)
Requests for Additional Information
Nine RFAIs, from a Senate candidate's contribution problems to a union fund's four-report cash mismatch

Nine RFAIs went to non-lawmaker committees, all dated 10/26 with responses due 11/30/2026. Four went to one union political fund, and two House campaigns got near-identical joint fundraising letters about 'Victory Fund' transfers. The Tafoya for Senate letter is the most substantive and is the Top Story.

  • FEC asks Tafoya for Senate to address apparent corporate or labor contributions, excessive contributions that have not been refunded, redesignated or reattributed, LLC contributions and gifts from unregistered organizations. Candidate committee of Michele Tafoya, Republican, open Minnesota Senate seat. The letter covers the October Quarterly Report (07/23/2026 - 09/30/2026), also flags a refund disclosed on the wrong line and an incorrect committee ID for Citizens United (C30002877), and is addressed to treasurer Chris Marston. (RFAI)
  • FEC asks Brinker Harding for Congress why transfers from 'Brinker Victory Fund' look like joint fundraising proceeds when the fund is not listed on its Statement of Organization. Candidate committee, Republican open-seat Nebraska House candidate Brinker Harding. The letter covers the 12 Day Pre-General Report (10/01/2026 - 10/14/2026) and is addressed to treasurer Taylor Moose. (RFAI)
  • FEC asks Shaun Finnie for Congress why transfers from the Shaun Finnie Victory Fund look like joint fundraising proceeds when the fund is not a listed joint fundraising representative. Candidate committee of Democratic Texas House challenger Shaun Finnie. The letter covers the October Quarterly Report (07/01/2026 - 09/30/2026) and is addressed to treasurer Blair Schuman. It uses the same template as the Harding letter. (RFAI)
  • FEC asks Caldwell for Congress to explain a negative ending cash balance of $4,548.27 on its July Quarterly Report. Candidate committee of Brent Caldwell, a Democratic North Carolina House challenger. The FEC says the figure suggests an overdraft, a math error or an unreported debt. An overdraft could be a prohibited bank contribution unless it was on an account with usual and customary overdraft protection. The letter is addressed to treasurer Meghan Caldwell. (RFAI)
  • FEC tells Local 1814 ILA Political Action and Education Fund that its Year-End 2025 beginning cash does not match its prior ending balance and that 11 Column B totals look wrong. PAC. The totals are on Lines 6(c), 7, 11a(i), 11a(iii), 11(d), 19, 20, 21(b), 21(c), 31 and 32. A third item flags Schedule A receipts from an organization not registered with the Commission, which may require a refund if it was not a collecting agent. Treasurer Frank Agosta received four letters the same day. (RFAI)
  • FEC tells Local 1814 ILA Political Action and Education Fund that its April Quarterly 2026 beginning cash does not equal its Year-End 2025 ending balance. PAC. A second item raises the same unregistered-organization receipts on Schedule A. (RFAI)
  • FEC flags Local 1814 ILA Political Action and Education Fund for Schedule A receipts from an organization not registered with the Commission on its July Quarterly 2026 report. PAC. The letter says a refund may be required if the entity was not a collecting agent. (RFAI)
  • FEC flags Local 1814 ILA Political Action and Education Fund for Schedule A receipts from an unregistered organization on its October Quarterly 2025 report (07/01/2025 - 09/30/2025). PAC. This is the oldest of the four ILA letters. (RFAI)
  • FEC tells X Party it must petition for an advisory opinion before using national party contribution limits. Party committee. Its Statement of Organization says in Line 5(d) that it is a national committee for the 'X' Party. The letter cites Advisory Opinion 1995-16 and is addressed to treasurer Randy Smith of Cresson, Pennsylvania. (RFAI)
Form 99 Correspondence
Eleven Form 99s: replies to FEC letters, corrections and a few one-line notes

Most of these are committees answering FEC letters or correcting earlier filings. One item is an FEC acknowledgment of a presidential candidate's debt settlement plan. Another is a PAC saying its pre-general report was late. A contribution-limit dispute between a corporate PAC and a Senate campaign is the most substantive reply.

  • Valero Energy Corporation Political Action Committee tells the FEC that its contributions to Ashley Hinson for Congress and Ashley for Iowa were for separate elections and were not excessive. The PAC answers an RFAI dated September 30, 2026. It says its April 30, 2025 contribution went to the 2026 House primary and its June 1, 2026 contribution to the 2026 Senate primary. It cites a November 6, 2025 letter in which Ashley for Iowa (C00706267) said it would reset donors' aggregate limits as of June 19, 2025. The PAC says no refund or redesignation is needed. (Form 99)
  • Chris Gober for Texas tells the FEC it will amend its July Quarterly Report and later reports after reviewing Schedule C. The Republican open-seat Texas House candidate's committee answers an RFAI dated October 25, 2026, one day before its October 26 reply. Most of the letter describes its best efforts to collect donors' occupation and employer information. It is signed by treasurer Cabell Hobbs. (Form 99)
  • Southwest Business Corporation PAC tells the FEC its 2026 pre-general report was late because staff were on vacation. The San Antonio PAC says it had no federal activity in the period. Late disclosure, by the committee's own account. (Form 99)
  • The FEC acknowledges receiving a debt settlement plan from Constance Johnson for President of the United States of America. This is an FEC letter, not a committee filing. The plan was received 10/20/2026 from the Democratic presidential candidate's committee, whose treasurer is Connie L. Johnson. The FEC says it is reviewing the plan and that the committee must keep reporting all debts, including those in the plan. The debt amount is not stated. (Form 99)
  • Civic Renewal Fund Inc. tells the FEC it understated the cumulative independent expenditure total for Alabama's 2nd Congressional District by $1,930.44. It corrected its October 22 24-hour notice by amendment on October 26. It says the individual expenditures were reported correctly. The letter is signed by treasurer Britney Gonzalez. (Form 99)
  • Republican Federal Committee of Pennsylvania amended six reports so Mary Ann Meloy, not Kaytee Isley, is named in the treasurer's signature. The reports covered June through October and the pre-election period. The committee says its filing software kept populating Isley's name after Meloy became treasurer on 6/20/2026, and that Isley remains an assistant treasurer. The note is signed by custodian of records Joel Jukus. (Form 99)
  • Fiscal Leadership and Knowing Economics PAC tells the FEC it has amended its reports after an RFAI about Schedule B. The PAC answers an RFAI dated 9/21/26 about its Q1 2026 report. It says the amended July Quarterly Report carries the correct numbers. (Form 99)
  • Tim Wilkins for Congress filed a 'Loan Forgiveness' Form 99. The Republican open-seat Florida House candidate's two-page filing has only header text in the readable PDF, so its terms and amounts are not known. (Form 99)
  • Laquita Wooten Campaign Committee tells the FEC it has not exceeded $5,000 for the quarter ending September 30, 2026. Presidential candidate committee, Republican candidate Laquita Yvonne Wooten, Opa-locka, Florida. (Form 99)
  • Mark Piper for Florida tells the FEC it had not met the $5,000 reporting threshold as of October 15, 2026. Candidate committee of Democratic Florida House challenger Mark Piper. (Form 99)
  • Patriot PAC for America tells the FEC it had 'no expenses or income'. The Chippewa Falls, Wisconsin PAC filed the note 10/25, two days before this edition. (Form 99)
