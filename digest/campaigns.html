Top Story
Pro-Espaillat PAC left $542,000 in independent-expenditure spending undisclosed for up to a week during New York's 13th District primary
FEC flags United for Progress for three late and two entirely missing 24-hour reports on a single vendor's spending

The FEC has sent an RFAI (Request for Additional Information) to United for Progress, an independent-expenditure PAC run by treasurer Daniele Campbell, over a run of compliance failures tied to spending on behalf of Rep. Adriano Espaillat's 2026 primary campaign in New York's 13th Congressional District.

Between June 10 and June 20, the committee paid Renaissance Campaign Strategies a total of $542,673.86 across five separate disbursements. Federal law requires any independent expenditure of $1,000 or more, made within 20 days of an election, to be reported to the FEC within 24 hours. Three of the five payments — $100,000, $111,759.36 and $200,000 — were reported, but only after the 24-hour window had closed. The remaining two payments, $52,914.50 and $78,000, appear not to have been reported to the Commission at all. The FEC also flagged an error in the committee's year-to-date independent-expenditure total for the district.

The FEC has given the committee until October 13 to explain the lapses and file the missing reports, warning that inadequate responses "could result in an audit or enforcement action." Rep. Espaillat's own campaign committee is not implicated — this is a PAC operating independently of his campaign, and the RFAI is directed solely at United for Progress.

Sitting Lawmakers
Three House incumbents cleaned up compliance letters from the FEC — duplicate donor entries, a double-counted debt, and refunded excess contributions
All three items below are responses or corrections filed by sitting members' own campaign committees, not new compliance problems raised for the first time — each closes out an FEC inquiry with amendments and, in two cases, refunds already issued.
  • Glenn Grothman for Congress (Rep. Glenn Grothman, R-Wisconsin) — Form 99. Responding to an RFAI over possible over-limit donors in its July Quarterly Report, the committee says two of the three flagged donors were duplicate entries caused by an administrative error and have been removed in an amendment; the third donor, Lawrence Wiley, was refunded his excess contribution on August 5.
  • Celeste for Congress (Rep. Celeste Maloy, R-Utah) — Form 99. In response to an RFAI on its Pre-Primary report, the committee says a $7,769.39 debt to District City Consulting had been listed twice by mistake; the duplicate has been removed by amendment so the debt is disclosed only once.
  • Fedorchak for ND (Rep. Julie Fedorchak, R-North Dakota) — Form 99. Closing out an RFAI on four apparent excessive-contribution issues in its July Quarterly Report: a $600 excess from Cunningham for NM was refunded June 30; a $25.25 excess from the American Israel Public Affairs Committee PAC and a $5,000 excess from donor George Haux are being refunded and will show up in the October Quarterly Report; and a contribution from Hunton Andrews Kurth LLP was properly redesignated within the 60-day window.

Four stand out — United for Progress's six-figure IE lapses (see Top Story), Come and Take It PAC's pattern of unreported independent expenditures in a Missouri Senate race, an apparent $10,000 contribution-limit breach by a corporate PAC, and personal-use-adjacent spending questions for a Guam House candidate.
