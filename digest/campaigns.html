Top Story
House Oversight Committee to Vote Today on Holding Leon Black in Contempt of Congress
A full committee markup this afternoon will consider a resolution recommending the House find the Apollo Global Management co-founder in contempt for refusing to comply with committee subpoenas.

The House Committee on Oversight and Government Reform is scheduled to hold a full committee business meeting at 4:00 p.m. ET today in 2154 Rayburn to consider a resolution recommending that the House of Representatives find Leon David Black, the private-equity billionaire and co-founder of Apollo Global Management, in contempt of Congress for refusing to comply with subpoenas the committee issued to him.

The committee has posted a formal report supporting the contempt resolution along with a notice and staff memo for members ahead of the markup. Chairman James Comer (R-KY) has filed an amendment in the nature of a substitute to the underlying contempt report, meaning the text members vote on may differ from the version originally noticed.

The meeting documents do not specify, in the material available so far, exactly what subject matter the underlying subpoenas covered. This newsletter will follow up once the committee publishes the resolution text and vote outcome.

Rep. Jeff Hurd's Campaign Corrects Contributor Records After FEC Inquiry

One filing this window came from a sitting member of Congress's own campaign committee: an incumbent House Republican's amended-report cover letter responding to an FEC request for additional information.

  • Jeff Hurd for Congress, the campaign committee of Rep. Jeff Hurd (R-CO-3), filed a Form 99 on 09/12/2026 responding to an RFAI dated 08/13/2026 about the committee's 48-hour contribution reports. The committee said a contribution originally attributed to Jim Pedersen actually came from his spouse, Jennifer Pedersen; a contribution attributed to Michael Ross was actually a joint contribution from Michael and his wife, Susan; and a contribution from Susan Whittlesey was misreported as $2,000 when it was actually $2,500. Filing.
Requests for Additional Information
End Citizens United Pressed on $177,000 Contribution From Its Own Non-Federal Account

Five RFAI letters went out in this window, all dated 09/13/2026 and all with an 10/19/2026 response deadline. The most significant flags a large contribution FEC staff say came from an organization not registered as a political committee.

  • End Citizens United was asked to explain a $176,978.93 contribution received 02/23/2026 and reported on its March Monthly report, listed as coming from "End Citizens United Non-Federal" — an entity FEC staff say is not registered as a political committee. The letter tells the committee that to accept money from an unregistered organization into a federal account, it must ensure the funds are permissible, and that if the contribution is prohibited, it must be refunded or transferred to a non-federal account within 30 days of receipt or the full amount must be refunded. Filing.
  • Committee to Elect Charles G. Ferry, a presidential campaign committee, was asked to clarify why it appears to have missed the 10-day deadline under 52 U.S.C. § 30103 to file its Statement of Organization (FEC Form 1) after treasurer Julia Monroe designated the committee. Filing.
  • Leadership in Action was told the aggregate year-to-date individual-contribution totals on its July Quarterly report (covering 04/01/2026–06/30/2026) appear incorrect, and was asked to amend the report with correct totals and to disclose all itemized contributions over $200. Filing.
  • New York State Democratic Committee received two nearly identical RFAIs the same day, both flagging its Schedule F coordinated-expenditure disclosures for failing to include the aggregate general-election expenditure total for each candidate supported — one on its amended August Monthly report (covering 07/01/2026–07/31/2026, received 08/31/2026) and one on its amended June Monthly report (covering 05/01/2026–05/31/2026, also received 08/31/2026). Both letters were signed by the same analyst and addressed to the same treasurer, David Alpert. August filing | June filing.

On the Record
House Pauses for 9/11 Anniversary, Introduces 25 Bills Before Brief Session

The Congressional Record's most recent Daily Digest, for Thursday, September 10, 2026, shows the House meeting only briefly — from 3:30 p.m. to 3:33 p.m. — to mark the 25th anniversary of the September 11, 2001 terrorist attacks. The House agreed to H. Res. 1520, sponsored by Speaker Mike Johnson (R-LA-4), expressing the sense of the House regarding the attacks, then adjourned as a further mark of respect for the victims. There were no yea-and-nay votes, no recorded votes, and no quorum calls. The Senate met only in a 30-second pro forma session and adjourned until Monday, September 14.

Despite the short session, members introduced 25 public bills (H.R. 10326–10350) and 10 resolutions (H. Res. 1520–1529). Notable introductions include Rep. Thomas H. Kean's (R-NJ-7) bill to award a Congressional Gold Medal to the Naval Special Warfare Development Group ("SEAL Team Six") for its role in the operation that killed Osama bin Laden; Rep. Christopher H. Smith's (R-NJ-4) bill to bar goods made with cobalt mined using child or forced labor in the Democratic Republic of the Congo from the U.S. market; and Rep. Mark DeSaulnier's (D-CA-10) bill to repeal Section 230 of the Communications Act. The full list follows below.

The House also received two Senate-passed bills by referral — S. 1051 and S. 1377, both referred to the Committee on Natural Resources — and filed seven committee reports, including on bills to sunset the Advisory Committee on the Records of Congress and to revise Government Publishing Office authorities.
