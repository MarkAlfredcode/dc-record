Top Story
Rep. Steve Cohen Files a New Resolution to Impeach Trump, One of 100 Bills Introduced in the House's Latest Session
The measure drew no cosponsors and was referred to the Judiciary Committee during a three-minute pro forma sitting on September 24 — the most recent House floor day reflected in the Congressional Record as of this edition.

Rep. Steve Cohen (D-TN-9) introduced H. Res. 1575, a resolution impeaching Donald J. Trump, President of the United States, for high crimes and misdemeanors, during the House's September 24 session. The one-page resolution lists no cosponsors and was referred to the Committee on the Judiciary the same day; no further action on it had been recorded as of the most recent Congressional Record issue available.

The resolution was one of 100 measures introduced that day — 85 bills (H.R. 10530 through H.R. 10614), two concurrent resolutions, and 13 more resolutions — during a floor session that opened at 2:30 p.m. and adjourned at 2:33 p.m., with no yea-and-nay votes, no recorded votes, and no quorum calls: the hallmarks of a pro forma sitting used to keep the chamber formally in session without conducting business. The full list, with every bill's sponsor and a plain-English description, runs in the On the Record section below.

Cohen has periodically filed impeachment resolutions against Donald Trump in prior Congresses; this edition did not independently verify how this filing compares to those, since the Congressional Record and congress.gov entries reviewed here cover only this bill's own introduction and current status.

Requests for Additional Information
Four RFAIs Land, Including an FEC Letter Questioning Whether a Presidential Committee's Own Paperwork Is Accurate

All four RFAIs in this window are routine compliance follow-ups except one: an FEC letter to Asian Legal Aid, a presidential-level committee, demanding it confirm that its Statement of Organization is "true, correct, and complete." That letter's citation of the federal false-statement penalty statute is what triggered this edition's automated keyword sweep for the word "fraud" — but a full read of the letter shows that language is the FEC's own standard boilerplate for any Form 1 verification demand, not a specific allegation against the committee. It is flagged below because the underlying question (is this committee's registration itself genuine) is still unresolved and worth tracking.

  • Flagged for red-flag keyword language ("fraud") that, on a full read, turns out to be the FEC's own boilerplate false-statement warning rather than a specific accusation: Asian Legal Aid (Baltimore, Maryland; FEC ID C00961722; treasurer Darline Graham), a presidential-office committee, received an RFAI titled "Verification of Form 1 (Statement of Organization)" on September 27. The FEC says it has reason to believe the committee's Form 1 filing may not include true, correct, or complete information, and warns that knowingly making a false statement to the Commission is punishable under 18 U.S.C. § 1001 and 52 U.S.C. §§ 30107(a)(9) and 30109. The committee must confirm the filing is accurate, correct it, or withdraw it by November 2, 2026; failure to respond within 35 days will get the Form 1 pulled from the FEC's public searchable database and placed in an "unverified filings" database instead. Filing.
  • Harris County Democratic Party (Houston, Texas; FEC ID C00161067; treasurer Traci Gibson) received an RFAI on September 27 tied to its Amended August Monthly Report, covering July 2026. The FEC says the report discloses contributions from individuals whose identification — full name, complete mailing address, occupation, and employer — is incomplete, and asks the committee to either supply the missing information or document its "best efforts" to obtain it. Response due November 2, 2026. Filing.
  • North Dakota Republican Party (Bismarck, North Dakota; FEC ID C00018929; treasurer Stephen Hillerud) received an RFAI on September 27 covering its combined May, June, and July 2026 Monthly Reports, which the FEC says disclose only limited payments for administrative expenses (rent, utilities, salaries, and the like). The committee must clarify whether required salary/wage disclosures and a Schedule H1 federal/non-federal allocation were properly made. Response due November 2, 2026. Filing.
  • WY Democratic State Central Committee (Cheyenne, Wyoming; FEC ID C00001917; treasurer Dudley Case) received an RFAI on September 27 with four items concerning its July 2026 Monthly Report: credit-card payments on Schedule H4 and Schedule B that are missing the original vendor's name and address, reimbursements to individuals that aren't itemized down to the original vendor, and a disbursement description that simply reads "Reimbursement (See Memoed)" without a real explanation of purpose. Response due November 2, 2026. Filing.
Form 99 Correspondence
An FEC Letter Rejects a Paper Filing, and a New York Presidential Campaign Files Two Nearly Identical Disability Notices

Four Form 99 items survived this window's dedupe. One is itself an FEC letter to a committee rather than a committee filing; two are unusually specific administrative submissions filed 26 minutes apart by the same presidential campaign; the fourth is a routine self-reported compliance follow-up.

  • The FEC sent American Commercial Barge Line LLC PAC (Jeffersonville, Indiana; FEC ID C00418269; treasurer Rochelle Dunn) a letter on September 27 rejecting a paper amendment the committee had filed September 8. Because the committee already meets the threshold requiring electronic filing, the FEC says the paper amendment will not be treated as an amended Statement of Organization (Form 1) — the committee must resubmit it electronically to make the change stick. Filing.
  • Friends of Montell C. Moseley (Flushing, New York; FEC ID C00959650), the committee for presidential candidate Montell C. Moseley, filed two Miscellaneous Submissions to the FEC 26 minutes apart on September 27. Both confirm the committee has cleared the $5,000 candidacy threshold via a TD Bank depository account and describe Americans with Disabilities Act accommodations — including proxy signature collection — the campaign says it has secured for ballot access. The second filing goes considerably further than the first, adding specific claims that the candidate has "permanent physical mobility challenges and orthopedic limitations," names five treating clinicians at NYU Langone Orthopedics, and describes "chronic equilibrium disruptions" and the "permanent requirement" of leg braces. The unusual specificity and near-duplication of these filings, 26 minutes apart, is noted here without any independent verification of the medical claims themselves. First filing, second filing.
  • Progressive Vet PAC (Billings, Montana; FEC ID C00948521) filed a response on September 26 to a prior FEC request concerning its July Monthly Report. The committee says two 24-hour reports — for $2,451.48 and $2,528.28 in payments to 1912 Collaborative LLC for communications disseminated May 26 and May 29, 2026 — were filed late due to an "unintentional oversight," and that it filed the missing 24-hour reports on June 2, 2026, once the gap was discovered. Filing.

Cosmic Latte: The Average Color of the Universe. Astronomers computationally averaged the light from roughly 200,000 galaxies in the 2dF Galaxy Redshift Survey to find the universe's overall perceived color: a pale shade of beige nicknamed "cosmic latte" (#FFF8E7). The mix has grown less blue over the past 10 billion years as redder stars have become more prevalent.
