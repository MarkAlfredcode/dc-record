Top Story

Embezzlement by Former Treasurer Forces Rabb for Congress to Explain a String of Amended Reports

Committee says ousted treasurer Yolanda Brown, now indicted on federal charges, is responsible for repeated "Unauthorized Disbursement" entries across three reports.

In a Form 99 filed with the Federal Election Commission on September 4, 2026, Rabb for Congress — the campaign committee of Pennsylvania Democrat Christopher M. Rabb, an open-seat candidate for the state's 3rd Congressional District — told the FEC that sweeping revisions to its 2025 Amended October Quarterly Report, 2025 Amended Year-End Report, and 2026 April Quarterly Report all stem from "the Committee's former treasurer's fraud, embezzlement, and mismanagement of the Committee's funds."

The committee said every transaction reported as "Unauthorized Disbursement" or "Partial Refund of Unauthorized Disbursement(s)" across those three reports traces back to conduct it first disclosed to the Commission in an earlier Form 99 filed April 17, 2026. It added that former treasurer Yolanda Brown "has been indicted on federal charges in connection with her embezzlement of the Committee's funds," citing public news reports, and said it would forward the Commission any further information it receives about Brown's handling of committee money.

Separately in the same filing, the committee said it is amending its 2025 October Quarterly Report again — this time to change a disbursement description from "Political Consulting" to "Political Strategy Consulting" — at the FEC's request. Because Rabb does not currently hold the PA-03 seat, FEC records classify his candidacy as "Open seat," not incumbent; see the Sitting Lawmakers section below for how today's edition separates sitting members' compliance issues from candidates like Rabb.

Form 99 Correspondence

California GOP Explains FEA Time-Tracking Twice; Bernie 2016 Still Sitting on Leftover Funds

Ten non-termination Form 99s came in, all read in full for this edition. The California Republican Party Federal Account filed two separate responses — both signed by treasurer Bryan Burch — walking the FEC through the same underlying policy: employees spending more than 25% of their time on Federal Election Activity are reported on Line 30b, those spending less are reported on the Schedule H4. Elsewhere, Fifth Third Bancorp Federal Political Action Committee disclosed a $2,500 excessive-contribution refund request, and Bernie 2016 told the Commission it still hasn't decided what to do with its residual campaign cash a decade after the campaign ended. Rabb for Congress's filing is today's Top Story, above, and is not repeated in full here.

  • Zillow Group, Inc. Political Action Committee (ZG PAC) — Responding to an RFAI on its April Monthly Report, ZG PAC said a review of its bank reconciliations from 2021–2024 turned up a $7,500 discrepancy versus FEC filings, which it attributed to unreported voided checks and payments rejected by its former bank, Silicon Valley Bank, after that bank's 2023 collapse. It disclosed the adjustment on its April Monthly Report and says it has since hired a new compliance firm and changed internal staff and procedures.
  • California Republican Party Federal Account (response 1 of 2, re: May Monthly Report) — Treasurer Bryan Burch said a payment to "Public Square Partners, LLC," originally reported March 5, 2026 as an administrative expense, was voided in April after the party determined the check had mistakenly been issued from the federal account for what was actually non-federal spending; the void was reported as a negative Schedule H4 entry on the May report. The letter also explains the committee's Federal Election Activity time-tracking policy and says "Digital Media Consulting" payments went to contractors for website, social-media, and graphic-design work with no clear reference to a federal candidate.
  • Dolores Huerta Action Fund — An attorney for the fund followed up on a prior Form 99 concerning its October Quarterly and Year-End 2024 reports, confirming the fund received no contributions for FECA-defined political activity during that period.
  • Rabb for Congress — See Top Story, above.
  • Fifth Third Bancorp Federal Political Action Committee — Treasurer Nathan Hallford responded to an RFAI on the July Quarterly Report: a $2,500 contribution to Quigley for Congress was re-designated from the primary to the general election; a $2,500 contribution to Sylvia Garcia for Congress was flagged as excessive and a refund requested; and a contribution to Friends of Dave McCormick was re-designated to the 2030 primary.
  • Democratic Party of Arkansas — Responding to a letter on its July Monthly Report, the party said its Statement of Organization has been amended to reflect a new joint fundraising committee, "Friends of State Democratic Parties."
  • MAGA KY — Treasurer Charles Gantt said a discrepancy the FEC flagged between a 24-hour independent-expenditure report and Schedule B of the July Quarterly Report was a clerical error in the disclosed dissemination date, which the committee corrected in an amended report filed the same day.
  • California Republican Party Federal Account (response 2 of 2, re: amended April Monthly Report) — Same treasurer, Bryan Burch, and the same FEA time-tracking and Digital Media Consulting explanation as the party's other filing above.
  • The Front Line Action — The committee said it amended its August Monthly Report to correct the disbursement date on an independent-expenditure entry.
  • Bernie 2016 — Responding to a September 2, 2026 FEC letter, the long-dormant committee said it "continues to actively evaluate options for the use of the residual campaign funds" and has made no decision yet.

House Introduces Six Bills and Two Resolutions in a Pro Forma Session; Senate Adjourns Until Tuesday

Both chambers held brief pro forma sessions on Friday, September 4, 2026 as part of the August recess. The House convened at 10:00 a.m. and adjourned at 10:02 a.m. with no votes, no quorum calls, and no reports filed, but still introduced six public bills (H.R. 10298–10303) and two resolutions (H.Res. 1512–1513). The Senate met for roughly half a minute and adjourned until 1:15 p.m. on Tuesday, September 8, 2026. Notable among the introductions: H.R. 10300, Rep. James P. McGovern's bill to "foster accountability for digital content providers," and H.Res. 1512, marking 50 years since women were first admitted to the U.S. service academies.

  • H.R. 10298 — Rep. Bill Foster (D-IL-11), with nine cosponsors. Would amend the Higher Education Act of 1965 to let certain payments by public service employees count toward Public Service Loan Forgiveness.
  • H.R. 10299 — Rep. David Kustoff (R-TN-8), with two cosponsors. Would name the Alamo, Tennessee post office the "Kenneth Nolen Davis Post Office."
  • H.R. 10300 — Rep. James P. McGovern (D-MA-2). Would create new accountability requirements for digital content providers.
  • H.R. 10301 — Rep. Donald Norcross (D-NJ-1). Would award a Congressional Gold Medal to Peter McGuire for his contributions to the American labor movement.
  • H.R. 10302 — Rep. Scott H. Peters (D-CA-50), with one cosponsor. Would amend the Federal Power Act to coordinate federal authorizations for transmission facilities.
  • H.R. 10303 — Rep. Nellie Pou (D-NJ-9), with two cosponsors. Would direct the FAA to set up a flight-deck training program for certain of its own employees.
  • H.Res. 1512 — Rep. Stephanie I. Bice (R-OK-5), with three cosponsors. Celebrates the 50th anniversary of women's admission to U.S. service academies and the Class of 1980.
  • H.Res. 1513 — Rep. Glenn Thompson (R-PA-15), with one cosponsor. Supports designating the week of September 6, 2026 as "Celebrate Community Week."
