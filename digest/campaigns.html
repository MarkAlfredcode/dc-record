Top Story
Rep. Al Green signals impeachment push against Trump as House debates pulling troops from Iran
The most consequential congressional action this window came not from a compliance filing but from the floor: a stated intent to impeach the president and an active War Powers vote on Iran.

In the September 14 session of the House of Representatives, Rep. Al Green announced his intent to offer a privileged resolution, H.Res. 1486, impeaching President Donald John Trump for high crimes and misdemeanors. The same day, the House took up H. Con. Res. 93, directing the President under Section 5(c) of the War Powers Resolution to remove U.S. armed forces from hostilities with Iran; debate occurred but further proceedings were postponed without a vote.

Separately, Rep. Anna Paulina Luna introduced H.Res. 1536, recommending the House find Neville Singham in contempt of Congress for refusing to comply with a subpoena from the House Committee on Oversight and Government Reform. And Rep. Pramila Jayapal led a 51-member bloc introducing H.J. Res. 215, a disapproval resolution against a Department of Homeland Security/U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement rule setting fixed admission periods for foreign students and exchange visitors.

Full rundown of the day's floor action and all 34 bills/resolutions introduced is in the On the Record section below.

Requests for Additional Information
Six new RFAIs, including a failure-to-file notice and a pair of post-election contributions

Six committees received Requests for Additional Information (Form FRQ/RQ-2) in this window. Most are routine amend-and-clarify letters, but Sholdon Daniels for the People received the more serious RQ-7 notice — a warning that it may not have filed its July Quarterly Report at all.

  • Sholdon Daniels for the People (Sholdon Daniels, R, open-seat candidate, TX), treasurer Alyse Daniels — flagged: received an RQ-7 notice, a step up from a standard RFAI, stating the FEC has not received the campaign's July Quarterly Report (05/07/2026–06/30/2026) or that it failed to cover the full period; the committee must file immediately or face civil penalties. Filing.
  • American Peanut Shellers Association Political Action Committee, treasurer Chris Chammoun — asked to explain two contributions made after their designated elections had already occurred: $1,000 to Conservative Tom Sell for Congress on 4/23/2026 (Texas primary held 3/3/2026) and $5,000 to David Rouzer for Congress on 4/23/2026 (North Carolina primary held 3/3/2026); must obtain a refund or written redesignation within 60 days of receipt. Filing.
  • Mayra for Congress (Mayra Rivera-Vazquez, D, open-seat candidate, SC), treasurer Christine Marjorie Devries — the beginning cash balance on its July Quarterly Report doesn't match the ending balance reported on its 12-Day Pre-Primary Report; must correct and amend any subsequent affected reports. Filing.
  • Trustworthy Republicans Always Serve Humbly PAC, treasurer Kevin Broghamer — its July Quarterly Report was not signed by the treasurer or another agent authorized on its Statement of Organization; must file a properly signed amendment or update its treasurer designation. The FEC also flagged, for future reference, that several Schedule B contributions to federal candidates lacked an election designation. Filing.
  • Massachusetts Republican Party, treasurer Mindy McKenzie — its August Monthly Report miscalculated year-to-date Schedule H4 allocated-activity totals for the Administrative category, and failed to check the required allocated-activity category box for a Line 21(a) payment; both must be amended. Filing.
  • Justice League PAC, treasurer Patrick Swymer — its April Monthly Report's Schedule B disbursement to a federal candidate omitted the required state, office, and district; separately, a contribution attributed to "Ammar for Congress" also listed the committee ID for Electing Ethical Democrats, and the FEC wants clarification on whether that was an earmarked pass-through contribution or a direct contribution to Electing Ethical Democrats with no say over the candidate's identity. Filing.
Form 99 Correspondence
Common paymaster defense, refunded corporate-PAC money, and a candidacy disavowal notice

Fourteen non-termination Form 99 submissions came in this window, all read in full. The most substantive: End Citizens United defending a six-figure inter-affiliate reimbursement, and a formal FEC notice ordering a Chicago congressional hopeful to either disavow fundraising done in his name or declare his candidacy.

The Republican Party of Minnesota – Federal filed twice the same day, responding separately to Schedule H4 allocation questions on its April and May Monthly Reports — both stemming from the same August 9 FEC letter.

Filing.

Filing.

Filing.

Webb's View of M64. Where Hubble saw only the dark dust lane that gave Messier 64 its nickname "the Black Eye Galaxy," the James Webb Space Telescope's infrared instruments see that same dust glowing as it absorbs and re-emits light from newborn stars. Astronomers believe a past merger with a smaller galaxy explains why M64's inner and outer gas regions rotate in opposite directions.
