Top Story
FEC Letter Reveals Guilty Plea in $250,000 Embezzlement Scheme That Left Two Montana PACs Un-Terminated for Years
A Request for Additional Information addressed to Montanans for a Better Congress surfaces the committee's own account: a since-indicted "designated agent" who told officers the PAC had been terminated years ago has instead pleaded guilty to embezzling from it and several other campaigns, and the termination paperwork was apparently never filed.

The single most alarming filing in today's FEC correspondence window is a Request for Additional Information sent to Montanans for a Better Congress (C00811224), treasurer Timothy Bechtold. The RFAI quotes the committee's own Form 99 response, filed a year ago after the FEC flagged both Montanans for a Better Congress (MBC) and a related committee, Montanans Against Corruption in Congress (MACC), as delinquent nonfilers. Bechtold wrote that he was "surprised and concerned" to get those nonfiler notices because he had been assured both PACs were terminated following the 2022 election cycle — by their then-"designated agent," Abbey Cook.

According to the treasurer's account as quoted in the FEC's letter, Cook has since been indicted and "today entered a guilty plea for embezzling more than $250,000 from numerous candidates and political committees — including MACC and MBC." The termination paperwork the committees believed had been filed years ago apparently never was. Bechtold's letter says he has been in contact with the FBI. The FEC's RFAI treats the matter as touching on "an apparent unauthorized disbursement of Committee funds" and, while noting it may pursue further legal action, asks for any additional clarifying information.

It's a reminder that not every FEC compliance letter is boilerplate: buried among today's 18 Requests for Additional Information and six Form 99s — a heavier-than-usual correspondence day, detailed in full below — is a live embezzlement case that swept up two federal committees and, per the treasurer, is now an active FBI matter. A second item in today's batch is also worth flagging: Center Aisle PAC disclosed a $78,031.41 debt for a "court-ordered restitution payment owed to Committee," which the FEC likewise ties to "apparent unauthorized use of Committee funds" — see the Requests for Additional Information section below.

Sitting Lawmakers
Four Incumbents Get RFAIs Over Excessive Contributions and Missing 48-Hour Notices
Four sitting members of Congress — three House Democrats, one Senate Republican — drew Requests for Additional Information in today's window, all routine-type compliance letters (apparent excessive/prohibited contributions, missing 48-hour notices) rather than anything suggesting personal wrongdoing. None involve dollar figures large enough to be the day's lead, but all four are broken out here rather than folded into the general RFAI list below.
  • Families for James Lankford (Sen. James Lankford, R-OK, incumbent) — RFAI, July Quarterly Report. The FEC flags apparently excessive contributions from three individuals/entities: Muscogee Creek Nation (four entries netting $7,000 toward the 2028 primary after a $1,000 refund), Patricia O'Neal ($4,500 combined), and Nicholas K. Powell ($5,000 combined) — all above the $3,500-per-election individual limit. The committee must refund, redesignate, or reattribute the excess.
  • Lou Correa for Congress (Rep. Lou Correa, D-CA, incumbent) — RFAI, July Quarterly Report, two items. First, apparently excessive contributions: Mike Chalan ($4,000 combined for the general election), Irving Chase ($7,000 combined for the primary), and Edwards Lifesciences PAC ($6,000 combined, above the $5,000 multi-candidate-committee limit). Second, contributions received after the 2026 primary but designated for it may exceed the committee's net debts outstanding from that primary — the FEC wants the accounting checked.
  • Young Kim for Congress (Rep. Young Kim, R-CA, incumbent) — RFAI, July Quarterly Report. The committee filed 48-hour "last-minute contribution" notices for four donors — Donald A. Best, Kenneth Ching-Earn Chen, Edward G. Lewis, and Richard Matros, $1,000 to $1,500 each, totaling $4,500 — but none of those contributions actually appear on the report's Schedule A. The FEC wants the report amended or the discrepancy explained.
  • Citizens for Waters (Rep. Maxine Waters, D-CA, incumbent) — RFAI, July Quarterly Report, three items: a $1,500 contribution from Nike Inc. that appears to be a prohibited corporate contribution; two in-kind contributions (Clement Abonyi Jr., $1,500, and Lisa Peto, $3,300) made before the primary but designated to the general election in a way the FEC says doesn't qualify for that treatment; and two apparently-missed 48-hour notices, for $2,500 from Fair Isaac Corporation PAC (FICO PAC) and $3,500 from Optiver Americas LLC PAC.
Requests for Additional Information
A Heavy RFAI Day: 14 Non-Incumbent Committees Flagged, Topped by an Embezzlement Case and a Six-Figure Restitution Debt
Fourteen RFAIs went to non-incumbent campaigns, PACs, and party committees today — on top of the four incumbent RFAIs above, that's 18 total, a notably heavy batch likely tied to the July Quarterly/Monthly reporting cycle. Most are the FEC's standard boilerplate categories: excessive or prohibited contributions, missing 48-hour or 24-hour notices, unclarified candidate loans, and missing salary/wage disclosures from state party committees. Two stand out from the pack — the Montanans for a Better Congress embezzlement matter and Center Aisle PAC's $78,031.41 restitution debt — both covered in the Top Story above. Every item below was read in full; none were subject to this edition's PDF-read cap.
  • Montanans for a Better Congress (PAC) — RFAI referencing the committee's FEC Form 99. Embezzlement matter detailed in the Top Story above.
  • Center Aisle PAC (PAC) — RFAI, July Quarterly Report. Discloses a $78,031.41 debt owed to the committee for "court-ordered restitution," which the FEC links to an apparent unauthorized use of committee funds — suggesting the PAC was itself a victim of misappropriation and has since obtained a court restitution order. The FEC is asking for clarifying detail.
  • Republican Party of Florida (party committee) — RFAI, July Monthly Report. Its May, June, and July Monthly reports combined show zero salary/wage payments, which the FEC says is inconsistent with Federal Election Activity rules requiring federal-fund-only payment of staff who spend over 25% of their time on federal election work; asked to clarify.
  • Working Families Party PAC (PAC) — RFAI, July Monthly Report, two items. First, three $5,000 contributions to candidate committees — Analilia Mejia for New Jersey, Poindexter For Congress, and The Congressional Committee To Elect Robert White 2026 — were disbursed after each recipient's primary election date, which isn't permitted unless applied to primary debt. Second, a $2,280 independent-expenditure payment to Community Labor Administrative Services (backing Harry Dunn's Maryland primary campaign) appears to have missed the 24-hour reporting deadline.
  • Hector Mujica for Congress (challenger, FL) — RFAI, July Quarterly Report, three items: a $7,000 combined excessive contribution from Greg Long; three general-election contributions ($3,500 from Navneet Alag, $1,500 from Miriam Rivera, $1,500 from Laurie J. Schecter) that appear un-refunded even though the candidate isn't on the general-election ballot; and an apparently impermissible transfer from a separate committee, Hector Mujica for Florida.
  • Ohio Republican Party State Central & Executive Committee (party committee) — RFAI, July Monthly Report, two items: no salary/wage payments disclosed across three combined reports, and a payment for "INSURANCE" and "PAYROLL" on Schedule H4 that the FEC says can't be allocated between federal and non-federal accounts if it covers staff spending over 25% of their time on federal election work.
  • More Jobs, Less Government (PAC) — RFAI, Amended July Quarterly Report. Two independent-expenditure payments to M2 Placement LLC — $8,750 backing Alani Ines Bankhead and $3,750 backing Reilly Neill, both in Montana's primary — appear to have missed the 24-hour reporting deadline.
  • Giralt for Wyoming (open seat, WY) — RFAI, Amended April Quarterly Report. Asked to clarify the nature of a $5,247.45 candidate loan reported only as "In-Kind: Candidate Loan – Personal Funds" — specifically whether it was truly personal funds or drawn from a lending source that would require additional disclosure.
  • Ohio Democratic Party – Federal (party committee) — RFAI, July Monthly Report, two items: an apparently undisclosed contribution received from DLZ Corp Infrastructure Advocacy Federal PAC, and a "Communications Consulting" payment that may need to be reported as Federal Election Activity with the benefiting candidate identified, or possibly as an independent/coordinated expenditure.
  • Joy for Virginia (challenger, VA) — RFAI, April Quarterly Report. The FEC says several cycle-to-date "Column B" totals on the Summary and Detailed Summary pages don't reconcile with the prior report's totals plus this period's activity, and wants an amendment correcting the math.
  • Dave Beckwith for Congress (challenger, VA) — RFAI, July Quarterly Report, two items: itemized Schedule B expenditures missing required purpose descriptions, and a candidate loan that needs to be clarified as personal funds versus a loan from a lending institution (which would also require a Schedule C-1).
  • Committee to Elect Lauren Jewett for Congress (challenger, LA) — RFAI, 12-Day Pre-Primary Report. Seven contributions totaling roughly $5,625 — from Crescent Canna, Gogo Borgerding Jewelry, Jennifer Anne Portraits, Leyla's Photo Magic, Olivia Grey Pritchard Photography, Pearls And Pelicans, and The Glitter Bar — appear to be from corporate entities rather than individuals, which would make them prohibited contributions requiring a refund.
  • Bob Archuleta for Congress 2024 (open seat, CA) — RFAI, April Quarterly Report. A previously itemized Schedule C candidate loan is missing required detail on whether it came from personal funds or a lending institution; the committee is asked to amend and clarify.
  • Invest to Elect Illinois Battleground Support 2026 (PAC) — RFAI, July Quarterly Report, two items: the report was filed on the wrong form and must be resubmitted as an FEC Form 3, and earmarked contributions passed through a conduit are missing the required conduit-identification detail on Schedule A.

Hubble: Decagon Around Saturn's South Pole
Saturn's North Pole has long been known for a stable hexagonal cloud pattern first spotted by Voyager in the 1980s. New Hubble observations reveal a different geometric pattern circling the South Pole: a ten-sided decagon, likely formed by wave interactions between fast- and slow-moving atmospheric gas near the pole.
