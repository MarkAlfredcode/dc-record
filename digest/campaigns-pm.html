Top Story
House Certifies Contempt Referral Against Financier Leon Black Over Oversight Subpoena
The Speaker notified the House Monday that Leon D. Black has been referred to the U.S. Attorney for refusing to produce documents or sit for a deposition in the Oversight Committee's investigation.

On Monday, September 21, 2026, the Speaker pro tempore announced on the House floor that, pursuant to H.Res. 1543, the Speaker had certified to the United States Attorney for the District of Columbia the refusal of Leon D. Black to produce documents to and appear for a deposition before the Committee on Oversight and Government Reform, as directed by a duly issued subpoena. The certification formally refers Black for criminal contempt of Congress prosecution — the step that follows a House contempt vote.

The House adopted H.Res. 1543 on September 16, 2026, without objection. The resolution, sponsored by Committee Chairman Rep. James Comer (R-KY-1), recommends that the House find Black in contempt of Congress for refusing to comply with subpoenas duly issued by the committee. Monday's floor announcement, recorded in the Congressional Record, completed the referral by sending the matter to prosecutors.

It is now up to the U.S. Attorney's office to decide whether to pursue charges — a step Congress cannot compel, and one that contempt-of-Congress referrals rarely result in. Still, the certification marks a significant escalation in the committee's dispute with Black, whose past business ties — including to the late financier Jeffrey Epstein — have drawn sustained congressional scrutiny.

Requests for Additional Information
Ten More RFAIs Went Out This Week, From a Corporate PAC's Overages to Recycled Candidate Names

The FEC sent ten more Requests for Additional Information this cycle — covering four House challenger or open-seat committees, a presidential committee, a corporate PAC, and two 'nominee fund' committees that share a treasurer and were both flagged for borrowing a federal candidate's name without authorization. All ten were received by the FEC 09/20 (one committee's second RFAI was received 09/19–09/20) but, like Senator Booker's letter above, only appeared in this newsletter's data today.

  • McGinnis for the People (Brian McGinnis, NC-02, challenger) — RFAI on the April Quarterly Report, received 09/20. Contributor records list employer and occupation as literally "blank/blank," which doesn't satisfy FEC disclosure rules; the committee must show it made "best efforts" to collect the missing information or amend the report. RFAI.
  • McGinnis for the People also drew a second RFAI, on the July Quarterly Report, received the same day — three items: cycle-to-date totals on several Detailed Summary Page lines don't foot correctly, the report's beginning cash balance doesn't match the prior report's ending balance, and the report shows a negative ending cash balance of $13,363.36, which the FEC says could reflect an accounting error, an unreported debt, or a prohibited bank overdraft. RFAI.
  • Scott Singer for Congress - EMB Nominee Fund FL-25 (treasurer Paul Kilgore) — RFAI on an amended Statement of Organization, received 09/20. The committee's name includes a federal candidate's name (Scott Singer) without it appearing to be his authorized committee; it must amend its Statement of Organization to reflect authorization or drop the name. RFAI.
  • Valero Energy Corporation Political Action Committee — RFAI on the July Monthly Report, received 09/20. The FEC flagged two contributions that together appear to exceed the $5,000-per-election limit for a multicandidate committee: $5,000 to Ashley Hinson for Congress on 4/30/25 and another $5,000 to a separate committee, Ashley for Iowa, on 6/1/26 — both designated to the 2026 primary and both benefiting Rep. Ashley Hinson (R-IA). Valero must obtain a refund or a written redesignation for the excess. RFAI.
  • Ed Fitzgerald for Congress (Ohio's 7th District, challenger) — RFAI on the July Quarterly Report, received 09/20, with four items: apparent missed 48-hour notices for last-minute contributions ahead of the primary; two contributions covered by 48-hour notices — $1,800 from Robert Jacobs (4/19/26) and $3,000 from Summer Mansour (4/27/26) — that don't appear on Schedule A; a negative ending cash balance of $76,245.87; and conduit contributions itemized without the underlying earmarked contributors' names. The FEC's attachment separately lists three missing 48-hour notices, for $2,500, $3,500 and $1,000, all from mid-April. RFAI.
  • Casey for Congress - EMB Nominee Fund FL-22 — RFAI on an amended Statement of Organization, received 09/20, citing the identical unauthorized-candidate-name issue as Scott Singer for Congress - EMB Nominee Fund FL-25, above. The two committees share the same treasurer, Paul Kilgore, and the same Athens, Georgia address. RFAI.
  • Joshua McKee for Congress (Alabama's 2nd District, open seat) — RFAI on the 12-Day Pre-Primary Report, received 09/20. The report shows a negative ending cash balance of $3,871.73. RFAI.
  • Dan Green for Congress (Florida's 9th District, challenger) drew two RFAIs.
