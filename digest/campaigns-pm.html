Top Story
FEC Flags a Wave of Suspect ‘Committees’ With Prank Names and Placeholder Filings
Four newly registered political committees — one invoking Elon Musk’s Boring Company, one bearing Robinhood chief executive Vladimir Tenev’s name, and one built entirely as a jab at Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent — drew identical FEC warnings this week questioning whether their organizing paperwork is genuine.

The FEC sent nearly identical “RQ-1” letters on September 8 and 9 to four newly registered committees — Boring Company Coin LLC, Vladimir Tenev, Global Consortium Project, and one registered under the name “Scott Bessent: Taco Don Is Setting You Up To Be His Fall Guy. Quit FAFO’ing With The World Economy” — formally questioning whether the Statement of Organization (FEC Form 1) each filed was “true, correct, or complete.” Under the letters, each committee has 35 days to confirm or correct its filing or be moved into the FEC’s public “unverified filings” database, and the Commission notes it can still refer a knowingly false filing for criminal prosecution under 18 U.S.C. § 1001.

The Boring Company Coin LLC filing stands out: the treasurer name and mailing address the FEC listed on its own letter — “DSDS SDSD SDSD SDSD SDSD” at “DSAFASD SDFSDAF” — are placeholder-style text, suggesting the underlying registration may itself have been a prank or test submission rather than a real committee. The Vladimir Tenev committee, whose listed treasurer is Shiv Verma, has separately claimed status as a national party committee for an unspecified “OTH” (other) party, which the FEC says it cannot do without first petitioning for an advisory opinion.

The cluster of joke filings is a reminder of how low the bar is to register a federal political committee — anyone can file a Form 1 and claim a name, even one built purely to troll a sitting Cabinet official. Separately and more conventionally, the RFAI section below includes an FEC letter to Democrats for Mike Rogers, an unauthorized committee whose name incorporates a sitting federal candidate’s name without his committee’s authorization — a distinct but related class of naming-compliance problem.

Elsewhere in this edition, a Form 99 from Talarico for Texas — James Talarico’s U.S. Senate campaign — illustrates why this newsletter widened its filing lookback window last week: the letter was filed the evening of September 8 but wasn’t indexed into the FEC’s public filings data until September 9, meaning a same-day-only search would have missed it entirely.

Sitting Lawmakers
Six Incumbents Answer FEC Compliance Letters This Week

Six sitting members of Congress — five House Republicans and one House Democrat — had their campaign committees respond to FEC compliance letters this window, mostly involving over-the-limit contributions that have since been refunded or redesignated. None involve allegations of personal use or embezzlement.

  • Rep. Victoria Spartz (R-IN, at-large) — Victoria Spartz for Congress confirmed that all contributions flagged on its July Quarterly Report as temporarily excessive, non-federal, or possibly corporate have been remedied — reattributed or refunded, including one contribution confirmed to be corporate and refunded — with the corrections to appear on the committee's October Quarterly Report.
  • Rep. Steve Stivers (R-OH) — Stivers for Congress notified the FEC it plans to file a termination report for the committee by year's end, and will continue filing on its regular schedule until then.
  • Rep. Celeste Maloy (R-UT-2) — Celeste for Congress responded to an RFAI, clarifying that a previously-omitted $7,769.39 debt to "District City Consulting" on its Pre-Primary report was a duplicate entry, now corrected via amendment to disclose the debt only once.
  • Rep. Julie Fedorchak (R-ND, at-large) — Fedorchak for ND responded to an RFAI on its July Quarterly Report flagging over-limit contributions: it refunded a $600 excess to Cunningham for NM on 6/30/26, refunded $25.25 in excess from the American Israel Public Affairs Committee Political Action Committee, refunded $5,000 in excess from an individual donor named George Haux, and received authorization to redesignate a contribution from Hunton Andrews Kurth LLP within the required 60-day window — all corrections to appear on the October Quarterly Report.
  • Rep. Glenn Grothman (R-WI-6) — Glenn Grothman for Congress responded to an RFAI on possible over-limit donors in its July Quarterly Report: an administrative error had duplicated two of three flagged donors (now corrected via amendment with the correct election designations), and the third donor, Lawrence Wiley, was refunded his excess contribution on 8/5/26, to be reflected on the October Quarterly Report.
  • Rep. Yvette Clarke (D-NY-9) — Clarke for Congress received a 2-item RFAI on its April Quarterly Report, due 10/15/2026: (1) Schedule D itemizes a previously-undisclosed $10,760.54 opening debt to "Berger Hirschberg Strategies" that wasn't on the prior report, requiring an amendment; (2) a debt owed to "Katz Compliance" shows a $2,800.00 opening balance that doesn't match the $2,844.52 closing balance on the previous report, also requiring correction.
Requests for Additional Information
104 RFAIs Hit Candidate Committees, PACs and Party Committees Outside Congress

The FEC sent 104 Requests for Additional Information to non-incumbent filers this window — candidate committees (including presidential and Senate campaigns), PACs, and party committees — most flagging routine issues like cash-on-hand discrepancies, missing disbursement detail, or Statement-of-Organization questions. Seven of the most substantive were read in full, including the DCCC, Goldman Victory Fund, and a committee called Democrats for Mike Rogers whose name drew its own compliance flag (see Top Story); the FEC's own indexing lag, flagged below, meant some of this window's letters didn't appear in FEC's public data until a day after they were filed.

Form 99 Correspondence
42 Miscellaneous Filings, From Loan Forgiveness to a Decade-Old Zombie Committee

Outside the termination filings (tallied separately below), 42 non-incumbent Form 99s were filed this window — mostly committees responding in free text to FEC letters or acknowledging administrative changes. Seven were read in full, including a candidate forgiving his own campaign loan after losing his primary and a former congressman's committee still distributing funds more than a decade after he left office.

  • Kevin Fagan for Congress (WA-04) — a Loan Forgiveness filing: after losing his primary, candidate Kevin Fagan forgave the remaining $13,973.59 balance of the personal loan he had made to his own committee.
  • Ohio Republican Party State Central & Executive Committee — responded to an RFAI on its amended report about allocating staff time between federal and non-federal accounts, saying employees log compensated time monthly, none currently exceed the 25% federal-election-activity threshold (staff are focused on the governor's race), and any employee who did cross that threshold would not be allocated between accounts. The committee filed three other RFAI/Form-99 responses this same week (see Terminations/RFAI entries below) — not read in full this edition.
  • Black Clergy of Philadelphia Political Outreach Coalition — FEC notified the group that its July Quarterly Report was filed on paper rather than electronically, despite having crossed the $50,000 threshold that requires electronic filing; continued paper filing risks being treated as a "nonfiler" subject to an administrative fine.
  • Allred for Texas (Colin Allred, D, running for U.S. Senate) — responded to an FEC letter on the July Quarterly Report by agreeing to amend the report so its disbursement purposes meet disclosure requirements; treasurer Steve Mele signed the response.
  • Friends of Cliff Stearns (the campaign committee of the former Florida congressman, who left office in 2013) — responding to an FEC letter, said its intent is to eventually terminate the committee or transfer its remaining balance to a multicandidate PAC, and that it continues to distribute its remaining funds — a still-active "zombie" committee more than a decade after Stearns left Congress.

  • Talarico for Texas (James Talarico, D, running for U.S. Senate) — response to an RFAI on the July Quarterly Report: says most of the flagged excessive or impermissible contributions have already been refunded or redesignated (to appear on the October Quarterly Report) and the rest are being remedied, and that it will amend its reports to align each Line 20(a) refund with the correct election cycle. House Marks 9/11's 25th Anniversary, Introduces 35 Bills and Resolutions Before Adjourning

The Senate met only in pro forma session on September 10 — gaveling in and out in 30 seconds — before adjourning until 3 p.m. Monday, September 14. The House introduced 25 bills (H.R. 10326–10350) and 10 resolutions (H.Res. 1520–1529) before also adjourning until Monday "as a further mark of respect for the victims of the terrorist attack," following floor consideration and adoption of H.Res. 1520 marking the 25th anniversary of the September 11, 2001 attacks. Seven committee reports were also filed, including bills to sunset the Advisory Committee on the Records of Congress and to revise Government Publishing Office authorities.

Notable introductions include a bipartisan bill from Rep. Thomas H. Kean to award SEAL Team Six a Congressional Gold Medal, Rep. Mark DeSaulnier's bill to repeal Section 230 of the Communications Act, and Rep. Christopher H.
