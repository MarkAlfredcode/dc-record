End Citizens United Faces FEC Inquiry Over $176,978.93 From Its Own Non-Federal Arm
A Request for Additional Information asks whether a six-figure contribution from "End Citizens United Non-Federal" broke rules that bar unregistered organizations from funding federal accounts.

The Federal Election Commission's Reports Analysis Division sent a Request for Additional Information (RFAI) on September 13, 2026 to Kimberly Coleman, treasurer of End Citizens United, questioning a $176,978.93 contribution the committee received on February 23, 2026 and disclosed on its March Monthly Report (covering February 1-28, 2026). The FEC's letter identifies the source of that money as "End Citizens United Non-Federal" — an entity the Commission says is not itself registered as a federal political committee.

Under 52 U.S.C. §§30116(f) and 30118 and 11 CFR §102.5(b), organizations that aren't registered federal political committees can only give money to federal committees if they can show the funds are permissible — either because the contribution came from a segregated account holding only funds allowed under the Act, or through a reasonable accounting method demonstrating the organization had enough permissible funds on hand. The FEC's letter asks End Citizens United to clarify whether the contribution meets that standard, and warns that if the funds turn out to be impermissible, the committee may need to refund the money or transfer it to a non-federal account within 30 days of receipt to avoid the funds being presumed impermissible.

The letter — the second the FEC has sent End Citizens United over this report, per its "RQ-2" label — sets a response deadline of October 19, 2026. Failure to respond adequately by then could trigger an audit or enforcement action, the letter warns. As of this filing window, End Citizens United has not filed a public response.
