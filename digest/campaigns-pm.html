Top Story
Federal Court Orders Former Center Aisle PAC Treasurer to Repay $78,031 in Restitution
A new Form 99 shows the bipartisan PAC has been in FEC correspondence over the matter for more than a year.

In a Form 99 filed with the Federal Election Commission on September 24, Center Aisle PAC (C00620443) disclosed that a federal court has ordered its former treasurer, Katherine Buchanan, to pay $78,031.41 in restitution to the committee. The PAC says it has listed that amount as a debt owed to it on Schedule D of its second-quarter 2026 report, citing two FEC advisory opinions — Advisory Opinion 1991-38 (DeConcini) and Advisory Opinion 2005-04 (Boehner) — as guidance for how to account for the judgment.

The filing, signed by current treasurer Lauren Decot Lee, says the matter traces back to Form 99s the committee has been submitting "since June 6, 2025 and subsequently" — meaning Center Aisle PAC has been in continuous correspondence with the FEC's Reports Analysis Division about the fallout from its former treasurer's conduct for well over a year. The filing does not spell out what Buchanan did to trigger a court-ordered restitution judgment, but the structure of the disclosure — money a former treasurer has been ordered to repay to the committee, resolved through litigation rather than a routine refund — points to some form of misappropriation of committee funds rather than an ordinary bookkeeping dispute.

The committee says it "continues to work with the Commission to fully resolve this matter," suggesting further filings are likely. This filing surfaced through a routine automated sweep the FEC compliance desk runs for red-flag keywords — including "restitution" — across incoming Form 99 and RFAI correspondence.

Sitting Lawmakers
Three Incumbents' Committees Draw FEC Attention

Three sitting members of Congress had their campaign committees in FEC correspondence this window — two facing open compliance questions, one closing one out.

  • Ralph Norman For Congress, the campaign committee of Rep. Ralph Norman (R-SC-5), received an RFAI on its July Quarterly report questioning receipts logged only as "Uncashed Check" on Schedule A. The FEC warns that if the committee received goods or services from a vendor without ever paying for them, that could amount to a prohibited corporate contribution requiring a refund or disgorgement within 30 days. RFAI.
  • Mike Bost For Congress Committee, the campaign of Rep. Mike Bost (R-IL-12), received a follow-up RFAI on its July Quarterly report. The FEC's letter acknowledges it already received "additional explanation" from the committee but says two issues remain open: $10,000 in contributions from the National Pork Producers Council Pork PAC that exceed the $5,000-per-election limit, and contributions received after the 2026 primary that may exceed the committee's net debts outstanding from that race. RFAI.
  • Friends of Jahana Hayes, the committee for Rep. Jahana Hayes (D-CT-5), filed a Form 99 closing out an August 20 RFAI about excessive contributions: the campaign says the excessive portions from Joan Rechnitz, Steve English, and Molly Munger were all refunded in July and will appear on the October 15 quarterly report. Response.
Requests for Additional Information
30 RFAIs Land on Campaigns and Committees, Led by a Six-Figure Transfer Dispute

The FEC's Reports Analysis Division sent 30 Requests for Additional Information in this window, all dated September 24 — the routine machinery of quarterly-report review. Most concern math errors, missing report descriptions, or excessive contributions in the low five figures. The outlier is SD PAC, questioned over $410,000 in transfers to three other PACs plus a $100,000 individual contribution, both well beyond anything else in today's batch. Two Colorado-area candidate committees — Wanda James For Congress and Alex Kelloff For Colorado — separately face unresolved general-election contribution problems tied to primary losses, and Friends of Michael McCord drew RFAIs on consecutive reports for the same recurring cash-balance discrepancy.

  • Visionary PAC (C00603134) was asked why a $5,000 contribution to Don Davis For NC was disbursed on March 16, 2026 — after that campaign's March 3 primary — since contributions can't be designated for an election that has already occurred unless applied to debt retirement. RFAI.
  • Gun Rights America (C00742635) appears to have missed filing 24-hour reports for roughly $18,000 in independent expenditures — paid to Colorspray Media, Mudshare and PXI Inc. in early May 2026 — supporting Thomas Massie and Ed Gallrein in Kentucky's presidential primary. RFAI.
  • National Creditors Bar Association PAC (C00491589) made two $5,000 contributions to Andy Barr For Congress, Inc. tied to the same election — together exceeding the $5,000-per-election multicandidate-committee limit. RFAI.
  • National Committee to Preserve Social Security & Medicare PAC (C00172296) must clarify whether $29,754.18 it paid its connected organization for "salary reimbursement" was meant to influence a federal election — a corporation generally can't fund a PAC's fundraising or administrative facilitation beyond what the law allows. RFAI.
  • International Association of Sheet Metal, Air, Rail and Transportation Workers Political Action League (C00007542) made two $5,000 contributions to Lilac PAC in the same calendar year, $10,000 total against a $5,000 annual limit on committee-to-committee giving, and separately mis-lined some refund disclosures. Response.

On the Record
House Introduces 100 Measures Including an Impeachment Resolution; Senate Passes Aviation Bills, Fails Iran War Powers Vote

Thursday, September 24 was a light House day and a busy Senate one. The House gaveled in at 2:30 p.m. and adjourned three minutes later with no votes and no quorum calls, but its members introduced 85 bills and 15 resolutions — the day's most notable being H.Res. 1575, introduced by Rep. Steve Cohen (D-TN-9), impeaching President Donald J. Trump for high crimes and misdemeanors, referred to the Committee on the Judiciary. Also notable: H.R. 10538 from Rep. Greg Casar (D-TX-35) would create a new Department of Artificial Intelligence to regulate advanced AI systems, and H.R. 10540 from Rep. Kathy Castor (D-FL-14) would give the FTC new rulemaking and civil-penalty authority over unfair or deceptive practices.

The Senate, by contrast, took three record votes. By 49 yeas to 50 nays, it failed to adopt H.Con.Res. 89, which would have directed the President to remove U.S. forces from hostilities with Iran under the War Powers Resolution. Separately, the Senate passed the John A. Hauser Mental Health in Aviation Act (S. 3257) and the Aviation Medication Transparency Act (S. 3258), both aimed at FAA medical-certification policy, and passed H.R. 2388, taking certain Washington State land into trust for the Lower Elwha Klallam Tribe. The Senate also invoked cloture (74-25) on the Protect College Sports Act (S. 4668), a name-image-likeness bill for student athletes, after adopting a substitute amendment (77-23) and tabling several competing amendments on the enactment date; floor debate and a passage vote are set to resume Monday, September 28. The Senate concurred in House amendments to two bills — the Illegal Red Snapper and Tuna Enforcement Act (S. 283) and the Crow Tribe Water Rights Settlement Act (S. 240) — and agreed to four commemorative resolutions, including S.Res. 905 designating September 25, 2026 as "National Lobster Day." The Senate introduced 74 bills and 14 resolutions of its own; consistent with this newsletter's House-focused bill-tracking scope, those aren't itemized below.
