Top Story
FEC sends Michael J. Rogers's Michigan Senate campaign two letters on excessive contributions, including some not yet refunded
Both letters to Rogers for Senate cite apparent over-limit contributions, and the July Quarterly letter adds receipts from an unregistered organization.

The Federal Election Commission sent two requests for additional information on October 14 to Rogers for Senate, the campaign committee of Michael J. Rogers, the Republican candidate in the open-seat Michigan Senate race. Treasurer Danny Peppe was the addressee. One letter covers the July Quarterly Report and the other covers the 12-Day Pre-Primary Report (07/01/2026 to 07/15/2026). The pre-primary letter's attachment runs 119 pages. July Quarterly letter; Pre-Primary letter.

In the July Quarterly letter the FEC says Schedule A shows contributions that appear to exceed the $3,500-per-election limit. It acknowledges the committee's earlier explanation of corrective action on some of them, but states that 'to date, one or more of the apparent excessive contributions have not been refunded, redesignated, or reattributed.' The pre-primary letter opens with the same type of excessive-contribution item.

The July Quarterly letter also says Schedule A discloses contributions from one or more organizations that are not registered political committees. The committee is asked to clarify whether those contributions are permissible, since such groups must use a segregated account or show they received enough permissible funds. A refund is required within 30 days of the treasurer learning a contribution was prohibited. The letters describe apparent problems only and make no finding of a violation. Responses are due 11/18/2026.

Twenty-five more RFAIs: excessive contributions, allocation errors and wrong-district candidate data

It includes over-limit contributions at party and PAC committees, Schedule H4 allocation problems at state parties, and two independent-expenditure filers who gave the wrong office sought for a candidate.

Two committees, Bold America and Retire Career Politicians, share Treasurer Christopher Koob and the same P.O. Box in Washington.
