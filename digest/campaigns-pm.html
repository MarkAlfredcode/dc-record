Top Story
House Oversight Committee Moves to Hold Leon Black in Contempt of Congress Over Epstein Subpoenas
A Tuesday markup follows two months of negotiation and a missed deposition date; Chairman James Comer plans to offer a substitute amendment.

The House Committee on Oversight and Government Reform will hold a full committee business meeting Tuesday, September 15, 2026, at 4:00 p.m. ET in Room 2154 of the Rayburn House Office Building to consider a resolution recommending that the House of Representatives find Leon David Black in contempt of Congress for refusing to comply with two committee subpoenas.

According to the committee's business-meeting memo, Black willfully failed to comply with both a deposition subpoena and a document-production subpoena issued as part of the committee's investigation into: the alleged mismanagement of the federal government's investigation into Jeffrey Epstein and Ghislaine Maxwell; the circumstances and subsequent investigations of Epstein's death; the operation of sex-trafficking rings and how the federal government can combat them; the ways Epstein and Maxwell allegedly sought to curry favor and exercise influence to protect their illegal activities; and potential ethics-rule violations by elected officials.

The memo says the committee negotiated with Black's counsel for more than two months, agreeing at his request to push his deposition date back by nearly two months and his document-production deadline by two additional weeks. Black nonetheless produced only one document responsive to the subpoena and refused to appear for his scheduled deposition on September 3, 2026 — prompting Chairman James Comer, a Kentucky Republican, to recommend the House find him in contempt.

Comer is expected to offer an amendment in the nature of a substitute to the contempt report at Tuesday's markup; the committee memo does not detail its contents. The outcome of the vote was not yet known as of this edition.

FEC Questions End Citizens United Over $177,000 From Its Own Non-Federal Account

Five RFAIs cleared dedupe this edition, all filed September 13, 2026 with an October 19, 2026 response deadline. The most notable involves a major Democratic PAC's transfer between its federal and non-federal arms; the rest are routine reporting-accuracy requests.

  • End Citizens United (treasurer Kimberly Coleman) was flagged over a $176,978.93 contribution recorded on its March Monthly report from "End Citizens United Non-Federal," an entity not registered with the FEC as a political committee. The FEC is asking the PAC to demonstrate the funds came from a permissibly segregated account — or refund the amount within 30 days if it can't. RFAI.

  • Leadership in Action (treasurer Katie Wenetta) was asked to correct its July Quarterly Report: its aggregate year-to-date total for individual contributions appears incorrect, and the FEC wants the itemized disclosure of all contributors who gave over $200. RFAI.
  • New York State Democratic Committee (treasurer David Alpert) received two RFAIs on the identical issue — its amended June and August Monthly Reports (both received 8/31/2026) each left the Schedule F coordinated-expenditure schedule without the required aggregate general-election-expenditure total per candidate supported. House Marks 9/11 Anniversary, Introduces 35 Bills and Resolutions Before Recess

The most recent Congressional Record available covers Thursday, September 10, 2026. The Senate met only in pro forma session (3:00:01 p.m. to 3:00:31 p.m.) before adjourning until 3 p.m. Monday, September 14. The House introduced 25 public bills (H.R. 10326–10350) and 10 resolutions (H. Res. 1520–1529), then adjourned at 3:33 p.m. after agreeing to H. Res. 1520, marking the 25th anniversary of the September 11, 2001 terrorist attacks, as "a further mark of respect to the memory of the victims." There were no recorded votes.

Among the day's bills: a bipartisan push led by Rep. Michael Cloud and 19 cosponsors to require Congressional Budget Office and Joint Committee on Taxation estimates to include debt-servicing costs (H.R. 10327); a Suozzi-Tenney bill strengthening Medicare fraud-and-abuse program integrity oversight (H.R. 10348); and a Christopher Smith bill barring goods made with child or forced cobalt labor in the Democratic Republic of the Congo from the U.S. market (H.R. 10346).
