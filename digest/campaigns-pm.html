Top Story
Valero's corporate PAC flagged for double-dipping into Ashley Hinson's House and Senate accounts
An FEC letter sent Friday asks Valero Energy Corporation Political Action Committee to explain two maxed-out $5,000 contributions tied to the same Iowa Republican, one to her old House account and one to her new Senate campaign.

Valero Energy Corporation Political Action Committee received a Request for Additional Information from the Federal Election Commission, filed 09/20/2026, questioning a pair of $5,000 contributions that together may exceed the legal per-election limit for a multicandidate committee. The letter, addressed to treasurer Carl Crawford, flags a $5,000 contribution made 04/30/2025 to Ashley Hinson for Congress — the House re-election committee of Rep. Ashley Hinson (Arenholz), R-Iowa-2 — and a second $5,000 contribution made 06/01/2026 to Ashley for Iowa, the new Senate campaign committee Hinson opened last September to run for Iowa's open Senate seat. Both contributions were coded to the 2026 primary election.

Federal law caps a multicandidate PAC's contribution to a single candidate at $5,000 per election. Because FEC records show both committees as vehicles for the same candidate, Ashley Hinson (Arenholz), the two primary-cycle contributions read on paper as $10,000 to one candidate for one primary. The FEC is asking Valero's PAC to either document that the contributions were properly attributable to two distinct elections or to obtain a refund or written redesignation within the required window.

The letter gives the committee no fixed response deadline beyond noting that adequate responses factor into whether the Commission opens an audit, and states the committee will not receive a further notice on this matter.

Sitting Lawmakers
Booker's Senate campaign asked to fix contribution-refund and redesignation paperwork

One sitting member of Congress drew FEC correspondence in this window: New Jersey's senior senator, over routine reporting-format problems rather than any substantive violation.

  • Sen. Cory Booker (D-NJ) — Cory Booker for Senate received an RFAI, filed 09/20/2026, on its July Quarterly Report. The FEC flagged two issues: Schedule B discloses refunds to two contributors — $397.50 to Sandy Bandy (06/21/2026) and $500 to Mary Genz (06/28/2026) — that don't correspond to any original contribution the committee had previously reported, which can happen when the original receipt was never itemized, was coded to the wrong election, or the refund exceeds what was actually given; and the committee's reported contribution redesignations and reattributions don't follow the FEC's required three-entry memo format (original entry, negative adjustment entry, and redesignated entry). Filing.
