Dolores Huerta Action Fund tells the FEC its 2024 independent-expenditure reports may be overstated
Counsel's review suggests general overhead was reported as independent-expenditure cost; amendments are delayed because the filing account is no longer active.

The Dolores Huerta Action Fund filed a Form 99 on October 3 (letter dated October 2) to Patrick Harkins, a Senior Campaign Finance and Reviewing Analyst at the Federal Election Commission. It concerns the group's October Quarterly 2024 (07/01/2024–09/30/2024) and Year-End 2024 (10/01/2024–12/31/2024) reports.

Outside counsel's ongoing review found that certain amounts may have been reported on an overinclusive basis, including legal, compliance and administrative costs, expenses shared with nonfederal or candidate-free voter engagement, and pay for staff who did both qualifying and other work. The group says general overhead not directly attributable to a specific express-advocacy communication should not count toward an independent expenditure's reported cost.

The letter adds that the compliance-software account used to prepare the original reports is no longer active, so the reports must be recreated in a new account before amendments can be filed. It asks the FEC's Reports Analysis Division for additional time and was signed by Tracey Frazier Wigglesworth of The Political Law Group. The letter gives no dollar figures or deadline.
