Top Story
No Labels PAC Discloses $7.6 Million Routed to Candidates in Both Parties Through Member-Solicitation Program
A Form 99 filed Sept. 18 details how the centrist group's 'internal communication' allowance moved money from its own membership to more than 100 House and Senate campaigns this cycle.

No Labels PAC filed a Form 99 with the Federal Election Commission on September 18 accounting for its use of the FEC's "internal communication" allowance — a provision that lets a connected political committee solicit contributions from its own restricted class of members to specific candidates without the committee's own money changing hands or being treated as a coordinated expenditure. The filing says No Labels used the Democracy Engine platform to solicit its members to contribute directly to "aligned candidates," and that members gave a combined $7,557,916.11 during this reporting period alone.

The filing lists dozens of individual candidates who benefited this period, including $19,000 to Rep. Michael Lawler (R-N.Y.), $20,400 to Johnny Garcia, $22,250 to Rep. Darren Soto (D-Fla.), and smaller amounts spread across senators and House members from both parties, from Sen. Susan Collins (R-Maine) to Sen. Jon Ossoff (D-Ga.).

A second table in the same filing discloses cycle-to-date totals for the full 2026 election cycle, and the sums are far larger: $714,028.81 to Sen. Collins, $710,845.38 to Sen. Bill Cassidy (R-La.), $403,602.90 to Rep. Josh Gottheimer (D-N.J.), $353,551.72 to Rep. Lawler, and $314,960.71 to Rep. Marie Gluesenkamp Perez (D-Wash.), among well over a hundred other House members, senators, and joint fundraising committees named in the filing. No Labels has used this same reporting mechanism throughout the cycle; Saturday's filing is a periodic accounting of it rather than a one-time disclosure, but the scale of the total — north of $7.5 million just in the period covered — is among the largest sums the group has reported moving through the program this cycle.
