FEC Tells Treasurer Behind Two Joke-Named Committees to Prove the Paperwork Was Real
Two RFAI letters dated September 17 — one to "John Madison and Martha Washington Philosophy Club Thoughts for Future Election Case Being Illustrating Hillary Clinton and Bernie Sanders" and one to "God Please Save Us I Repeat Phil Murphy Vs Bernie Sanders" — share a treasurer and a Truckee, California address.

The Federal Election Commission's Reports Analysis Division sent identical "Verification of Form 1" letters on September 17 to two committees with strange, meme-like names, both organized by the same treasurer, Jaleel Rashid Shabazz-Blue (signing the second letter as "Jaleel R. Shabazz"), from the same Truckee, California P.O. box. The committees are John Madison and Martha Washington Philosophy Club Thoughts for Future Election Case Being Illustrating Hillary Clinton and Bernie Sanders (FEC ID C00961078) and God Please Save Us I Repeat Phil Murphy Vs Bernie Sanders (FEC ID C00960872).

Both letters state it has "come to the attention of the Commission" that the committees may have failed to include true, correct, or complete information on their FEC Form 1 Statements of Organization. Each gives the treasurer until October 22, 2026, to confirm the filings are accurate or amend them — warning that failure to respond within 35 days will get the committees pulled from the FEC's public searchable database and moved to an "unverified filings" list. Both letters also recite the FEC's standard boilerplate that knowingly making a materially false statement to the agency is punishable under 18 U.S.C. § 1001 — language this edition's automated keyword sweep flagged for the word "fraud." Having read both letters in full, that language is the FEC's stock warning included in every Form 1 verification notice, not a specific fraud allegation against either committee.

It's unclear what, if any, campaign activity either committee is tied to; neither filing discloses a candidate. The pairing — two oddly named committees, one treasurer, one address, flagged on the same day — is the kind of registration the FEC's verification process exists to catch.

Five filings from sitting members of Congress survived this edition's dedupe window. Two are RFAIs over unresolved contribution and disbursement-reporting issues, one is a committee's pledge to keep operating rather than terminate, and two are routine termination approvals.

Requests for Additional Information
RFAI Cluster Hits Two California Challenger Committees Sharing a Treasurer, Plus a Florida Senate Bid With Four Open Items

Twelve RFAIs survived dedupe this window, all dated September 17. Two relate to a California House challenger's pair of affiliated committees; four hit a single Florida Senate campaign; two are the joke-named committee letters covered above.

  • James Gallagher (R), a challenger for California's 1st District, runs two authorized committees that share a treasurer, Vona L. Copp: The Gallagher Committee (C00942458) and Gallagher For Congress (C00935114). Both received RFAIs on the same underlying issue — Schedule A transfers between the two committees were disclosed as if from an affiliated committee, but Gallagher for Congress isn't listed as an affiliate on The Gallagher Committee's Statement of Organization. The FEC asked either the Statement of Organization or the reports be amended to resolve the discrepancy. Pre-Primary RFAI (The Gallagher Committee), July Quarterly RFAI (The Gallagher Committee).
  • Gallagher For Congress separately received two more RFAIs: one flagging that its Post-Special report listed the wrong special-general election date (it used 6/22/2026; the FEC says the election was held 6/2/2026), cited apparent excessive contributions including three $2,500 payments from the National Association of Home Builders PAC (Build PAC), and listed roughly 40 contributions still designated to a 2026 runoff election the candidate will not participate in that the FEC says have not been refunded or redesignated. A second RFAI flagged incorrect cycle-to-date Column B totals on the committee's July Quarterly report. Post-Special RFAI, July Quarterly RFAI.
  • Angela Nixon (D), a challenger for a Florida U.S. Senate seat, saw her committee Angie For Florida (treasurer David Geller) hit with four separate RFAIs. The Year-End 2025 letter cited apparent excessive contributions (including $7,000 each from Ava, Chris, and Noah Touchton, and $5,000 from the Rashida Tlaib Campaign), a $300 contribution from an apparently unregistered organization (NW St Johns County United For Progress), an unitemized conduit disbursement, and missing election designations on several contributions. The April Quarterly letter cited more apparent excessive contributions plus a $78,941.28 unexplained drop in receipts versus the original report. Two more RFAIs (12-Day Pre-Primary and July Quarterly) flagged incorrect cycle-to-date Column B totals and additional apparent excessive contributions from donors including Zouhaier Hamami, Leslie Jean-Bart, Samir Kakli, Stephanie Porta, Barbara Stiefel, and Autumn Stokes. Year-End 2025, April Quarterly, 12-Day Pre-Primary, July Quarterly.
  • Two more RFAIs — the "Verification of Form 1" letters to John Madison and Martha Washington Philosophy Club Thoughts for Future Election Case Being Illustrating Hillary Clinton and Bernie Sanders and God Please Save Us I Repeat Phil Murphy Vs Bernie Sanders — are covered above in the Top Story.
Form 99 Correspondence
A Candidate Forgives His Own Loan, a Committee Explains a Software Bug, and One Florida Challenger Files Six Strange Submissions

Thirteen non-termination F99s survived dedupe. Most are routine committee responses to prior RFAIs explaining corrective action; one is a personal loan forgiveness, and six come from a single Florida House challenger committee whose submissions range from campaign ephemera to an unusual legal claim naming a sitting member of Congress.

  • Jotham Stein, a challenger for California's 16th District, filed a Loan Forgiveness notice through Jotham Stein For Congress LLC: he forgave the remaining $25,799.73 balance of a $32,000 personal loan he made to his own committee, after the committee had already repaid him $6,200.27. Filing.
  • The Campaign Committee To Elect Clifford G. Fleetwood For U.S. Congress (Clifford Gene Fleetwood, a Florida 20th District challenger) filed six separate Miscellaneous Submissions to the FEC on September 17: a claimed NRSC "member in good standing" verification plaque, two campaign poster/sign images, a "Congratulations" note, a claimed honorable-discharge and law-enforcement-association commemorative plaque, and — most notably — a filing asserting Fleetwood was "illegally removed from all Florida elections ballots" and stating that documentation would be used as evidence in a purported federal injunction case naming Rep. Debbie Wasserman Schultz, Florida Secretary of State Cord Byrd, a Broward County chief judge, and other named officials as defendants, before U.S. Chief Judge James E. Boasberg. The FEC filing itself makes no ruling on these claims; DC Record could not independently verify a matching federal case. Filing.
  • Save America Fund (treasurer Edward Galvin) responded to an RFAI about an untimely Form 24, explaining that a $60,000 media buy was reported on time but $10,875 in additional production costs surfaced later and were amended into the report within 24 hours of the committee learning of them. Filing.
  • Common Defense Action Fund (treasurer Jose Vasquez) responded to an RFAI, attributing a $200,000 independent-expenditure memo and debt that appeared on its Q2 report to a FECFile software quirk — an entry for a planned-but-cancelled ad was deleted from a 24/48-hour report but not from the underlying Schedule E — and attributed late 24-hour reports on other independent expenditures to an internal miscommunication it says it has since fixed procedurally. Filing.
  • NetChoice PAC (treasurer Kayla Glaze) responded to an RFAI, explaining that an amended July Quarterly report added $300,000 in receipts and $150,025 in disbursements that were inadvertently omitted from the original filing, and that a non-federal contribution originally misreported on Line 23 was moved to the correct Schedule B line. Filing.
  • Working Families Party PAC responded to an RFAI over a late-reported expense, explaining that a digital-ad billboard buy supporting Robert Peters didn't originally include design costs, which were reported once the vendor's invoice arrived weeks later. Filing.
  • Mike Beltran For Congress (treasurer Paul Kilgore), the committee for Florida 14th District challenger Mike Beltran, responded to an RFAI on donor occupation/employer information, describing its practice of requesting the information at solicitation and following up in writing within 30 days for donors who don't provide it. Filing.
