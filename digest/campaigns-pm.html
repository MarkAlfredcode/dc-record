Top Story
Sen. Jim Justice's first annual disclosure as senator shows a $50 million-plus Carter Bank judgment amid a sprawling coal-and-resort empire
The West Virginia Republican's Calendar Year 2025 personal financial disclosure, filed September 8, catalogs more than 140 family business holdings and 13 separate liabilities.

Sen. James Conley Justice II (R-WV) filed his Annual Report for Calendar Year 2025 with the Senate Office of Public Records on September 8, 2026 — his first annual personal financial disclosure since being sworn in as senator in January 2025 after two terms as West Virginia's governor.

The filing runs to more than 140 line items cataloging the network of family-controlled coal, hospitality and real estate companies built by James C. Justice Companies, including dozens of coal-mining and land-holding entities plus hospitality operations such as Southern Coal Corporation, Bluestone Resources, Inc. and The Greenbrier Hotel Corporation (owner of the Greenbrier resort) — several individually valued at "Over $50,000,000." Six separate lines list accounts receivable owed to Justice personally by his own companies, four of them — Justice Receivable, Southern Coal Receivable, Justice LLC Receivable and Bluestone Receivable — each valued at "Over $50,000,000."

Part 7 of the filing lists 13 liabilities. The largest is a judgment of more than $50,000,000 owed to Carter Bank & Trust of Martinsville, Virginia, incurred in 2024 and accruing at 6% interest "on demand." Others include a $25 million-to-$50 million judgment to Lexon Insurance Company of Mount Juliet, Tennessee (incurred 2025, 3.57% interest) and a $5 million-to-$25 million judgment to Western Surety Co. of Sioux Falls, South Dakota (incurred 2024, 6% interest) — both surety firms that commonly hold judgments tied to coal-mine reclamation bonds. Two more lines list $1 million-to-$5 million promissory notes, both dated August 31, 2021, owed to Charleston, WV figures Bray Cary, Jr. and the Cary Foundation, Inc.

On the income side, Justice reported comparatively modest earned income for 2025: $7,500 in partial-year salary from the State of West Virginia covering his final months as governor, and $3,500 as head coach of the Greenbrier County Board of Education's girls high school basketball team. His spouse drew a salary of more than $1,000 from Greenbrier Hotel Corporation. The filing also confirms a prior sale of Peoples Bancorp Inc. stock (ticker PEBO) valued at $250,001-$500,000 on 08/28/2025, previously disclosed on a periodic transaction report.

On the Record
House marks 25th anniversary of Sept. 11 attacks, adjourns "as a further mark of respect"

In the most recent Congressional Record (Thursday, September 10, 2026), the House agreed to H. Res. 1520, sponsored by Speaker Mike Johnson (R-LA-4), expressing the House's sense on the 25th anniversary of the September 11, 2001 terrorist attacks. The House then adjourned at 3:33 p.m. "as a further mark of respect to the memory of the victims." The Senate met only in a pro forma session, gaveling in and out in 30 seconds without conducting business, and will next convene September 14.

Several other bills and resolutions introduced that day referenced the anniversary: H.R. 10338, from Rep. Thomas H. Kean (R-NJ-7), would award a Congressional Gold Medal to the Naval Special Warfare Development Group ("SEAL Team Six") for its role in the operation that killed Osama bin Laden; H.R. 10340, also from Kean, would direct the Library of Congress to start an oral-history program on the 9/11 attacks; H. Res. 1525, from Rep. Doris O. Matsui (D-CA-7), designates September 11, 2026 as a "National Day of Service and Remembrance"; and H. Res. 1526, from Rep. John R. Moolenaar (R-MI-2), backs designating September 11-17 as "Patriot Week."

In all, the House introduced 25 public bills (H.R. 10326-10350) and 10 resolutions (H. Res. 1520-1529) that day; 23 of the bills and seven of the resolutions were unrelated to the anniversary. The full list, with sponsors, follows.
