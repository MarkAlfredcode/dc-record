Top Story
FEC filing details “years-long embezzlement” by seafarers PAC's former treasurer
A Form 99 letter filed September 23 says former Treasurer Chelsea Diab diverted committee funds into her own account and falsified reports before a bank recovered a portion of the money.

The Seafarers Political Activity Donation - Seafarers International Union of NA-AGLIW (C00004325) filed a Form 99 letter with the FEC on September 23 responding to four pending RFAI letters tied to three amended monthly reports from 2025 and its July 2026 Monthly Report. In it, the committee tells the Commission that the three amended reports “each discloses one or more misappropriations of funds” by its former treasurer, Chelsea Diab, describing what it calls “a years-long embezzlement of Committee funds and falsifications of Committee reports to the Commission” that Diab concealed from other personnel at the union that employed her.

The committee says Diab diverted committee disbursements into her own personal bank account rather than to the payees actually listed on its reports — including entries recorded as if paid to “Fairwater Tanker” on four separate dates between April and July 2025, and one payment on May 8, 2025 recorded as if paid to Fetterman for PA, the principal campaign committee of Sen. John Fetterman of Pennsylvania. The committee says those entries were false: the money went to Diab, not to the named payees. It has since amended every report it filed from February 2025 through May 2026 to reflect the misappropriations discovered so far, and says internal and external investigations are continuing.

Separately, the committee's July 2026 Monthly Report discloses a partial recovery. Capital One, the bank that handled payroll-deduction remittances from one employer that regularly contributes to the committee by check, determined that checks had cleared with endorsements that were either fraudulent or missing entirely, and traced them to Diab's personal account at Wells Fargo. Capital One recovered a portion of the diverted funds from Wells Fargo before Diab had withdrawn the rest, and passed that recovery on to the committee — in effect, restitution obtained without Diab's participation.

Sitting Lawmakers
Two incumbents' committees draw RFAIs over unrefunded general-election money

Two sitting members of Congress had campaign committees flagged by the FEC's Reports Analysis Division this window, both over the same recurring issue: contributions collected for a general election that the committee wasn't set up to use and needed to return.

  • Rep. Diana DeGette (D-CO-1) — Her campaign committee, Diana DeGette for Congress, drew an RFAI on its July Quarterly Report (06/11/2026-06/30/2026) covering two items: a batch of contributions collected for the 2026 general election — spanning reports back to April 2025 — appear never to have been refunded or redesignated; the FEC's letter says DeGette “will not participate in the general election” and that such funds cannot be used to cover primary-election debts; and Schedule A suggests the committee missed one or more required 48-hour notices for three contributions — $3,000, $3,500 and $2,500 — received June 12-14, 2026, after the books closed on its 12-Day Pre-Primary Report. Filing.
  • Sen. Cynthia Lummis (R-WY) — Her campaign committee, Lummis for Wyoming Inc., drew two separate RFAIs. One, on its Year-End 2025 report, tells the committee that because "the candidate is not seeking office and will not participate in the general election," more than 220 general-election contributions dating back to 2020 — totaling over $580,000, per the attached schedule — must be refunded to donors, since the Act bars using that money to pay down primary debt. Filing. The second, on its July Quarterly report, flags 13 refunds itemized on Schedule B — including $5,000 to Buddy PAC and $5,000 to a group calling itself "Making A Responsible Stand For Households In America" — that don't correspond to any original contribution the committee ever reported receiving. Filing.
