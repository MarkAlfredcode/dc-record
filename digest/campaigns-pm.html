Top Story
FEC Flags End Citizens United Over $176,978 Contribution From an Unregistered Affiliate
A routine compliance letter raises a six-figure question about the source of funds behind one of the best-known Democratic-aligned PACs.

The Federal Election Commission has sent End Citizens United, the national Democratic-aligned PAC, a Request for Additional Information over a $176,978.93 contribution reported on its 2026 March Monthly report as coming from "End Citizens United Non-Federal" on February 23, 2026. The problem, per the FEC's letter dated September 13, 2026: that entity is not itself a political committee registered with the commission, and federal law generally bars registered committees from accepting money from unregistered organizations unless the organization can show the funds were kept in a separate account containing only permissible dollars, or can otherwise demonstrate through a reasonable accounting method that it had enough properly screened funds on hand to cover the contribution.

If End Citizens United can't make that showing, the FEC's letter says the contribution is presumed impermissible, and the committee would have to either refund it or move it into a non-federal account — and disclose whichever it does on a future Schedule B. The commission's letter, signed by senior campaign finance analyst Michael Adamsky, warns that failure to adequately respond by the October 19, 2026 deadline could trigger an audit or enforcement action.

It's a compliance question, not an allegation of wrongdoing yet — RFAIs of this type are common when a report shows a contribution from an entity the FEC's database doesn't recognize as a registered committee, and committees frequently resolve them with a same-name registered affiliate's paperwork or a refund. But the dollar figure here is large enough, and the PAC prominent enough, that how End Citizens United answers is worth tracking.

Sitting Lawmakers
Rep. Jeff Hurd's Campaign Corrects Contributor Mix-Ups in Three 48-Hour Reports

One FEC filing this window came from a sitting member of Congress's own campaign committee — a housekeeping correction, not a compliance fight.

  • Rep. Jeff Hurd (R-CO-3), through Jeff Hurd for Congress, filed a miscellaneous submission responding to an August 13, 2026 Request for Additional Information by amending three 48-hour contribution reports. A contribution originally attributed to Jim Pedersen was actually made by his spouse, Jennifer Pedersen; a contribution credited to Michael Ross was actually a joint contribution from Michael and his wife, Susan; and a contribution from Susan Whittlesey was corrected from a reported $2,000 to the actual $2,500. The committee separately amended its Year End 2025 report after its compliance software mistakenly pulled memo-only contributions from the Truck Renting and Leasing Association PAC ("Tralapac") — tied to Transportation Trust Fund joint-fundraising transfers — into a 48-hour report filed June 26, 2026, because the transfers' receipt date had been misdated as June 24, 2026 instead of the actual June 24, 2025.
