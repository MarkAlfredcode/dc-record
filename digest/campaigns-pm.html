Top Story
FEC Presses Steve Daines's Senate Committee Over Money Collected After He Said He Wouldn't Run Again
A Request for Additional Information sent September 16 flags primary- and general-election contributions the campaign kept after the senator's announcement he would not seek reelection, plus a refund made outside the required 60-day window.

The Federal Election Commission sent a Request for Additional Information (RFAI) dated September 16, 2026 to Steve Daines for Montana, the campaign committee of Sen. Steve Daines, a Republican, over its April Quarterly Report covering January through March 2026. The letter states that the candidate "was running for Congressional office until their announcement on 3/4/2026 not to seek office/re-election," ending his status as a 2026 cycle candidate. That statement is corroborated by the FEC's own candidate database, which now flags Sen. Daines's Senate candidacy record as inactive for the cycle, even though his committee remains active and continued raising and banking money afterward.

The Commission's letter identifies three problems. First, a $3,500 contribution from donor Michael Gearon was not refunded, redesignated, or reattributed within the required 60-day window after an earlier excessive contribution from the same donor; the committee's own records show the money wasn't returned until June 23, 2026, months after receipt. Second, the committee kept primary-election contributions received after the March 4 withdrawal announcement, money the FEC says must be refunded except to the extent the campaign had outstanding primary debt. Third, the letter flags a large, multi-year backlog of general-election contributions — from individual donors and from corporate and trade-association PACs including Marsh & Mclennan Companies Inc PAC, Doordash Inc. Political Action Committee (DASHPAC), and Citizens Financial Group Inc PAC, some dating back to 2020 — that were never returned because Sen. Daines is not on the 2026 general-election ballot. The committee's July Quarterly filing shows it has already begun issuing some refunds, including $5,000 back to Marsh & Mclennan Companies Inc PAC and $2,500 back to Citizens Financial Group Inc PAC on May 2, 2026.

The committee has until October 21, 2026 to respond. The FEC's standard warning applies: failure to adequately address the items could result in an audit or enforcement action, and any response becomes part of the public record.

A Treasure Chest in the Carina Nebula. A James Webb Space Telescope image shows a dust pillar in the Carina Nebula, about 7,500 light-years away, being carved open by radiation from nearby massive stars. Astronomers estimate a compact cluster of roughly 70 young stars, only about 1.3 million years old, sits inside the pillar.
