Top Story

Discharge petition on cancellation of removal sits one signature short of 218

Both chambers met only briefly on Tuesday, so the closest-to-threshold discharge petition is the day's most consequential item.

The House met for just three minutes on Tuesday (1:30 p.m. to 1:33 p.m.) with no recorded votes, and the Senate held a 24-second pro forma session. Neither chamber took up substantive floor business.

The most consequential item in the day's data is on the discharge petition front: Discharge Petition No. 25, sponsored by Rep. Sylvia R. Garcia, stands at 217 of 218 signatures, one short of forcing a House floor vote. It would bring up H.R. 1589-style consideration, per the petition listing, of the bill (H.R. 1589) to authorize the cancellation of removal and adjustment of status of certain aliens. No petition gained signatures or reached 218 since October 11, 2026.

Discharge Petition No. 27, sponsored by Rep. Thomas Massie on H.R. 185, is at 216 of 218, two signatures away.

On the Record

Pro forma sessions in both chambers; 16 measures introduced and one committee report filed

The Senate met at 3:30:02 p.m. in pro forma session and adjourned at 3:30:26 p.m. until 9:30 a.m. on Thursday, October 15, 2026. No Senate committee meetings were held. The House met at 1:30 p.m. and adjourned at 1:33 p.m., with Rep. James Baird serving as Speaker pro tempore; there were no Yea and Nay votes, no recorded votes and no quorum calls, and no House hearings were held. The House meets again in pro forma session at 11 a.m. Thursday.

Fifteen House bills (H.R. 10795-10809) and one resolution (H. Res. 1624) were introduced, and the Committee on Small Business reported one bill. Most are routine; the broadest is a bill to abolish the Department of Homeland Security. Every measure follows.

  • H.R. 10238, sponsored by Rep. Tony Wied (R-WI-8) — Cybersecurity for Small Businesses Act of 2026, reported by committee (the Committee on Small Business, with an amendment, H. Rept. 119-851). It would require the Administrator of the Small Business Administration to disseminate cybersecurity information and resources to small businesses. Referred to the Committee of the Whole House on the state of the Union.
  • H.R. 10795, sponsored by Rep. James Baird (R-IN-4) — Sudan IMPACT Act; would authorize a United States-Sudan Agriculture and Minerals partnership. Referred to the Committee on Foreign Affairs.
  • H.R. 10796, sponsored by Rep. Don Bacon (R-NE-2) — United States-Israel Defense Technology Cooperation Act; would direct the Secretary of Defense to designate an executive agent to synchronize U.S.-Israel cooperative efforts. Referred to the Committee on Armed Services and, additionally, the Committee on Foreign Affairs.
  • H.R. 10797, sponsored by Bacon — Aerial Transport and High Consequence Infectious Diseases Act; would direct the Secretary of the Air Force to run a program for safe, long-range aerial transport of people infected or suspected of being infected with high-consequence infectious diseases. Referred to the Committee on Armed Services.
  • H.R. 10798, sponsored by Rep. J. Correa (D-CA-46), with Rep. Dexter — would establish a grant program for silicosis treatment and education. Referred to the Committee on Energy and Commerce.
  • H.R. 10799, sponsored by Rep. Glenn Grothman (R-WI-6) — would amend the Immigration and Nationality Act to modernize the wage required for exempt H-1B workers. Referred to the Committee on the Judiciary.
  • H.R. 10800, sponsored by Rep. George Latimer (D-NY-16) — would amend the Immigration and Nationality Act to set up a process for designating a safe third country and for review of those determinations. Referred to the Committee on the Judiciary.
  • H.R. 10801, sponsored by Rep. Nancy Mace (R-SC-1) — would direct the Secretary of the Treasury to run a program in which federal elementary and secondary education funds either go directly to parents for certain educational expenses or are approved by parents for distribution to their child's public school. Referred to the Committee on Education and Workforce and, additionally, the Committee on Ways and Means.
  • H.R. 10802, sponsored by Rep. Johnny Olszewski (D-MD-2), with Rep. McBride — would direct heads of federal agencies to support increased access to voting. Referred to the Committee on House Administration, with additional referrals to the Committees on Oversight and Government Reform; Science, Space, and Technology; Armed Services; and the Judiciary.
  • H.R. 10803, sponsored by Rep. Delia Ramirez (D-IL-3), with Reps. Rashida Tlaib, Grijalva and McGovern — would abolish the Department of Homeland Security and establish a Department of Community Safety. Referred to the Committee on Homeland Security, with a dozen additional committees for provisions within their jurisdictions.
  • H.R. 10804, sponsored by Rep. Josh Riley (D-NY-19) — Utility Accountability Act; would establish penalties for investor-owned electric utilities that fail to meet state performance targets. Referred to the Committee on Energy and Commerce.
  • H.R. 10805, sponsored by Rep. Bradley Schneider (D-IL-10) — Don’t Block LGBTQ Act of 2026; would amend the Communications Act of 1934 to prohibit schools and public libraries receiving universal service support from blocking internet access to LGBTQ resources. Referred to the Committee on Energy and Commerce.
  • H.R. 10806, sponsored by Rep. Claudia Tenney (R-NY-24) — would amend title XVIII of the Social Security Act to decrease fraud related to home health agencies in Medicare. Referred to the Committee on Ways and Means, with additional referrals to the Committees on Energy and Commerce and the Judiciary.
  • H.R. 10807, sponsored by Rep. Rashida Tlaib (D-MI-12) — Get the Flock Out of Our Communities Act; would prohibit the use of automated mass surveillance systems and associated components. Cosponsors include Reps. Garcia of Texas, Lee of Pennsylvania, Omar, Ramirez, Velazquez and Watson Coleman. Referred to the Committee on Oversight and Government Reform and, additionally, the Committee on the Judiciary.
  • H.R. 10808, sponsored by Rep. Matt Van Epps (R-TN-7) — STORK Act; would amend the Internal Revenue Code of 1986 to allow additional health savings account contributions upon the birth or adoption of a child. Referred to the Committee on Ways and Means.
  • H.R. 10809, sponsored by Rep. Eugene Vindman (D-VA-7) — Make Data Centers Pay Act; would amend the Federal Power Act to protect ratepayers from costs relating to the electric loads of data centers and high-density computing facilities. Referred to the Committee on Energy and Commerce.
  • H. Res. 1624, sponsored by Rep. Marcy Kaptur (D-OH-9), with Rep. Latta — recognizes the 110th anniversary of the Nation's first Presidential library, the Rutherford B. Hayes Presidential Library & Museums. Referred to the Committee on Oversight and Government Reform.
