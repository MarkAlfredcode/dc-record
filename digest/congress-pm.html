Top Story

Raskin bill would put the Trump presidencies on permanent display at the Smithsonian

The House held a two-minute session on October 6, but 22 bills and resolutions were still introduced.

H.R. 10765, introduced by Rep. Jamie Raskin (D-MD-8), would direct the Smithsonian Institution to collect, preserve and display, in a permanent exhibition at the National Museum of American History, artifacts reflecting “the rise of governmental propaganda, corruption, and authoritarianism” during Donald J. Trump's two presidencies (2017–2021 and 2025–2029, as the bill text defines them).

It was referred to the Committee on House Administration and, for a period to be set by the Speaker, the Committee on Natural Resources. No Republican cosponsors appear in the introduction listing, which shows Raskin as the sole sponsor.

No votes were held in either chamber that day, so the bill is the most notable text introduced.

On the Record

Pro forma sessions, no votes; 22 measures introduced in the House

The most recent Congressional Record available is dated Tuesday, October 6, 2026. The Senate met in a pro forma session at 1:31 p.m. and adjourned 33 seconds later until 1:30 p.m. on Friday, October 9. The House met at 4 p.m. and adjourned at 4:02 p.m.; there were no recorded votes, no Yea and Nay votes and no quorum calls.

Even so, the House logged 17 bills (H.R. 10751–10767), one concurrent resolution and four simple resolutions. Besides H.R. 10765, the most newsworthy include H.R. 10759, a tariff-funded relief program for manufacturers and farmers, and H.Res. 1618, marking the third anniversary of the October 7 attacks. Every measure follows.

  • H.R. 10751, sponsored by Rep. Jack Bergman (R-MI-1) — would remove restrictions on dental therapist services under the Community Health Aide Program in the Indian Health Care Improvement Act; referred to the Committee on Natural Resources and the Committee on Energy and Commerce.
  • H.R. 10752, sponsored by Rep. Troy A. Carter (D-LA-2) — would create a task force on artificial intelligence inside the Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency; referred to the Committee on Homeland Security.
  • H.R. 10753, sponsored by Rep. Elijah Crane (R-AZ-2) — the Tribal Veterans Voucher Flexibility Act, which would let tribes voluntarily reallocate vouchers under the Tribal HUD-VASH housing program; referred to the Committee on Financial Services.
  • H.R. 10754, sponsored by Rep. Elijah Crane (R-AZ-2) — the Rural Disaster Fairness Act of 2026, which would update indicators of a disaster's local impact under the FAA Reauthorization Act of 2018; referred to the Committee on Transportation and Infrastructure.
  • H.R. 10755, sponsored by Rep. Jasmine Crockett (D-TX-30) — would name the Cedar Hill, Texas, post office at 475 East FM 1382 the “Eugene Gates Jr. Post Office Building”; referred to the Committee on Oversight and Government Reform.
  • H.R. 10756, sponsored by Rep. Mike Ezell (R-MS-4) — would reauthorize Regional Ocean Partnerships; referred to the Committee on Natural Resources.
  • H.R. 10757, sponsored by Rep. Josh Gottheimer (D-NJ-5) — would limit United States contributions to the United Nations; referred to the Committee on Foreign Affairs.
  • H.R. 10758, sponsored by Rep. Jeff Hurd (R-CO-3) — would have the Secretary of Veterans Affairs award grants to state entities to expand structured outdoor recreation programs for veterans; referred to the Committee on Veterans' Affairs.
  • H.R. 10759, sponsored by Rep. John James (R-MI-10) — would set up a tariff-funded program to protect American manufacturers, workers, farmers and supply chains from economic harm caused by tariffs and foreign retaliation; referred to the Committee on Financial Services and the Committee on Ways and Means.
  • H.R. 10760, sponsored by Rep. Thomas H. Kean (R-NJ-7) — the Healthy Minds for a Better Future Act, which would create a multiagency Middle School Mental Health Task Force at the Department of Education and the Department of Health and Human Services; referred to the Committee on Education and Workforce and the Committee on Energy and Commerce.
  • H.R. 10761, sponsored by Rep. Ro Khanna (D-CA-17) — the Campus Accountability and Safety Act of 2026, which would amend the Jeanne Clery Campus Safety Act to combat campus sexual assault; referred to the Committee on Education and Workforce and the Committee on the Judiciary.
  • H.R. 10762, sponsored by Rep. Michael Lawler (R-NY-17) — the Anthony Amoros Law Right to Know Act, which would require used-vehicle sellers to keep a vehicle's airbag system functional or clearly disclose when one is missing or disabled; referred to the Committee on Energy and Commerce and the Committee on the Judiciary.
  • H.R. 10763, sponsored by Rep. Julia Letlow (R-LA-5) — the Know Your Risk Act, which would direct the Secretary of Health and Human Services to report to Congress on breast cancer risk assessment tools; referred to the Committee on Energy and Commerce.
  • H.R. 10764, sponsored by Rep. Gwen Moore (D-WI-4) — the Executive Settlement Review Act, which would require certain procedures for compromise settlements or awards involving executive branch officials; referred to the Committee on the Judiciary.
  • H.R. 10765, sponsored by Rep. Jamie Raskin (D-MD-8) — would direct the Smithsonian Institution to display a permanent exhibition at the National Museum of American History on the rise of governmental propaganda, corruption and authoritarianism during Donald J. Trump's two presidencies; referred to the Committee on House Administration and the Committee on Natural Resources.
  • H.R. 10766, sponsored by Rep. Keith Self (R-TX-3) — would direct the Secretary of Defense to add assets critical to the Golden Dome missile defense system to the Department of Defense Critical Asset List; referred to the Committee on Armed Services.
  • H.R. 10767, sponsored by Rep. Adrian Smith (R-NE-3) — the Fair Play Enforcement Act, which would impose an excise tax on colleges and universities that allow specified individuals to take part in intercollegiate athletics; referred to the Committee on Ways and Means.
  • H. Con. Res. 123, sponsored by Rep. Teresa Leger Fernandez (D-NM-3) — would recognize the wage disparity faced by Latinas and its effect on women, families and the economy; referred to the Committee on Education and Workforce.
  • H.Res. 1617, sponsored by Rep. Earl L. “Buddy” Carter (R-GA-1) — would recognize October as “American Pharmacy Month”; referred to the Committee on Energy and Commerce.
  • H.Res. 1618, sponsored by Rep. Josh Gottheimer (D-NJ-5) — would mark the third anniversary of the October 7, 2023, attacks on Israel, honor the victims and hostages, and call for the complete disarmament of Hamas; referred to the Committee on Foreign Affairs.
  • H.Res. 1619, sponsored by Rep. Glenn Thompson (R-PA-15) — would reaffirm German-American friendship and support continued cooperation between the United States and Germany; referred to the Committee on Foreign Affairs.
  • H.Res. 1620, sponsored by Rep. Nikema Williams (D-GA-5) — would support the goals of “National Nonviolence Week” to raise awareness of youth violence; referred to the Committee on Oversight and Government Reform.
