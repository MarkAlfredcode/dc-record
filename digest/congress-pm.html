Top Story

Representative Jamie Raskin's bill would put a Trump-era "authoritarianism" exhibit in the Smithsonian

The House held a two-minute session with no votes, so the most notable item of the day was a bill introduced in it.

On Tuesday, October 6, the House met for two minutes and the Senate for 33 seconds, both in pro forma sessions with no recorded votes. The day's only substantive record is the 22 measures introduced in the House. Among them, H.R. 10765, from Rep. Jamie Raskin (D-MD-8), would direct the Secretary of the Smithsonian Institution to collect, preserve and display, in a permanent exhibition at the National Museum of American History, artifacts reflecting "the rise of governmental propaganda, corruption, and authoritarianism" during the two presidencies of Donald J. Trump.

The bill's text defines the two periods as January 20, 2017 through January 20, 2021, and January 20, 2025 through January 20, 2029, so it covers a term that has not yet ended. It was referred to the Committee on House Administration and, additionally, the Committee on Natural Resources. The Congress.gov record lists Raskin as the sole sponsor.

On the Record

Pro forma sessions in both chambers; 22 House measures introduced

The Senate met at 1:31 p.m. in pro forma session, adjourned 33 seconds later, and is next scheduled to meet at 1:30 p.m. on Friday, October 9, 2026. The House met at 4 p.m. and adjourned at 4:02 p.m.; the Daily Digest records no Yea and Nay votes, no recorded votes and no quorum calls. Two committee reports were also filed: on H.R. 6698 (Board of Veterans' Appeals annual report on untimely appeals) and H.R. 7260 (annual report on the National Cemetery Administration).

Seventeen House bills (H.R. 10751–10767), one concurrent resolution and four simple resolutions were introduced. Besides the Raskin exhibit bill, the more newsworthy items are Rep. Josh Gottheimer's resolution marking the third anniversary of the October 7 attacks, and Rep. John James's tariff-funded relief program for manufacturers, workers and farmers. Most of the rest are routine. All 22 follow.

  • H.R. 10751, sponsored by Rep. Jack Bergman (R-MI-1) — would remove restrictions on dental therapist services under the Community Health Aide Program of the Indian Health Care Improvement Act; referred to the Committee on Natural Resources and the Committee on Energy and Commerce.
  • H.R. 10752, sponsored by Rep. Troy A. Carter (D-LA-2) — would create a task force on artificial intelligence within the Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency; referred to the Committee on Homeland Security.
  • H.R. 10753, sponsored by Rep. Elijah Crane (R-AZ-2) — the Tribal Veterans Voucher Flexibility Act, which would let the Department of Housing and Urban Development voluntarily reallocate vouchers under the Tribal HUD-VASH program; referred to the Committee on Financial Services.
  • H.R. 10754, sponsored by Rep. Elijah Crane (R-AZ-2) — the Rural Disaster Fairness Act of 2026, which would update indicators of the local impact of a disaster under the FAA Reauthorization Act of 2018; referred to the Committee on Transportation and Infrastructure.
  • H.R. 10755, sponsored by Rep. Jasmine Crockett (D-TX-30) — would name the Cedar Hill, Texas, post office at 475 East FM 1382 the "Eugene Gates Jr. Post Office Building"; referred to the Committee on Oversight and Government Reform.
  • H.R. 10756, sponsored by Rep. Mike Ezell (R-MS-4) — would reauthorize Regional Ocean Partnerships; referred to the Committee on Natural Resources.
  • H.R. 10757, sponsored by Rep. Josh Gottheimer (D-NJ-5) — would place a limit on United States contributions to the United Nations; referred to the Committee on Foreign Affairs.
  • H.R. 10758, sponsored by Rep. Jeff Hurd (R-CO-3) — would have the Secretary of Veterans Affairs award grants to state entities to expand structured outdoor recreation programs for veterans; referred to the Committee on Veterans' Affairs.
  • H.R. 10759, sponsored by Rep. John James (R-MI-10) — would establish a tariff-funded program to protect United States manufacturers, workers, farmers and supply chains from harm caused by tariffs and foreign retaliation, and to encourage domestic investment; referred to the Committee on Financial Services and the Committee on Ways and Means.
  • H.R. 10760, sponsored by Rep. Thomas H. Kean Jr. (R-NJ-7) — would create a Middle School Mental Health Task Force across the Department of Education and the Department of Health and Human Services; referred to the Committee on Education and Workforce and the Committee on Energy and Commerce.
  • H.R. 10761, sponsored by Rep. Ro Khanna (D-CA-17) — the Campus Accountability and Safety Act of 2026, which would amend the Jeanne Clery Campus Safety Act to combat campus sexual assault; referred to the Committee on Education and Workforce and the Committee on the Judiciary.
  • H.R. 10762, sponsored by Rep. Michael Lawler (R-NY-17) — the Anthony Amoros Law Right to Know Act, which would require sellers of used motor vehicles to maintain a functional airbag system or clearly disclose when one is missing or disabled; referred to the Committee on Energy and Commerce and the Committee on the Judiciary.
  • H.R. 10763, sponsored by Rep. Julia Letlow (R-LA-5) — the Know Your Risk Act, which would direct the Secretary of Health and Human Services to report to Congress on breast cancer risk assessment tools; referred to the Committee on Energy and Commerce.
  • H.R. 10764, sponsored by Rep. Gwen Moore (D-WI-4) — the Executive Settlement Review Act, which would require certain procedures for compromise settlements or awards for executive branch officials; referred to the Committee on the Judiciary.
  • H.R. 10765, sponsored by Rep. Jamie Raskin (D-MD-8) — would direct the Smithsonian Institution to collect, preserve and display in a permanent National Museum of American History exhibition artifacts reflecting the rise of governmental propaganda, corruption and authoritarianism during the two presidencies of Donald J. Trump; referred to the Committee on House Administration and the Committee on Natural Resources.
  • H.R. 10766, sponsored by Rep. Keith Self (R-TX-3) — would direct the Secretary of Defense to add assets critical to the Golden Dome missile defense system to the Department of Defense Critical Asset List; referred to the Committee on Armed Services.
  • H.R. 10767, sponsored by Rep. Adrian Smith (R-NE-3) — the Fair Play Enforcement Act, which would impose an excise tax on colleges and universities that allow specified individuals to participate in intercollegiate athletics; referred to the Committee on Ways and Means.
  • H.Con.Res. 123, sponsored by Rep. Teresa Leger Fernandez (D-NM-3) — a concurrent resolution recognizing the disparity in wages paid to Latinas and its impact on women, families and the economy, with 22 listed cosponsors; referred to the Committee on Education and Workforce.
  • H.Res. 1617, sponsored by Rep. Earl L. "Buddy" Carter (R-GA-1) — would recognize October as "American Pharmacy Month" in honor of pharmacists and pharmacy teams; referred to the Committee on Energy and Commerce.
  • H.Res. 1618, sponsored by Rep. Josh Gottheimer (D-NJ-5) — would mark the third anniversary of the October 7, 2023, terrorist attacks on Israel, honor the victims and hostages, and call for the complete disarmament of Hamas; it lists dozens of bipartisan cosponsors and was referred to the Committee on Foreign Affairs.
  • H.Res. 1619, sponsored by Rep. Glenn Thompson (R-PA-15) — would reaffirm German-American friendship and support continued cooperation between the United States and Germany; referred to the Committee on Foreign Affairs.
  • H.Res. 1620, sponsored by Rep. Nikema Williams (D-GA-5) — would support the goals and ideals of "National Nonviolence Week" to raise awareness of youth violence; referred to the Committee on Oversight and Government Reform.
