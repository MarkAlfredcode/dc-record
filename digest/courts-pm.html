Top Story

Texas Border-Area Landowners and a Conservation Group Sue Homeland Security Over a Sealed Agency Action

The complaint itself is under seal, but the docket shows a sitting U.S. senator named alongside the Department of Homeland Security and Customs and Border Protection.

A group called Conserve Big Bend, along with individual plaintiffs Joe Pineda, Leo Nunez Pineda, Nadine Pineda Mata, John Ferguson, David Keller and Charles L. Cecil, IV, sued the U.S. Department of Homeland Security, U.S. Customs and Border Protection, Customs and Border Protection Commissioner Rodney S. Scott, and U.S. Senator Markwayne Mullin in the U.S. District Court for the District of Columbia on September 14, 2026 (docket 1:26-cv-03198).

The case is docketed under the Administrative Procedure Act as a judicial-review action challenging an unspecified agency decision. The complaint itself was filed under seal, along with the plaintiffs' addresses, pursuant to the court's local rule on sealed filings — meaning the specific agency action being challenged, and the relief the plaintiffs are seeking, is not publicly visible on the docket.

Naming a sitting member of Congress as a defendant in an Administrative Procedure Act suit against an executive agency is unusual; the docket does not explain what role Senator Mullin is alleged to have played, and the underlying complaint that would explain it is not available.

Because no complaint document is publicly available through RECAP (none of the docket's filed documents, including the sealed complaint itself, are flagged as available for download), this item is being reported from the docket caption and case-management entries only, not from having read the underlying pleading.
