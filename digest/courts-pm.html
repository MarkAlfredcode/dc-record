Top Story

Immigrant Sues Homeland Security Secretary Mullin and Three Other Top Officials Over Agency Decision

The suit names the same Department of Homeland Security and Immigration and Customs Enforcement leadership already facing a separate polling-place lawsuit this week.

On September 23, 2026, Ismael Sanchez Valadez sued Secretary of Homeland Security Markwayne Mullin, Acting Director of U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement David Venturella, Deputy Attorney General Todd Blanche, and Commissioner of U.S. Customs and Border Protection Rodney Scott — all sued in their official capacities — in the U.S. District Court for the District of Columbia (Valadez v. Mullin, No. 1:26-cv-03328).

The docket lists the case as brought under the Immigration and Nationality Act and the Administrative Procedure Act, seeking review of an agency decision. The complaint itself is not freely available on PACER/RECAP, so the specific decision being challenged, the relief sought, and the underlying facts cannot be confirmed beyond the docket caption.

The case is notable chiefly for whom it names: the same four-official chain of command — Secretary Mullin, Acting Director Venturella and the Department of Homeland Security — already facing a separate suit filed the same week by Democratic congressional and party committees over a reported plan to station armed federal agents at polling places, suggesting this week's immigration-enforcement leadership is drawing scrutiny in U.S. District Court for the District of Columbia on multiple fronts at once.

New Litigation

Federal Employees and a Student-Loan Borrower File Suits Against Interior and Education Officials

Three more cabinet-related suits cleared today's screen, none with a publicly available complaint yet, so each is described here from its docket caption only.

A group of law-enforcement-adjacent plaintiffs also filed a tort claim against the federal government, and two more individuals filed routine visa-delay suits against the Secretary of State.

  • Tanya McHenry and 25 other individual plaintiffs sued William K. Marshall and the United States of America under the Fair Labor Standards Act on September 24, 2026 (McHenry v. Marshall, No. 1:26-cv-03334). A separate document in the case was filed under seal the same day; its contents are not public. Complaint text not available, docket caption only.
  • Craig Michael Bingert and 12 other individual plaintiffs sued the United States under the Federal Tort Claims Act on September 24, 2026 (Bingert v. United States, No. 1:26-cv-03340). A notice filed in the case states it is related to an earlier case, No. 1:25-cv-03029-EGS. Complaint text not available, docket caption only.
  • Mandy Woods and Jorge Cortes sued the U.S. Department of Education under the Fair Credit Reporting Act on September 24, 2026 (Woods v. United States Department of Education, No. 1:26-cv-03335), a consumer-credit claim rather than the education-policy disputes more typically filed against the department. Complaint text not available, docket caption only.

Also filed: 2 new suits over delayed visa or immigration-petition adjudications, naming Secretary of State Marco Rubio and other State Department officials (Shareef v. Rubio, No. 1:26-cv-03341; Ajmal v. Goodman, No. 1:26-cv-03333).
