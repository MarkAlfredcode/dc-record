Top Story
Ballard Partners registers two Venezuela-focused oil and gas clients
Both new lobbying registrations describe advocacy on Venezuelan energy opportunities.

Ballard Partners filed new Lobbying Disclosure Act registrations for Ocean Energy Services, Inc. ("advocacy regarding Venezuelan oil and gas business opportunities") and Calistoga Energy Reserves, LLC ("advocacy regarding energy matters in Venezuela and the United States"), both under the Fuel/Gas/Oil issue code.

The same firm also registered King Ocean Services, Ltd, a cargo shipper, for logistics planning and negotiations; the filing lists the Taxation/Internal Revenue Code issue code. The filings do not name specific officials or bills in the descriptions reviewed.

Personal Financial Disclosures & Stock Trades
Senate annual and candidate reports are the only new disclosures; no new stock-trade filings

The Clerk of the House index shows no House filings since October 3, 2026 that weren't already reported in earlier editions. The Senate Office of Public Records eFD search returned two new filings, both non-trade reports, and neither is a Periodic Transaction Report, so there are no trade tables today. Both detail pages were read.

  • Sen. Tim Scott (R-SC) filed an amended Annual Report for calendar 2025 on October 6, 2026. It lists $94,563 in royalties from HarperCollins Publishing LLC, a $4,685 payment from the South Carolina Retirement System, and wages for the spouse from MEB LLC. Assets include a 50% interest in Rivertown Investments, LLC (three residential rentals valued $100,001–$250,000 each), individual stocks and funds in a Charles Schwab account, and a spouse-held Amazon.com Inc position valued $250,001–$500,000. Liabilities include several mortgages, the largest a spouse mortgage of $500,001–$1,000,000.
  • Troy D. Jackson, a candidate in Maine (candidacy commenced July 9, 2026), filed a Candidate Report on October 5, 2026. Income includes $90,000 from the International Association of Machinists and $48,000 from the International Union of Painters and Allied Trades (both consulting), plus smaller sums from Food and Medicine and the Eastern Maine Labor Council. Assets are modest: a Maine public pension ($15,001–$50,000), a life insurance policy, a Key Bank checking account, and spouse-held pension and property interests. No liabilities are reported, and a comment says the filer reported a longtime partner's income and assets under "spouse" though they are not legally married.
