Top Story

NASA buys a mission-modified Ford F-59 from Enterprise Rent-A-Car for about $70,000 without competition, citing the Artemis moon schedule

A company best known for car rentals supplies a specialty truck for the Artemis launch closeout crew, and the agency says there was no time to compete it.

NASA's John F. Kennedy Space Center plans to acquire an existing, mission-modified Ford F-59 from Enterprise Rent-A-Car for an estimated $70,000, with no competition. The justification invokes unusual and compelling urgency. It says the vehicle supports the Exploration Ground Systems Closeout Crew, the team that works with the Artemis launch processing.

According to the justification, the vehicle is already configured and operationally integrated into Artemis launch processing. It says losing it would create operational risk, disrupt mission readiness and potentially affect Artemis launch schedules, and that the timeline does not allow another vehicle to be acquired and modified. It ties the purchase to Executive Order 14369, "Ensuring American Space Superiority," which it describes as directing federal efforts to return American astronauts to the lunar surface no later than 2028.

USAspending shows a federal contract on record under Enterprise Rent-A-Car going back to October 2004, so this is not the company's first government work. The notice carries solicitation number 80KSC026P0014, while the justification text itself refers to 80KSC026P0011.
