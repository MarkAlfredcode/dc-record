TSA cuts short a five-year sole-source award after a protest and will re-compete it

The Transportation Security Administration says it is taking corrective action on a no-bid help-desk contract it gave to its incumbent in August.

According to a justification posted this week, the Transportation Security Administration awarded task order 70T01026F7670N008 to the incumbent, Quasars, on August 20, 2026 as a five-year sole-source contract to run the Online Learning Center help desk, which supports about 70,000 users. The document says that "following an agency-level protest, TSA has taken corrective action to conduct a competitive procurement."

To keep services running in the meantime, a modification shortens the period of performance to no later than May 19, 2027. The justification does not state a dollar value. The earlier incumbent task order had been competitively awarded as a small-business set-aside through the National Institutes of Health's CIO-SP3 contract vehicle.

Education Department plans $81.4 million more for contract attorneys deciding student-loan borrower defense claims
Vendor redacted  ·  $81,382,398 estimated  ·  logical follow-on (federal supply schedule sole source)  ·  Department of Education, Federal Student Aid
Two task order modifications (91003126F0014 and F0015) would extend attorney adjudication work on borrower defense applications. The department says changing contractors would waste the onboarding and training of the 144 contract attorneys already in place and set back its goal of clearing the backlog by 2028, and that the original scope "has proven to be larger than initially anticipated." The same justification was posted as two notices with the same estimate, so the figure is one total, not two.

ICE adds a temporary 100-bed detention structure in Batavia, New York to an incumbent's contract without competition
Akima Infrastructure Protection, LLC  ·  value redacted  ·  only one company can do it  ·  U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement
Akima Infrastructure Protection, LLC would get a modification to its existing detention services contract for a temporary detention structure for up to 100 detainees, supporting medical and environmental infrastructure and armed guard services at the Batavia Service Processing Center. The agency says the company already provides related services there and that using multiple vendors would create operational and security risk. The justification states Enforcement and Removal Operations has "an increased need for detention beds in New York due to changes affecting the availability of state and local detention space."

General Services Administration picks the same contractor for a second security-systems upgrade across the White House Complex
Convergint Technologies LLC  ·  value redacted  ·  only one company can do it  ·  General Services Administration, Public Buildings Service
Convergint Technologies LLC would get a design-build follow-on to a 2020 contract, completed in 2024, to upgrade existing systems at the White House Complex and at Joint Base Anacostia-Bolling in Washington, D.C. Sites listed include the Dwight D. Eisenhower Executive Office Building, the Winder Building, 1724 F Street NW, the New Executive Office Building and 726 Jackson Place NW. The justification cites the company's existing security clearance and White House Complex experience as additional factors, and the notice describes the work as a head-end system upgrade.

State Department hires 65 contractors without competition after staffing gap it blames on retirements and USAID's absorption
Nakupuna Federal LLC  ·  $7,487,184 estimated (base period)  ·  urgent need, no time to compete  ·  Department of State
The Bureau of Global Acquisitions placed a sole-source order with Nakupuna Federal LLC for 65 contractor positions supporting acquisition and federal assistance work while it runs a replacement competition. The justification cites "unprecedented" retirements and the integration of the U.S. Agency for International Development, with a September 16 deadline, and says staff reductions limited the personnel available to address the coverage issue.
