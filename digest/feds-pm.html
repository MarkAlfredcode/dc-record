Top Story

Army put armed guards in Erbil, Iraq, to work on September 10 and justified skipping competition on October 8

The task order supports U.S. Army Central and the Operation Inherent Resolve task force, and its dollar value is redacted.

The Department of the Army's contracting command at Rock Island issued a notice to proceed on September 10, 2026 to GardaWorld Federal Services for armed guard services near Erbil International Airport, then awarded the work as an undefinitized task order under the Global Protective Services contract. The justification for an exception to fair opportunity, a post-award document, is dated October 8, 2026.

The Army says about 20 days remained to mobilize and obtain the approvals needed for armed performance, and chose GardaWorld as the vendor most immediately able to perform based on responses to a request for information. The requiring activity is U.S. Army Central and the Combined Joint Task Force – Operation Inherent Resolve. The value is redacted in the public document.

Also in today's justifications: a $152.8 million ceiling increase for CACI at the General Services Administration, and a bridge contract for Air Force fuel-system maintenance while a protest is pending at the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers.

Homeland Security extends court-ordered family reunification help to the International Organization for Migration, after the old contract ran out
International Organization for Migration  ·  value redacted  ·  urgent need, no time to compete  ·  U.S. Citizenship and Immigration Services
This is the follow-on to a 2021 sole-source contract set up to comply with a court order in the Miss L. v. Immigration and Customs Enforcement class action, which required the government to return separated families to the United States. The prior contract's options ran through September 12, 2026. The justification says the Secretary of Homeland Security decided in late fiscal year 2025 to move administration from U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement to U.S. Citizenship and Immigration Services, and that the agency lacked the roughly 120 days needed to compete the work, citing the risk of violating the settlement if services lapsed.

Army Corps of Engineers buys a sole-source bridge to keep Air Force fuel-system maintenance going while a protest is pending
Vendor redacted (incumbent)  ·  value redacted  ·  urgent need, no time to compete  ·  Department of the Army, U.S. Army Corps of Engineers Huntsville Engineering and Support Center
The justification covers up to 336 days of preventive and corrective maintenance and emergency repair for the North Atlantic Division–Air Force fuels program, starting September 29, 2026, the day after the current task order expires, with a 66-day base and three 90-day options. The Corps says the bridge is needed because protest litigation over the follow-on contract is still active and the successor contractor cannot begin transition until a notice to proceed is issued. The requiring activity is the Defense Logistics Agency's energy office.

General Services Administration lifts a task order ceiling by $152.8 million for CACI without a new competition
CACI  ·  $152,779,419 ceiling increase  ·  skipped the competition among existing contract holders  ·  General Services Administration
The agency's Federal Systems Integration and Management Center intends to raise the ceiling on a task order for intelligence, surveillance and reconnaissance capabilities and threat analysis support for an Army command-and-control center. It calls the increase a logical follow-on to an existing competed order, with scope unchanged but mission demand above estimates. The document says earlier internal funding realignments are exhausted, making a ceiling increase the only way to sustain operations.

Immigration and Customs Enforcement buys 366 reserved parking spaces for its Washington headquarters, citing the return-to-office order
Potomac Center CF, LLC  ·  value redacted  ·  only one company can do it  ·  U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement
The sole-source buy is for 366 spaces in the Potomac Center South underground garage at 550 12th Street SW in Washington, D.C. The agency says the spaces provide overflow capacity because of a surge in reasonable-accommodation requests and other authorized parking under the Return to In-Person Work executive order, and that the garage is the only facility with a secure connection to its headquarters and security modifications suited to an elevated threat environment that includes frequent protests.
