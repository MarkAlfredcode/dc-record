Top Story

Immigration and Customs Enforcement asks vendors to run 12 live artificial-intelligence applications and build autonomous software agents

A request for information read in full this evening describes an enterprise artificial-intelligence platform already supporting the agency's operational and investigative missions.

Immigration and Customs Enforcement, part of the Department of Homeland Security, is seeking industry input on operating, enhancing and designing its enterprise artificial-intelligence, data and technology strategy and architecture. The request for information (solicitation RFI-ICE_ADTS_Sep2026) was read in full today, so this is a document finding on a notice posted earlier. The attachment was posted to SAM.gov on September 30.

According to the document, the agency's STELLA platform includes "a portfolio of 12 fully deployed, production-ready AI applications (low, medium, and high complexity) currently actively supporting ICE's operational and investigative missions." It also describes an enterprise-wide, agency-branded generative-AI chatbot, and says governed data products are consumed from a Databricks Lakehouse.

The agency also says it is building toward autonomous software. Among its current initiatives is an "Enterprise Agentic Software Factory," and the request describes "a governed agentic orchestration and runtime layer that coordinates AI and software agents, including model abstraction and routing, task planning and decomposition, tool access, context and memory, budgets, and human escalation and stop conditions." The document also refers to processes for managing the agency's AI use case inventory, including high-impact evaluations and impact assessments.

The notice is a request for information, so no vendor has been chosen and the input gives no dollar value.

Sources Sought  ·  No value in the input  ·  Response deadline not given  ·  Department of the Air Force
The Air Force Life Cycle Management Center says it "intends to establish an agile, direct multi-award Other Transaction Agreement (OTA) under 10 U.S.C. § 4022 immediately and 10 U.S.C. § 4023 forthcoming" to fix spare-part shortages.
