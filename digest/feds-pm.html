ICE seeks a no-bid temporary detention structure for 100 detainees in Batavia, New York

The agency cites a loss of state and local detention space; the sole-source value is redacted.

U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement plans to modify its existing detention services contract with Akima Infrastructure Protection, LLC to add a temporary structure for up to 100 detainees, with medical infrastructure and armed guards, at the Batavia Service Processing Center. It says it has an increased need for detention beds in New York because of changes affecting state and local detention space and cooperation.

The agency says only the incumbent can do the work without duplicated cost and security risk, and calls the structure a temporary measure while longer-term capacity in the Buffalo area is arranged. The value is redacted in the public document.

Separately, the General Services Administration is raising the ceiling on an Army intelligence support task order held by CACI by $152,779,419 without new competition.

ICE gets a no-bid temporary detention structure for 100 detainees at its Batavia, New York facility
Akima Infrastructure Protection, LLC  ·  value redacted  ·  only one company can do it  ·  U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement (Department of Homeland Security)
U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement plans to modify its existing detention services contract with Akima Infrastructure Protection, LLC of Herndon, Virginia to add a temporary structure holding up to 100 detainees of all security levels, plus medical and environmental infrastructure and armed guard services, at the Batavia Service Processing Center. The agency says it has "an increased need for detention beds in New York due to changes affecting the availability of state and local detention space and cooperation with local detention facilities," and that using another vendor would duplicate cost and add operational and security risk. The justification describes the structure as a temporary solution while longer-term capacity for the Buffalo area is arranged; the dollar value is redacted.

Army intelligence support task order gets a $152.8 million ceiling increase without new competition
CACI  ·  $152,779,419 ceiling increase  ·  skipped the competition among existing contract holders  ·  General Services Administration, for the U.S. Army C5ISR Center
The General Services Administration is raising the ceiling on the Global Operational Support, Information Insight, and Threat task order, awarded to CACI on May 20, 2024 with a ceiling of $414,857,176, for the U.S. Army Combat Capabilities Development Command C5ISR Center. The justification calls it a logical follow-on and says demand for intelligence, surveillance, reconnaissance and counter-drone support exceeded estimates, citing the war in Ukraine, tensions involving Iran and drone incursions in the northeastern United States. It also says internal funding realignments "have been completely exhausted," leaving a ceiling increase as the only way to sustain operations.

ICE reserves 366 parking spaces near its Washington headquarters from a single garage, citing the return-to-office order
Potomac Center CF, LLC  ·  value redacted  ·  only one company can do it  ·  U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement (Department of Homeland Security)
U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement is the lead tenant of Potomac Center South at 550 12th Street SW in Washington, D.C., and wants 366 indoor spaces in its underground garage for government-owned vehicles from Potomac Center CF, LLC. The justification cites the executive order on returning to in-person work and says the garage offers emergency access and egress to ICE headquarters during protests and building lockdowns. For market research, officials phoned four nearby facilities; two SP+ locations could have accommodated the need but were under construction. The request went to one vendor by informal quote, and the value is redacted.

TSA's sole-source help desk award is cut back after a protest, and the agency will now compete it
Quasars  ·  value redacted  ·  only one company can do it  ·  Transportation Security Administration (Department of Homeland Security)
The Transportation Security Administration awarded Quasars, the incumbent, a five-year sole-source task order on August 20, 2026 for the Online Learning Center help desk, which serves about 70,000 users. After an agency-level protest, the justification says the agency "has taken corrective action to conduct a competitive procurement," and this modification shortens performance to no later than May 19, 2027 to keep services running meanwhile. This is an award made before competition that the agency is now partly unwinding.
Notice  |  J&A document
Court-ordered family reunification services go to the International Organization for Migration again because the government ran out of time to compete them
International Organization for Migration  ·  no value given  ·  urgent need, no time to compete  ·  U.S. Citizenship and Immigration Services (Department of Homeland Security)
U.S. Citizenship and Immigration Services is giving the International Organization for Migration a follow-on contract for support services tied to the Miss L. v. Immigration and Customs Enforcement settlement, under which families separated at the border are reunified in the United States. The incumbent contract began August 13, 2021 and ran through September 12, 2026 after option periods. The justification says the Secretary of Homeland Security decided in late fiscal year 2025 to move administration of the services to the agency, and that the timing of later decisions left too little time for a competitive procurement.
