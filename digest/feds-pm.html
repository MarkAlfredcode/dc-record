Top Story

Navy awards Raytheon $6.1 billion in missile deals without competition, citing the public interest

Three Standard Missile awards invoke a rarely used exception that requires the agency head's own determination and written notice to Congress.

The Naval Sea Systems Command awarded Raytheon Company a $5,905,358,647 multiyear contract on September 30 for fiscal 2026 production of the Standard Missile 6, without competition. The award notice, posted October 6, cites 10 U.S.C. 3204(a)(7). That is the public-interest exception, which lets an agency skip competition only on the agency head's own determination and with written notice to Congress.

Two more Raytheon awards in the same window cite the same exception. One is a $160,892,270 undefinitized contract action on September 24 to move the Standard Missile 6 Block IAU, which the notice calls "a critical obsolescence upgrade," toward full-rate production. The other is $69,051,583 on September 29 for Standard Missile 2 test equipment and Standard Missile 6 spares. Together the three total $6,135,302,500. None of the three notices has a justification document or the public-interest determination attached.

These are the largest of several no-bid missile awards in this edition. Strategic Systems Programs, the Navy office behind the Trident II D5 missile, added $194.1 million in sole-source fire-control work on the Nuclear-Armed, Sea-Launched Cruise Missile for General Dynamics Mission Systems. Separately, the Defense Logistics Agency plans to sole-source depot test equipment for Ukraine's PATRIOT air-defense system to Raytheon.

Immigration agency seeks an 'SI-powered' system to mine detainee calls, messages and video, and to build voice profiles
Sources Sought (request for information)  ·  Questions due October 16, 2026  ·  U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement, Department of Homeland Security
U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement wants information on an "Integrated SI-Powered Detention Communications and Video Intelligence Suite" for its detention facilities. According to the attached requirements, the system would correlate detainees' phone, messaging, tablet, video-visitation and camera data and would "create and improve voice profiles from authorized existing recordings without requiring a separate enrollment session, subject cooperation, or new recording hardware." It would also flag fights, crowd formation and person-down events and run automated headcounts against the roster. In a separate notice the same day, the agency's technology office defined SI as "Super Intelligence."
