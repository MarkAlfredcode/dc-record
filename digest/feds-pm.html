Top Story

Pentagon gave Marriott a no-bid deal to house 600 attendees at a Secretary of War conference, posted after the event ended

The sole-source justification and award notice for the September 29 to October 1 Washington conference both went public on October 13.

Washington Headquarters Services, part of the Department of Defense, awarded a sole-source contract to Marriott Hotel Services Inc. (spelled "Marriot" in the justification) for lodging and catering for 600 government attendees. They were attending a conference hosted by the Office of the Secretary of War at a single hotel in the Washington area. Check-in was September 29, 2026 and check-out October 1. The justification is dated September 21. The award notice lists $724,742, signed September 25. Both were posted October 13, after the stay was over. The justification itself redacts the dollar value and says only that it covers a three-day period of performance. It lists Marriott's address as 901 Massachusetts Avenue NW and says the contract will use Official Representation funds.

The agency's stated reason for not competing is that major Washington venues were already booked. It cites "pre-existing citywide bookings, recurring annual international summits, and large-scale national conventions," and says no other property could meet scale, schedule and security at once. It describes its market research as "cold calls" to high-capacity hotels. According to the document, all 600 people had to be housed "under one roof" for "operational security, force protection, and strict scheduling synchronization." The hotel also had to provide a dedicated, secure space for uniform distribution during attendee in-processing. The action was done as a simplified-procedures commercial-item sole source under the Revolutionary FAR Overhaul, citing 41 U.S.C. 1901.

NASA releases technical requirements for commercial space stations
Special Notice  ·  National Aeronautics and Space Administration (Johnson Space Center)
A technical-library data drop for the Commercial Low Earth Orbit Destination contract says NASA "seeks to purchase end-to-end services from a commercial service provider," including crew and cargo transportation. It requires the systems to be "two failure tolerant to catastrophic hazards and for a controlled Destination Deorbit." Another requirement says an unpiloted visiting spacecraft must autonomously abort its approach if its communications link to the station is lost. The file was posted October 9.
