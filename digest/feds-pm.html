Top Story

Marshals Service gives Coinbase an $8.1 million no-bid cryptocurrency custody deal after a theft at its previous contractor

The sole-source justification says the Coinbase award is urgent because the incumbent was ordered to stop work after an employee allegedly stole seized cryptocurrency.

The U.S. Marshals Service plans an 18-month, firm-fixed-price contract with Coinbase Inc. for custody, management and disposal of seized cryptocurrency in its Class 2-4 inventory. The justification puts the estimated value at $8,128,125 and invokes the urgent-need exception, "unusual and compelling urgency." The file name carries a September 14 date; the notice was posted today (notice, justification).

According to the justification, the prior Class 2-4 contract went to Command Services and Support Inc., and an unsuccessful bidder protested that award at the agency level and at the Government Accountability Office without success. The document says a stop-work notice was issued on January 23, 2026, after an employee of that contractor allegedly stole seized cryptocurrency, with a loss of "at least $46 million." The screening pass could not match that passage word for word, so the $46 million figure is a lead, not a verified fact.

The Marshals Service says Coinbase is "the only company with a current Authorization to Use (ATU) designation" that could perform without substantial interruption. The justification adds that such a designation typically takes six months or longer to obtain, per the screening pass, which could not verify that passage verbatim. USAspending shows Coinbase's first federal contract on record in May 2020.

Must read
NASA buys Artemis secondary-payload avionics from Space Vector without competition, citing urgency and a race with China
Space Vector LLC  ·  value redacted  ·  urgent need, no time to compete  ·  National Aeronautics and Space Administration
The National Aeronautics and Space Administration's Marshall Space Flight Center is buying Secondary Payload Avionics Units and engineering support for the Artemis IV and V missions, including a spare, from Space Vector LLC. The units control deployment of secondary payloads from the Space Launch System. The justification says Space Vector designed and built the units that flew on Artemis I and II and that any new supplier would add months, and it cites Executive Order 14369 on returning astronauts to the Moon by 2028. It states: "It is critical to the national interests of the United States... that the Artemis program return American astronauts to the Moon before China reaches this milestone."
