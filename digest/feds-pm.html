Top Story

CACI gets a $152.8 million sole-source ceiling increase on an Army support task order

The General Services Administration says internal funding realignments are exhausted and only a ceiling increase can sustain operations.

The General Services Administration, acting for the Army's C5ISR Center, posted a justification for an exception to fair opportunity that raises the ceiling on the Global Operational Support, Information Insight, and Threat task order held by CACI by $152,779,419. The task order was competitively awarded on May 20, 2024 with a ceiling of $414,857,176 and runs through May 2029.

The justification says earlier funding realignments are completely exhausted and an immediate ceiling increase is the only viable way to sustain operations.

Elsewhere in today's postings, two bridge contracts trace to protests: the Army Corps of Engineers wants a bridge for fuel-system maintenance, and the Navy wants a third bridge for Naval District Washington custodial services after two Government Accountability Office protests.

Army Corps of Engineers seeks sole-source bridge for fuel-system maintenance at 45 installations while a protest of the follow-on award is pending
Vendor not named in the posted document  ·  Value redacted  ·  Urgent need, no time to compete  ·  Department of Defense (U.S. Army Corps of Engineers, for the Defense Logistics Agency)
The Army Corps of Engineers Huntsville center wants a bridge contract of up to 336 days (a 66-day base plus three 90-day options) for preventive and emergency repair of the Air Force fuels program, starting September 29, 2026, right after the current task order expires on September 28. The justification says the bridge is needed to avoid a gap in service while protest litigation over the successor contract remains active; it adds that the incumbent already has the workforce, installation access and maintenance records to continue without a transition period.

U.S. Citizenship and Immigration Services sole-sources follow-on support for court-ordered family reunification of separated families
International Organization for Migration  ·  Value redacted  ·  Urgent need, no time to compete  ·  Department of Homeland Security
The follow-on covers help with parole and work-authorization applications, travel booking, pre-departure briefings and a help desk for families covered by the Miss L. v. Immigration and Customs Enforcement settlement. The incumbent contract, first awarded to the International Organization for Migration on August 13, 2021, ran through September 12, 2026 on option periods; the document says responsibility moved from Immigration and Customs Enforcement to the agency in late fiscal 2025, and that a lapse would violate the settlement.

Immigration and Customs Enforcement sole-sources 366 parking spaces near its D.C. headquarters, citing protests and the return-to-office order
Potomac Center CF, LLC  ·  Value redacted  ·  Only one company can do it  ·  Department of Homeland Security (U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement)
The agency would buy 366 underground spaces at the Potomac Center South garage in Washington, D.C. for government vehicles. The justification says the spaces are needed for overflow capacity because of a surge in accommodation requests and parking demand under the Executive Order on Return to In-Person Work, and says the garage provides emergency access and egress for employees during protests or building lockdowns.
