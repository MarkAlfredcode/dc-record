Top Story
National Fraternal Order of Police Refiles Nearly Three Years of Lobbying Disclosures in a Single Day
Eleven straight quarterly amendments, from the fourth quarter of 2023 through the second quarter of 2026, all posted to the Senate's lobbying disclosure database within a 24-hour window.

The National Fraternal Order of Police, a self-registered in-house lobbyist representing its own membership, amended eleven consecutive quarterly Lobbying Disclosure Act reports — covering the fourth quarter of 2023 through the second quarter of 2026 — all posted to lda.gov within roughly a day of each other (September 28-29, 2026). That is an unusually large retroactive batch: a filer correcting nearly three full years of quarterly disclosures at once, rather than the routine single-quarter amendment this database sees most days.

The underlying subject matter across those amended filings is consistent with the organization's long-running public priorities: the Thin Blue Line Act (H.R. 130/S. 459 in earlier Congresses, H.R. 378 in the current one), which would add killing or targeting a law-enforcement officer to the list of statutory death-penalty aggravating factors; the LEOSA Reform Act (H.R. 354/S. 1462, later H.R. 2243), expanding concealed-carry rights for active and retired law enforcement officers; the Social Security Fairness Act (H.R. 82/S. 597) and its implementation; and the 9/11 Responder and Survivor Health Funding Correction Act addressing a funding shortfall in the World Trade Center Health Program.

The Senate Office of Public Records' filing database exposes only the finished, re-filed document for each quarter through its API and print view — not a redline against the version it replaces — so it is not possible to verify from the source what specifically changed in each amendment (a lobbyist roster correction, an income/expense figure, an added issue code, or something else). Nothing in the filings themselves is presented as improper; LDA amendments are a routine compliance mechanism, and this note flags only the unusual size and timing of the batch, not any wrongdoing.
