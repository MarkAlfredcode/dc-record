Top Story
Former Labor Secretary Lori Chavez-DeRemer's termination filing and two new certificates of divestiture lead a quiet day for federal ethics disclosures
OGE's latest batch includes a Cabinet-level departure disclosure and fresh divestiture orders for a Surface Transportation Board member and an ambassador, while FARA, LDA, and House/Senate stock-trade filings stayed light.

The U.S. Office of Government Ethics posted 50 filings in the past several days, the most notable being Lori Chavez-DeRemer's termination financial disclosure, filed as she formally closes out her tenure as Secretary of the U.S. Department of Labor (she left the position on April 20, 2026, after previously serving as a U.S. Representative from Ohio). The same batch included two new certificates of divestiture — for Karen Hedlund, a Member of the Surface Transportation Board, and Joseph Burkhalter, the newly confirmed Ambassador to the Republic of Moldova — though OGE makes the underlying certificates available only by request, so only the fact of each divestiture is confirmed here, not the asset involved.

Elsewhere, DOJ's FARA system logged a single new foreign-agent registration (Hellerman Communications LLC) and no terminations. The Senate's lobbying-disclosure system logged 11 new K Street registrations and 84 additional quarterly activity filings, including six lobbying-relationship terminations — most notably Forbes-Tate dropping both Anywhere Real Estate Inc. and Rainbow Energy Center as clients in the same window. On the personal financial disclosure side, every House and Senate filing found in today's lookback window had already been reported in a previous edition, so there's nothing new to add there today.

OGE Ethics Filings
Former Labor Secretary Lori Chavez-DeRemer's termination disclosure headlines a batch of new OGE filings that also includes two fresh certificates of divestiture

The U.S. Office of Government Ethics posted 50 filings in its September 27-30 window, 40 of them newly reported since the last check. Nine were flagged for a closer look, including a termination financial disclosure from former Labor Secretary Lori Chavez-DeRemer and new certificates of divestiture for a Surface Transportation Board member and an ambassador to Moldova. The rest of the flagged items were an annual disclosure, a periodic transaction report, and four ethics-agreement compliance certifications from other Trump administration appointees; the remaining 31 new filings were routine annual reports and periodic transaction reports listed below by agency.

  • Lori Chavez-DeRemer, who served as Secretary of the U.S. Department of Labor until her termination date of April 20, 2026, filed a termination financial disclosure report listing her assets and transactions as of her departure. Termination Report.
  • Karen Hedlund, a Member of the Surface Transportation Board, received Certificate of Divestiture OGE-2026-159; only the fact of the divestiture is confirmed, not the asset, since the certificate itself is available only by request from OGE.
  • Joseph Burkhalter, confirmed as Ambassador to the Republic of Moldova (U.S. Department of State), received Certificate of Divestiture OGE-2026-158; only the fact of the divestiture is confirmed, not the asset, since the certificate itself is available only by request from OGE.
  • Stephen A. Vaden, Deputy Secretary of the U.S. Department of Agriculture and a former federal judge on the U.S. Court of International Trade, filed his 2026 annual financial disclosure. It reports individual stock holdings including Alphabet, Amazon, Microsoft, Meta Platforms, and JPMorgan Chase, mutual and index fund positions worth up to $500,000, and roughly 20 residential and commercial real estate parcels plus three farm-land tracts in Obion County, Tennessee, and Fulton County, Kentucky. Annual Report.
  • Scott A. Kupor, Director of the Office of Personnel Management, filed a periodic transaction report disclosing the sale of Erasca Inc. common stock (ERAS) and Revolution Medicines Inc. common stock (RVMD), each valued between $1,001 and $15,000, on July 30, 2026. Periodic Transaction Report.
  • Johnny Teehee, U.S. Marshal for the Eastern District of Oklahoma (U.S. Department of Justice), certified compliance with his ethics agreement, including a September 16, 2026 conflict-of-interest authorization under 5 C.F.R. § 2635.502(d) covering matters involving the Muskogee Police Department. Certification of Ethics Agreement Compliance.
  • William Long, U.S. Ambassador to Iceland (U.S. Department of State), certified compliance with his ethics agreement; a reviewer at the U.S. Office of Government Ethics added a comment noting his agreement required no resignations and that his response to that question should have been marked "N/A." Certification of Ethics Agreement Compliance.
  • Jason Holt, U.S. Marshal for the Northern District of Oklahoma (U.S. Department of Justice), certified compliance with his ethics agreement, including a September 16, 2026 conflict-of-interest authorization under 5 C.F.R. § 2635.502(d) covering matters involving the Oklahoma Department of Public Safety. Certification of Ethics Agreement Compliance.
  • Mark Cruz, Director of the Indian Health Service within the U.S. Department of Health and Human Services, certified compliance with his ethics agreement. Certification of Ethics Agreement Compliance.

LDA Registrations & Activity
Eleven new K Street registrations filed, including a drone-research pitch for Zenadrone and a DLA Piper filing tied to a Senate resolution against an EPA maritime-emissions rule

The Senate's lobbying disclosure system logged 11 new lobbying registrations and 84 additional quarterly activity reports, amendments, and terminations in the September 29-30 window (95 filings total). Six registrants terminated existing lobbying relationships, including Soleno Therapeutics, Inc., the Ohio Hospital Association, and Americans for the Arts ending their own in-house lobbying, and Forbes-Tate dropping both Anywhere Real Estate Inc. and Rainbow Energy Center as clients. One new registration — DLA Piper LLP (US) on behalf of Stax Engineering, Inc., a maritime emissions-capture operator — names a specific piece of pending legislation, S.J. Res. 209, a joint resolution disapproving an Environmental Protection Agency rule related to California vessel/port emissions requirements.
