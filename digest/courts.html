Top Story

A Visa Mandamus Suit Also Names a Sitting U.S. Senator as a Defendant

Most of today's new District of Columbia federal filings were routine, but one immigration mandamus case stands out for an unusual defendant list.

Yash Sanjaykumar Patel filed a petition for a writ of mandamus in the U.S. District Court for the District of Columbia, No. 1:26-cv-03275, seeking to compel action on what the court's docket classifies as an immigration matter (nature-of-suit code 465, "Immigration: Other Immigration Actions," brought under 28 U.S.C. § 1361). The petition names Secretary of State Marco Rubio and U.S. Citizenship and Immigration Services Director Joseph B. Edlow as defendants — the standard targets in the wave of mandamus suits filed against State Department and USCIS officials over delayed visa, green-card, and naturalization decisions.

Also named as defendants, according to the docket's own party list: Reed D. Rubinstein, John Armstrong, Mora Namdar, and Laura Williams — additional State Department officials — and, unusually, U.S. Senator Markwayne Mullin.

Naming a sitting senator as a defendant is atypical for this kind of suit: mandamus relief under 28 U.S.C. § 1361 runs against a federal officer with a clear duty to act, and a member of Congress ordinarily has no adjudicatory role over an individual immigration case. No complaint has been posted to RECAP for this docket as of this writing, so it isn't possible to confirm from the filing itself why Senator Mullin is named or what role, if any, his office is alleged to have played.

The case is one of at least three immigration-delay suits filed in the District Court for the District of Columbia over the past three days naming Secretary Rubio or Director Edlow — see New Litigation, below, for the other two.
