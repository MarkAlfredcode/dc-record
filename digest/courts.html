Top Story

No New Cabinet-Agency or D.C. Government Litigation Survived Today's Screen

Twenty-one new civil filings turned up in D.D.C. since Friday, all dated the same day, but none named a cabinet department, a cabinet-level official in an official capacity, or the District of Columbia government as a qualifying party.

This edition reviewed every new civil case filed in the U.S. District Court for the District of Columbia since September 18, 2026 — 21 filings in all, every one of them dated September 18. After collapsing duplicate document rows to one per docket and dropping the 12 dockets already logged in prior editions, nine dockets remained for fresh review. None qualified for coverage here.

Four were private-party disputes with no government party at all: consumer and employment claims against companies including Aramark, American Express National Bank, and Celestial Seasonings, Inc., plus a trademark-style dispute brought by the Veterans of Foreign Wars of the United States against a private veterans-benefits consulting firm, VA Claims Insider, LLC.

Two more were cases this edition has already screened out once before, on an earlier day's filing date: Sims v. Paoletta, an employment discrimination suit naming Mark R. Paoletta, general counsel of the Office of Management and Budget — a subordinate legal officer, not a cabinet department head — and Judicial Watch, Inc. v. Office of the Director of National Intelligence, a FOIA suit against an agency that is not one of the 15 executive-branch cabinet departments this edition tracks.

The remaining two were a duplicate docket entry for the same matter, United States v. Thurston (1:26-mj-00163), a magistrate proceeding excluded by design, and Power the Future v. National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration, whose case caption and docket record name only the sub-agency rather than the U.S. Department of Commerce or a cabinet official, so it does not clear this edition's screening bar even though the agency sits inside a cabinet department organizationally.

New Litigation

A second quiet day for cabinet-agency and D.C. government cases

Of the nine newly evaluated D.D.C. filings since the last edition, none named a U.S. cabinet department, a cabinet-level official sued in an official capacity, or the District of Columbia municipal government as a party under this edition's screening rules.

  • Veterans of Foreign Wars of the United States v. VA Claims Insider, LLC (1:26-cv-03284) — a private dispute over a veterans-benefits consulting business; no government party.
  • Ross v. Aramark (1:26-cv-03280) — a private employment/consumer claim; no government party.
  • Walton v. Celestial Seasonings, Inc. (1:26-cv-03276) — a private consumer claim; no government party.
  • Blackett-Dibinga v. American Express National Bank (1:26-cv-03268) — a private consumer claim; no government party.
  • Sims v. Paoletta (1:26-cv-03282) — an employment discrimination suit against Mark R. Paoletta, general counsel of the Office of Management and Budget; he is a subordinate legal officer, not a cabinet department head, so it falls outside this edition's scope.
  • Judicial Watch, Inc. v. Office of the Director of National Intelligence (1:26-cv-03277) — a FOIA suit against the Office of the Director of National Intelligence, which is not one of the 15 executive-branch cabinet departments this edition tracks.
  • Power the Future v. National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration (1:26-cv-03266) — the docket caption and available record name only the sub-agency, not the Department of Commerce or a cabinet official, so it is excluded under this edition's literal-marker screening rule.
  • United States v. Thurston (1:26-mj-00163) — a magistrate proceeding, out of scope by design (indexed twice under two separate docket IDs for the same matter).

21 new civil filings were reviewed in total; 12 of those dockets had already been evaluated and logged in a prior edition, and the remaining 9 were excluded above as private-party disputes, a non-cabinet federal agency, a subordinate (non-agency-head) federal official, or a magistrate proceeding out of scope.
