Top Story

No New Cabinet-Agency or D.C. Government Litigation Survived Today's Screen

Twenty-seven new civil filings turned up in D.D.C. since Thursday, but none named a cabinet department, a cabinet-level official in an official capacity, or the District of Columbia government as a party.

This edition reviewed every new civil case filed in the U.S. District Court for the District of Columbia since September 17, 2026 — 27 filings in all after collapsing duplicate document rows down to one per docket. None qualified for coverage here.

Most were private disputes with no government party at all: employment and consumer claims against companies like American Express National Bank, USAA Federal Savings Bank, and Aramark, plus a trademark-style suit by the Veterans of Foreign Wars of the United States against a private company called VA Claims Insider, LLC. Two entries were the same magistrate proceeding (docket 1:26-mj-00163), which this edition excludes by design.

One filing came closer: a Title VII employment discrimination suit, Sims v. Paoletta, names Mark R. Paoletta, general counsel of the Office of Management and Budget, as defendant. He is a subordinate legal officer, not the head of a cabinet department or agency, so the case falls outside this edition's cabinet-official/agency-head scope and is not covered.

A new FOIA suit, Judicial Watch, Inc. v. Office of the Director of National Intelligence, was also screened out: the Office of the Director of National Intelligence is not one of the 15 executive-branch cabinet departments this edition tracks, so it does not meet this edition's cabinet-agency threshold even though Judicial Watch, Inc. is a frequent filer against departments that do qualify.
