Top Story

Conservation Group Sues Interior Secretary Doug Burgum Over Fish and Wildlife Service Action

WildEarth Guardians names the Interior secretary, a Fish and Wildlife Service director, and the agency itself in a new environmental citizen suit.

WildEarth Guardians, a Santa Fe, New Mexico-based conservation nonprofit, filed suit September 15, 2026 in the U.S. District Court for the District of Columbia against Doug Burgum, in his official capacity as U.S. Secretary of the Interior; Brian Nesvik, in his official capacity as director of the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service; and the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service itself. The case is WildEarth Guardians v. Burgum, No. 1:26-cv-03215.

The docket classifies the case as an "Environmental Matters" action under the cause 33 U.S.C. § 1365 — the citizen-suit provision most often invoked in Clean Water Act and Endangered Species Act enforcement litigation, which lets private parties sue federal officials directly over alleged violations of environmental statutes rather than waiting on agency enforcement.

The complaint itself is not yet available through CourtListener's RECAP archive — it is marked as not publicly accessible, meaning it sits behind PACER's paywall — so the specific agency decision or inaction WildEarth Guardians is challenging isn't yet known from the docket alone. This item will be updated if the complaint becomes available.

The suit is notable chiefly for naming a sitting cabinet secretary directly as a defendant, which is unusual even among the routine volume of agency litigation that runs through this court daily.
