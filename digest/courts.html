Top Story

No New Cabinet-Agency or D.C. Litigation Clears Today's Filters

A fresh check of the federal docket in Washington found new filings, but none naming a cabinet department, a cabinet official, or the District of Columbia government as a party.

Today's check of the U.S. District Court for the District of Columbia's civil docket covered filings from September 16 through September 19, 2026, and turned up 15 case entries not previously logged in this edition's tracking file. None of them qualified as litigation involving a cabinet-level federal agency or official, or the District of Columbia municipal government, under this edition's scope rules.

Most of the new entries were private-party civil suits with no government party at all — contract, employment, and pension disputes against companies including USAA Federal Savings Bank, American Express National Bank, and Aramark Services, Inc. One filing was a sealed grand jury subpoena nondisclosure application brought by federal prosecutors, a routine criminal-investigation tool rather than a substantive suit against agency policy, and so is treated here like other criminal-track filings. Two entries were duplicate docket records tied to the same sealed magistrate case and excluded on that basis alone.

A handful of borderline filings did not meet the bar: a nonprofit veterans' organization's suit against a private consulting firm, an advocacy group's suit against the National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration (a sub-agency of the Department of Commerce, but not named as such in the caption), and a federal employment-discrimination suit against an individual official identified only by surname in the docket caption. None of these named a cabinet department, secretary, or the District of Columbia government by the markers this edition uses to screen matches, so none is written up below.

No cases were added to this edition's tracking manifest today as a result.

New Litigation

Today's Filings Were Private Disputes, Not Agency Litigation

Of the new docket entries reviewed today, none involved a cabinet-level department, a cabinet official sued in an official capacity, or the District of Columbia government as a party.

15 new D.D.C. civil docket entries were reviewed and none qualified: private-party contract, employment, and pension suits against corporate defendants; a nonprofit's suit against a private consulting firm; an advocacy group's suit against a Commerce Department sub-agency not identified by a cabinet marker in the caption; a federal employment-discrimination suit naming only an individual official by surname; and a sealed grand jury subpoena nondisclosure application treated as a criminal-track matter.

FOIA Litigation

No New FOIA Suits Against a Cabinet Agency or the District of Columbia Today

One new Freedom of Information Act suit was filed in this window, but it does not meet this edition's cabinet-agency scope.

Judicial Watch, Inc. filed a new FOIA suit against the Office of the Director of National Intelligence, but that office is not one of the 15 cabinet departments this edition tracks and the caption named no cabinet official, so it falls outside this edition's scope and is not counted here.
