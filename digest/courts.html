Top Story

A quiet stretch for cabinet-agency and D.C. litigation in federal court

Nothing filed in the U.S. District Court for the District of Columbia over the past three days met this edition's bar for a cabinet-level or District of Columbia government case worth featuring.

Two new civil suits filed against the federal government survived this edition's screening criteria over the past three days, but neither can be characterized beyond its docket caption: PACER has not yet coded a nature-of-suit or cause of action for either filing, and no complaint is available in the RECAP archive for either one.

No new lawsuit against a named cabinet department, a cabinet secretary, or the District of Columbia municipal government cleared the bar for individual coverage today. A handful of other filings in the lookback window named federal defendants — including two Freedom of Information Act suits against the United States Postal Service — but the Postal Service is an independent establishment, not one of the fifteen cabinet-level departments this edition tracks, so those suits are outside its scope.

See the New Litigation and FOIA Litigation sections below for the full rundown, including the two bare-bones suits against the United States and a note on why no new FOIA case against a covered agency appears today.

New Litigation

Two suits against the United States, with nothing yet on the docket to explain them

Two individual plaintiffs filed suit directly against the federal government in the U.S. District Court for the District of Columbia this week. Neither docket has been coded with a nature-of-suit category or a cause of action by the court yet, and no complaint has been made available in RECAP, so the underlying claims cannot be described beyond the case captions.

Perna v. United States of America (No. 1:26-cv-03230, filed Sept. 15, 2026) and Johnston v. United States of America (No. 1:26-cv-03228, filed Sept. 15, 2026) — both filed by individual plaintiffs; nature of suit and cause not yet listed in PACER, complaint not available in RECAP.

FOIA Litigation

No new FOIA suits against a covered agency today

No new Freedom of Information Act lawsuit against a cabinet-level federal agency or the District of Columbia government was filed in the U.S. District Court for the District of Columbia in the past three days.

Two FOIA suits were filed against the United States Postal Service during the period — American Oversight v. United States Postal Service (No. 1:26-cv-03223) and Project on Government Oversight, Inc. v. United States Postal Service (No. 1:26-cv-03217) — but the Postal Service is an independent establishment rather than a cabinet-level department, so they fall outside this edition's scope and are not counted as new FOIA litigation here.

Coverage window: New civil case filings in the U.S. District Court for the District of Columbia dated September 13–15, 2026, checked against a persisted dedupe manifest so no case already reported in a prior edition appears twice.

Source: CourtListener/RECAP, U.S. District Court for the District of Columbia only.

This edition covers only new filings in the U.S. District Court for the District of Columbia (D.D.C.). A suit against a cabinet agency filed in a different federal district — for example, a facility-specific suit filed in a border-state court — will not appear here, nor will purely local District of Columbia litigation filed in D.C. Superior Court or the D.C. Court of Appeals, which is not on PACER/RECAP at all. A later phase is expected to add nationwide coverage of cases naming a cabinet agency or the District of Columbia filed outside D.D.C. Independent federal agencies such as the U.S. Postal Service are outside this edition's cabinet-level scope even when sued directly.
