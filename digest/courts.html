Top Story

Pardoned January 6 defendants file a wave of tort claims against the government that prosecuted them

Four separate suits filed within minutes of each other in D.D.C. name former Attorney General Merrick Garland and other Justice Department officials as defendants.

Four Federal Tort Claims Act lawsuits were filed the same afternoon in the U.S. District Court for the District of Columbia on September 25, 2026, each naming the United States of America as defendant: Eisenhart v. United States of America (1:26-cv-03350), Harrelson v. United States of America (1:26-cv-03351), Schwartz v. United States of America (1:26-cv-03352), and Nester v. United States of America (1:26-cv-03353). The four consecutive docket numbers point to a coordinated batch of filings rather than unrelated cases arriving by coincidence.

Three of the plaintiffs — Kenneth Harrelson, Lisa Eisenhart, and Peter Schwartz — were each convicted for their roles in the January 6, 2021 attack on the U.S. Capitol and later received clemency from President Trump as part of his sweeping day-one pardons and commutations for January 6 defendants. A fourth plaintiff, Lynnwood Nester, filed an identically structured suit against the same categories of defendants; the docket does not establish his connection to the events of January 6. All four suits invoke the Federal Tort Claims Act, 28 U.S.C. § 2671, which allows private parties to sue the federal government over the conduct of its employees.

The docket party lists for three of the four suits name former Attorney General Merrick Garland and former U.S. Attorney for the District of Columbia Matthew Graves — the two officials who oversaw the Justice Department's prosecutions of January 6 defendants under the Biden administration — along with unidentified "John Doe" FBI agents. Eisenhart's suit additionally lists Donald W. Washington and Robert Anthony Dixon as parties; the docket does not describe their specific roles.

None of the four complaints are available through CourtListener's free RECAP archive — each is marked as PACER-paywalled with no public copy uploaded — so this account rests on the docket captions, cause of action, and listed parties only. The specific injuries alleged and damages sought are not confirmed by anything publicly accessible as of this writing.

New Litigation

A visa fight pulls in the FBI director and the deputy attorney general

Apart from the tort claims detailed above, new D.D.C. litigation against cabinet-level officials was otherwise light in this window. One case stood out for its unusual roster of named defendants; two more were narrower single-agency filings.

  • Sandeep Shrikant Shukla filed an Administrative Procedure Act suit, Shukla v. Rubio (1:26-cv-03332), naming Secretary of State Marco A. Rubio as lead defendant. The docket's party list also includes FBI Director Kash Patel, Deputy Attorney General Todd Blanche, and White House personnel official Sergio Gor. The complaint itself is not freely available through CourtListener's RECAP archive, so it is not possible to confirm each official's specific role in the case or what relief Shukla is seeking.

One additional mandamus suit, Gerardo Fuad Bendeck Gonzalez v. Mullin (1:26-cv-03356), was filed over a delayed immigration adjudication and names U.S. Citizenship and Immigration Services Director Joseph B. Edlow among the defendants. A self-represented plaintiff, Furgiuele, separately sued the U.S. Department of State (1:26-cv-03338); the docket does not yet list a nature-of-suit code for that case, and no complaint is publicly available to clarify the claim.

FOIA Litigation

A law firm, not a repeat filer, sues the Agriculture Department for records

New FOIA litigation against a cabinet-level agency was minimal in this window; only one case met this edition's scope.

  • Metropolitan Law Group, PLLC sued the U.S. Department of Agriculture (1:26-cv-03349) under the Freedom of Information Act. The firm is not among the handful of repeat FOIA filers this edition typically sees, but its complaint is not freely available through CourtListener's RECAP archive, so the specific records it is seeking are not confirmed by anything publicly accessible.

No other new FOIA suits against a cabinet-level agency or the District of Columbia government were filed in this window. Two other new FOIA suits touched federal agencies but fell outside this edition's scope: one against the Central Intelligence Agency and one against the National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration, neither of which is a cabinet department.
