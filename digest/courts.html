Top Story

Democratic Campaign Committees Sue ICE, DHS and FBI Over Alleged Plan to Station Armed Agents at Polling Places

The suit, filed days before the 2026 general election, asks a federal judge to bar armed federal officers from voting sites nationwide.

On September 23, 2026, the Democratic Senatorial Campaign Committee, the Democratic Congressional Campaign Committee, the Democratic Governors Association, Senate Minority Leader Charles E. Schumer and House Minority Leader Hakeem S. Jeffries sued U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement, the U.S. Department of Homeland Security and the Federal Bureau of Investigation, along with three officials sued in their official capacities — Acting ICE Director David Venturella, Secretary of Homeland Security Markwayne Mullin, and FBI Director Kashyap Patel — in the U.S. District Court for the District of Columbia.

The complaint alleges that senior administration officials have repeatedly declined to disavow what it calls a "Polling Place Policy," under which armed federal agents could be stationed at or near polling places during the 2026 general election, citing public remarks including one from President Trump that he would "do anything necessary" to hold what he called an "honest election."

Plaintiffs argue the policy would violate Section 11(b) of the Voting Rights Act's ban on intimidating, threatening or coercing voters, and would be arbitrary and capricious under the Administrative Procedure Act, because the mere prospect of encountering armed, masked federal agents at a polling place would by itself deter voters — particularly naturalized citizens who fear being questioned about their immigration status — from casting ballots.

The suit asks the court to declare the policy unlawful, set it aside under the Administrative Procedure Act, and enjoin the defendants from stationing or threatening to station armed officers at any polling place, drop box or curbside voting location, from early voting through any post-election ballot-curing period.

It marks a notable departure from how party committees typically wield the Voting Rights Act — against state election administrators — turning it instead against federal law-enforcement agencies just ahead of the 2026 midterms.

Read the complaint: Democratic Senatorial Campaign Committee, et al. v. U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement, No. 1:26-cv-03330 (D.D.C. filed Sept. 23, 2026)
