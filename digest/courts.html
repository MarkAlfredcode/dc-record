District of Columbia Sued Over $97,483 in Special-Education Attorneys' Fees

A quiet day for cabinet-agency and District litigation produced a single new case matching this edition's scope: a fee-recovery suit against the District of Columbia government.

Chabrina Williams, individually and as parent and next friend of her minor child, identified in the complaint only as C.N., sued the District of Columbia on September 28, 2026, in the U.S. District Court for the District of Columbia, seeking $97,483.36 in attorneys' fees and costs. The complaint names the District as a municipal corporation and lists Muriel Bowser, the Mayor of the District of Columbia, and Brian Schwalb, the Attorney General for the District of Columbia, only as the officials to be served with process — the lawsuit is not against them individually, and the sole defendant is the District government itself.

The case is a fee petition, not a fresh dispute over the underlying facts. According to the complaint, Williams and C.N. filed an administrative complaint against the District on April 21, 2025, alleging the District failed to implement C.N.'s Individualized Education Program, including missed occupational therapy and speech-language services during the 2023-2024 school year and missed behavioral-support services during the 2024-2025 school year, in violation of the Individuals with Disabilities Education Improvement Act. After a three-day hearing held July 21, July 22, and August 4, 2025, a hearing officer ruled for the family on August 21, 2025, ordering the District to place C.N. in a full-time special-education program and fund 60 hours of speech-language pathology, 55 hours of behavioral-support services, and 25 hours of occupational therapy as compensatory education.

Having prevailed at that hearing, Williams is now invoking the fee-shifting provision of the Individuals with Disabilities Education Improvement Act, 20 U.S.C. § 1415(i)(3)(B), which lets a prevailing parent recover reasonable attorneys' fees from the school district. She is represented by James E. Brown & Associates, PLLC, a District of Columbia law firm that specializes in special-education law and regularly files fee-recovery suits like this one against the District on behalf of families who prevailed at the administrative level; the mechanism is procedurally routine, though the dollar figure sought here, nearly $97,500, is larger than most.

Read the complaint: Williams v. District of Columbia, No. 1:26-cv-03361 (D.D.C., filed Sept. 28, 2026)
