Top Story

Energy nonprofit sues OMB and Energy Department over records on a discredited climate-warming scenario

Two FOIA suits filed the same day both seek the same thing: paper trails showing how federal scientists used the abandoned "RCP 8.5" warming scenario in official climate assessments.

Power the Future, a Washington-based nonprofit that says it monitors government activity on energy and environmental policy, filed two Freedom of Information Act lawsuits in the U.S. District Court for the District of Columbia on September 12, 2026, against the Office of Management and Budget and the United States Department of Energy. Both suits, filed by the same counsel, seek records concerning the same subject: how federal scientists relied on a climate-warming projection called "representative concentration pathway 8.5," or RCP 8.5, that the complaints describe as since discredited and abandoned as a realistic forecast.

In Power the Future v. Office of Management and Budget, the group says it asked OMB on August 12, 2026, for the email correspondence of Dr. Bryan Parthum, an economist at the Environmental Protection Agency's National Center for Environmental Economics who was detailed to OMB as a senior economist in 2023 and 2024. The complaint says OMB acknowledged the request the same day, assigned it a tracking number, and then never made a determination on it. The suit says the records concern Dr. Parthum's use of RCP 8.5 as a "business-as-usual" reference scenario in the EPA's Framework for Evaluating Damages and Impacts, which feeds into federal climate-damage estimates.

In Power the Future v. United States Department of Energy, the group says it filed three separate FOIA requests between July 20 and August 12, 2026, for records belonging to Dr. Michael F. Wehner, described in the complaint as tied to the department's climate-science work, including his emails referencing RCP 8.5 and the federal government's Fourth National Climate Assessment. The complaint says the Energy Department confirmed receipt of all three requests through FOIA.gov but never made a determination on any of them, with response deadlines that passed on August 17, September 4, and September 10, 2026.

Both complaints, filed by attorney Matthew D. Hardin, ask the court to declare the agencies in violation of FOIA, order them onto a court-supervised production schedule with firm deadlines, and award attorney fees. Neither complaint accuses the agencies of wrongdoing beyond the FOIA processing failures themselves — the underlying aim, as both complaints put it, is to "inform the public about" the agencies' conduct and "the basis for federal climate policy." Read together, the two suits reflect a coordinated push by one advocacy group to force disclosure of the government's internal record on a specific, contested piece of climate modeling.
