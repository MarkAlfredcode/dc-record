Top Story

Civil rights coalition sues DHS, DOJ and FBI over armed agents near polling places ahead of 2026 midterms

The NAACP and eight other organizations accuse the administration of an intimidation campaign, from a nationwide noncitizen-voter dragnet to threats of federal law enforcement at polling sites, that they say violates the Voting Rights Act.

The National Association for the Advancement of Colored People, Mi Familia en Acción, OCA–Asian Pacific American Advocates, National Urban League, MN8, Service Employees International Union, American Federation of Teachers, United Food and Commercial Workers International Union and International Union of Painters and Allied Trades filed suit September 18, 2026 in the U.S. District Court for the District of Columbia against the U.S. Department of Homeland Security, the U.S. Department of Justice, and the Federal Bureau of Investigation, along with three officials sued in their official capacities: Markwayne Mullin as Secretary of Homeland Security, Todd Blanche as Attorney General, and Kash Patel as FBI Director. Also named are U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement and its acting director, David Venturella, and Thomas Homan, described in the complaint as White House Executive Associate Director of Enforcement and Removal Operations.

The 65-page complaint alleges the defendants have reversed longstanding policy against sending armed federal agents near voting locations and are running an "Unlawful Voter Initiative" that reassigns immigration agents to hunt for noncitizen voters nationwide using what the plaintiffs call flawed and incomplete data. It cites two fatal shootings of U.S. citizens by immigration agents in Minneapolis in January 2026 during an operation the complaint calls "Operation Metro Surge," and an episode in which the administration allegedly conditioned withdrawal of that deployment on a state turning over voter-roll data. The plaintiffs, who together say they represent millions of members including naturalized citizens and voters in mixed-status families, argue this conduct and rhetoric has already chilled their members' willingness to vote or help others vote ahead of the November 2026 midterm elections.

The single claim for relief invokes Section 11(b) of the Voting Rights Act of 1965, which bars intimidating, threatening or coercing anyone for voting or for aiding others to vote. The plaintiffs ask the court to declare that the defendants' conduct violates Section 11(b), to enjoin the defendants and anyone acting with them from deploying agents in a way that unlawfully intimidates voters or those helping voters, and to award attorney's fees and costs under the Equal Access to Justice Act.

The case is notable both for its scale — nine organizations representing constituencies across all fifty states — and for naming the FBI director and acting ICE director personally, in their official capacities, over election-season deployment plans rather than a specific enforcement action already carried out.

Read the complaint: National Association for the Advancement of Colored People et al. v. U.S. Department of Homeland Security et al., No. 1:26-cv-03263 (D.D.C. filed Sept. 18, 2026)
New Litigation

A trade group seeks an emergency injunction against Treasury; a wrongful-injury claim names a former attorney general

Beyond the voting-rights suit above, three more cases filed against cabinet agencies this week stand out enough to describe individually; the rest were either routine visa-delay filings or too thin on public detail to characterize beyond their docket captions.

  • Freedom Economy Business Association sued the U.S. Department of the Treasury, Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent, the Community Development Financial Institutions Fund, and an individual identified in the docket only as Chris Miller, filing both a complaint for declaratory and injunctive relief and an emergency motion for a temporary restraining order and preliminary injunction the same day, September 21, 2026. The docket classifies it as an Administrative Procedure Act challenge to agency action; the complaint itself is not yet publicly available, so the specific Treasury or CDFI Fund decision being challenged is not yet known. Freedom Economy Business Association v. U.S. Department of the Treasury, No. 1:26-cv-03288 (D.D.C. filed Sept. 21, 2026) — complaint text not available, docket caption only.
  • Core Care Technologies, Inc. sued Robert F. Kennedy, Jr., the U.S. Secretary of Health and Human Services, on September 18, 2026. The docket shows a complaint and an electronic summons issued to the U.S. Attorney's office and the Attorney General's office, the standard service pattern for suits naming a federal officer in an official capacity, but carries no nature-of-suit or cause code and the complaint itself is not available. Core Care Technologies, Inc. v. Kennedy, Jr., No. 1:26-cv-03283 (D.D.C. filed Sept. 18, 2026) — complaint text not available, docket caption only.
  • Joseph Lino Padilla brought a Federal Tort Claims Act personal-injury claim against the United States of America and several individually named officials, including former Attorney General Merrick Garland, former U.S. Attorney for the District of Columbia Matthew Graves, U.S. Marshals Service Director Donald W. Washington, Kathleen Landerkin, Robert Anthony Dixon, and twenty-five unnamed "John Doe" defendants. The underlying complaint was filed under seal, so the specific injury alleged is not public. Lino Padilla v. United States of America, No. 1:26-cv-03281 (D.D.C. filed Sept. 18, 2026) — complaint sealed, docket caption only.

Also filed against cabinet agencies this week, with no complaint text yet available and nothing in the docket caption to suggest an unusual claim: a Title VII employment-discrimination suit by Makebah Childress against Secretary of Defense Pete Hegseth (No. 1:26-cv-03285); a suit by Aven against the Office of Foreign Assets Control at the U.S. Department of the Treasury (No. 1:26-cv-03273); and pro se, in-forma-pauperis complaints against the U.S. Department of the Army (Newton, No. 1:26-cv-03291), the United States Patent and Trademark Office (Dominguez, No. 1:26-cv-03290, which includes a temporary-restraining-order motion), and the United States itself (Rae, No. 1:26-cv-03279, and Curzio, No. 1:26-cv-03278, both filed partly under seal).

Three new suits sought court intervention over delayed visa, green-card, or naturalization adjudications, naming Secretary of State Marco Rubio and U.S. Citizenship and Immigration Services: Abdul v. United States Citizenship and Immigration Services (No. 1:26-cv-03297) and two unrelated cases both captioned Patel v. Rubio (Nos. 1:26-cv-03275 and 1:26-cv-03265).
