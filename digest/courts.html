Top Story

Justice Department Seeks Forfeiture of Over $5 Million in Cryptocurrency

A civil in rem action targets more than five million dollars in USDT, a dollar-pegged stablecoin, in one of the larger cryptocurrency forfeiture filings to hit the D.C. federal courthouse this year.

The U.S. Department of Justice filed a civil forfeiture action today in the U.S. District Court for the District of Columbia seeking to seize approximately $5,018,405.45 in USDT, a U.S. dollar-pegged cryptocurrency commonly known as Tether. The case, captioned United States v. Approximately 5,018,405.451351 USDT (case no. 1:26-cv-03301), was filed September 22, 2026, and assigned to U.S. District Judge Jia M. Cobb.

Civil forfeiture cases like this one proceed in rem — against the property itself rather than against a person — which is why the case caption names the cryptocurrency rather than an individual or company defendant. The government filed a companion motion the same day asking the court to issue a warrant authorizing arrest, or seizure, of the digital currency, the cryptocurrency equivalent of a warrant to seize physical evidence.

The underlying complaint is not available to read on PACER/RECAP (marked not publicly available, with no document filed to the free RECAP archive), so the specific allegations behind the seizure — whether the funds are tied to fraud, a sanctions violation, money laundering, or another offense — cannot be confirmed from the docket caption or entries alone.

The filing is notable mainly for its scale and asset class: cryptocurrency forfeiture actions of this size remain relatively uncommon filings in this court, and the case is worth watching as it moves toward a forfeiture judgment or a claimant's contest.
