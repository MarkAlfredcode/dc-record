Top Story

Government-transparency groups sue Trump, HHS Secretary Kennedy, Transportation Secretary Duffy and FBI Director Patel under the Administrative Procedure Act

American Oversight and the Campaign for Accountability filed the D.D.C. suit today; the complaint itself is not yet available on RECAP, so its specific claims cannot be confirmed.

American Oversight and the Campaign for Accountability, two Washington-based government-transparency nonprofits, filed suit today in U.S. District Court for the District of Columbia against President Donald J. Trump, White House aide Natalie Harp, White House Deputy Chief of Staff for Communications Daniel Scavino, Jr., the Executive Office of the President, the White House Office, Secretary of Health and Human Services Robert F. Kennedy, Jr., the U.S. Department of Health and Human Services, Secretary of Transportation Sean Duffy, the U.S. Department of Transportation, FBI Director Kash Patel and the Federal Bureau of Investigation (American Oversight v. Trump, No. 1:26-cv-03258).

The docket lists the case's cause of action as arising under the Administrative Procedure Act, 5 U.S.C. § 702, with a nature-of-suit code of 440 (Civil Rights: Other), and jurisdiction as a federal question.

The complaint itself is marked not available in CourtListener's RECAP archive as of this writing, meaning it is behind the PACER paywall and could not be read for this report. That leaves the specific agency action or policy being challenged unconfirmed; this item reports only what the docket caption and cause-of-action code show, not the underlying factual allegations.

The breadth of named defendants — spanning the White House, the Department of Health and Human Services, the Department of Transportation and the FBI — combined with two watchdog groups as plaintiffs makes this suit worth tracking as the docket develops and any complaint becomes available.
