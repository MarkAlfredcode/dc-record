Cabinet and D.C. Litigation Docket Stays Quiet After the Weekend

Only one new D.D.C. filing cleared this edition's cabinet-agency/District of Columbia filter over the three-day lookback window.

This edition checked new civil filings in the U.S. District Court for the District of Columbia from September 19 through September 21, 2026, a window that includes a filing-free weekend. Six new docket entries showed up in that stretch, but most fell outside this edition's scope.

Two of the six — suits naming the U.S. Department of the Treasury and the U.S. Department of the Navy — were already reported in the prior edition and are not repeated here. Three others did not name a cabinet-level department, a cabinet secretary, the Attorney General, or the District of Columbia government in the case caption, so they fell outside this edition's scope: an employment-discrimination suit against a U.S. Postal Service official, a habeas corpus petition, and a press-access lawsuit brought by several news organizations against President Trump and White House staff.

That left a single qualifying case: a routine suit by an individual plaintiff against U.S. Citizenship and Immigration Services, detailed in the New Litigation section below.
